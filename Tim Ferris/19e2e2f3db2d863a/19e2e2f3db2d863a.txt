Blog post I’m reading
---------------------

“Getting closer to Her: From Moshi to Thinking Machines Interaction Model ( https://click.fourhourmail.com/75u0qzo2qkh8h67lzdvszhw4wvqk6tnhz7r/m2h7h5h3pkzxnnsm/aHR0cHM6Ly9wb2x5bWF0aDcwNy5zdWJzdGFjay5jb20vcC9nZXR0aW5nLWNsb3Nlci10by1oZXItZnJvbS1tb3NoaQ== ).” How do we actually get to the Scarlett Johansson companion in the movie Her ( https://click.fourhourmail.com/75u0qzo2qkh8h67lzdvszhw4wvqk6tnhz7r/dpheh0hegznx7qfm/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuYW1hem9uLmNvbS9IZXItSm9hcXVpbi1QaG9lbml4L2RwL0IwMEtBVFkyNTAvP3RhZz1vZmZzaXRvZnRpbWZlLTIw )?
​
From the above piece: “Real-time voice assistants [have been] small and dumb because anything bigger could not respond inside a human’s patience window. Frontier reasoning models [have been] astonishing in benchmark tables, but their real-time voice versions [feel lacking].”
​
So what to do? Sure, you can try and cascade models together, but then you’re “adding latency, each handoff losing context, each silence between turns reminding you that you were talking to a machine.”
​
Diving back into the blog post:
​
​The interaction-model / background-model ( https://click.fourhourmail.com/75u0qzo2qkh8h67lzdvszhw4wvqk6tnhz7r/e0hph7h7g9ozn0c8/aHR0cHM6Ly94LmNvbS90aGlua3ltYWNoaW5lcy9zdGF0dXMvMjA1MzkzODg5MjE1MjQzNTE3ND9zPTIw ) split is the first credible answer to that triangle. By assigning the two halves of intelligence to different hardware on different latency budgets, the architecture sidesteps the trade-off rather than negotiating with it. The half that has to be present is allowed to be small, fast, multimodal, and continuously attentive — five times a second, no exceptions. The half that has to be smart is free to be enormous, slow, deliberative, and tool-using, because it is never on the critical path of the 200ms cadence. Neither side is asked to compromise on what it is good at. The user experiences both, fused.
​
What does that unlock concretely? Consider the experiences that have been technically promised for a decade and have never quite worked:
​
​…
​
​A cooking companion that watches your hands through your phone camera, calls out that the onions are about to burn, and pulls up a substitute when you realise you’re out of cumin — without any of those interactions feeling like separate apps. The visual perception is handled in the 200ms loop. The recipe knowledge, the substitution reasoning, the meal-planning context lives in the background.
​
...
​
A workplace assistant that is actually in the meeting with you, not transcribing it for later. It can interject with the relevant figure when someone mis-cites a number, surface the Slack thread that supersedes a decision being re-debated, and do all of that without the awkward “OK assistant, are you there?” preamble that makes today’s tools feel like talking to a Roomba.
​
…
​
The pattern is the same in every case: the experience becomes natural when being there and being smart stop competing for the same compute budget.
​
…
​
This is what people mean when they say voice is the next interface. It was never really about voice. It was about whether intelligence could show up in the room without making you wait for it. The split architecture ( https://click.fourhourmail.com/75u0qzo2qkh8h67lzdvszhw4wvqk6tnhz7r/e0hph7h7g9ozn0c8/aHR0cHM6Ly94LmNvbS90aGlua3ltYWNoaW5lcy9zdGF0dXMvMjA1MzkzODg5MjE1MjQzNTE3ND9zPTIw ) is the first proof that it can.
​
​Be sure to peruse the entire piece.
​
Not to sound like Yogi Berra ( https://click.fourhourmail.com/75u0qzo2qkh8h67lzdvszhw4wvqk6tnhz7r/7qh7h8h97v5e49fz/aHR0cHM6Ly9lbi53aWtpcGVkaWEub3JnL3dpa2kvWW9naV9CZXJyYSMlMjJZb2dpLWlzbXMlMjI= ), but the future is coming a lot sooner than expected.

“The first objective was to give as many students as possible an early sense of achievement within the first half hour. Using one of his many heuristic techniques—described as ‘mental hooks’—[Michel Thomas ( https://click.fourhourmail.com/75u0qzo2qkh8h67lzdvszhw4wvqk6tnhz7r/owhkhqhwv3de8giv/aHR0cHM6Ly9lbi53aWtpcGVkaWEub3JnL3dpa2kvTWljaGVsX1Rob21hcw== )] demonstrated to the class that they already possessed a French vocabulary of as many as three thousand words or more. And that these were not ‘baby’ words, but complex adult words, so they did not have to begin learning as children. He pointed out that most English words ending in -tion, -able, -ence and -ism were the same in French (condition, capable, experience, realism). And that many English words ending in ‘y’ had an ‘e’ in French: fraternity, paternity, liberty and so on.”
