Article I’m reading
-------------------

“Why Do Mind-Altering Drugs Make People Feel Better? (
https://click.fourhourmail.com/mvu9xermxzs5hq5vv68fmhr2785w3h3hlr9/08hwh9h2ekpn0zal/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cubmV3eW9ya2VyLmNvbS9jdWx0dXJlL2FubmFscy1vZi1pbnF1aXJ5L3doeS1kby1taW5kLWFsdGVyaW5nLWRydWdzLW1ha2UtcGVvcGxlLWZlZWwtYmV0dGVy
)” How do you break psychedelic molecules into parts, like cars
in a chop shop, and build new ones? This is a thought-provoking
New Yorker piece by Clayton Dalton (
https://click.fourhourmail.com/mvu9xermxzs5hq5vv68fmhr2785w3h3hlr9/8ghqhohodx7wekhk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuY2xheXRvbmRhbHRvbi5jb20v
), an ER doc and Ferriss-UC Berkeley Psychedelic Journalism
Fellow (
https://click.fourhourmail.com/mvu9xermxzs5hq5vv68fmhr2785w3h3hlr9/vqh3hrho0dqm2mug/aHR0cHM6Ly9mZWxsb3dzaGlwcy5qb3VybmFsaXNtLmJlcmtlbGV5LmVkdS9iY3NwLw==
).
​
I like that this is a controversial piece. I hope it helps drive
the science and conversation forward.
​
On one hand, the “neuroplastogen” approach is often driven by
business and financial incentives. If you can remove the
hallucinations from psychedelics, your ability to fit within
current medical structures (take-home meds, easier insurance
reimbursement, etc.) helps you to scale, scale, scale. And if
someone can make millions or billions of dollars by sticking to
their hypothesis, expect conflicts of interest and a biased path,
even with the best of intentions. To quote Charlie Munger: “Show
me the incentive, and I will show you the outcome.”
​
On the other hand, I completely agree with David Olson when he
says “If you absolutely need the hallucinogenic effects, then
these new compounds are not going to be the revolution in
psychiatry that people are hoping they will be.” At least, not
likely at the scale of millions of patients per year.
​
If I were a betting man, I’d say that the set and setting—and,
indeed, the trip content itself—seems to matter for applying
psychedelics to certain conditions like PTSD and alcohol-use
disorder. Otherwise, we’d all have friends with spontaneous
remission of these conditions when using drugs recreationally.
Personally, I have never witnessed this being the case, but I’d
be happy to be proven wrong.
​
At the same time, I’m funding research into the anti-inflammatory
effects of certain psychedelics, which are profound, even at
miniscule doses. I do think the “content matters” folks too
easily dismiss the incredible physiological changes attributable
to these compounds. So to take the opposite stance, I believe
that conditions like treatment-resistant depression and chronic
anxiety disorder may not always need the trip. It could be that
most such cases never need a trip. The jury is out.
​
We can all get attached to the binary extremes. As usual, the
truth is more likely somewhere in the middle.
