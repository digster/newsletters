Here’s How A Small Group Of Anti-Trans Influencers Hijacked Instagram Ahead Of The Midterms |
—by Josh Chapdelaine, Adam Bumas, Lester Feder, and Ryan Broderick |
We can’t always talk about the work that Garbage Day’s consulting arm, Garbage Day Media Intelligence (GDMI), does, but today, we’re pulling back the curtain on one important project we’ve been running this year. For the last six months, we’ve been working with the Transgender Law Center, a national organization promoting trans equality. Our research has taken down us the rabbit hole of anti-transgender content on short-form video platforms. And what we found surprised us. |

And according to our research, yes, users in these five states are, in fact, hearing about it constantly, usually in the form of paid content from Republican candidates and their allies. Organic content looks very different, and is not impacting every platform equally. A relatively small network of accounts on Instagram — most of which are connected to well-funded political groups — are responsible for the lion’s share of anti-trans content about the ballot initiatives. But these accounts are largely absent on TikTok, or get very little traction when they post there. And we’ve been trying to figure out why. |
Both TikTok and Instagram are controlled by oligarchs close to the Trump administration, so you’d assume both are seeing the same levels of anti-trans content. We initially thought that the difference might have something to do with the platforms’ algorithms. The former is a Frankenstein’d hand-me-down from ByteDance, the latter is Meta’s best approximation of how it thinks ByteDance’s algorithm used to work. |

Instagram referred us to Meta’s [public policies](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.ZE3yp4K2Y6WBUz6JXxBv2xo1PkY7DUIgJ64kds2UDYik2kqddwMip1_4lJqA60qE1oCyEqGfzhtfVbuAVOOpHKEZilQ0HQdqlGu_jyFy3tu1NpSdBY2kDHwCR0LAPvAIH6WF3W4-rrmvMeRwJCFFYhOa1HFm-GeqnQfzMm0qK0NWoh7ELNiK-FYz2WclS-VpLayBBC4uMu3vamEPRL6XrT8WeHjKrEqk43ljDWl2gKY5sl2NAhtyS1SERhipYrCxeMeVQGGvwS1Q8tbby9hXAJIDu_ORbzl7JWKwDiyc6ptq-2rrJCnevA9W-gHAGURnz4dPUgRYdICo-e5Xb9W0mw/4ve/q7-AwMlZS_iKyzZOBw3rwg/h12/h001.WO5XWqsVkuY02zdzOMdJcDu_xCsTvzfCLUjgA3IheLs), which says Meta “may recommend political content from accounts people don’t already follow.” We sent them follow-up questions about whether or not Instagram recommends content based on their location, and they just sent us a link to an FAQ page about how their algorithms work. TikTok was a little more forthcoming, thankfully. |
A spokesperson for TikTok US told Garbage Day that the only thing that determines what shows up on a user’s For You Page is how they interact with the app. Meaning that if a user isn’t actively watching or seeking out videos about these ballot measures, they won’t see more of them.
