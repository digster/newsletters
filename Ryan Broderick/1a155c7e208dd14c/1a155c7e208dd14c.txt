## The AI Companies All Know That Software Is About To Die

Over the weekend, the official Instagram page for the No Kings protest movement went offline for about 90 minutes, right as activists were mobilizing across the country. The idea that the outage’s timing was a coincidence feels impossible to accept, but that’s the story Meta is sticking to.

Meta’s head of communications, Andy Stone, on Sunday, [explained](https://x.com/andymstone/status/2111920686306627816) that the account was mistakenly flagged by Instagram’s system due to “abrupt, high-volume, sequential requests in a short period” that resembled bots. A spokesperson for No Kings [said on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@nokingscoalition/post/DenR0s0jsXI) that doesn’t make sense and that they had only posted a few times on Saturday.

Questions still abound about Meta’s little whoopsie, a bizarrely similar whoopsie to one [that happened](https://abcnews.com/International/wireStory/meta-denies-accusation-anti-lula-political-bias-brazil-137336666) during the first round of Brazil’s presidential election earlier this month, along with all the [other whoopsies](https://popular.info/p/meta-shuts-down-no-kings-account) around the world that Meta has been making the last few months. But I’m actually less interested in all that. Call me cynical, but I’ve long thought of Meta as a rogue state and they will continue to operate like one until they are smashed to pieces by regulators (if we ever have those again) or driven into bankruptcy if the AI bubble pops in a very specific way. Though, what I am far more interested in here is actually related to the latter.

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/05cd8091-e8ac-4f69-a21d-9c75e3731a93/GettyImages-2300224576.jpg?t=1792437395)
Caption: (Photo by Apu Gomes/Getty Images)

In Stone’s X post about the No Kings account, he said that Meta’s systems, “detected the use of a Claude tool reading profiles, posts, and hashtags, the rate of which sharply increased (~4x volume) in intense bursts on October 17.” In other words, Meta thought someone logged into the No Kings page and was browsing, or possibly scraping, Instagram with Claude and Meta has systems in place to ban users who do that. Huh! That sure is interesting. In fact, as far as I can tell, this is the first time Meta has publicly said that they ban accounts for browsing via an AI.

This appears to be a fairly new stance. Meta [spent](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/meta-microsoft-want-stop-employees-131000092.html) the summer trying to get their own engineers to use Claude less, but it seems like they are still allowed to use it. And, back in June, a few different Meta MCPs, or Model Context Protocols, were [listed](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/mcp) on Facebook’s developer page. An MCP is basically a little specialized connector between an app or a website and your AI interface of choice. Meta offers three, at the moment, one for Meta ads, one for developer tools, and one for WhatsApp Business. And all of them supposedly support Claude. Though, there’s [chatter](https://www.reddit.com/r/FacebookAds/comments/1wiqw1n/is_the_official_meta_mcp_really_safe_to_connect/) on the r/FacebookAds subreddit that Claude users have been banned using the MCP. Which wouldn’t be surprising.

So what could have happened between, let’s say, June and the No Kings march this weekend? Well, Meta just so happened to have [launched](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) their own AI agent, Muse, last month. Making Meta yet another company now both cashing in on the AI boom and, also, making sure they aren’t devoured by it, themselves.

All around the web, websites and platforms are trying to block AI from accessing their content. Delta, JP Morgan, Charles Schwab, and even Amazon have tried to build — to borrow the AI industry’s favorite buzzword — a moat that can block out pesky agents. And there are some practical reasons for this. As _The Atlantic_ [wrote last week](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/10/ai-agent-chaos-e-commerce/689010/?gift=nwn-guseqS6cY1kVeEKZAXUjKx5Mwv1ctbdC_UgaKHY), “Imagine every AI agent on Earth buying the same cheap paper towels.” Or, in Amazon’s case, imagine every AI agent on Earth scraping everyone’s orders and reverse-engineering their own e-commerce platform. This is why Amazon emails [don’t say](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/977733/amazon-order-emails-google-gmail-ai-agents-data) what you’ve purchased anymore. As one AI evangelist on X [whined](https://x.com/trikcode/status/2111622265284546620) recently, “The internet is starting to block AI agents that were supposed to do everything for us.”

But it’s hard not to shake the feeling that the Delta’s and JP Morgan’s of the world aren’t fully grasping what’s happening. The world’s biggest tech companies this year quietly entered into a winner-takes-all battle royal for the future of the internet. They all want their AI to be the one that unravels everyone else’s moat. And it doesn’t just end with scraping websites or racing to buy the same paper towels.

Over the weekend, I came across a [very sober video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qFEuYUfauNY) from Ed Woodruff, better known as Low Level, a vulnerability researcher who reverse engineers software professionally. He did a deep dive into a new MCP on Github called “Reverse Engineer Anything,” or REA. As the name implies, it’s meant to reverse-engineer anything you point it at. It’s likely what’s behind the _working_ video game mash-ups and vibe-coded app clones popping up right now. “It kind of sets a more bleak tone for the future of not only software development, but software ownership as well,” Woodruff said.

One of the most high-profile projects to likely come from REA [was ArtCraft](https://gizmodo.com/someone-vibe-coded-a-free-knockoff-of-adobe-creative-suite-2000823322), a messy open source version of the Adobe suite and, now, a whole bunch of Microsoft apps, as well. These apps do not work well, but they’re open source and, likely, close enough to build on top of. Which makes Adobe’s giddy [announcement](https://business.adobe.com/blog/workfront-mcp-ai-powered-work-management) in July that they were releasing their own MCP and a whole host of AI features feel especially painful. Imagine getting so hyped up you jump off the cliff first. Baby, no one’s going to need to pay for your MCP if everyone else’s MCPs get good enough. They’ll just copy your whole app and connect it directly to their own AI agent. And, oops, MCPs just got good enough to do that.
