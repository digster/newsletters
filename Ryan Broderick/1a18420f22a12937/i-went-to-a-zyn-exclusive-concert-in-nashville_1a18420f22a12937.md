| Since his rise to fame in 2017, Childers has served as a litmus test for the politics of white southern culture and country music. If you’re not from the south and have a hard time telling if someone is a red neck (non-derogatory) or a straight up fascist, ask if they like him. In my experience, Childers’ listener base is 20% NPR southerners and 80% people who are about as “apolitical” as southerners can get. Meaning they’ll occasionally say something that makes you cringe in shame and in equal measure, they’ll hit you with something that makes you seriously consider if they’ve read Marx. | According to my emails, ZYN Presents has held five other rewards-only live events in the past year to date: Lainey Wilson in Nashville, The Black Keys in New York City, The Red Clay Strays in Austin, Nate Bargatze in Nashville, and Noah Kahan in Denver. Even for the leading tobacco giant, this seemed like a lot of time, money, and effort to spend on a relatively small, sparsely publicized event. And despite the mainstream media whining about “Zynfluencers” promoting the brand on social media, I found very little content about it online. | At the door of the concert, the staff asked if my friend and I were both nicotine users (we are) and gave us blue wristbands. Had my friend said no, she would have been given a white wristband that restricted her from purchasing ZYNs at the event. At the end of check in, we were handed two drink tickets each. | Directly to the left of the entrance, a collection of microphones were bolted to a circular board colored the brand’s signature blue.
