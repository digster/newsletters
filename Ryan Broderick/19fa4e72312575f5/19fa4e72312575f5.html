Meta Is Trying To Stop Perverts From Using Their Pervert Glasses

Back in May, I wrote that Meta Ray-Bans are “a Cybertruck for your face” and “great for weird fucking losers that want to look down your shirt and plaster it all over Instagram.” Well, a little update lol: Instagram is finally cracking down on that last part.

As Business Insider reported last week, Instagram head Adam Mosseri is banning “pickup line kind of videos.” As with every moderation decision Meta makes, this is probably going to be very vaguely defined, not target any users with actual audiences, and ultimately result in more insidious behavior from the aforementioned weird users. I know this will happen because that’s how Meta’s business model has always worked. They invent some new torment nexus and then spend all their time tweaking the moderation to softly police all the horrible behavior caused — and rewarded — by the torment nexus. But the Meta Ray-Bans are the most egregious example of this yet.

I’ve long said that good augmented-reality glasses could be very cool. I actually think a lot of the horrors of the modern world are caused by the one-app-per-screen experience of the smartphone and bringing multiple windows — and context — to mobile computing would be a net positive. I would happily buy a pair of glasses that both extended and opened up my smartphone’s screen and meant I no longer had to use it as a computer in public. Open a bunch of windows while I’m sitting on a train, use built-in cameras to orient the augmented reality features, make all of it end-to-end encrypted, and maybe even block the ability to use those cameras as actual cameras.

And if a user were to use those cameras as cameras, there’s certainly no reason to not have a big unavoidable flashing light letting people know. Meta Ray-Bans have an LED that flashes when the camera is in use, but there are all kinds of complaints that it’s not immediately visible to those not wearing the glasses. I, also, in the course of researching this piece discovered a whole world of (mostly) men teaching each other how to bypass it. So perhaps this entire idea is dead on arrival. Fun fact: In Japan, it’s illegal to sell smartphones that let you mute the shutter sound. Because of perverts!

Meta Ray-Bans are not trying to solve real problems with mobile computing. They want you to think they are, of course, but the rotten core of the whole project means they can’t actually admit what they’re doing. They’ll tell you the lack of end-to-end encryption is so their AI model can work as an assistant for you, and they’ll say that you need the AI assistant because it’s a better hands-free experience than glasses that work as a mobile display for your smartphone, and they’ll say that the video capabilities are perfect for posting to Instagram or letting the AI assistant tell you information about a flower you’re looking at or whatever. It goes on and on and on, but the truth is very clear.

They need the glasses because Meta’s attempts at making phones and operating systems have been a flop. They dumped their AI into the glasses because they need a way to sell their AI. And the content-creation tools they’ve built in, like taking photos and videos, are meant to populate Instagram feeds because it’s a great way to get users to advertise the glasses for free. But this puts Meta in a hilariously awkward position. They want users to post Ray-Ban footage to Instagram, an app still overwhelmingly popular with young women, and they clearly know their glasses are perfect for creeping on young women. So what have they done? Well, they’ve recently partnered with Kylie Jenner and Blackpink’s Jennie Kim to paper over that little conundrum. As Salon recently wrote, “Meta recruited a celebrity to make smart glasses feel safe for women. They aren't.”

(tiktok.com/@vaderrthegolden)

There is some evidence that Meta’s marketing campaign is working. Sales of the glasses tripled in 2025. And smart glasses-created content — not made by perverts — is beginning to organically populate my feeds. Over the weekend, I came across a video of “Vader the Golden,” a very cute golden retriever that wears Meta Ray-Bans to record his point of view.

His owner, who posts under the handle @katyymorgann, doesn’t appear to be working directly with Meta. Though, when Vader’s Ray-Bans videos are shared to Instagram they have a tag on them that reads, “Ray-Ban Meta glasses,” which is auto-generated from the video footage metadata. And when you click on that tag it takes you right to the Ray-Bans shop. The perfect little loop. And one must assume Instagram’s algorithm is looking for and promoting posts with that tag. They’d be stupid not to!

The glasses are no safer than they were a few months ago and the moderation policies about what kind of footage you can upload are no clearer — to say nothing of all the creepy footage that isn’t being uploaded publicly — but who cares right? Don’t worry about that stuff. Put them on your dog. Make content. It’ll go viral.

A Whole Bunch Of Claude Conversations Leaked

(reddit.com/r/ClaudeAI)

A whole bunch of Claude conversations leaked over the weekend. Users noticed that you could search “site:claude.ai/share” and it would list hundreds of Claude conversations that had been shared between users. If you don’t use AI at all, most of these chatbots have the ability to generate a link to a specific conversation, so you could send it to someone else. It’s not great that these links were indexed by Google and it’s good that Anthropic quickly removed them, but you would also think that people would be a bit smarter about what they’re telling AI chatbots and what they’re sharing with other people.

Per Reddit’s r/ClaudeAI subreddit, the leaked conversations included a crypto wallet and keys, a lawyer discussing contract details, and “a truly staggering amount of incestuous erotica, some of which was bizarrely ‘shared by Anthropic.’” Would love to hear more about that last one!

This is not the only AI-related security breach to be concerned about at the moment. There’s a new exploit called ShareRoot that uses Claude’s Cowork to access to every file on a Mac. And last week OpenAI’s new GPT-5.6 Sol autonomously hacked Hugging Face’s internal databases. OpenAI and Hugging Face now say they’re working together to investigate exactly what happened.
