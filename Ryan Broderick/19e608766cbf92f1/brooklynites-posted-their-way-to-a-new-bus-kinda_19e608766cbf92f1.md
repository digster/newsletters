Fine, I’ll Get On The Discourse-Powered Skincare Bus |
This week brings us a new milestone in the internet bleeding into real life. For two weeks, starting tomorrow, there will be a free public bus route in New York City that wouldn’t exist without a round of discourse on X from earlier this month. |
This isn’t an official part of the city’s public transit, it’s a pop-up by the skincare brand The Ordinary. But the [website](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.ZE3yp4K2Y6WBUz6JXxBv2-_O-lT6RWSFRfEBYPyOxLVOZ-VPa8HSibKYQciN4MIaQfQ4HbA2NyqPvS_Ipus_0OESu7f14wSdyZRl4fgWn5Gf7TaeJS_SXK8qZLG1u6M-208VTrPdQlf1OMwD6V5ZPLcLRvrnMddDCvGt3z-cwfbemhakHAvX1Smf0Vp8qQcGPRNDwlnUzm0S28IQydL-ENWf4-PnuGcImCB_8Ue7JdC5xQ0op38M-zLSpR738xjcdP2zDbbsYv-Mz36gTjpVEQ/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h4/h001.QP7TXgqkrviVq-MAnnRNqYgGxtg84bmi_grdkOniLZM) for “The Ordinary Bus” treats it like serious municipal infrastructure. Which means that today I get to talk about New York’s MTA, which I’ve probably spent more time and energy studying than anything to do with the internet. |
The inspiration for the bus seems to have been a [post](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001._-qCIiYyp6dD21H99lwa0uwD7stY_VO3t1cIjx-mUdXy3W6ZRolqyUtTLTCvD38rNPUiuwFYZ4sd3Bep9mx_BcHkZZVp2dZGn-uClrOWrX8BRI_mDL_HrBWSNsI4lG6v-Y5kqT1Ujridc3jCX5xIUXnTeF3QK_kSnGPR1zz4BoQraF8rRWXzKsoc1EtrcGRuuLtp8J6dM_gc2wjt3MNBxNf77Za4FtTPf531-85pUI2V6jDRgpirMmDjwGHfV4nxiznTXSPFpkeTP7T4tC5_RQQDJDTjJCwqlhOZtC4Ns7Y11paos_0EzUkNqGDNjSqQ/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h5/h001.OpUGQc2PjwrrmwQMRG4psUcZxn5qo2a9Z5hrTFeExOE) on X written on May 2nd, by @nazzobetweeting, who wrote “i love having to go through manhattan to go from brooklyn to brooklyn”. They added an [image](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001._-qCIiYyp6dD21H99lwa0uwD7stY_VO3t1cIjx-mUdXy3W6ZRolqyUtTLTCvD38rH7A-Vs1J2_Jlh1gMgpqeZp8sDT-0xRzoM6t63BETBKuEUPENG4hkk7B2n4YS8ZqoJW2RTY0MGujThhyrtuS_l-n2bidukoXsAhBagxo3TMkikhqB0mQgFXXdyYSqk0VbMnfzQXbvtsgf3SM_P1AC1HcXJW-8656ITQaaizOGF2EyFdUhh_pJootHDt-40du-IcTzFvpIfopvgPQ_afj3jz48qgfcN3IEQx8B-_SpUbpZoP0SA81k3Uk4rgV_qeFu/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h6/h001.teYYjkT3r7GBDyHkJq6lEEQrR2sni5pT-YBcwwZy6iI) of Google Maps’ recommended public transit route between Williamsburg and Prospect Park, where the quickest option really is to go all the way into Manhattan and back. |

Because this one post seems to have made its own waves. The “brooklyn to brooklyn” post has received over 2 million views and 14,000 likes on X, and kicked off noticeable [discourse](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001._-qCIiYyp6dD21H99lwa0g0YvWGAHCssoErWjRiSlPFJcl6sOg96PWwi-ZZsTS14lWwzySmrzhoXgS0Ngihvo53LjWwKwWIJIfd1hA6Ff30EEsWqP_wz1IRyM-A1dEFz1O4v4ggoi2Gi0xX09xsWcB1Km8K7OmPpSJDuC2jVXRz5jS1OWUtpJAxUZdcWzrfeMFUJhzJ8T1w7Q7GvVxPrZJaXg5hBKbBvXHdWs-7pppn8j8I10wEXOkmUl4MF1KAz6BxAorN6UYIPtYCB2heuwX2gGbjT3bZUoyawovyOpgdRkZQlEbdsDzG40fyqHDFE/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h12/h001.dM2pOcYYVLJVPfi0k6UoAjyuLmaBKNUf1P_5OO4vruE) about getting around in Brooklyn. This one bit of impulsive frustration seems to have been enough to shape the route the Ordinary Bus will take, if not inspire its creation outright. The free bus goes from one corner of Prospect Park to the west side of Williamsburg — without the post, I would have expected its northern end to be somewhere in Bushwick, which has inspired its own share of [viral discourse](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001._-qCIiYyp6dD21H99lwa0r-jwanniR6sqLFzzrmcvZPlYuwWXZCj9lhoWk8g-n-wEiLkuF3SbCtgWZb3ahhHvQ30YKWJyGbG2UYSYg3-vQSWz5-rOy0qom8lyv6OJYv_IzuXEmVWFwqlDtUD2hoTgCv2bK06MrqzEf-ErLts-P_I4FLnDXQGOZrkL_B4GkDHjqrEU8BbUIST4dWy1su0B8VX8Cvs3mL9dshy54e6qq04uiI62_4MlmHaKSGz2RA5BJoSIatwkjYlmdfanWTySuFlXgKJoulXYT4FvobksCc/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h13/h001.H39xMOVmTldZ6TNE65TIE5nr3iro_-LTuJ4yFKVJkjg). |
Doing all this because of one X conversation cycle isn’t crazy in terms of the actual resources being committed. All The Ordinary had to do was rent a bus for a couple weeks, hire a driver, spin up a website and announce it. It was probably simpler and cheaper than their previous vaguely populist-looking pop-ups, like the indictment of [celebrity endorsements](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.aAeN6yp8dFKxVQSiya0p6tKajdjlIdlHxBKHM6Zn7HZSll90zSeqtqRlI8qJQSrCFI1UeBujdQdxByQ5IOc6O121A7PQj-hGtVLF1J5uO8EIIsV7lXPxYhcN2Zy2LUZfMlRi9U1BkMeSvEn1hcjSjQSrVgMb5-q3h4wFAnO7UvQB6PEEdtVFmpghBYhmqVvHh5SGpxOcGsFHInWMm6vRRtEFNnTdqRi0YB6LiRviRy8iju_lkk7_nK951BEBkXGFZq_P57muHA8nGCkE67JFLLyuCtbuAGpvsiQKL_yJ6HiqOboq_OGEEeGFx2GwytamO454R8YubTdJwO6E_O2TqZ3kZfoXiMado2V5vdKYL35jCxhtURL0OtD09z9H4tVZ/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h14/h001.THQsTmNckBplcbTTgqntsfR-t2PWTWyNJV5NcBu6O-M) they did last year, which didn’t stop them partnering with influencers to show it off. |

Here, they’re treating the bus as something similarly noble. Their [announcement](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.Qr6Ovh5taOidqUa5P-dRpQYE8RIusqUvBbezVOx50XpJcPQKu1n-HYhoHmWTJvt1OiRFfMNApP7hhV7s2b0tKuCRo45N7WVViFvYUMZSD3oymqN8kZNtdg-S9-Mk0R5nnssTlUTebhWiT4ZEMZspfEkZh12yeKlaj-NCq17gWpm4bqC5qmEaKqHxGfwF2-67lQEIyafud0ns7lCh0pLPUULwvIqvxrgS5tR_iHGLo6CXlN13AdcTQBbUgqZPszW3Ujp6qj1DOI3xmgttWQM88FI4To11n_yQCscflA73B8osb9-uBNtgFVAyvMU0LR4x/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h16/h001.XOXb1COyjyxmh19rJu9ZHtxB_GpmLBQ6fdYQQfrZypE) starts “Brooklyn has a transit gap that shouldn’t exist”. The website gets even more high-minded, saying the company’s philosophy “doesn’t stop at skincare”, and they’re applying it to “the city itself”. Which is a little out of pocket if it’s really happening because of one person’s annoyed post. But one post isn’t the only factor here. |
Free buses were a signature policy of Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s campaign — there were some pretty idiotic [complaints](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.Qr6Ovh5taOidqUa5P-dRpQYE8RIusqUvBbezVOx50Xp7CtUuMLWws0dcq_ny6eHyKxImtQYQqsy-_KxjH2GfddKWN5R-RJs8zX5Dy7nsCq4T0IyzhyaRHQNPoMToVM3ISzCtZO3VwzpxflSVHgJ4yH4BdU7PQoX_fyGCZhX63l-0BfNjmakMSFBE-hWDYa4ywJNaRRvjMGdA9h6p1meCaW8bfDN0ASJG9txqQrmTo8ZSWpEkJVgyxE39SEhCaYx6BO9hIjd7T47mLHrFlSujTaz_vNFeUMbwAzZvjL8LPdM/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h17/h001.Pmbxyo8UbiJ8zoFNA_u-HfCEvtWV8a-8HL5c4-5M0dw) when he took office the same day as a fare increase. The bus wouldn’t be the first pop-up directly inspired by one of his campaign promises. That honor goes to the [free grocery store](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.Qr6Ovh5taOidqUa5P-dRpUSZDdCffi-LSZI-bzeqf7vQCqAKW501suBlJsm9kN48W5Luolh3T3gsPdEuTd8gBb5AfvzjKh3eM2JDofKH04R-M6p8OvZipnQaK_WP-F5Zg_Py8spr9Zhn0ls-2hBNxeOYuok6wb2SklcmaS82mUMm3DgTxmdWtotEVojrGobXdIy8O8RfQfMZe91eQJe21QH1uwvMN5W8NtX9X8DejH0NncxoQbAN5AEnC9_1Sp1Z0utLsb2ltAe2wepB49ucTjMTOtVm43xDOk04hXuoIlKVgXj5jo6jByrZsLDtfX5ccRorUBUxV3krydk-AUB2frEqeGP-9KQ0uQLQkyHrCyFlirFPQEPtQLNdMU3St5lB/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h18/h001.2HLXpJ8kKcr0PD002MRUHilUD0Vmt6UDXjQDxcBux2g) that the prediction market Kalshi briefly opened in February (Polymarket opened [their own](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.Qr6Ovh5taOidqUa5P-dRpZYGjAhgVtmZZ1D2u9N4oUbLRjaCa47bAyKcRD_9pnHcJafIr5xnE5KXO1bu2nJ6_3X0wWwwYPV6gWvQl6DJuG_l1n6bhUZ_v-qljbvu6KxQIVjlsQ3zpRF9S-iK096taQWUQWK7Fr_0PZE3uOAUUyw3mqdllBQsB8w0jxWvjQDPPNAwIFRXQAJPKh5yuuP1e6kQOsQdKqetpqn3jaJRykhst79QRjXOdOBV7KXwrdVL9YQ_kRzMYqHvVvt5IORMouPYdNZhUZPvaGhyfcfkrxS3MoixUUbFVozx6E5ZuKTJ8g7qJMty1q0SCln9SXgBSFm29VMWP95ppa3vLPSt2GE/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h19/h001.6GspeQ2k0bkpWha7-Kvy_p2YDHxRJasL5m3BU0QVVms) not long after). It seems to barely matter to these companies that Mamdani is a politician — he’s a popular online figure, and whatever attention they get from these promos will primarily be downstream from him, like they’re guests on his show. |
Even so, mass transit is a lot more serious of a space to play in. The Ordinary’s ad copy shows they understand that, and they’re cosplaying urbanism for the audience of a single X post anyway. In some ways, it’s the same thing I’ve called “[LOLgislation](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.Qr6Ovh5taOidqUa5P-dRpe1McpL1EdNErS546cDbqU3XJ5_sZuba1vfLHc5Y1Rn6NdELsLB4DhennJbxb9zIY8_XL0YCB73jaDq8HD0__Ed6eN2VAtNXSpxFfWdRl1gryP5Fz0VF9Mv3C6m2uTbYoLnok7zwizvDSVppCmxPMJ50DI8WmXmZUrQEThxqY4urJFmp9Cni8x7rRDRL4NFcPp0Gk_KImMEqV4WPm3HGCHjXS119P7NyGsBTr9quikfbXZwSFCRYYl3qTTRZl1GFq7byPfg5O4ig6jS-g82xioJiBp_de5Bn1p6cpNHpssEnAkdt52L8BG5uRw4c4Ync5LkFmjSwe2pxBZXWck9F4919llYWmgaSixgIvMYPUHTkb_uYdtoLeBtL8jGFHWGBjCzwJY3LdrEA4XWNrTopYqI0HGI6zts8OuMLRc2vKfzha84wtxhs-1LSq3EPvfoVwwlrVj62uS2pUdSlNCh-tXBHwkGa-K2fTzp7eoTocguP6tH4NgTPzOWzDis0lNjor7KUN-cgm4b6hBvJIYgibenlgSVJrptWC7psEMB-Hld1qPazhuPdl96xnXc8Lkff0D_K7J6v-elqV20Ee7GKudrjGD5zTTS3Z2uBB4aYMRFjTUe2LMLrxUn1oqA8P5r2ebhGWZcHfgxES6EHTEfsloQSIQklhnoOZcwH-B-lwpqEAEhlB5ZVV85Moe80nn_WOtClZhDML52RlGWIW5-gkbjpDZblbMPW5pqC9xxATj2VVvAyG6EKfMAcfQAJWYvQQGCoe1eO08Tf0GLG8boFnwN5h57qoRIJm1T8EbrZFfdt/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h20/h001.R6a1uUjpJuoX2ctfHe5v_3om5wnCv_mjvrDMofiT7XQ)”, treating random posts, ads, and serious government policy as one and the same. |
But this is one of the few times I’ve seen the process work on a policy I don’t just support but personally yearn for. And it’s definitely given me perspective on how intoxicating it can be. In my very earliest days of browsing the web, going through the archives of [NYCSubway.org](https://elink835.www.garbageday.email/ss/c/u001.lCjQVVVSMXEwG__Y0-pC7D5Pen7ihrlcWbd2IpxqYVTlzlGQpOIHt4fF3tHdd9je9ccZHvO08HrU7CYKrUTb-gs4hhud_i23yyH8yGQ4kf9NxGK-cc6GnMyOqwAW3KuuWwxvOVUQro2668u23i5jyVlm1gJmI3OULDyFmoxtbIHtDGjvu6IkmKbAC6URLD3j7HkhMwc0XLi0NCVEquge6VIFumPzkekXL6smAibzHu4pcyw4L1XZZHKDITO8smAiSd2HXaggHbqXTDPP9hMnbA/4qx/uBeArBqwRquNzBrnrBtRBg/h21/h001.WCC9IotE21HW6r3KQY0pax4sslocj50a_xMQkCGlFZg) on my parents’ iBook G3, the whole thing felt impossibly magical. The idea that if enough people complain about the buses online they’ll just start a new one is similarly magical. |
It’s not real infrastructure, but everything has to start somewhere. And if it really does make Brooklyn travel a little easier for a brief time, then I’ll take all the cleanser samples they give me in the bargain. |
