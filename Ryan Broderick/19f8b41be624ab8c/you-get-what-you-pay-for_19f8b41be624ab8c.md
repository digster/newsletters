| There’s been a question hanging over the amorphous blob of platforms, media formats, and personalities we now call “entertainment” since COVID. And it’s question more average people are asking with more intensity than ever. And it’s actually a fairly simple one: Why do things feel worse now? Why do good TV shows, good movies, good songs feel so rare? Why does everything feel so stale and so boring? And I think the lost millennial decade has a lot to do with it. Making art, making culture, has always involved some sort of dark deal with the infernal machinery of capital, but the rise of big tech and venture capital in the early 2010s changed the terms of the deal. Culture stopped being something the consumer paid for and cultural industries became kindling for digital furnaces that only wanted to monetize our data anyways. An entire generation of young creatives were paid to dump terabytes of posts and videos in a black hole, trying to entertain users long enough to be surveilled and train AI.
