And they certainly could not influence the box office of Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey. And that last one seems like it might be the straw that finally breaks the camel’s back. |
But before we lift up the rock and look at out how the right wing is scrambling this morning amid The Odyssey’s massive opening weekend box office, let’s do a little refresher on how we got here. Because I think it will explain some of the issues they’re having currently. |
Elon Musk was convinced by a bunch of reactionary tech CEOs during COVID to purchase Twitter, believing the site was basically the only platform that mattered. A mistake every rich guy in tech and media made at some point in the 2010s. Musk has since converted Twitter into X and pushed through a bunch of changes — paid verification, Community Notes, the Grok AI, an algorithmic For You page, and the hard pivot to video — that actually have made the site feel like a fundamentally different app. Sure, there are Twitter-like things that happen on X, but it’s simply not the same place anymore. |
Musk did not seem to understand, however, that what made Twitter valuable couldn’t actually be bought: The uncanny combination of extremely important people, anonymous maniacs, and shitposting teenagers that felt like it might self-destruct at any moment (and often did). The secret sauce of Twitter was that an editor or producer or studio head or a politician or a CEO might see something insane on their feed and be influenced by it. It’s been nearly four years since Musk bought Twitter and there’s very little of that secret sauce left. The important people either stayed on X and had their brains — and cultural influence — melted by it, or decamped for Bluesky (or nowhere). Same with the anonymous maniacs and shitposting teenagers, most of which are over on Instagram these days. It didn’t happen all at once, but X is basically just a newsreader service for stuff happening elsewhere on the web, used primarily by the world’s worst people. Which is really only useful for people with jobs like mine, sadly. Very little of what happens there matters to normal people because all the normal people left. This has throttled the Trump administration’s public messaging and I also suspect it’s why right-wing influencers like Tim Pool and Ben Shapiro are feeling a financial crunch right now. No one ever cared what happened on 4chan until it broke containment, but Twitter was where it would usually spread and now there are the same users on both sites and everyone’s lost interest. And that is extremely obvious with The Odyssey this week. |

How many people are actually using AI? It’s one of the more pressing questions of our time. Unlike search and social media, AI usage leaves behind very little evidence, and I think it’s making everyone feel a little paranoid right now. |
