Good Morning Ishan,
Fifty-seven people in Uganda. Thirty-one in Zambia. Thirteen in Kenya. That is how Airtel Money counts its staff in the prospectus for its London Stock Exchange listing, where trading in its shares starts today.
The African mobile-money company is worth about $7 billion and has just over 300 employees in all. In FY26, it clocked $1.35 billion in revenue. That is more than $4 million per head.
Most of the actual work, though, is done by people who aren’t on the payroll. Airtel Money has more than 2.3 million agents, mostly small-shop owners across 13 African countries.
An African fintech founder The Ken met at last week’s India Mobile Congress described how people actually use it. “I put Rs 100 into my Airtel Money wallet, then immediately go to an Airtel Money agent’s shop, show my phone, and withdraw Rs 100 in physical cash, which I then use to buy food, pay rent, or pay a shopkeeper,” he said. “If I need to send money to someone else, I put cash back into Airtel Money, send it, and the recipient withdraws it again.”
The wallet, as he put it, is “essentially a digital envelope for moving cash”. A June report on Africa’s cash dependency found that more than 90% of mobile-money value is withdrawn as soon as it arrives.
Airtel Money isn’t even the biggest maker of these envelopes. Its roughly 53 million monthly users trail MTN Momo (70.8 million) and Vodacom (103 million). What Airtel Money does have is a very useful parent.
Airtel Africa, the phone company that controls it, offers this to all its customers, since only Airtel’s mobile subscribers can open a wallet. It is also one of Airtel Money’s biggest customers. Last fiscal year, Airtel Africa companies paid Airtel Money about $265 million, a fifth of its revenue, to sell airtime, collect bills, and make bulk payouts.
Airtel Money’s shares began trading on 9 October, but only conditionally: the trades counted only if the deal went through. Today it does, with formal admission and trading open to everyone. At about $7 billion, it is said to be London’s biggest IPO in five years.
