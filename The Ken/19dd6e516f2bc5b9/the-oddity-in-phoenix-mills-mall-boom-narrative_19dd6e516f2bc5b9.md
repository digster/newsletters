The Phoenix Mills, one of India’s largest mall owners, couldn’t have asked for a better way to close out the fiscal. Revenue was up over 20% and Ebitda, a measure of operating performance, surged 34% in the March quarter.
What’s even more impressive is that 10 of its 12 malls have leased out over 95% of the available area. Between all its malls, Phoenix offers 11.5 million sq ft to retail stores and restaurants. And it plans to expand that area by over 50% by 2030.
It’s hard not to infer from these numbers that Indian cities have mall rats aplenty, and not enough malls for them to frequent.
A prominent property consultant said as much earlier this month. The total area leased by the retail sector across the top eight cities in the March quarter fell 10% from a year ago because there weren’t enough storefronts to lap up, according to [Cushman & Wakefield](https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/en/india/news/2026/04/india-retail-sector-growth-in-q1-2026). Malls accounted for half of the total leasing.
This sustained demand for organized retail translated into further tightening of vacancies and firming of rentals across premium retail assets. Grade A mall vacancy declined further to 5.7% in Q1 2026, while Grade A+ assets remained particularly tight at 2.6%, reflecting sustained occupier preference for well‑located, institutionally managed retail destinations.
The veneer of optimism has a few cracks, though.
