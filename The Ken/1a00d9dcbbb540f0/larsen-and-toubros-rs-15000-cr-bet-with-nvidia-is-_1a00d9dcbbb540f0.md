Good Morning Ishan,
In early August, Larsen & Toubro (L&T) signed a deal with US-based AI cloud platform Together AI to build a 10,000-GPU Nvidia cluster in Chennai, worth up to Rs 15,000 crore. E2E Networks—the Indian GPU cloud provider L&T once saw as its route into AI compute—was nowhere near the deal.
Odd, because this is exactly the kind of business L&T had found in E2E when it paid Rs 1,079 crore for a 21% stake in the listed Indian cloud provider in 2024.
“It was mainly the platform,” a senior executive in L&T’s data-centre division said of their decision to initially partner with E2E. “We already knew how to build and operate physical infrastructure. E2E had the cloud and AI layer sitting above it.”
The easier job for a GPU cloud is providing its clients access to Nvidia’s chips. The real sell is everything that makes the access worth paying for—the software, the orchestration layer, the guaranteed uptime, and even the security. It is essentially a gamble that you can earn back the cost of a chip before you have to buy a better one that makes it worthless.
It’s also why Nvidia, in the same week L&T signed the Together AI deal, [lined](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-partners-with-apollo-blackrock-blackstone-brookfield-goldman-sachs-and-kkr-to-establish-ai-compute-infrastructure-financing-platforms-to-mobilize-over-500-billion-of-third-party-capital) up six major financing companies to underwrite up to $500 billion in loans for the companies buying its GPUs. It effectively became a bank that covers part of the loss when those chips depreciate. E2E’s fleet has been running at 35–40% of its capacity, against its own target of 80%.
L&T didn’t wait for those numbers to turn around. Now none of its new AI infrastructure runs through E2E.
