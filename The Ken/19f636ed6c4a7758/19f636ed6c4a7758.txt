Indigo walks like a low-cost airline, talks like a full-service one. With a 9% dip, India’s largest flight operator doesn’t want to take any chances

For two decades, the boundaries of Indian aviation were clearly demarcated: low-cost carriers (LCCs) stripped away luxuries to sell cheap tickets, while full-service carriers (FSCs) bundled hot meals and baggage allowances into a single premium. In 2026, these boundaries have nearly collapsed. 

This matters beyond the cabin. Interglobe Aviation, Indigo’s parent company is the only listed airline stock in India that investors can actually put money in. Over the past year, its share price has declined by 9%, also underperforming the Nifty 50 by 4%. 

The anguish over what happened last December might still be fresh for flyers, but investors have largely stayed steady and no, not because of sunk-cost fallacy.  

In the past twenty years, the number of flyers in India has surged from 15 million to over 210 million, making the country the fifth-largest aviation market, according to the International Air Transport Association. In the next twenty, that number will further triple to almost 640 million. Only China and the United States will have more passengers. 

A public-market investor could gain from that swell by buying shares of an airport operator or even a hotel chain. But there’s no proxy https://the-ken.com/story/the-indigo-conundrum-investors-worship-it-flyers-hate-it/ for an airline with Indigo’s dominance. Spicejet, which is listed, has been on the decline for years and operates a mere 35 planes. Air India will have to get its house in order before even dreaming of going public. Akasa Air is at least a few years away from an IPO. 

So Indigo is not just the biggest carrier but also the only carrier for Indian investors in the foreseeable future. 

Why Indigo would annoy flyers and not investors, The Ken https://the-ken.com/long_and_short/why-indigo-should-annoy-flyers-and-not-investors/

But Indigo cannot rely on that alone. Investors will have to see that the airline is doing its bit to improve its source of revenue.

On 1 July, India’s largest flight operator launched its “Lite” fare, a new pricing segment marketed as a ‘cheaper’ alternative for Economy Class passengers flying only with a cabin bag of up to 7 kg. 

This is an LCC strategy that doesn’t align with its other programs, such as Stretch and BluChips. 

Regardless of customer base, both bring in the bucks. And right now, Indigo needs to prove that this is sustainable and will translate into good numbers for everyone who puts money into it, not just for passengers.
