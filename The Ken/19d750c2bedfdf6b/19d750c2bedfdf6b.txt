Can NPCI’s BHIM take on the giants it created? It gave Google Pay and Phonepe the road, now it has to race them on it 
 By Uddantika Kashyap https://the-ken.com/writers/uddantikathe-ken-com/

Somewhere right now, there was a ti-ding. And now another one. And another. Someone is splitting a restaurant bill, or someone just bought groceries. Maybe someone scanned a QR code at a vada pav stall in a lane that does not even have a proper shopfront. That sound, that small, unremarkable ti-ding, is, in fact, one of the most consequential things to have happened to how money now moves in India.

I vividly remember having to carry cash everywhere. If I was out and my mom called asking me to pick something up from the market, I would come home to a scolding because I never had money on me. I was that person. And then UPI arrived, and slowly, I stopped thinking about it. 

I will be honest, the change didn’t happen overnight, and it took me until late 2022 to fully switch. For a while, I would still have that mini heart attack every time I left the house. Wallet? Wallet? Oh. Right. Now I know I just need two things: data (my dad keeps me on the family data plan, so I do not worry about that. Thanks, Dad!) and a charged phone. I even went to Nepal without cash and used UPI to make payments in Nepalese rupees. How cool is that?

This is what UPI did. It made money feel lighter. And you do not see the pile getting smaller the way you do with cash. Which is either beautiful or dangerous, depending on the day.

This episode is about the heart of all of that. And about how the one app that started it all, slowly disappeared, but has now come back loud and energised.

The OG 

When demonetisation happened in 2016, and India was scrambling for a cashless alternative, the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) needed to show that its unified payments interface, better known to us as just UPI, worked. So they went to Juspay, which had been building payment infrastructure and asked them to build a demo app. Within two weeks of actual development (out of a three-week sprint), BHIM, or Bharat Interface for Money, was born.

When Narendra Modi went on television and asked people to use BHIM, the app got 10 million downloads in almost 10 days. People loved it! It was clean, it was simple, it had no agenda. Our guest describes the old BHIM as “the Switzerland of UPI apps”. It was just payments and nothing else.

And then, by design, it faded. The ecosystem it was meant to kick off took over. In 2016–17, Phonepe and Google Pay came in, while Paytm had already established itself. BHIM quietly stepped back and let them run.

But that’s not a bad thing. That was the plan—BHIM was always meant to be the example, not the winner.

The plan did not go as planned

The ecosystem that BHIM was supposed to create and lead to—this thriving, competitive, diverse universe of UPI apps all fighting for their slice—did not quite happen. One by one, the players got knocked out. Whatsapp UPI was restricted for years, and by the time the restrictions were lifted, nobody cared anymore. Paytm ran into regulatory trouble and lost significant ground.

What was left then? Phonepe and Google Pay, and between them, they account for over 80% of UPI transactions in India. But to prevent this exact scenario, the NPCI had set a rule outright: that no single player could hold more than 30% market share. But its implementation has been postponed twice. 

And if you’re wondering where BHIM stands today? It processes about 0.8% of UPI transactions. This is where PGK makes an analogy that everyone agrees with: BHIM is the BSNL of UPI. This is exactly what happened to Indian telecom. You had all these players, everyone was excited, but then slowly you ended up with the government desperately trying to keep its own product, BSNL, alive. 

Except, our guests point out, BHIM has a much more powerful parent.

So why is it back?

MS Dhoni is now the face https://the-ken.com/kaching/npci-resurrects-its-own-upi-payments-app-dreams-with-bhim/ of BHIM. The government has put Rs 1,500 crore into promoting it. The cashbacks it is offering right now are, according to one of our guests, genuinely concerning in how aggressive they are. And BHIM is suddenly everywhere.

But why now? And why this loudly?

To dig into this, PGK and Rahel brought in two people who, between them, have seen almost every chapter of this story. Arundhati Ramanathan https://www.linkedin.com/in/arundhati-ramanathan-3ba24810/, former deputy editor at The Ken, wrote what our other guest calls a “Rohit Sharma-debut-level” first story https://the-ken.com/story/unlikely-story-of-bhim/?searchTerm=bhim%20the%20upsetter%20of%20plans&utm_source=search&utm_medium=page about BHIM back in 2017. She has been covering fintech for nearly a decade and was reporting on this when most of us were still figuring out what UPI even was. Abhishek Madan https://www.linkedin.com/in/abhishekjmadan/, co-founder of Alt Inc and former VP of Product at Paytm, was inside the machine when a lot of this was happening. He is as candid as someone who has signed NDAs can be, which turns out to be quite candid.

Their answers to why BHIM is back involve words like concentration risk, sovereignty, and insurance policy. None of those phrases sounds exciting, but all of them are more important than they seem.

Arundhati said, “Today, if BHIM came and said I am just payments and nothing else, I think it would attract a lot of users because some of us just want payments, we do not want anything else.” 

Abhishek, who has spent years inside the belly of this beast, says the problem is exactly that, but BHIM cannot advertise it as such. Because the NPCI cannot outright say all the other players in the ecosystem that I created are bad, I am good, come use my app. That would be like the referee blowing the whistle and then joining one of the teams.

The question with no clear answer is whether BHIM making a comeback actually threatens anyone.

The big two are watching while the smaller players are nervous. And somewhere in the middle, NPCI, which runs BHIM and also sets the rules for everyone else, has to convince the world that it can be unbiased while also playing the same game.

It is a test for NPCI on whether it can maintain neutrality and, at the same time, push BHIM

Arundhati Ramanathan

That tension is what makes this episode genuinely worth your time.

I am a Paytm girl and have been since 2021. I collect gold coins on the app, though I don’t know what to do with them, except that it makes me happy. I am not switching anytime soon. But I will say this: if BHIM came up with something actually cool and actually easy, maybe, just maybe?

This episode gets into exactly what that something could be. So, go give it a listen, and I’ll leave you with this question: what is the one thing that might make you want to switch your UPI app?
