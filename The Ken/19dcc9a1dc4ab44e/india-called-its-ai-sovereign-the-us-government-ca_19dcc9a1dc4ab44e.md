Humans have always wanted to own things. Land, resources, gold, oil. Now, apparently, AI.
Ever since we learned to make weapons out of pointy objects, we have built empires just to stay independent of someone else’s influence. Different currencies, strict immigration controls, data localisation laws—all of it, at its core, an attempt to stay in charge of our own resources. To become “sovereign”.
The AI universe is no different. The problem is, no one quite knows what the term means. “What even is sovereign?” an analyst at a VC firm asked me. “Is it when data is stored in India? Is it when startups use the IndiaAI Mission’s compute?”
An undefined standard means that the gaps get filled by someone else.
Let’s say an American cloud provider gets a US warrant for data stored in India, it has two options: comply and potentially violate Indian law, or refuse and violate US law.
That’s a contradiction that already exists inside every hyperscaler like AWS, Google, and Meta, operating in India. It just hasn’t been tested yet.
Meanwhile, India is still figuring out what “sovereign AI” even means.
On 13 April, the government set up the Technology and Policy Expert Committee (TPEC)—a six-member council tasked with designing India’s AI governance framework. Among other things, it will identify regulatory gaps and define the contours of India’s AI policy.
There’s no deadline for its first report. Even though India is already spending Rs 10,300 crore on building what it calls sovereign AI.
And it’s calling a lot of things “sovereign”.
