Good Morning Ishan,
The flagship Airtel store in Bengaluru’s Indiranagar is always packed.
A long row of customer-service agents filter through billing queries and Wi-Fi complaints. When asked what add-ons come with their network plans, one agent slides over a pamphlet. The last row shows the latest addition—a free 12-month subscription to content-creation platform Adobe Express. Four months ago, Perplexity AI occupied that spot.
In a smaller Jio store nearby, pamphlets are replaced with stickers on phone boxes promoting the telco’s partnership with Google Gemini.
That is to say, one partnership’s been quietly replaced. The other’s going strong. Both barely advertised until you ask.
But these partnerships are about more than AI.
During Bharti Airtel’s latest earnings call on 14 May, vice chairman Gopal Vittal complained that wealthy Indians still pay too little for internet access. It was a familiar frustration. India’s telecom industry has spent years battling a paradox of its own making: the world’s cheapest mobile data created one of the largest internet markets, but not necessarily one of its most lucrative.
Streaming video, social media, and endless scrolling pushed data consumption through the roof. Yet converting heavy users into higher-paying ones has proved far harder. AI may be the industry’s latest attempt to change that.
For AI companies too, it’s the intuitive way forward…
