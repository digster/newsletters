Good Morning Dear Reader,
Joy Narayan stumbled upon geriatric caregiving quite by chance.
When the 36-year-old first approached an employment agency in Bengaluru in 2017, he expected they’d stick him in housekeeping. Instead, they offered him a job in patient care. His first patient, a paralysed man in his 60s from Jayanagar, ended up teaching him everything he knows about caregiving. “He was my guru,” said Narayan.
Over seven years and a dozen patients with different ailments, Narayan learnt on the job. He earned Rs 24,000 a month for a 24-hour shift, of which the agency kept Rs 5,000. But when he got married, he started looking for a job that let him go home at night—something he couldn’t find in caregiving.
That’s when he shifted to being an office assistant, where he works eight-hour days, earning Rs 24,000 a month. Broken down on a per-hour basis, he’s now making over 3X what he did earlier.
That, in a nutshell, is the problem of senior care in the country.
Already, 10.5% of the population or around 150 million people, are above the age of 60. And this number is increasing, as over 19,500 Indians turn 60 each day. Of these, around 24% struggle with at least one daily activity, 48% with things like shopping and handling money, over half of those above 65 suffer from chronic illness, and a fourth of them have comorbidities.
In response, the senior-care sector is expanding too—albeit not fast enough.
