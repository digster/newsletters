Order an Iphone from an e-commerce platform. What if it’s fake or the site’s a scam? Would you go through the endless back-and-forth with the seller and the platform, or would you rather let your insurance handle it?
You would only choose the latter if you have cyber insurance.
Cyber insurance is the comparatively newer cool kid on the insurance block. Though, to be honest, Indians haven’t exactly rushed to buy insurance of any kind.
But cyber insurance rides on something else entirely: UPI frauds, e-commerce scams, and cybercrimes like “digital arrests”. Things that are making a lot of buzz these days, and for all the wrong reasons.
India often boasts about UPI payments as its superpower. Over [500 million](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-upi-the-global-benchmark-for-digital-payments) users, 65 million merchants, making it the world’s [largest](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/2025/09/indias-frictionless-payments-maria-peria) real-time payment system. But where money moves instantly, fraudsters move faster.
I’ve personally received messages like: “I mistakenly sent you Rs 44,000. Please return it.” Others I’ve spoken to have gotten the really scary one: “Your parcel containing illegal drugs has been caught. You are digitally arrested. This is your Aadhar number, correct? And that’s when the real trouble begins.
Every other day, a new way to defraud people comes to light.
These aren’t isolated. They’re everywhere. And people are falling for them. According to the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (NRCP), reported cybercrimes have jumped 10X since 2021, and the money lost is up a staggering 40X.

India, in fact, is now the [most](https://www.businessworld.in/article/india-leads-cyberattack-exposure-globally-ai-phishing-threats-intensify-568344?utm_source=GaggleAMP-Acronis&utm_medium=LinkedIn%20(GaggleAMP)&utm_campaign=none%20(GaggleAMP)&utm_content=india-leads-global-cyberattack-exposure-and-ai-is-making-it-wo-4957209&activity_id=4957209) targeted nation for cyberattacks
Amid this surge in frauds, big insurers like HDFC Ergo, Bajaj Allianz General Insurance, Kotak General Insurance, and SBI General Insurance have launched cyber insurance policies for individuals in the past few years. HDFC Ergo’s cyber sachet insurance offers customised plans for working professionals, shopaholics, families, and entrepreneurs. SBI has come up with Cyber Vaultedge.
These policies generally offer a standard safety net against:
– Theft of funds from your bank account or digital wallet
– Identity theft, where someone uses your identity to steal money
– Hardware replacement if your device is damaged by a malware attack
– Cyberbullying, cover the cost of removing objectionable content and psychological consultations
– Reputational damage, including legal costs for copyright violations stemming from social media posts.
However, a large focus for these insurance players remains B2B. Companies, not individuals. Afterall, that was cyberinsurance’s first customer. But since product development is often outsourced to tech giants like TCS, Infosys, or Capgemini, insurance is pushed as the first priority and tech is in the background.
Now, this format isn’t as good as a preventive measure.
This is where 63 Moons Technologies, mainly known for exchange technology and trading software, enters the game with a different approach. Essentially, they flip the script and bring tech (specifically cybersecurity) at the front and push insurance in the background. It’s the tech player selling the security, with insurance acting as an additional layer of protection.
In August last year, its cybersecurity arm, 63 Sats, launched an app called CYBX, a mobile security app that bundles cyber insurance. The CEO himself had the firsthand experience of a “digital arrest” call and realised what he would have wanted from a cybersecurity app.
Since launch, CYBX claims to have crossed 1 million downloads and sold over 2 lakh policies. The company has now launched version 2 of the app, which offers call security features and even your mobile’s security rating, similar to a Cibil score.
The app charges around Rs 5,000 a year for a Rs 10 lakh cover. Depending on subscription, insurance cover varies from Rs 10,000 to Rs 10,00,000.
Here’s how the different offerings stack up:
Bajaj Allianz: Appears the cheapest, starting from Rs 400 to Rs 1,800 per year, but the cover is capped at just Rs 50,000.
HDFC Ergo: Basic plan charges around Rs 1,000 for a Rs 1 lakh cover (with higher limits available up to Rs 5 crore)
SBI General: Charges Rs 3,200 for a Rs 10 lakh cover, and Rs 6,865 for a Rs 1 crore cover
Unlike a static insurance policy, CYBX acts as an active shield. It flags threats in real time with pop-up warnings and blocks suspicious links or QR codes before money leaves your account.
But here’s the catch. To do all that monitoring, CYBX needs a long list of permissions: camera, contacts, location, microphone, nearby devices, photos, videos, and even calls and notifications. That’s a lot of access. For many users, that could be a red flag.
Pathare counters that the app is certified by the STQC lab (Standardisation testing and quality certification lab under Meity). He claims the company doesn’t store your data; it just watches “like a sharpshooter with a sniper” and shoots down threats. Still, trust is a big ask when an app wants to see almost everything you do.
There’s also the fine print. While HDFC Ergo and SBI General clearly list what’s not covered, like crypto theft or losses from gambling, those exclusions aren’t as easy to find on CYBX.
Whether you choose CYBX (which currently partners with ICICI Lombard) or a traditional insurer, most brochures gloss over the same hard truth. Try claiming insurance after a UPI fraud.
“You’ll need an FIR, a bank statement, a screenshot of the fraudulent transaction, and a written complaint to the platform,” explains a cybersecurity analyst. “Still the police won’t file the FIR until they see a copy of your complaint registered on the NCRP portal.”
The sum insured is in big, bold letters. The claim journey is always in the fine print. Cyber insurance is no an exception.
