Good Morning Ishan,
Every November or so, Bajaj Finance’s senior leadership lets loose by travelling to places like Kodaikanal or the Himalayas, trekking over rocky outcrops, and “tearing their company apart”.
“Ruminate”, as the non-banking financial company’s retreat is dubbed, is somewhat of an annual ritual. For the first day, CEO Rajeev Jain books a hotel banquet hall, and the entire team sits knee to knee on the floor. They have one goal: red-teaming.
Are their loan-processing costs too high? Are they ripe for disruption by the new breed of fintechs? Are they cross-selling enough?
Their reason is simple—they want to find flaws in their company before someone else does.
This level of frankness is unimaginably rare. Even startups aren’t so open about disrupting themselves; forget about the fifth generation of a family-owned and -operated business. Naturally, whatever’s said in the room stays in the room.
But the retreat isn’t the only place for takedowns. Even in their day-to-day processes, they study what some of the world’s greatest companies are doing.
