Good Morning Ishan,
Udaan spent nearly a decade trying to build an asset-light marketplace for India’s wholesalers. Now it’s trying to become one of them.
The company that raised over $2.3 billion promising to organise India’s fragmented B2B trade is abandoning the business model it spent six years selling to investors. Instead of connecting buyers and sellers, it increasingly wants to buy products itself, source staples cheaply, and sell them at scale.
“The model now is to become the Dmart for shopkeepers,” said a person familiar with the strategy. “The focus is getting better-quality products at lower costs and building sourcing capabilities around staples.”
This is not exactly a voluntary reinvention. Earlier in July, global creditors, including Nomura, Standard Chartered, and Tor Investment Management, filed for insolvency proceedings in the Singapore High Court against Udaan’s offshore holding entity after it defaulted on $170 million convertible notes.
Udaan has maintained that the proceedings won’t affect its business in India, and has since announced a $160 million fundraise, which is a combination of new equity and debt and conversion of a part of its convertible bonds into equity. Blackrock, the world’s largest asset manager, is reportedly part of the funding round, offering $45 million in private credit.
The reset began even before the debt drama.

The company trying to survive today looks very different from the one that its operations architect, Sujeet Kumar, helped build years ago.
