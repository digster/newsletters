India were struggling at 70/5 at the Hagley Oval in Christchurch on Saturday when Tilak Varma walked out to bat. It was his anchoring innings of 51 off 26 that ensured India crossed the line with a ball to spare. 

Just two days earlier, Nike had announced a multi-year deal with Mumbai Indians (MI) as their official kit partner for both the men’s (IPL) and women’s (WPL) teams. 

It was quite a week for Nike in India. 

Tilak, a Nike-sponsored athlete and an MI player, was everywhere online, and yet Nike could capitalise on the moment only so much. While Tilak was making headlines in Indian colours, the jersey carried three stripes. That has been Nike’s story in India, especially when it comes to cricket. 

The brand has had visibility, access to some of the country’s biggest sporting moments (it sponsored the Indian jersey from 2006 to 2020) and a strong aspirational pull. However, converting all that into a profitable business has been harder.
