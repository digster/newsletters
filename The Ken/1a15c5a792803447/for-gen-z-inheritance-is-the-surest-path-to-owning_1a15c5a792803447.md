Good Morning Ishan,
Twenty-nine-year-old Harsh, who works in the social sector, never considered inheriting his parents’ South Delhi property. Instead, he’s been saving 40% of his salary each month in the hopes of buying his own house. Just like his parents did back in 2008.
But he recognises that apart from their dreams of home ownership, the two generations have little in common. “Of course, housing prices were rising for our parents’ generation, but they weren’t skyrocketing like today. Plus, our incomes are stagnating,” said Harsh.
He’s not wrong.
A 2023 paper by Delhi-based public-policy think tank, Centre for Social and Economic Progress (CSEP), showed homes cost 11X annual income, against an affordability benchmark of 5X. In Mumbai and Delhi, house prices doubled between 2010 and 2025, per data from real-estate consultancy Knight Frank. In Gurugram, they jumped by 3.5X.
The oldest among the Gen Zs, youngsters like Harsh are confronting a far tougher macroeconomic environment than earlier generations—with unemployment rising, inflation spiralling, and wages stagnating.
Of the 5 million students who graduated each year between 2005 and 2023, only about 2.8 million found employment, according to a study published by Azim Premji University. Fewer still found salaried employment. Simultaneously, real wages, i.e., income adjusted for inflation, actually fell by 4% between 2012 and 2024. To make matters worse…
