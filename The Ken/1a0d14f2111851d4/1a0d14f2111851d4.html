Chloe, Iggy, Billy, Jessie, and Banu aren’t doctors. They don’t have a medical degree between them or any lab experience. What they do have is a weakness for treats—and a 90.8% sensitivity in detecting cancer from human breath.

They are employed by Dognosis, a Bengaluru startup building a cancer screening test around them. They are dogs, yes.

The test is simple enough: breathe into a surgical mask for 10 minutes, seal and freeze the mask, and have a handful of trained dogs take turns sniffing it. The concept couldn’t be any simpler either: cancer cells leak volatile organic compounds, or VOCs, into the breath and dogs possess the ability to pick up their smell.  

The dogs are only half of it, though. Dognosis feeds the animals’ responses into a Bayesian model that weighs them against what is already known about the patient—age, family history, tobacco use—to generate a score for the likelihood of cancer.

In a series of trials conducted across six Karnataka hospitals between March 2024 and June 2025, involving over 1,500 participants, the test achieved 90.8% sensitivity and 91.3% specificity, holding steady from stages I through IV, according to a study published by Dognosis in the Journal of Clinical Oncology in June.

Early detection is the holy grail of cancer screening, and different companies are trying to address it in different ways.
