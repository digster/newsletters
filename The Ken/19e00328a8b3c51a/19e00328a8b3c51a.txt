Anthropic just dropped https://github.com/anthropics/financial-services 10 AI agents for financial services. These agents are designed to assist, or even replace, analysts’ jobs. They come as plugins that you can install in your Claude Cowork or Claude Code, or use with any Claude-managed agent. 

Dario Amodei, the man behind Anthropic, has an interesting personality.

On even days, he launches extremely powerful AI products such as Mythos, about which my colleagues wrote https://the-ken.com/story/what-anthropics-mythos-can-hack-in-hours-indian-banks-take-months-to-fix/?utm_source=web&utm_medium=homepage&utm_campaign=con_1_unit_8 about this Monday. On odd days, he keeps warning everyone about how his own creations could be very dangerous. 

Feels contradictory, right? 

I think it’s more about seizing AI opportunities while also creating opportunities from the risks that AI poses.

With the Mythos buzz grabbing headlines, maybe it was the perfect time for Anthropic to roll out these plugins, which also come built into Claude Opus 4.7. These are:

The Fin-10 takeover?

Source: Anthropic

These pre-built agents can draft pitch decks, analyst work, models, research notes, and more. Another agent, a KYC screener, uses KYC/AML rules to process onboarding data, assign risk, verify documents, and also provide notes on the KYCs.

A human is then supposed to review and sign off. 

This is Anthropic’s disclaimer: “They do not make investment recommendations.”

This might make it seem like the AI company has done its due diligence. But when it comes to integrating new systems into existing ones, some variables are not so visible.

“Companies could cut analyst headcount and rely more on AI agents.”

Senior consultant, Deloitte

And he is worried. Not just for himself, but for retail investors as well. 

“Not far from here, an audit/broking firm will just feed data with a prompt: ‘Calculate valuation using DCF or NAV, whichever is chosen.’ Then another agent writes the analyst’s notes based on the very same particulars produced. And what are you left with? A fully AI-generated picture of the stock. Would you believe it? You have no choice.” 

In valuation exercises, regardless of the method, you take certain judgement calls. For instance, a 2% variation in the growth assumption could swing the valuation wildly, he explained.  

These agents are capable of drafting presentation materials for client meetings, auditing financial statements, and even escalating cases for compliance review. 

Exactly a year ago, I remember, Amodei himself wrote in his blog https://darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability: “When a generative AI system does something, like summarize a financial document, we have no idea, at a specific or precise level, why it makes the choices it does—why it chooses certain words over others, or why it occasionally makes a mistake despite usually being accurate.”

And true to his contradictory self, he went and launched plugins that could soon be influencing your investments or other financial decisions indirectly. It is also true that much has changed in one year, but that has made things more unpredictable than ever. 

Mandatory two-step verification 

Analysts are already using AI agents to parse data and for many other use cases, including audit purposes. Deloitte has also partnered with Anthropic for financial services agents.

Slowly, as analysts see the agents succeeding in small tasks and giving them the answers they need, will our analysts still maintain their rigour before sharing the AI-assisted  research reports about the companies that many of us read up on before investing?

Sharat Chandra, founder of advisory firm Empoweredge Ventures put it bluntly: “The efficacy of Claude’s financial agents is untested. What if an agent goes rogue? Remember the ‘Gone in 9 seconds’ scenario https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/claude-powered-ai-coding-agent-deletes-entire-company-database-in-9-seconds-backups-zapped-after-cursor-tool-powered-by-anthropics-claude-goes-rogue—where an AI deleted a company database? Retail investors should treat any firm using these agents for valuation or KYC with extreme caution.”

For the danger is twofold for them. First, at the information level, which would ultimately impact their investment. Secondly, and most importantly, the money in their banks.

Just as Israeli spying named Pegasus has been widely used https://www.nytimes.com/2022/01/28/magazine/nso-group-israel-spyware.html by multiple country heads for snooping, imagine a foreign government machinery gaining access, using a model like Mythos, and attacking a bank. 

India is the second-most https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2025/09/mapping-indias-cybersecurity-administration-in-2025 targeted nation for cyber attacks. In this scenario, a company or a research report touting “AI-powered research” or “agent-driven valuations” may not be something to trust blindly.

I would do my due diligence, for sure.
