When you are a journalist, it’s impossible not to be one even when on vacation. Your curiosity invariably gets the better of you. When I was in Hong Kong years ago, I was completely taken with convenience-store chains Circle K and 7-Eleven. I would seldom pass a store that I didn’t step into, and I bought way more Pocari Sweat, a Japanese energy drink, than was good for me. 

My recent trip to Berlin, to visit my sister, was no different. Only this time, the object of my obsession was much larger retail stores than neighbourhood ones. I had read a lot about discount grocers Aldi and Lidl. As a retail reporter, these were at the top of my list.  

But my sister and brother-in-law made sure I visited a few others, too, including hypermarket chain Edeka and cosmetics-and-health-foods retailer DM-Drogerie Markt. To me, each of these was the proverbial candy store. 

There are seemingly minor things—such as caps tethered to bottles, which is mandatory, and juice and milk cartons that can be opened by bending back the top flaps and not by snipping the corner off—that go a long way in recycling.
