Long and Short
Wed, 11 Mar 26
The story behind the numbers and motivations driving India's stock market.
Good Morning Ishan,
Even though Shashikanth Suryanarayanan has built Sedemac as a hard-nosed entrepreneur who values traditional metrics of a sound business more than narratives, it’s hard to take the professor out of him.
(Even brokerages and finance platforms are addressing him as “Professor” in their IPO analysis.)
In the last few years, he has carved out time from running the company to periodically educate investors on what it takes to build an IP-based technology product company. The $100 million round in 2024 came mostly from such investors and was almost entirely a secondary round.
Despite significant investor interest, Sedemac did not raise primary capital.
The Rs 1,087 crore IPO today is once again an offer for sale (OFS), in which newer investors, such as A91 and 360 One, among others, will sell their shares. An OFS-only IPO is not unusual these days. LG Electronics, WeWork, and, prior to that, Hyundai Motors India, raised nearly Rs 43,000 crore on the stock exchange without taking any money into the company.
But Suryanarayanan goes to great lengths to explain why Sedemac isn’t selling shares to capitalise itself. In the nine months of FY26, the company reported revenue of Rs 770 crore, more than the Rs 658 crore it garnered in FY25. Its profits reflect that operating leverage is kicking in—at Rs 71 crore in 9M FY26, it’s 51% higher than in the previous fiscal.
For every additional dollar of revenue, [he says,](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AkkGf1sk-fI) the company needs less than $0.2 of capex and $0.2 of operating capital. The company can generate this from internal accruals. Suryanarayan believes 20%+ of Ebitda can “easily fund 30–40% growth”. If the company grows faster than that, it’d need to take on debt. It knows the drill; for six years until June 2024, the company did not raise any money.
That’s efficient capital use in an automotive electronics business that is R&D- and capex-heavy. At an annualised forward FY26 price-to-earnings multiple of 62–63, it’s more expensive than Indian competitors such as Sona Comstar (54–56X) and global leaders like Bosch (37X).
In late 2024, when [I asked](https://the-ken.com/story/45m-two-wheelers-in-india-use-his-world-beating-tech-its-improbable-and-built-on-a-whim/?searchTerm=Sedemac&utm_source=search&utm_medium=page) Suryanarayanan why, even as a private company, he had been holding half-yearly investor calls, he said: “Most people do not believe in [or understand fresh technology]. They will not even know what technology is. For most investors, technology means software companies. And deep tech means stuff that even software companies cannot do. This is what it is.”
