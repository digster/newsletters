Good Morning Ishan,
Ronnie Screwvala is “allergic” to the term edtech, which, unfortunately, is what’s used to describe his company Upgrad.
And I don’t blame him. One, the sector has imploded for all sorts of avoidable reasons. Two, his company has ingested several edtechs in its acquisition journey—16 and counting. The latest one, Internshala, was last week.
But what’s in a name? Let’s stick to the moniker he likes: “forever learning space”.
The learn-once-lean-on-it-forever model had broken down long before AI giants began releasing LLM features at a weekly cadence. (Even as I write this, on Thursday evening, Anthropic released Claude-scientific-skills with 140 skills across every major research domain baked into one plugin.)
Fast learning is the only survival strategy for professionals or businesses. Now, how that learning is happening or will happen is one giant, live experiment.
It’s incredible that in these 10 years, Upgrad has acquired so many companies—a rollup edtech, if there was one—that it will soon dabble in all parts of the professional lifecycle, from that first internship after graduation to executive courses for mid-career professionals.
Still, in a post-AI and -Byju’s world, upskilling courses by edtech platforms will have to sell harder than ever. Job placements haven’t eased up, either. The company, for its part, claims that Internshala is not purely a financial asset. That makes sense, given that it’s posted losses of Rs 10–12 crore in each of the last three years. So, then what exactly is it for?
Debanjali has some answers in her [story today](https://the-ken.com/story/ronnie-screwvalas-answer-to-upgrads-profitability-woes-buy-a-loss-making-company/?utm_source=daily_story&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=daily_newsletter).
