Good Morning Ishan,
On 12 August, when Jio Financial Services announced that Bank of America would buy nearly half of Jio Credit, its lending arm, many hailed it as yet another display of Mukesh Ambani’s deal-making chops.
After all, this is the third marquee foreign player that Jio Financial Services, backed by Asia’s richest man, has roped in just the past two years.
First, it partnered with global investment giant Blackrock for asset management, broking, and wealth management. Next, German insurer Allianz joined hands for insurance. And now, Bank of America will be putting in Rs 18,268 crore ($1.9 billion) for a 49.9% stake in the fully owned lending subsidiary of Jio Financial Services.
The deal gives Bank of America a 26.5% stake in Jio Credit initially via equity, and the rest through warrant conversion within 18 months. On paper, it’s a win-win.
“Jio Credit receives further capital to support its growth in India and the expertise of a global financial-services firm,” read the press release. “The investment supports Bank of America’s commitment to its global franchise with a strong local partner in India.”
Dig deeper, and the deal reveals itself.
Jio Financial Services has sold Jio Credit, its crown jewel, for cheap.
