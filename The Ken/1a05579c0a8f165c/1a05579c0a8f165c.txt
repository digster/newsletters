At Yantian Port on China’s southern coast, a fleet of electric Foton Auman trucks navigates the terminal, pausing periodically to exchange depleted batteries for fully-charged ones.

At Jawaharlal Nehru Port (JNPA) near Mumbai, a fleet of Ashwa electric trucks does the same.

But the resemblance goes beyond mere operational similarity. 

Energy in Motion (Eim), the Indian electric vehicle (EV) company behind Ashwa, holds an exclusive agreement with Foton to rebadge and sell its electric trucks in India. 

The Ashwa is the Auman, with an Indian sticker affixed.

Eim could not avail the subsidies under India’s PM e-drive scheme for the rebranded Auman, as the truck does not meet the scheme’s localisation requirements.

The Ashwa is a telling illustration of a broader problem obstructing India’s push to electrify heavy commercial vehicles.
