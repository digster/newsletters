An engineering degree at Mahindra University costs in the region of Rs 20 lakh. But it is free, the institution announced earlier in June, for any student who qualifies the national entrance exam JEE with a high rank. A generous offer, surely. But it might not find many takers.

That, after all, is what history suggests.

The private university bankrolled by the billionaire industrialist Anand Mahindra launched six years ago seeking to disrupt elite engineering education in the country, much like Ashoka and OP Jindal Global universities had done with liberal arts. But it has struggled to attract the kind of students it needs to “re-imagine growth-oriented education”. 

Not for lack of trying. The university recruits faculty from reputed institutions and pays them competitive salaries. It has tie-ups with foreign universities such as Cornell, Babson, and Southampton. It offers programmes that are unique in India, like B.Tech with LLB and BBA in Infrastructure Management. 

Still, its student body comes almost entirely from Telangana, where it’s based.
