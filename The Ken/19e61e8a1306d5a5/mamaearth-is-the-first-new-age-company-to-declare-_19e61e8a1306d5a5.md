Good Morning Ishan,
Two consumer companies, in their annual results last week, announced they would pay out 50–80% of their net profits as dividends.
One is an FMCG giant that has been giving out [dividends](https://ticker.finology.in/discover/market-update/itc-dividend-history) since 1994. The other is a beauty and personal care startup that listed just two years ago.
The first company is ITC. The brand behind everything from Aashirvaad atta to Gold Flake cigarettes. For FY26, it announced 85% of its net profit, about Rs 18,000 crore, as dividends.
The second company in question is Honasa Consumer, the parent behind popular beauty brands such as Mamaearth, Dr Sheth’s, and others. They announced dividends of Rs 3 per share, adding up to nearly Rs 100 crore, which is about 50% of their net profits for the year.
“Honasa is the first new-age company who has announced dividends,” co-founder Ghazal Alagh [declared](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYmZ1-XwvXF/) to her 710,000 followers on Instagram on Friday.
With this move, the Gurugram-based company seems to be swapping its digital-native persona for conventional FMCG ways of doing business.
The recent decision to give out dividends, like its FMCG peers such as ITC, Hindustan Unilever, Marico and others, underlines that posturing, analysts told The Ken.
Usually, digital-first, D2C companies in their early years are growth-hungry, funnelling back excess cash to expand their reach, rather than paying it out to shareholders. But for Honasa, the move is less about shelling out cash and more about what it signals.
Honasa went public in November 2023, and it has been profitable, on an annual basis, ever since (except for a blip in FY22). And two years later, it is paying up.
Compare it to other new-age brands.
Zomato, the food aggregator turned consumer tech company now housed under Eternal, returned a maiden profit in June 2023 and has continued to do so since FY24. Even beauty platform Nykaa, which listed in November 2021, has been profitable for six years now.
But they have both not declared dividends.
Eternal, with its profits, has been expanding its network of dark stores for Blinkit, its quick commerce platform, and moving the stock to an inventory model. It is also spending on growing other verticals, such as the [District](https://the-ken.com/story/district-isnt-even-2-of-eternals-business-but-its-enough-to-rattle-bookmyshow/), its platform for booking movies and events.
Nykaa has been allocating excess cash on its balance sheet to open new stores (with more than 300 stores, it’s already the country’s largest beauty chain) and acquiring new brands. The Ken wrote about how Deepika Padukone’s [skincare brand](https://the-ken.com/tradetricks/what-does-nykaa-see-in-deepika-padukones-withering-beauty-brand/) is next on its radar.
Honasa, for its part, seems to be choosing a stable FMCG narrative rather than an aggressive challenger.
In the Instagram video from 21 May, Alagh said Honasa reported its “highest ever quarterly revenue and profits”. Their own brand, Mamaearth, has been growing in the high teens, while acquired brands such as Dr Sheth’s and Bblunt have been growing over 40% collectively.
She said the dividends show that, as a business, they don’t just prioritise capital allocation. “We are only getting started,” she added.
But if they are just getting started and are seeing double-digit growth, why pause and pay a dividend? Why not focus on growth like its “new-age” peers?
Honasa’s Mamaearth trails only HUL and L’oréal as the country’s [third-largest](https://entrackr.com/2024/10/mamaearth-ranks-as-indias-3rd-largest-skincare-brand-euromonitor/) skincare brand. But with an annual turnover of Rs 2,400 crore, it’s only at about one-tenth the scale of HUL’s beauty and personal care units.
Here, Honasa had two ways to tell a compelling investor story:
One option was to lean fully into the growth narrative and paint a roadmap of how it could scale from a Rs 2,500 crore business into a Rs 10,000 crore one. It has already outgrown similar brands like Sugar Cosmetics and Plum Goodness.
But after the distribution troubles Mamaearth faced over the last two years—when excess inventory was pushed into the market through super-stockists—that would have been a harder sell, industry insiders told The Ken. The company has since been fixing those issues through a direct distribution network and steadily growing its core brands, they added.
Instead, Honasa appears to be leaning into a different message.
The dividend announcement fits neatly into that positioning, analysts told The Ken.
The numbers on its balance sheet support this. As of 31 March 2026, cash on Honasa’s balance sheet stood at roughly 1.25X its working capital requirements. For Nykaa and Zomato, it was less than 0.6–0.75X their respective working capitals for the same period, according to back-of-the-envelope calculations done by The Ken.
“Honasa started with growth capital from VC and PE investors. But as you travel along the journey, you will see a churn in investors. The new investors (such as mutual funds) would prefer stability and maturity over hypergrowth,” said Bisen.
If the rate-of-return on equity is less than a certain percentage, then investors prefer that the company pays out dividends, a buy-side analyst explained. They gave the example of how Warren Buffett’s Berkshire Hathaway has made more money on dividends in certain companies than the money they paid to buy that stock.
But being an FMCG company comes with its own costs. For instance, in the coming year, FMCG majors are likely to face high packaging costs, linked to the West Asia war.
It also doesn’t hurt that the Alaghs, as owners of roughly 30% of the company, get to make Rs 35 crore with the dividends.
