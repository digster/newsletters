Good Morning Ishan,
When the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) publicly denied Nisarga Adhikary’s discovery of critical vulnerabilities in its Class 12 board exam portal, the 19-year-old simply broke back in. He filmed himself navigating the board’s systems and left memes and anime videos playing on its servers to prove his point.
It wasn’t an isolated find. Since then, Adhikary has reported around 30 vulnerabilities to the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In), the government’s cybersecurity agency. Most, he says, remain unpatched, unacknowledged, or both.
“The sheer incompetence of our authorities baffles me,” he told The Ken. “This is the kind of stuff that, if left to me, I can fix in a matter of hours.”
India suddenly needs people like him.
…
The government has begun looking for talent in places it long overlooked. Shortly after the CBSE breach, the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Kanpur hired Adhikary as a threat intelligence engineer. The same week in June, it launched India’s first undergraduate cybersecurity programme, designed to admit young hackers through hackathons rather than just JEE scores and train them inside “government security organisations”.
But like many of India’s best cybersecurity researchers, Adhikary is already weighing offers from California.
