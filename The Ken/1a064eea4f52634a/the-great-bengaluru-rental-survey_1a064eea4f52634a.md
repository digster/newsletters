“When can you move to Bengaluru, Vishnu?”
“I have my exams now; I can move as soon as they are over. So, in about two weeks?”
... the moment I started house-hunting, I realised the risk had paid off big time. Landlords were calling me back and dropping rents without me even asking. Ka-Ching!
Sitting in my fully furnished (mini-chandelier-hanging-in-the-living-room-level-furnishing) 2 BHK right next to Silk Board for Rs 14,000, I did not realise what a steal I had stumbled into. Even more so because I was sharing it with three friends!
Ah, how the tables turn.
Now, about 5 N95-free years later, that memory feels like a lucid dream.
...
About a month ago, Anand made a [compelling case](https://the-ken.com/story/bengalurus-rents-have-gone-too-far-its-time-to-cap-them/?utm_source=web&utm_medium=homepage&utm_campaign=con_1_unit_1) that the city’s rents have outpaced all reason, and that it might be time to cap them.
When he started working on the story, he asked if I could help with a survey, and I jumped at the chance.
We ended up with 621 responses: 373 tenants, 85 owner-occupiers, 58 landlords, and 105 watching from outside the city, probably trying to decide whether Bengaluru is still worth the move.
The eventual story could only carry a handful of those responses and numbers. This free newsletter is everything else: the rest of the data along with my own reflections after playing around with the numbers and the five interactive dashboards Anuj designed.
Do take some time to hover around the charts; filter by your age, your point in your career, your corner of the city, and see how alone (or not) you really are.
Every city owes some basics to the people who live in it and make it tick.
