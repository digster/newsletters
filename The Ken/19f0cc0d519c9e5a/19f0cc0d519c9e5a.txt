2. Monthly theme update and a shoutout 

Hello, Tanim here with the books section. 

Most weeks, reading is one of those things that stays permanently in the not-urgent, not-immediate quadrant, until Friday morning, when I remember that I have a section due this Sunday. 

This month’s theme is investigative reporting. Two hours of (non-AI) research later, the books that surfaced were overwhelmingly from the global north, whereas very few were from the subcontinent. Maybe my search was biased because I rely heavily on X (formerly Twitter) and Reddit to find my recommendations. 

Finally, my pick wasn’t exactly an obscure find. It was a very popular book that comes up if you search for “Indian investigative journalism books”. Nothing wrong with that. But a few weeks in, it started to feel like it was a memoir of a journalist’s encounter with corruption. It doesn’t pick a hypothesis and test it, but ends at “This happened, and then this happened”. 

Not to say that that’s wrong. These books are useful to give us an overview of how corruption works, but they stop short at trying to find solutions, for systemic problems will always exist, and it’s our question to ask, “Is there a way around it?”

Everyone knows that bribes are an everyday thing. How many of us know what to do when we find ourselves in that situation? 

Does a part always represent the whole of a system/institution? If not, how do we learn to separate that? If yes, how do we pluck the rotten apples and let the tree grow anew?

I haven’t finished the book, so this isn’t a verdict. 

I am more curious about what you have been reading. Those of you who are here because you read The Ken means you like richly reported stories, the kind that makes you think, “I didn’t know things worked this way” or “that’s a sharper way to frame this than I’d have thought”. If you have read a book recently that made you think along those lines, all of us would like to know.

You can use the form https://theken.typeform.com/FPBooks?typeform-source=the-ken.com if you want structure, or just write to me at fp@the-ken.com mailto:fp@the-ken.com. 

Next month’s theme is Reader’s Choice, so start reading something fun or recollecting your thoughts of the most fun you had while reading in the past six months. 

And with this Sunday, we are almost done with half of the year. Thank you for your recommendations and emails that keep this community reading. 

Huge shoutout to Girish Menon, Amar Chandramohan, Avinash Subramaniam, Kirti Manian, and Pritha Saha for regular recommendations. 

Hopefully, by the end of the year, we will have more names on that list.

3. Movie pick of the week

Hey there!

It’s Vishnu with the movie recommendation for this week.

But before that, thank you for all the support you showed for last week’s horror title picks. I even got a good list of watchlist items out of it for myself:

Top left to down right: Incantation (2022), Bhoot (2003), Gehrayee (1980), Kairo/Pulse (2001)

So this week, I am balancing things out with a nice, breezy, happy movie that quickly became one of my go-to ‘watch this to feel happy’ picks:

The Fundamentals of Caring (Comedy/Drama/Road movie, 2016)

After a personal tragedy, Ben (Paul Rudd) decides to be a caregiver. His first job puts him with Trevor, who has Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy—making him wheelchair-bound and unable to walk.

Trevor is a sarcastic, cynical teenager who sees the dark side of everything, with a twisted sense of humour (I LOVED his lines!). The duo grows closer through some amazing banter, helped along by the road trip they take to tick off American roadside attractions.

The movie has some really heartwarming moments, great jokes that land, and an ever-so-charming Selena Gomez as well, if that helps :)) It’s the perfect movie to watch on a lazy Sunday afternoon. Streaming on Netflix.
