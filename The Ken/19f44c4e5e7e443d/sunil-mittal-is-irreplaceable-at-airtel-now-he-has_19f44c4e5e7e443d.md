Good Morning Ishan,
There is a rule at Airtel that every employee must be itching to get back to work on Mondays. If not, they can shake their boss by the collar and let them know what was causing them stress. It is called the “reverse Monday syndrome”.
It looks like a strange rule. But it was necessary.
After all, Airtel’s employees always operated in wartime mode, fighting for the survival of the company. This is a company that has been on the receiving end of every regulatory change in telecom, all while trying to keep up with the transformations in technology and Reliance’s incessant attacks.
“We were so used to a wartime style of working that we created our own fires. We still have some pockets that don’t like calm, so they create fires and then try to douse them.” This is how Sunil Mittal describes the culture at Airtel. A company perpetually at war.
For instance, in 2002, the same year that Airtel listed on the stock exchange at Rs 45 per share, Reliance waged a war against it, setting call rates that were half of its competitor’s. Airtel’s stock price crashed to Rs 19, yet only a few people left.
This deep dive into the culture at Airtel is based on episode three of Intermission, The Ken’s long-form podcast. [Listen now](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_dONpkY7-U).
This was, in large part, due to the culture of the company, that bred loyalty.
Consider that when Reliance was preparing to disrupt the market, the Mittal brothers took the top executives to an offsite in Nepal. Mittal’s pitch to them was neither alarmist nor incendiary. Instead, he asked them for their loyalty. “Profit and money are secondary, I need your help to build a world-class company.”
