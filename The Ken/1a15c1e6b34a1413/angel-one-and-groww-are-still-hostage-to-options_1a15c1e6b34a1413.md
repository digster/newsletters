The rally last Tuesday lifted all boats, but brokerages had more than one reason to celebrate this tide.
The finance minister’s comments on long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax and securities transaction tax (STT) came amid calls for relief in a bear market. She didn’t promise any cuts, but the possibility was enough to give investors a ray of hope.
Then came Sebi’s revised rules for the Closing Auction Session (CAS), which focused on futures and options (F&O).
On 15 October, as reports of the changes came in, shares of Groww, Angel One and other capital market companies rose over 3%, as the investors welcomed these changes with open wallets. That evening, Sebi issued a circular confirming the changes.
The enthusiasm is understandable. Both tax relief and a more familiar settlement mechanism could encourage trading activity, and that’s the brokerages’ bread and butter.
But their Q2 results, reported last week, suggest that a trading recovery would only be part of the story. Groww’s profit fell sequentially, while Angel One’s rose sharply (helped by a cost that didn’t recur). Yet neither escaped the wrath of the slowdown in equity derivatives revenue.
