Good Morning Ishan,
Sector 62 in Noida is remarkably boring. There are office buildings and scattered roadside tea stalls. But of late, tucked amid this mundane hum is a data centre. A Rs 744-crore joint venture between Adani Group and American data-centre provider Edgeconnex.
“The plot used to be mostly sleepy, with lush peepul trees,” said a 78-year-old resident who has lived nearby for decades.
There were eight peepul trees, to be exact. And according to an environmental-clearance report accessed by The Ken, they were transplanted on-site with permission.
Operational since last year, the Adaniconnex data centre gets its electricity, for now, from the Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited (PVVNL), the government-owned distributor. Uttar Pradesh Power Corporation Limited (UPPCL), the state-level power utility, estimates just 32 minutes of outages in six months, so the data centre’s backup diesel generators, according to Adani’s environmental-clearance application, shouldn’t have much to do.
Its electricity appetite, however, is decidedly less modest: an estimated 80 MW.
Satellite image of the Adaniconnex Noida Data Centre plot 2021–2026
It may look like just another plot in Sector 62. But with an 80 MW appetite, this is exactly the sort of customer an electricity company would want for itself.
