Zero Shot Sat, 15 Aug 26 |
Good Morning Ishan,
We’re shutting down [Zero Shot](https://the-ken.com/zero-shot/).
Brady, Praveen, and I started it last September as a collective endeavour to cover the spread of AI technologies on a regular basis. Here’s what we said it would be:
Multi-perspective column and podcast covering the big ideas, real-world implications, and practical applications of artificial intelligence in every setting—in the boardroom, at the workplace, in the lab, and at the workbench.
Praveen wrote the first column, “[India goes from free internet to free AI](https://the-ken.com/columns/zero-shot/india-goes-from-free-internet-to-free-ai/)”.
The biggest risk that India faces today is ironically what the campaign couldn’t free Indians from—the long-term dependence on American tech companies. Meta still won: WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook are now all ubiquitous. If things again unfold the way they did before, then ChatGPT, Perplexity, and others like them will be the only consumer AI companies that Indians are locked into as users and producers of data for decades.
Then, four days later, the three of us hosted our first podcast, with Brady logging in from Hong Kong with a typoon hovering around. We discussed if the world’s biggest AI companies were using a colonial playbook to enter markets like India, and whether India [risked becoming](https://the-ken.com/podcasts/zero-shot/a-zebra-striped-dinosaur-ai-giants-colonising-the-world-and-tiktoks-deal-in-the-us/) a “DAU factory”.
Over close to 100 columns and podcast episodes later we’re confident that we’ve reported, analysed, and learnt a lot. And hopefully, so have you.
We’re not ending Zero Shot because AI got boring. Far from it. We’re doing it because mere analysis has stopped being enough. Every week as we explained what happened, each of us felt the sting of the unanswered question: so what should I actually do about it? Watching the field is not the same as taking charge of your place in it. That gap started to bother me more than any single launch.
It led us at The Ken directly to our last event, [Intelligence Independence](https://the-ken.com/events/intelligence-independence/).
In today’s war for the intelligence rail, India is mostly the object. We are among the largest markets for proprietary AI models, prime targets for lock-in, and, as this year showed, candidates for being locked out overnight. The models learn from every prompt, file, and correction we feed them. Even Microsoft’s CEO is asking everyone to pay attention: “In consuming intelligence, you are creating intelligence. And what you create should belong to you.”
This is now critical for every professional and business using AI. Which models do you use, when the best ones can be withdrawn by another country’s government? Open-weight models now match the proprietary ones we run on, at a fraction of the price. What do you feed them, when it all trains your replacement? What should stay in assets you own: your prompts, context, the definition of good work? These choices exist today that didn’t a year ago.
Ever since we wrapped up that event two weeks ago, I’ve been thinking that the theme isn’t something that gets “done” with just one event. It needs to be an ongoing project. That project will be called Intelligence Independence.
The premise is simple. Models are becoming commodities. There will be dozens, open and closed, large and small, and no single one will rule. The fight that matters was never about whose model is best (never mind the attempts by a few labs to make us believe so). Instead, it is about our data and our “alpha”. How we decide, what we have learned, the way we work. Most of us hand all of that to a handful of companies every day and call it productivity. We get the output while they keep our compounding.
Intelligence Independence is about the other path. How a professional, a startup, an enterprise, or a country builds a foundation to protect, compound, and sharpen its own intelligence as AI gets more powerful, instead of quietly renting it by the month.
Frankly, I don’t have a clear idea of the forms this project will take. Will it be columns or articles? Podcast episodes? Interviews? Community? Multi-city events?
I will reach out to you shortly to ask you what you’d want from Intelligence Independence. What would make it valuable and useful for you?
If you followed Zero Shot to keep up with AI, there are a hundred places to do that. I hope you will follow Intelligence Independence to decide what you are going to do about it.
—Rohin Dharmakumar, Brady Ng, and Praveen Gopal Krishnan
