Four years ago, Cloudnine Group of Hospitals couldn’t convince investors that it was ready to go public. Today, four of the world’s well-known private-equity firms are in a bidding war for a seat on its cap table.

TPG Newquest, Advent International, CVC Capital Partners, and Permira are reportedly conducting due diligence on the maternity and childcare chain, which is currently valued at over Rs 11,000 crore. That’s much higher than the Rs 4,000 crore it was estimated to be valued at during its last IPO attempt.

A lot has changed since.

The two-decade-old chain has brought in fresh institutional capital, retired its debt of about Rs 75 crore, nearly doubled its footprint to more than 40 centres, and made big acquisitions, including the maternity, childcare, and fertility business of Apollo Health and Lifestyle.

“The goal was to bulk up, acquire revenue, and scale before going back [for an IPO],” an analyst with a top PE firm in India told The Ken.

…

But even as its network and revenue have climbed sharply, Cloudnine’s operating margins have remained largely unchanged.

Suggesting that index rules should now include qualitative metrics is a dangerous idea, whichever side it comes from. It opens a big can of worms and hands even more power to index providers, which, believe me, they don’t want, because of the responsibility that comes with it.

Separately, my complaint with this story is that it blatantly ignores the mechanics of passive funds. A whole class of investors prefers them precisely because they’re transparent and low cost. That low cost comes almost entirely from the rules-based nature of the index, plus the index provider’s fee. Change that equation into anything else and costs go up, and suddenly passive funds are way more expensive and way less transparent.
