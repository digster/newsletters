Good Morning Ishan,
If you were to visit a data centre on the outskirts of Shenzhen, Guizhou, or some other city in China, there’s a good chance you’ll be handed earplugs before you walk through multiple security checkpoints in the facility. You’d shove the conical foam bits into your ears, walk through the last door, and feel a blast of heat. Fans spin; the noise is constant. Even through blocked ears, it might remind you of the whine of drones zipping through aerial races.
All of this was built to process queries about, say, the closest [tea shop](https://the-ken.com/columns/zero-shot/what-120-million-cups-of-tea-and-a-100-year-runway-say-about-ai/), or to fire off commands to agents to place orders for groceries. That inference burns energy, and much of it runs on barrels of crude that used to arrive at China’s shores at a steep discount. But something drastic has happened to that arrangement.
Let’s be honest: the cost advantage of China’s AI industry has been built upon a steady supply of sanctioned oil. With US military incursions in Venezuela and Iran, the energy that China needs to compete as an AI superpower is becoming much more expensive, and India could catch stray shots along the way…
Read the full column: “[The US’s oil wars are a compute war against China](https://the-ken.com/columns/zero-shot/the-uss-oil-wars-are-a-compute-war-against-china/)”
How often do you use Claude? I am Vidhatri, the producer of Zero Shot, and that’s the question I want to ask everyone these days. My thesis is that it’s the most preferred AI tool for everyone—from developers to journalists—at least at The Ken!
Now, the choice of what AI tool one gets to use is not simple and straightforward, especially at work. Enterprise contracts and vendor bundling mean that you are often left with tools and processes that you do not enjoy. You might even find that your work is getting more cumbersome than you want it to be.
And if you talk to dozens of developers, like my wonderful colleague and The Ken’s AI reporter Mrunmayee Kulkarni does every single day, this frustration is real.
Some are even ready to go back to no tools. Painstakingly parsing through every line of AI-generated code is almost like paying “verification tax”, argues Mrunmayee in her richly-reported and vivid [story](https://the-ken.com/story/engineers-gag-as-amazon-tcs-and-cognizant-ram-mandatory-ai-into-everyday-work/).
She joined us on the latest episode of Zero Shot and dissected the story along with Ashwin Sekar, the chief product and technology officer at Incred, a loan financing company.
Ashwin had some great observations on Mrunmayee’s story and added his own insights. The conversation captured the tensions between managers and engineers, and what it’s like within organisations that are transforming now.
