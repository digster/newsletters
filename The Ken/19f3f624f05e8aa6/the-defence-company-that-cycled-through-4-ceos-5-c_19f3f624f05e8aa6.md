Last week, after the announcement of CEO Prakash Hari Khose’s resignation, Nibe Ltd’s stock fell by 3%. Not anything significant for any other day. But for a listed defence manufacturing company which has seen three previous CEO’s, five CFOs, and four auditors leave within the last five years, it raises a few eyebrows. And even more questions come up when the stock has surged 35X compared to only around 2.5X rise in the BSE Industrials index.
These achievements stand in stark contrast to the fact that this company was a textile manufacturer until 2020. Back then, the company’s revenue was around Rs 1.4 crore and now it has jumped to Rs 474 crore in FY26.
It is also trading at 145X its last 12 months’ profits, which is three times the industry average, and has a market cap of around Rs 2,500 crore, similar to Ideaforge Technology (established 2007) and DCX Systems (established 2011).
These kinds of numbers are hardly recorded when a company switches sectors.
Not to mention that it also made its debut in this year’s Republic Day Parade (as shown in the image above, sourced from the “President of India” Youtube channel) with [Suryastra](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2264604®=48&lang=2), which is supposed to be India’s first 300km universal rocket launching system.
Was the company in the right place at the right time to scale itself to those numbers? Maybe. But it also positioned itself as a domestic manufacturer to ride the defence wave, as investors are still [bullish](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/india-s-defence-sector-enters-structural-growth-phase-as-brokerages-see-deep-tech-exports-driving-next-leg-13963880.html) about its stocks.
One of the above statements is not true.
