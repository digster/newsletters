Agents on the loose

What does Razorpay know about agentic AI that OpenAI, Amazon, Ebay don’t?

The same week OpenAI rolled back its in-chat shopping agent, Razorpay launched what it called the biggest disruption to payments since UPI. But is it really?

Razorpay is betting big on agentic payments in India. At FTX26, it showed a Zomato demo where a user could order by voice and pay without opening a checkout page or UPI app. This could weaken the power of payments apps like Phonepe and Google Pay. In an agentic model, AI handles discovery and the initial transaction, while payments apps risk being reduced to a backend authentication layer. In-app agents, like Zomato’s, mostly optimise shopping within one platform, but neutral cross-platform agents could be more disruptive by comparing sellers across marketplaces. Agentic commerce raises unresolved questions around trust, fraud, consent, data use, and liability, making regulation crucial before these systems scale widely. [Read the full story]

Inside Bihar’s plan to disrupt VC-fuelled farm advisory

India is laying down public digital farm-advisory infrastructure that could complement or rival agritech firms. And Bihar is showing the way

Bihar’s digital farm-advisory platform, Bihar Krishi, is a part of India’s “agri stack”. The AI-powered app seeks to reshape a market caught between a public-advisory system that is broken and a private agritech ecosystem that comes with strings attached. Since farm advisory isn’t really monetisable, agritech companies link it to marketplaces for farm inputs.
