India’s MSME fund has an MSME problem. Even though it was designed to solve just that.
On the face of it, things are looking up. The Self Reliant India (SRI) Fund saw a top-up of around Rs 2,000 crore in the Union Budget 2026–27, nearly 4X the previous year’s contribution.
The Rs 50,000-crore fund—of which the government will contribute 20% with the rest coming from private investors—was conceived as India’s biggest attempt to channel venture-style capital towards MSMEs that banks struggled to finance and venture capitalists often ignored. The idea was to zero in on traditional manufacturers and service businesses starved for growth capital even as VCs continued to chase tech startups promising higher and faster returns.
Since the start of the fund in 2020, it has backed around 750 companies and catalysed more than Rs 16,000 crore of investment.
But six years in, the government’s own data suggests that the fund has fallen into the same trap it was created to sidestep. Nearly seven out of every 10 companies it has backed are tech-heavy businesses. Only three are in traditional manufacturing.
