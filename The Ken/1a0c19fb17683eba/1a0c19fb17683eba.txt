Akhil Dasawani faces a dilemma. “I promise you, if I move my programme to begin at Grade 7, there will be takers.”, he told The Ken. 

Despite similar moves by competitors, Daswani, co-founder of Oncourse Vantage, a premium study-abroad consultancy based in Mumbai, found it tough to justify capitalising on parental anxiety about college admissions at such an early stage in a child’s life. 

The competition for a place in the  top-20 universities in the United States, he argues, is fierce—so fierce that parents are willing to pay him and his competitors upwards of Rs 5 lakh a year to help their children enhance their profiles with the singular goal of admission at a top college. 

The widely reported fall in American college admits for Indian students does not apply to Oncourse’s clientele, according to the co-founder. 

“If I were applying to a tier-2 university in the US, now I would definitely be reconsidering,” said Daswani, in the same breath that he asserts that the clientele in India that wants to go to a tier-1 college has “absolutely exploded”.
