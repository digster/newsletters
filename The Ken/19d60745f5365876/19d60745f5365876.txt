It would be a strange sort of piggy bank that charged a Rs 500 penalty for every missed deposit.

Strange, because saving money is a discretionary act, not an obligation. At best, it’s a promise made to your future self, and even when it lapses, the effects don’t travel beyond that. A diligent nine-year-old may feel mildly disappointed for having fallen short, but the piggy bank, having neither exposure nor claim, has little basis for a penalty.

Yet, if your piggy bank was a commercial bank, and your savings habit a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP), skipping a month, it turns out, becomes a punishable event, which costs Rs 500 in penalties, plus 18% GST. 

The same thing across millions of such lapses compounds into  a revenue channel for banks worth thousands of crores annually.

In March, Raghav Chadha https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zmbrlT_2Zm0 took to the parliament floor and called out banks for collecting Rs 19,000 crore over three years solely as minimum balance penalties—fees charged by a bank when your account balance falls below a certain threshold. Chadha termed this “legalised pickpocketing”, which is a vivid image, though perhaps unfair to pickpockets, who generally assume a high level of personal risk.

For banks, on the other hand, the Rs 19,000 crore figure is only the more visible layer in a far larger penalty structure, levied at rates that often bear little resemblance to what it actually costs the bank in processing, risk, or anything else. 

This figure, for all its shock value, tells only part of the story. It does not include the money banks make in penalties from bounced transactions—most remarkable of which is the missed SIP.
