There is an art to drinking piping hot chai in a moving vehicle without spilling it, even on the smoothest roads. Ram, a senior official in Mysuru’s City Corporation, has it down to a T(ea). 

In the back seat of his car, he can grip a paper cup in one hand, flip through a file with his other, and balance a phone on his shoulder. “The potholes have been here long enough,” he told me. “I know when and which way the car will swerve.”

He was chatting with me on said phone on the way to his office—incidentally, the very body responsible for fixing said potholes—while I took notes in awe of this near-Cirque du Soleil feat. Ram, like ancient man before him, had adapted to his environment to survive it in the best possible way.

The broken road and Ram’s muscle memory are indicative of a larger problem plaguing several city corporations in Karnataka. Their funding under the 5th State Finance Commission (SFC) untied grants—the civic money cities get to spend at their own discretion without a formal scheme—has been whittled down to almost nothing.
