“So you’re the enemy.”

That’s what a Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) engineer heard, not from a rival but a colleague worried about losing their job. Though said in jest, there was some truth to it. The engineer, after all, works for Digitate, the TCS venture responsible for Ignio, an AI platform that’s becoming one of the Tata Group company’s main engines to run IT and business processes for its clients.

Digitate uses Ignio for things like automating systems management. The company noted during a client-review meeting in July that Ignio had cut manual handling of a Canadian financial institution’s server infrastructure by 40%. 

More AI intervention means less human work and, consequently, fewer jobs. 

“Some clients have instructed us to reduce a 10-person team to four people for certain tasks after the year-long AI implementation ends,” the engineer says. 

Given it promises such operational efficiency, businesses must be scrambling for Ignio?

I get the sentiment behind the article – but as an industry insider, I can confidently say that these numbers are far from real.

If a company is operating a 100 MW colo datacenter – they would atleast have 1500 FTEs across all disciplines – business, HR, projects, operations, legal etc. Just ask Kaw or Sen about how much live capacity do they have, and what is their headcount. For every MW that is switched on, there is effort from sales to secure the order, projects, delivery and ops team take the DC live, customer success teams onboard the customers and service the account.

Colo operators offer a wide range of complementary services such as connectivity, pvt cloud etc for which separate BUs are set up, staffing scores of FTEs.

You get the drift.

So yes – datacenter investments do create jobs. You just need to see the big picture.
