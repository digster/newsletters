Ather Energy’s initial public offering wasn’t one for the history books. The almost Rs 3,000 crore share sale, in April 2025, was subscribed a lacklustre 1.4 times.

When it went back to institutional investors last week for a top-up, it realised that the once-indifferent spectators had turned cheerleaders. The Rs 1,300 crore fundraise got Rs 10,000 crore in bids, or nearly eight times the requirement.

It didn’t matter that the price per share had almost quadrupled between the two events. And herein lie some valuable lessons for capital-hungry companies that are still getting the lay of the land in India’s equity market.

But Ather’s virtues aren’t really virtues unless placed next to the egregious missteps of two other companies that went back to investors after their IPO.

Ather went public nine months after Ola Electric Mobility, then India’s largest maker of battery-powered two-wheelers. Ola’s IPO was subscribed 4.3 times, and its shares ended the first day of trading 20% higher than the issue price. (Ather’s had to make do with a 2% rise.) But by the time Ather joined Ola on the bourses, Ola’s hold over the e-scooter market had begun to slip, and its market share had halved to under 20%. TVS Motor Company and Bajaj Auto had edged past Ola.

Within a few months, even Ather was selling more scooters than Ola. And Ola was in the eye of the storm over its reported sales figure being considerably lower than the number on a government portal. The board of the Bhavish Aggarwal-led company was forced to approve a plan to raise Rs 1,700 crore in debt within a year of its IPO.
