Good Morning Ishan,
In mid-August, electronics major Voltas announced a joint venture (JV) with the relatively pubescent Atomberg Technologies. The intention was simple: to localise Voltas’s supply chain for AC compressors, a quintessential component of an AC that makes up a fifth of its cost.
For long, the company has been synonymous with its ACs, and has been the market leader in the segment since 2012. But what should have been a buoyant moment turned rather flat.
Significantly, Voltas’s revenue has fallen in three of the last five quarters, even as its profits have swung between extremes. This as the stock continues to trade close to its 52-week low, with a market cap of around Rs 40,300 crore. Even the fact that the company’s room-AC sales jumped 45% year-on-year and its market share climbed to over 17%, from 15.9% earlier, doesn’t seem to have elated investors.
The JV itself has its detractors.
For starters, this isn’t Voltas’s first attempt at localising its supply chain. In 2022, it had announced a JV with Hong Kong-based component manufacturer Highly International to do just that—before the deal fell through due to government restrictions on Chinese investment in India. The truth is that the compressor supply chain is almost entirely reliant on two Chinese players, Highly (the parent company of the earlier-mentioned Highly International) and GMCC.
This, then, should have made the Voltas-Atomberg partnership even more prescient. After all, it was bringing a critical, high-value component onshore for the first time. Atomberg, itself, is no noob and has already been supplying “critical motors and electronics components” to AC makers such as Voltas, Godrej, and Bluestar, according to its draft papers.
