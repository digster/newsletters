Last week, a company to which few pay attention announced something with outsize consequences.
Shalimar Paints, a once-prominent firm that is now the runt of the litter in its industry, said that its parent, building-materials company Hella Infra Market, could become its subsidiary through a Rs 10,440 crore share-swap. Shalimar added that it would explore merging with Infra Market, in addition to approving a Rs 1,000 crore share sale to institutional investors.
The “reverse merger” will allow Infra Market to become a publicly traded company without an always-laborious, often-abortive initial public offering. In October last year, the company made a confidential filing for a Rs 5,000 crore IPO, which the markets regulator Sebi approved in January.
That Infra Market is weighing a backdoor listing despite the nod is telling, not least because this is far from the outcome its venture-capital backers would have wanted.
