20,000 organ transplants a year, and ‘success’ is whatever hospitals say it means

More than one in four hospitals don’t report how many organ transplants they do, and what the rest report tells you nothing about how patients fare

India is drastically improving its organ transplant capabilities. Transplants have risen over 2.5 times in the past decade. While the volumes look promising, the regulatory infrastructure tasked with supporting these transplants does not account for long-term outcomes post-surgery, as is the global standard. The infrastructure is fragmented, and enforcement mechanisms for non-compliance remain unclear. The government is launching a centralised portal that includes long-term outcome monitoring, but will digital infrastructure solve the problem when enforcement is poor? [Read full story here]

In India, falling in love with Chinese open-weight models is the easy part

But trusting them is a whole different ball game. And legacy banks are not there yet

Chinese open-weight AI model Qwen blends the security of local hosting with the knowledge sharing of cloud-based LLMs like ChatGPT. In India, new-age financial-services players love open-weight models, courtesy of their customisability and cost savings, while staying compliant with RBI mandates. But it’s a harder pill to swallow for legacy players who lack the tech chops and distrust Chinese models. Building trust has become the need of the hour, as Indian AI makers realise the urgency and try to fill the gap. [Read full story here]

The times they are a’-changin

HDFC Bank’s losing streak and SBI’s dream rally have run their course

Beset by troubles, HDFC Bank lost much of its lead over its state-owned peer by market value. But with a new CEO in a month, it could again pull ahead

Once an investor darling that could do no wrong, HDFC Bank has been in the market doghouse for quite some time. The country’s largest private sector bank has been put through the wringer, due to the overhang of its mega-merger with HDFC. Meanwhile, SBI, India’s largest public sector bank, has been on a roll, notching up big wins and gains for its investors. But the tables are set to turn. The slow one now will later be fast. As the present now will later be past. The order is rapidly fading. [Read the full story]

Sniffing dogs join the race to catch cancer early in India

D2C wellness firms are selling urban rich Rs 25,000 full-body scans that promise more than they can deliver. Dognosis is betting on, well, dog noses to attain the holy grail of cancer screening at a third of the cost

Dognosis is using trained dogs and Bayesian statistics to detect cancer early. By calling Breatheeasy, its first health-monitoring service launching next year, a “triaging” tool, the Bengaluru startup sidesteps regulatory approval. While some experts question whether using animals in this manner is scalable, Dognosis claims that just 15 dogs can screen 2,50,000 patients a year. In a country where less than 1% of the population is screened for cancer, Dognosis is banking on undercutting expensive and invasive tests to make inroads. [Read the full story]
