Bharat Electronics Limited (Bel) had a strong first quarter—a 25% year-on-year jump in revenue, a swelling order book valued at over Rs 72,000 crore, and an overall sentiment still very much in favour of the defence manufacturing bellwether, despite a post-earnings dip in the stock price. 

The only place where there could be questions about Bel is its profitability.

In the first quarter of FY27, its margins took a hit. Gross margin contracted to 45.5% versus 53.2% last fiscal, and Ebitda margin fell 3 percentage points to 25.1%. 

The company attributed this to its product mix on its post-earnings call last week. And when asked about the particulars of it, Chairman and Managing Director Manoj Jain replied, “Correlating that this particular project has less margin or this has more margin is not correct to estimate.”

Curiously, though, a few moments later, on the same call, in response to a question on managing future margin volatility, management announced a five-year plan to eliminate its non-semiconductor imports. This came just after Jain, having dodged several input-related queries, clarified that there wasn’t any “pressure because of raw material-related consumption or (the) input cost suddenly increased.” 

The implication is that greater indigenisation should help protect margins over time.

But when a nearly Rs 3-lakh-crore market-cap company says what’s a problem and what’s not, few question the logic.
