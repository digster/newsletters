Good Morning Ishan,
India’s big banks have long begrudged the big three fintechs—Paytm, Google Pay, and Phonepe—for gatekeeping over 80% of the country’s UPI payments. The irony is that four of those same banks also control 80% of India’s credit-card market.
HDFC, SBI, ICICI, and Axis banks take roughly three-quarters of every rupee spent on a credit card in India. Everyone else fights for the remainder. So for a small finance bank, competing in the credit-card market was a reach.
Then, in 2023, the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), UPI’s governing body, stitched credit into its payments network and called it Credit-Line-on-UPI (CLOU). At launch, it was widely called a “revolutionary” product, on its way to cross a trillion dollars in transaction value by 2030.
The promise was simple, yet radical. CLOU is the infrastructure that allows banks to extend a line of credit through any UPI app, and lets a customer spend from it exactly the way they’d spend from their savings account. Scan a QR, tap, pay, done. Interest-free like a credit card, and available at every merchant in the country that takes UPI.
Unlike a credit card, CLOU can enable paying for a Rs 15 cup of chai on credit at a local tea shop. Credit would stop feeling like credit and fade into everyday payments. The result is a product that can match the ubiquity of UPI without inheriting its zero-revenue problem.
Yet for two years, CLOU saw very little adoption. Until the last couple of months.
