Good Morning Ishan,
“Instead of accelerating funding in the startup ecosystem, the RDI fund has slowed it down,” said the managing partner of a VC firm that manages a corpus of over $1 billion.
Around 12 alternative investment funds (AIF)—all managing anywhere between $35 million and $1 billion—and two non-banks The Ken spoke to agreed with this.
This is with regard to the Rs 1 lakh crore Research, Development, and Innovation (RDI) fund launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in November 2025. Its purpose is to provide patient capital for an area in which Indian companies’ record is abysmal—research and innovation. The focus is particularly on sectors with long gestation periods, often ignored by private venture capital. Think startups building DNA-based data storage, drone-defence systems, and satellites.
The initial timeline was ambitious. In May, Shivkumar Kalyanaraman, the CEO of the Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF), the year-old body housing the RDI fund, had claimed that the first cheques to approved AIFs would go out “in the coming days”. The whole process was supposed to be conducted at “record speed”.
The reality has been anything but. “We submitted our application for RDI funding in January and started calling for RFPs (requests for proposals) and shortlisting startups in April,” said another Bengaluru-based fund manager. “But now, the entire funding cycle is delayed by almost a year, as we’ll have to renegotiate with private LPs after RDIF approval.” In fact, until the first week of August, over 20 additional fund managers were still “due to be appointed”.
The disbursement itself has fared no better…
