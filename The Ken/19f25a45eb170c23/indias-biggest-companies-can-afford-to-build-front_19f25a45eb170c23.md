Two By Two
Fri, 03 Jul 26
An abridged, narrative version of the latest episode of Two by Two, The Ken’s premium weekly business podcast.
Good Morning Ishan,
I guess you all saw the good news yesterday.
In case you forgot what happened, the short version goes something like this: three weeks ago, the world woke up to the news that the US government had ordered a complete and immediate rollback of Fable (also known as Mythos), the latest frontier model by Anthropic. Initially, the order was to restrict Fable only to Americans, but since Anthropic had no reliable way of knowing who was a citizen and who wasn’t (yet), they decided to play it safe and did a general rollback.
So developers all over the world, including in India, woke up to error messages, with the frontier layer many of them had built products on simply having disappeared.
In the same week that the US government stepped in to block access to a product built by a private company, two other things happened:
HCLTech put $150 million into Sarvam, the Bengaluru-based foundation-model AI company. This is the first instance that a major Indian corporate bet serious money on owning the layer that had just been taken away. Of course, HCLTech’s decision wasn’t a reaction to the Anthropic development, but the fact that it happened in the same week wasn’t lost on me.
And then, a few days later, on the day we recorded this episode, Mukesh Ambani, Chairman and Managing Director of Reliance, stood in front of Reliance shareholders at their annual general body meeting and said India cannot remain a consumer of AI built elsewhere. He argued that AI self-sufficiency has to become a national mission, on par with energy.
So in this week’s episode, I start by saying—sure, please go ahead, but I have a caveat.
“His $110 billion that he’s investing into all of this in AI is going into data centres, which is land, power, chips that are bought from Nvidia, and some of it is leased straight back to Meta. So it’s a very safe layer. It’s almost like a capex or a refinery with GPUs built into it. And the part that is actually at risk and got banned, which is the model, the intelligence, all of that, does not get much of an attention.”
Everyone agrees India needs its own intelligence. But most arguments about “self-sufficiency” are equated to companies owning more chips, land, power, and especially data centres. The part that’s actually at risk, i.e., the part that got switched off—the model, the intelligence itself—gets almost none of the money or attention.
Why is this? What’s stopping them?
To answer these questions, I bring in two guests from two completely different vantage points.
Nitin Pai is the co-founder and director of the Takshashila Institution, which he describes as having grown from “a person walks into our lobby” to India’s largest independent public policy school, graduating over 1,000 people a year across defence, tech, biotech, and civil services. Nitin argues from a place of pragmatism and nationalism—and his general position is that India must do more… of everything.
Manav Garg is the guest who’s actually built the thing everyone’s arguing about. He started Eka Software in India, scaled it, and sold it to a US private equity firm. He then spent time as a VC—co-founding Together Fund before jumping back into an operations role as executive chairman at Emergent, one of the few Indian AI companies with real global traction. I wanted Manav in this episode because he represents everything across the stack—AI, VC, operator, builder.
And most importantly, he’s lived through the supply chain risk that Anthropic caused.
