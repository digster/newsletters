The protests after a recent student death have brought an old divide into the open, raising questions about the original aim of giving engineers a “broader outlook on life”
In the “hyper-apolitical” campuses of IITs, engineers and humanities (HSS) students are strange bedfellows. The perpetually-simmering divide between the two groups is frequently brought centre-stage. Most recently, in IIT Bombay, engineering and HSS students clashed during the protests in the aftermath of Sahil Wakode’s death. While IIT administrators remain broadly supportive of HSS faculty, their work is often put into question by Btech students and on social media, playing into the tension felt on campus. It is a divide that emerges out of the differences in personality and notions of merit as a consequence of one group giving the JEE. According to some faculty, the HSS instinct to marginalise diverse viewpoints adds fuel to the fire. Inox Clean Energy filed draft papers for a Rs 10,000 crore initial public offering last week. For now, however, Sebi has put a hold on it. But if not the regulator itself, the company’s lack of experience in its line of work is an obstacle here.
