450K families, 90 questions: India’s first income survey can’t get people to reveal their income

The National Household Income Survey is running into several grey areas on the ground, including what, exactly, constitutes a household

India is conducting its first national household income survey, trying to get a more nuanced picture on its GDP story. But income remains one of the most difficult variables to measure in any population, both because of unintentional misreporting, and intentional underreporting. India, like many other countries, has used consumption expenditure for a long time, as a proxy for income—but it comes with its own challenges. So as the Income survey is underway, economists are working around the challenges of capturing income with all its difficulties. [Read full story here]

At IITs, a long-brewing ‘culture war’ between engineering and humanities boils over

The protests after a recent student death have brought an old divide into the open, raising questions about the original aim of giving engineers a “broader outlook on life”

In the “hyper-apolitical” campuses of IITs, engineers and humanities (HSS) students are strange bedfellows. The perpetually-simmering divide between the two groups is frequently brought centre-stage. Most recently, in IIT Bombay, engineering and HSS students clashed during the protests in the aftermath of Sahil Wakode’s death. While IIT administrators remain broadly supportive of HSS faculty, their work is often put into question by Btech students and on social media, playing into the tension felt on campus. It is a divide that emerges out of the differences in personality and notions of merit as a consequence of one group giving the JEE. According to some faculty, the HSS instinct to marginalise diverse viewpoints adds fuel to the fire.  [Read full story here]

Inox Clean’s ‘masterclass’ in growing revenue 43X in one year is a cautionary tale

Inox Clean Energy now has a seat at the table with established renewable energy players; but it’s come at the cost of multiple acquisitions and subsequent debts

Inox Clean Energy filed draft papers for a Rs 10,000 crore initial public offering last week. For now, however, Sebi has put a hold on it. Thanks to a nearly unprecedented acquisition spree, the company’s revenue, module-manufacturing capacity, and power-production capacity have all skyrocketed. But so has debt, which has increased by 45X. Unsurprisingly, Inox Clean Energy has earmarked 60% of the issue for debt repayment. Eventually, they need vast sums of money to hold on to the soaring financials they’ve bought their way into. But if not the regulator itself, the company’s lack of experience in its line of work is an obstacle here. [Read the full story]
