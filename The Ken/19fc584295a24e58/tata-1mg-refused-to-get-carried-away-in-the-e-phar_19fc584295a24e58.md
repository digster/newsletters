Good Morning Ishan,
Vikas Chauhan, one of the co-founders of digital healthcare company Tata 1mg, recalled handing Prashant Tandon, his co-founder, a framed note from 2012. This was in 2025, at a gathering to celebrate the 10th anniversary since 1mg had shed its earlier avatar as Healthkartplus.
“Tandon had drawn what we wanted to do in the next five to seven years,” said Chauhan. Tandon’s flowchart included plans to start a diagnostics vertical and retail clinics—everything 1mg, which was acquired by Tata Digital in 2021, has spent the last decade building.
And despite going against the grain by not prioritising quick deliveries and dialling back on discounts, it has been doing well.
1mg’s revenue stood at Rs 309 crore when Tata bought a 55% majority stake. Five years later, its revenue has grown 10X, reporting nearly Rs 3,000 crore in FY26, according to Tata Digital parent Tata Sons’ annual report, released in late July. The brand is net-profitable, including its diagnostics vertical, which alone crossed Rs 600 crore in annualised revenue.
All this while the rest of the industry has been trying to crack “the classic consumer-tech playbook in India,” as one pharma e-commerce startup professional put it. “Raise aggressively, discount more than the next guy, grab market share first, and then figure out the business.”
…
So when Tata Digital bought the company six years ago, many predicted the same fate for it.
Yet 1mg has managed to stay true to its model.
