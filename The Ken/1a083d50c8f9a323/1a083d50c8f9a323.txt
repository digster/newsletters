For the first time, more passenger vehicles were sold on clean fuels than on petrol. CNG, hybrid, and electric vehicles (EV) together accounted for 41.95% of registrations, overtaking petrol’s 40.85%. Just a year ago, petrol led by nearly 11%, according to the Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations’ August sales report https://fada.in/images/press-release/16a9e2ff1d6502FADA%20Releases%20August%202026%20Vehicle%20Retail%20Data.pdf.

In the weeks leading up to World EV Day, India saw a flurry of EV launches, including Ola Electric’s S1Z, Ather Energy’s Konarc, and Simple Energy’s Wave 3.7 in the electric two-wheeler segment alone.

However, it is Ola’s latest ad campaign, “Zyada ya kam (More or less)”, that caught my attention.  From unveiling “AI chips https://www.livemint.com/ai/artificial-intelligence/ceo-bhavish-aggarwal-announces-olas-first-ai-chip-for-india-set-for-2026-launch-everything-announced-at-sankalp-event-11723724960649.html” to “super cars https://www.deccanherald.com/business/ola-electric-to-enter-electric-car-segment-to-launch-first-model-by-2024-1136309.html”, for a company that has built its reputation on exaggeration, the irony was hard to miss. 

It was a rather combative ad, targeting Ather’s Konarc just as it was launching in Whitefield, Bengaluru.

Source: X (formerly Twitter)

Ola claimed 3X the range of the Konarc at Rs 99,999. Impressive, but Ola has made such claims before. 

Reality, though, is much different.
