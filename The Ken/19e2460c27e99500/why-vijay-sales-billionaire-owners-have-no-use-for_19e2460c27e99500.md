Good Morning Ishan,
Nilesh Gupta is used to saying no to private-equity firms. “They know our doors are closed. They have stopped coming,” said the director of Vijay Sales, a family-owned chain of electronics stores, when The Ken first met him at the retailer’s unremarkable headquarters in suburban Mumbai in March.
By the time of our second conversation, over a month later, Gupta had been proved wrong. An American PE giant had sent a feeler not to Gupta but to his older son, Karan. The investor’s pitch was uncomplicated: “In five years, you will be too big even for the biggest PE firms. So this is your chance.”
Karan knew his father’s answer, but still suggested they meet the PE firm. “I told Karan, ‘What’s the point? We will never do this. So what if my mouth waters at the valuation?’” laughed Gupta, sitting next to a bookcase lined with the usual suspects: Ray Dalio, Yuval Noah Harari, Phil Knight.
It is easy to see why Vijay Sales doesn’t want to give eager investors the time of day…
