“This is Act 2.0 [for Nykaa]… In the first phase, they tried to build a marketplace which also had fashion. In the second phase, they are trying to build an ecosystem which will facilitate growth of premium brands. And they have the consumer base that is apt for that. 

The interesting question is—premium fashion is smaller than mass fashion, yes, clearly. But whether being the best premium platform creates more value than being the third or fourth largest mass platform.”

That’s Aalok Dubey, who spent three decades running some of the most well-known premium fashion brands in India—including launching US Polo Association (arguably the biggest brand success story of the decade) and turning around Flying Machine during 18 years at Arvind. He has since launched La Martina and revived Gas at Reliance Brands, been CEO of Apparel Group India and worked with Ace Turtle, the licensee behind Lee and Wrangler in India. He is now an independent consultant in strategy, operations, and brand across lifestyle, apparel, and fashion.

In today’s episode of Two By Two, he poses (and ultimately answers) the question for Nykaa—is it better to be a mass fashion e-commerce platform in fourth place or be first place with premium fashion? 

Nykaa has already picked a side. And the first set of results from the new strategy has started to trickle in. In its latest quarterly results, fashion grew 54% and turned profitable at the same time. Its Ebitda margin rose to 0.1% from −6.2% a year earlier, making the vertical profitable for the first quarter ever. 

And well, just see what it did to Nykaa’s stock price.

The story of this turnaround (if it can indeed be called one is still up for debate) came from a decision that Nykaa took a few quarters back with fashion. My colleague Nuha Bubere, who originally reported this story, and appeared as the second guest on today’s episode, wrote about this decision last week. 

The fix, when it came, was decisive.

“The management decided that we wouldn’t fight the discount war at all. Instead, we would double down on our premium positioning by recruiting brands with pre-existing pull and diversifying into less-contested categories,” said the second Nykaa manager. Significantly, the vertical wanted to stop being seen as a “fourth platform, where brands listed their leftover products”, added the manager.

Ever since, Nykaa Fashion has been on the hunt for premium Indian and global brands. Already, it’s selling the likes of Vero Moda, Fabindia, and Nike on its platform.

Both Nuha and Aalok told me complementary stories about Nykaa and how this turn fared for the company. Specifically, with Aalok, I spent most of the conversation discussing how premium brands grow, thrive, and die in India, and what platforms like Myntra and Nykaa pitch to them, and how they make decisions on whom to choose.

He began by describing premium brands through watches, “Titan is a mass merchandise brand with premium imagery… You’re available in 8,000 outlets, but the consumer sees you as aspirational because every single act that you do at his touchpoint is telling you that I’m slightly better than the other guy.”

Nuha described this feeling from the consumer side and what they experience when they land up on websites like Nykaa. “You grow the brands so the brands have more loyalty or fealty towards you… But when you look at a user that comes online to shop, that’s not the perception that they have. They want to shop from a platform where they see the kind of brands that they want to, if not today, then wear tomorrow.”

The private labels allure

All fashion e-commerce platforms go through this phase where they switch from wooing premium brands to building brands in-house, which they use as a mechanism to sell to their existing customers. Private labels are like a drug—the margins are much fatter, and the platforms feel in control. 

And yet, after some point, they hit a wall. 

Here’s Aalok.

“There is always low-hanging fruit, and you tend to pivot towards that fast. Private label seems like a very easy game. I heard some companies saying we want to launch a value fashion brand in India. I was doing some strategy consultancy with them, and they said, why do we need a design team? Let’s go to London, shop from Primark, bring the samples, replicate.”

Even for Nykaa, which built a “House of Nykaa” with private labels it either built, invested in, or outright acquired, the story is the same. After a while, these brands tapped out. 

The future of Nykaa

A lot of this comes down to strategy, but Nykaa has also been fortunate. Specifically, they took advantage of a narrow window of opportunity to turn their business around. “Many of the brand partners who came to India post-Covid and were into three- to five-year agreements are renewing… this is a very, very good opportunity for Nykaa to get a hold of building exclusive partner brands, let them prevail, and grow in their ecosystem”, said Aalok. 

This is one of the reasons why Nykaa’s Act 2.0 is gathering momentum. 

Nykaa must figure out a way to keep the brand pipeline going. And this is not that straightforward, especially because brands can choose Myntra for scale and sales, and Reliance for the prestige of being in the most sought-after offline storefronts. Nykaa must offer something fundamentally different to either. 

One of the companies that saw something different in Nykaa was Nike. Earlier this year, Nykaa took over all operations and fulfilment of Nike’s India website, which is also a big contributor to Nykaa’s financial turnaround. 

Nykaa needs more Nikes. 

But how? And what are all the ways things may work? Or not work?
