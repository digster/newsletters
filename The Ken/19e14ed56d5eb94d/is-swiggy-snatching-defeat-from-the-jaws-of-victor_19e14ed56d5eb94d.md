Good Morning Ishan,
Swiggy’s earnings call last week struck a familiar refrain.
“We are not going to buy growth,” said Sriharsha Majety, Swiggy’s founder and CEO. “Instead, our focus for the next few quarters is going to be to increase [Instamart’s] contribution margin to zero first, while maintaining the growth proposition.”
The contribution margin is basically what’s left from an order after paying the direct costs of fulfilling it—which, for Instamart, has been negative for far too long. Put simply, Majety is saying that the company has shifted gears from a growth-at-all-costs mindset to bolstering its margins.
The food-delivery and out-of-home businesses are already profitable, and Swiggy is now turning its attention to its quick-commerce arm.
It seems like sound logic…until you look at the numbers.
In FY26, Swiggy burnt Rs 4,200 crore on advertising and promotions compared to rival Eternal’s Rs 3,350 crore. This is after it cut back on discounts offered on Instamart earlier this year. Losses widened by a third to over Rs 4,000 crore this fiscal, even though Swiggy had assured investors in its last fundraise that losses would shrink.
Meanwhile, Instamart’s average order value dropped to Rs 700 from Rs 746 in the previous quarter. Sure, its monthly transacting users (MTU) were up by half a million to touch 13.3 million. But this pales in comparison to Blinkit’s addition of 3.6 million MTUs, taking its total to over 27 million. Tellingly, it took Swiggy over 15 months to add the 3.6 million MTUs that Blinkit added in a quarter.
This is the contradiction Swiggy is grappling with…
