With $50 million from KKR, the entertainment company has to worry more about fixing the sorry state of concert venues, licences, and equipment than about fending off its arch-rival
India’s live-entertainment boom has arrived before the infrastructure required to support it. Now, Bookmyshow needs to build from the ground up to make this growth sustainable. Live-entertainment events in India face volatilities due to the lack of proper venues and equipment and the tedious compliance requirements; this is compounded by the tricky economics of events. With recent funding from the private-equity giant KKR, Bookmyshow is now pushing for the next phase of growth. It is trying to ease pressure points across fronts, hoping to win the battle of live entertainment for the most populous country in the world. [What we learnt building a voice agent on two frontier Indian AI models](https://the-ken.com/story/what-we-learnt-building-a-voice-agent-on-two-frontier-indian-ai-models/?utm_source=weekly_email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
Ironically, the one that is free and open is the last you would reach for, while the other carries the weight of the entire AI Mission
The Ken built a voice-AI transcription agent on BharatGen, Sarvam, and Eleven Labs. The latter two outperformed the first. BharatGen has the largest allocation under India’s AI Mission, but a fraction of the compute power and a Section 8 structure that keeps it from raising money. Sarvam, a private company with venture funding and a government equity stake, has become the AI Mission’s de facto flagship model. If BharatGen fades, India will be left with just one frontier-AI company, not the ecosystem it wants. [IIT, IIM reunions and the warm, fuzzy feeling of … making a donation](https://the-ken.com/story/iit-iim-reunions-and-the-warm-fuzzy-feeling-of-making-a-donation/?utm_source=weekly_email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
From Rs 8-lakh hostel rooms to Rs 100-crore schools, India’s elite institutes are turning to alumni at silver-jubilee reunions as public funding gets harder to rely on
IITs and IIMs are turning 25th-year reunions into fundraising opportunities as alumni donations become more important. Batch coordinators bring alumni of their respective batches together to decide what they can raise and what the money should fund, with some batches raising as much as Rs 100 crore. But the push for donations can alienate alumni as well, some of whom feel pressured or uncomfortable with the commercialisation of reunions. Despite the friction, institutions are putting more resources into cultivating donors, with operational expenses rising at the dedicated fundraising foundations attached to these institutes. From finding scripts and storyboarding to generating entire shots and replacing chunks of VFX, AI is creeping into every stage of filmmaking
Filmmaking has always been a risky undertaking, but Bollywood has been in an especially sticky spot since Covid, with flatlining footfall in theatres. AI is almost like a panacea here, making it quicker and cheaper than ever before to make films, with ever-slimming crews. It’s being deployed across the value chain, from ‘faint idea’ to ‘final release’. With AI-native filmmaking, some are using it to generate shots altogether. The average filmmaker has little choice but to get on the bandwagon.
