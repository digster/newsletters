Good Morning Ishan,
It’s a rare retreat by the house of Reliance.
In April, Jio Blackrock Asset Management signalled it would engage with distributors too to sell its mutual-fund schemes to investors. This came less than a year after the launch of the fund house. At that time, the company was categorical about how it would sell its schemes—straight to investors through “direct plans”. No “regular plans” through distributors earning a commission. No commission meant lower costs and better returns to investors.
The gap can be big over the long term.
For instance, just 0.5 percentage points higher annual returns—say 12.5% in direct plans compared with 12% in regular ones—can mean about Rs 1.5 lakh more for an investor putting Rs 10,000 monthly over 10 years. Starting 2013, market regulator Sebi mandated mutual funds to give investors the direct-plan option. Almost all of India’s 50 fund houses today offer both direct and regular plans. Except for two who kept away from the latter.
One of these is now joining the crowd. From direct-only to direct and regular plans, it’s a big shift in Jio Blackrock’s strategy, forced by a reality check of outcomes falling short of expectations. The Ken had earlier flagged how the joint venture’s plan to disrupt India’s mutual-fund industry with direct, online-only, low-cost plans seemed impractical.
This USP was not new in India’s mutual-fund space, The Ken had pointed out, with Zerodha* Fund House, launched in 2023, having a similar premise. As of March, of the Rs 79 lakh crore average assets under management of the entire industry, Rs 39 lakh crore is through direct plans. The average AUM of Jio Blackrock and Zerodha together through direct-only plans is just a little over Rs 31,000 crore.
A closer look at often-overlooked data reveals why the former is giving in and yielding to distributors while the latter is holding out.
