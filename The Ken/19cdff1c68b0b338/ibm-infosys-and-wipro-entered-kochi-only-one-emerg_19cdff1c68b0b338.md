Good Morning Ishan,
Did you know that Kochi’s Infopark has grown to become one of the country’s largest tier-2 technology hubs?
“In the last five years alone, we’ve added around 25,000–30,000 jobs as companies expanded distributed delivery models after the pandemic,” said an Infopark official. The Ken [reported](https://the-ken.com/story/tech-city/) last year on how state governments are increasingly creating tech hubs in tier-2 and tier-3 cities.
But while some companies are getting this tier-2 expansion right, others simply aren’t. On one side sits IBM, which is shifting its Kochi raison d’etre from older infrastructure upgrades to software and AI-related products. It has hired nearly 4,000 engineers in the last three years, all while ensuring that its revenue per engineer remains high.
On the other side are IT-services firms such as Infosys and Wipro, which are using tier-2 cities’ lower costs to push volume-driven expansion. Projects flow through sprawling delivery pyramids, where junior engineers handle costing, testing, and maintenance, while senior developers manage coordinated delivery.
But as AI fundamentally rewires the industry to squeeze more efficiency from fewer people, the purpose of tier-2 hubs like Kochi may be poised to shift. Which side will be vindicated?
