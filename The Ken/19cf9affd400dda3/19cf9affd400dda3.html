Can India’s employability crisis be solved with 15 hours of yoga each semester? Most probably not, but that’s what the government’s latest efforts in education have led to.

It’s widely known that the country’s graduates are not particularly employable. In fact, the 2025 economic survey pointed out that only just over eight out of 100 undergraduates are working in jobs that match their qualifications. The government’s solution? Merge traditional degrees with vocational training as part of the New Education Policy.

Five years later, the results are not looking good.

Courses ranging from Python to yoga have been given the same credits, leading students—whether they study science or humanities—to take the easiest ones to fulfil mandatory requirements. No wonder in 2024, 16,000 students took the course on personality development, while only 4,000 took the one on Python.

Worse, roping in the private sector in the country’s educational goals is not going exactly as planned. Universities were supposed to turn to ed-techs for select, industry-relevant electives. Instead, they’re busy passing off their core courses to them as well.
