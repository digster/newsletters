Gradually, then suddenly, the chorus calling for a cut in long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax on equity has become a crescendo. And the government seems to be swaying to the sound.

On 13 October, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman’s statement https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/ltcg-tax-govt-may-restructure-rates-soon-how-much-could-investors-save-at-10-5-or-2-11793073136641.html that she would listen to all views on LTCG tax and securities transaction tax (STT) set off a hope rally in the beleaguered Indian stock market. Nothing was promised, yet the market cheered.

Desperate times, it appears, call for desperate measures. Reports now suggest that the government may take a call on restructuring LTCG tax on equities in the coming weeks. 

Two measures are being considered, according to The Indian Express, to boost market sentiment.

– Lowering the tax on equity LTCG from 12.5% for both domestic and foreign investors

– Allowing foreign investors to pay this tax on capital gains made in dollar terms, as opposed to in rupee terms

Officials say the urgency to act fast is due to two key concerns. One, zero returns or losses in stock markets over the last two years come on top of poor wage growth, and this is a big sentiment dampener. It is felt across a larger spectrum of middle class with surging retail participation in markets now.

Reduced tax on stock gains? Centre may cut capital gains levy from current 12.5%, The Indian Express https://indianexpress.com/article/business/nirmala-sitharaman-budget-2027-capital-gains-tax-india-cut-foreign-portfolio-investors-10936600/

The trouble is that both the concern and the solution are misplaced. The government should not buckle under pressure and cut LTCG tax on equity. Here’s why.
