[Who is Jio Blackrock’s Rs 350 investment advice for? Clue: not investors](https://the-ken.com/story/who-is-jio-blackrocks-rs-350-investment-advice-for-clue-not-investors/?utm_source=weekly_email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
India’s latest investment advisory promises accessible wealth management for small investors. What it doesn’t promise is a wide range of options to choose from
In February, Jio Blackrock launched a Sebi-registered, app-based investment-advice platform, offering services for as cheap as Rs 350 a year. By pairing a low price with Jio’s massive distribution and the Ambani-brand trust, it could grow India’s overall wealth-management pie, pulling first-time, low- and middle-income investors into formal, regulated advice. But the product universe is highly restricted. For a portfolio below Rs 1 lakh, recommendations draw only from Jio Blackrock’s own mutual-fund schemes, creating the risk of biased advice. Meanwhile, some early users interpret the platform’s use of Aladdin-powered portfolios as a kind of “magic AI” solution. In reality, it doesn’t guarantee superior returns. [Mahindra’s Rajesh Jejurikar on how its cars became polarising—and popular](https://the-ken.com/story/mahindras-rajesh-jejurikar-on-how-its-cars-became-both-polarising-and-popular/?utm_source=weekly_email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
And why the #2 automaker focuses on segments in which it has a “fundamental right to win”
Rajesh Jejurikar has every reason to be chuffed, even if he doesn’t quite let on during an hour-long chat with The Ken. Mahindra & Mahindra, where he is chief executive of auto and farm businesses, became India’s second-largest carmaker in 2025, dislodging Hyundai Motor India. While its long-time workhorses, Scorpio and Bolero, continued to deliver the goods, the Thar and the 3XO punched above their weight, too. As did the electric vehicles BE 6e and XEV 9e, which sold more than 40,000 units. Jejurikar, who has spent two decades with the company, spoke about going against the odds while launching the EVs, why he was fine with their designs not appealing to everyone, and what it took for Mahindra cars to break out of the demographic it was associated with for years. [Bengaluru’s fix for the water-tanker “mafia”: yet more tankers. What could go wrong?](https://the-ken.com/story/bengalurus-fix-for-the-water-tanker-mafia-yet-more-tankers-what-could-go-wrong/?utm_source=weekly_email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
Even as summer rolls around and demand for water begins to peak, Bengaluru isn’t embracing the public utility’s cheaper alternative to the private “tanker mafia”
Bengaluru grew faster than its water utility could keep up, leaving newer parts of the city reliant on private tanker suppliers, the government labelled a “mafia”. The government then launched the Sanchari Cauvery scheme as a cheaper alternative to the private tanker operators. Years of reliable service have made residents loyal to their private suppliers, muting the reception to the new scheme. Compounding the city’s water crisis, BWSSB’s piped water is priced in a way that pushes apartment complexes away from taking connections, relying instead on tankers.
