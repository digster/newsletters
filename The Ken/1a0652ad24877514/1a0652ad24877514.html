After JSW entered into a joint venture with MG Motor India, the management style has increasingly diverged from what it used to be at the automaker, according to executives. 

This is because Sajjan Jindal, even as a 35% shareholder in MG India, has sought to put his stamp on the company, rather than remain just a financial investor. Earlier this week, Jindal’s son Parth Jindal was named chairman of JSW MG Motor India. 

The investment itself, which JSW bought for over Rs 3,500 crore in 2023, has not turned in any financial returns yet. But it has done one thing.

“Investments in MG Motor India changed things for JSW,” said the JSW executive who has been involved with the group’s auto plans. 

A few years ago, when Jindal’s team approached the Skoda Volkswagen group, the executive said the response was blunt: JSW did not have any experience in automobiles. Today, JSW is negotiating a joint venture with the global automaker. 

But MG was never meant to be the destination. 

Since entering the joint venture, JSW has set up a string of companies to make cars, electric buses and trucks, and auto components. Jindal has so far announced roughly Rs 50,000 crore in commitments to build his auto empire.
