Good Morning Ishan,
What does a Rs 8 lakh donation get you at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Bombay as an alumnus? A hostel room named after you, for as long as the building stands.
Your name is displayed on a coloured marker outside the room you are sponsoring. “Rm. 701, Jayadev Kulkarni”, reads one such marker. And for those who can’t afford the price upfront, instalment-based payment options are available as well.
At IIT Delhi, the same privilege costs slightly more: Rs 10 lakh. For those with deeper pockets, Rs 1.5 crore can buy naming rights to a less glamorous corner of campus—a designated cycle-parking area.
The incentivisation of donations is only part of a broader shift playing out across India’s top public institutes. As IITs and the Indian Institutes of Management (IIMs) are forced to reduce their reliance on central grants, alumni donations and endowments are becoming increasingly important funding sources.
The shift has been underway for more than a decade. But the latest iteration of this playbook is the silver-jubilee reunion, or the coming together of a batch 25 years after graduation. Unlike individual donations, these reunions offer institutes a chance to turn an entire graduating class into a fundraising pool…
