Mumbai is always in a state of flux.
Drive along the metro lines—red, blue, aqua (which is somehow different from blue), yellow—and the city looks like it’s permanently under construction. Pillars going up, flyovers widening, glass towers appearing where old buildings stood just last year, all surrounded by shinier vada pav and Bombay sandwich carts. And everywhere I looked, I saw trucks plying wire rods, rebar, sheets, and casings of steel—the very lifeblood of India’s infrastructure.
Inside a Mumbai-sized apartment just metres from one such metro pillar, two Tata Steel product marketers were on a call with their manager, trying to figure out how to sell that same steel a little better. More downstream products, better mix, higher-value steel, improved pricing. On the Powerpoint, it all looked sensible.
“That’s all very well,” the boss cut in, “but we need better NR.”
I heard that line repeated multiple times over the course of an hour. The glare coming through the screen could’ve melted a billet.
Net realisation. The number that decides whether all that steel you forge actually turns a profit. And right now, it feels like every major steel company in the country is obsessed with it.
For good reason. Tata Steel has already indicated that after 2030, it may source only about half its iron ore from captive mines, compared to nearly 100% today. It has also committed to recycle 10% of its output and reusing it in a scrap plant being set up in Ludhiana.
All of this is unfolding just as India enters one of the biggest construction cycles in its history, with government projections putting steel demand at over 220 million tonnes in the coming years. This year’s budget alone set aside over [Rs 12 lakh crore](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/03/06/building-infrastructure-capacity-budget-allocation-across-sectors/) in capital spending for roads, rail, metros, ports, and power projects.
Amid all this, something inevitable is heading for the industry. Over the next few years, a large number of iron ore mining leases in India are due for renewal. Auctions are getting more [competitive](https://infra.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/construction/urgent-call-for-more-iron-ore-auction-blocks-by-super-smelters-kakani/129338478), ore quantity is [declining](https://www.bigmint.co/insights/detail/india-iron-ore-concentrate-prices-remain-firm-w-o-w-market-awaits-omc-auction-results-624388), there are more bidders than before, and costs per tonne are slowly rising. That matters, because the pecking order in steel has always been decided at the mine.
