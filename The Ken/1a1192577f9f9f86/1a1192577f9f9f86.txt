The repo rate cycle finally turned north. Yesterday, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) in its monetary policy meeting raised the rate by 25 basis points (0.25 percentage points). The repo, which is at 5.5% after the rise, is the rate at which the RBI, as the country’s central bank, lends to other banks in India. A change in this rate—the first hike since February 2023 after a long run of cuts and pauses—flows over to others in the economy through increases in interest rates, both on loans and fixed deposits.

More hikes seem to be on the cards in the coming months, with the RBI governor Sanjay Malhotra speaking of “calibrated tightening”. After all, inflation has been heading up even as domestic economic growth seems comfortable, at least going by government data. Inflation control—through repo rate hikes—seems top of the RBI’s priority list now.
