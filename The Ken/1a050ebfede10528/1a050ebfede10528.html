Lost in transactions

Flagged: 3M ‘mule’ accounts. Declined: Rs 25K cr in fraud transactions. Then: who knows?

Across 14 cases, the trail after fraud detection leads to frozen accounts, disputed ownership, court battles, and money that does not always make it home

A flagged account can be frozen even when only a small part of its balance is linked to a fraud trail, leaving legit customers to challenge the restraint. Banks can initiate their own fraud reviews, while police and I4C can trigger separate actions, creating different routes for the same suspicious account. Establishing who actually controlled a mule account can be harder than identifying the account itself, particularly when account holders, recruiters, and fraud operators are different people. Even when money is lien-marked, or a court orders its restoration, competing claims and gaps between banks, police and victims can delay the final recovery. [Read full story here]

Analytics is becoming the next frontier for Indian spacetech. With $96M behind it, Pixxel may be furthest ahead

Pixxel has spent five years building satellites that can see what ordinary systems miss; now it must make that data useful. 72-year-old Voltas turns to 14-year-old Atomberg for help

A joint venture for compressors attempts to stabilise the AC supply chain. It isn’t the answer to the Tata company’s woes, though

Voltas has tied up with the 14-year-old Atomberg Technologies to localise its compressor supply chain. Atomberg’s compressor prototype is only five months old and is largely unproven in the mass market as of now. This comes as Voltas’ profitability remains subdued, owing to industry-wise commodity pressures and weakness in its own businesses. The in-house supply chain could support profitability over the years, but for now, the JV is another piece of a larger effort to fix a business that has struggled to turn its scale into sustained profitability.
