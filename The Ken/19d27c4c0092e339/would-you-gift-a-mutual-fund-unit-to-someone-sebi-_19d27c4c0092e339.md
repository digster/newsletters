Back in 2017, Prashant Sharma, head of growth at Springboard, was getting married to Niti Shree. Instead of the usual gifts, he requested guests to gift in Bitcoin. And it worked. Around 200 guests ended up gifting him Bitcoin worth about Rs 1,00,000 in total.
At the time, it became [news](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-india-42390013). But honestly, not everyone is comfortable with crypto. It falls in the grey area.
Now, there seems to be a new option in the making that would be much more accessible and acceptable.
This Tuesday, India’s market regulator, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi), released a consultation paper that could let us gift mutual fund (MF) units. The idea is simple. You buy a gift card or prepaid payment instrument (PPI) worth a certain amount and give it to a friend or family member. They can then use that card to purchase mutual fund units. It’s just like giving a gift voucher, except instead of a shopping spree, you’re giving someone a hand in investing.
Sebi does not want this to go without restrictions. For instance, you can’t use gift cards worth more than Rs 50,000 in a financial year. And each card itself is capped at Rs 10,000.
“The registrar and transfer agents (RTAs), on behalf of AMCs [asset management company], will track how much each investor has invested per AMC per financial year through Gift PPI, e-wallets and cash. If the transaction resulting from a Gift PPI redemption crosses Rs 50,000, the RTA will reject the transaction, and the PPI face value will be refunded to the issuer’s escrow account.”
Consultation Paper on introduction of Gift Card/Gift PPI (Prepaid Payment Instrument) for Mutual Funds, [Sebi ](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/reports/mar-2026/consultation-paper-on-introduction-of-gift-card-gift-ppi-prepaid-payment-instrument-for-mutual-funds-_100505.html)
The flow of money from purchaser to recipient will follow the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI) rules, while Sebi will handle the mutual fund subscription part. The consultation is open for comments until 16 April 2026, so this isn’t set in stone yet.
| Source: Sebi’s consultation paper |
So why this now? “It’s about breaking barriers,” founder of a mutual fund distribution platform explained the thinking in simple words.
“Indians have a deep-rooted culture of gifting money during weddings, engagements, and other occasions. If we already gift cash, why not gift mutual funds instead? The goal isn’t to create a massive spike in inflows overnight. It’s to slowly popularise mutual funds and investment habits among the retail investors.”
The development comes at a time when, for the first time since Covid, inflows into equity mutual funds have declined. In the first 11 months of last year, they stood at Rs 3.22 lakh crore, [down](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/news/equity-mf-inflows-in-2025-set-to-see-first-dip-since-covid/4095705/?utm_term=Autofeed&utm_medium=Echobox&utm_source=Facebook&fbclid=IwZXh0bgNhZW0CMTAAYnJpZBExWWEzNDI1Vk1xSGh2OWdPa3NydGMGYXBwX2lkEDIyMjAzOTE3ODgyMDA4OTIAAR7X-1GOOP-ikMHJ1iJezNQw6xCCBK_bJE1vaYU2Ht536YvBBGTucpFNwqh9mA_aem_YlKR9W_IMAMGyT_cvnm7dg#Echobox=1767371730) from Rs 3.94 lakh crore in the same period of 2024, according to AMFI.
The new tax regime has also changed the game for equity-linked savings schemes (ELSS) funds. With more people, 5.27 crore out of 7.2 crore income tax returns (ITRs) [filed](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2040669®=3&lang=2) for FY25, opting for the new regime, the tax incentive for ELSS has also taken a hit.
The gift card idea could ease access for a whole new set of investors. Experts believe this will help in the long run by bringing in people who might never have thought of buying mutual funds on their own.
Sebi has also clarified that even if the PPI purchaser selects a scheme for the PPI, the same shall not be binding on the redeemer and the latter could choose on their own.
While the overall regulation is going to be the same that Sebi allows for [e-wallet](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/master-circulars/jun-2024/master-circular-for-mutual-funds_84441.html) use case for MFs, each gift card will be valid for a year from the date of purchase. If the person you gifted it to doesn’t use it within that time, the money goes back to the gifter’s account.
And for redemption, there’s a verification process. The cardholder’s details have to match the mutual fund folio details, or the transaction gets rejected, and the money is refunded to the purchaser.
There are a few areas which the Sebi must address before it becomes law, says the executive we quoted earlier.
For instance, grievance redressal has not been clearly defined from the recipient’s perspective—the person who is going to subscribe to the MFs. “The AMC should be the single point of contact for the recipient. At no point, the PPI issuer or even purchaser be part of the investor’s interface.” This saves time for all parties involved.
Further, the cost disclosure, as well as the requirement that “full value of PPI shall be utilised”, must be re-examined. The consultation paper does not provide the full picture. If statutory levies GST or stamp duties apply to the mutual fund subscription, the PPI value won’t be sufficient to cover both the investment and the levies.
“Similarly, even if it says that the AMCs will bear the cost, AMCs will adjust the amount by charging the same to unitholders,” the founder we spoke to earlier added. While industry stakeholders have welcomed the move, adopting new investment habits often takes its own sweet time.
Would you gift someone a PPI for an MF inside the envelope, or simply put the money as usual?
