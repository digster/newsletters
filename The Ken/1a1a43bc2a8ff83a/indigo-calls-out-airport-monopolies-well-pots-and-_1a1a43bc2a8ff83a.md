In 2018, Willie Walsh took no prisoners in a Financial Times [column](https://www.ft.com/content/06518064-f7a1-11e7-a4c9-bbdefa4f210b) slamming the operator of London’s Heathrow airport. As chief executive of the company that owns British Airways, he was taking aim at the airport’s expansion plans.
If Britain is to become a global trading powerhouse after Brexit, Heathrow will have to go from being one of the most expensive airports in the world to the most competitive. But this will not happen unless changes are made to the mandate of the Civil Aviation Authority, which approves and oversees all capital spending, and effectively guarantees the airport’s profit margin.
Walsh also made good use of startling statistics to back up his argument.
Recently, British Airways asked Heathrow to provide three self-boarding gates at each of the 12 conventional boarding gates in Terminal 5. We know from suppliers that each self-boarding gate costs approximately £20,000, bringing the total spend to about £720,000. Heathrow’s estimate to complete this work was £7.8m.
The reality is that Heathrow can charge monopoly prices because the CAA lets it get away with it. There is no benchmarking, no transparency and very little recourse. When airlines question the overruns or challenge particular cost items, we are told they are “best estimates”.
Walsh wasn’t wrong. Heathrow’s [expansion](https://www.economist.com/britain/2026/02/24/heathrows-expansion-is-on-track-to-be-eye-wateringly-expensive) has turned out to be one of the priciest airport projects ever, and it already charges passengers more than any other airport in the world, save one.
On Monday, Walsh turned his ire to Indian airports, this time as chief executive of Indigo, the country’s largest airline.
...
Two industrial houses, Adani and GMR, hold sway over India’s airports, managing 14 in all, including those in Mumbai, Delhi, Ahmedabad, and Hyderabad.
It’s amusing that Walsh is calling out “effective monopolies” in airports when his own airline has been battling that tag for a while now.
