Good Morning Dear Reader,
A 24-year-old sat across from a stranger at a long table in Bengaluru one September evening. He had six minutes to get acquainted before the chairs shuffled and a new stranger slid into his place.
“I was so nervous,” he recalled. “I’m pretty sure I was blushing.”
About 80 candidates cycled the table over four rounds. They were each in search of someone they could, if all went well, spend the next many years building a shared dream. They were all “speed-dating” to find themselves a co-founder.
Or “carousel”, as Entrepreneurs First (EF), a London-based firm that describes itself as a “talent investor”, calls the ritual. EF claims to admit “cracked people”, from PhDs to high-school students, to its three-to-six-month India programme to build a VC-scale business that is eventually incorporated in the US. Long before they even have a business idea or a co-founder.
Participants are encouraged to break up as many times as it takes to find their match. EF then helps each pair zero in on an idea and sprint to get it investor-ready in three months. At the end of it, the surviving few, winnowed from some 40 teams, are flown to San Francisco to pitch their ideas to Silicon Valley investors.
“It is not,” one of the firm’s talent investors clarified, half-smiling, “a Love Island for aspiring founders.”
