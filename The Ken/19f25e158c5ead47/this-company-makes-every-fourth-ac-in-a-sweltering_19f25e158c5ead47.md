Good Morning Ishan,
In June, Amber Enterprises said it would start assembling smartphones for Oppo India, Oneplus, and Realme. It’s an unusual bet for a company that’s spent over three decades building a business with very different economics.
Amber, headquartered in Gurugram, manufactures more than a quarter of the air conditioners sold in India and supplies to eight of the country’s 10 largest AC brands, including Voltas, Blue Star, LG, and Daikin. The AC segment also made up nearly 70% of Amber’s FY26 revenue of over Rs 12,000 crore ($1.2 billion).
Now, the company plans to sub-lease a part of Oppo India’s factory in Noida, set up surface mount technology (SMT) lines, and start assembling phones there. “To start with, we are not putting in any capex,” Jasbir Singh, Amber’s chief executive, told investors after the announcement. “It will be minimum…below Rs 50 crore.”
“Once we touch 14–15 million [in volumes], we’ll add the component side,” Singh added.
On paper, the move to a new business segment looks like a clean pivot. And Amber, a $2.7 billion market-cap company, has already built a foundation there.
…
But smartphone assembly is one of the toughest businesses in electronics manufacturing.
