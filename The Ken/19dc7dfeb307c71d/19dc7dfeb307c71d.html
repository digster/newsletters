What’s left of Jio Financial when you strip away Reliance?

Over three fourth of the company’s value comes not from its core business but from the 6.1% stake it holds in Reliance Industries  

Jio Financial Services’s revenue in Q4 FY26 doubled to over Rs 1,000 crore even as its profit fell 14%. A chunk of the company’s value comes from its 6.1% stake in Reliance Industries, which has catapulted it into the Nifty 50 index. Its bets span lending, payments, insurance, and asset management, but everywhere, it’s up against older, more established rivals. It’s on the hunt for adjacencies where competition and entry barriers are lower, but these haven’t completely taken off, either. [Read the full story]

Delhi vs new Noida airport: one gets flights, so the other fights for your time

GMR-operated Delhi airport oversaw all flights in the region for decades. Now with Noida in the mix, it’s trying to stay relevant by monetising passenger behaviour

Delhi-NCR has two airports now. So the passenger traffic is bound to split. That’s leaving both Noida airport, and the GMR-operated Delhi airport eyeing your time and wallet. From entry to boarding, airports are monetising every step of the way, including opening more retail and duty-free outlets. For flyers, it reshapes how you move through the airport and what they end up paying for. [Read full story here]

How many ‘bad’ schools make a good PE investment?

Fresh off a fundraise from Chryscapital and Warburg Pincus, the company behind Orchids schools needs to find more struggling schools, and fast

K12 Techno Services, which owns Orchids International Schools, just closed out another funding round. The company, which sees a lot of interest from private equity, saw yet another change of hands in its stakes. What seems to be attracting the funds is K12’s unique proposition of finding “bad schools” and bringing them under the Orchids banner. K12 is placing a long bet on these schools, signing 50-year contracts. But, PE firms, that typically look for shorter turnarounds, seem to be more than happy to stick around. [Read the full story]

The AI founder with an Nvidia-shaped dream—and just $4 million in the bank

Kamalakar Devaki’s Sandlogic automated workflows for Bajaj Finance, Indira IVF. Now he wants to take the next step and sell a full stack by building chips. Easier said than done

With $4 million in funding and over half a million in government grant, Bengaluru-based Sandlogic wants to build a full-stack edge AI play, starting with its own chips. The shift to chip-making is already proving costly. Once profitable, Sandlogic has been recording losses for the past two years. Now that it’s built enterprise-AI tools, Sandlogic is aiming to match Nvidia’s A100 chip’s performance with its own chip. But building AI software is one thing, and becoming the infrastructure it runs on is another. Becoming “the Nvidia of India” is easier said than done. [Read the full story]
