Delhi’s mass surveillance: track 50 million faces, identify them in five seconds

The Safe City Project is a privacy nightmare, but over 180 police stations are nearly ready to match your face to your voter’s ID, driver’s licence, passport, and more

The Delhi police’s Rs 857-crore Safe City Project was started in the name of women’s safety, but became a mass surveillance system. The system is supposed to track criminals. But it can as easily identify ordinary citizens and vehicles. The police have built a data lake of the personal details and pictures of nearly four million individuals by integrating over two dozen databases held by various government agencies. The surveillance system operates in a legal vacuum and, experts say, runs counter to the Supreme Court’s ruling recognising privacy as a fundamental right. [Read full story here]

A peptide party that never happened has raised questions Indian sellers are yet to answer

Wellness clinics, fitness trainers, and D2C websites push peptides for mood, skin, and libido. No one is checking what is inside the vial, though

Peptides have become popular given the GLP-1 movement in the last year, but not all are regulated. A whole range of wellness peptides have made it consumers through B2B marketplaces like Indiamart, D2C websites, and now parties in cities like Bengaluru.
