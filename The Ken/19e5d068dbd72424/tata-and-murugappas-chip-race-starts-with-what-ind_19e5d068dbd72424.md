Good Morning Ishan,
Axiro Semiconductor has had a good year.
The spearhead of Chennai-based Murugappa Group’s entry into chipmaking contributed about Rs 500 crore to the top line of its parent CG Power in FY26. This comprised 4% of CG Power’s revenue and CEO Naveen Yandaru expects Axiro’s revenue to grow by 25% in FY27.
Not a bad start for a year-old company that could emerge, in short order, as a key component of India’s ambitious plan to host a globally competitive semiconductor industry.
Axiro is the fundamental layer in the fabless manufacturing stack that CG Power has been building since 2024, rolling out chips for a Rs 7,600-crore sister company to assemble and test in Sanand, Gujarat, where pilot production is already underway.
It isn’t the only game in town, though.
A few hundred miles away from CG Power’s Sanand facility, Tata Electronics is setting up a Rs 91,000-crore chip fabrication factory in Dholera. It’s the most capital-intensive semiconductor bet in India yet.
Murugappa and Tata are trying to solve the same problem by going in different directions.
