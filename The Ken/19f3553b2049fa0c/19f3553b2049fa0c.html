You can buy a bank, of sorts, off a website.

It’s as easy as shopping for furniture, basically, and may very well be the ultimate investment for the ambitious. All you need is some money, the right playbook, and…a questionable conscience. 

“NBFC for sale in Gujarat,” the website says, “starting at Rs 1.90 crore”. The specs are very important: “incorporated in 1990” and “fully compliant and transferable (100%)”, so no worries about the regulator asking questions. The older the company, the easier it is to make the investment work. A 1990 model is priced higher than a 2000 one. 

Regardless, the returns can be astronomical. Dev-Aashish Capital, a 1995 RBI-registered non-banking financial company, went from a modest revenue of about Rs 1.7 lakh in FY24 to Rs 81 crore in FY25, as per its MCA filings. A near 4,800% jump in one year. Or Solomon capital, another NBFC, which made Rs 22 lakh in 2024, grew its revenue 500X to Rs 105 crore a year later. 

And the business model is simple. Lend money through unsecured personal loans, at rates higher than you pay to borrow that money. Just how high? Well, since the RBI does not enforce a cap on interest rates, charge an exorbitant amount. Say, loans at annualised interest rates of 600% or more. 

Rest assured…
