Good Morning Ishan,
For the longest time, Shambhavi Srivastava refused to have a biological child—not because she didn’t want one but because she had no faith in women’s healthcare in the country.
After marriage, she had two abortions. She couldn’t forget the “judgement” and “unsolicited guilting” she encountered, first at Manipal Hospital and then at Motherhood Hospital in Bengaluru. Worst of all, the paternalism was disguised as “medical care”.
But that changed in 2023, when she started going to Aastrika Midwifery Centre, a birthing facility that claims to be an alternative to heavily medicalised hospitals. In a nutshell, centres like Aastrika put the midwife, not the obstetrician, at the centre of childbirth. Doctors are present, sure, but they work with the midwives.
“I didn’t want to be treated like cattle. I wanted to be listened to, informed, and made part of the decision-making process,” said the 37-year-old. And she got what she came for. She’s now a mother to a three-year-old and a two-year-old she adopted.
Srivastava is hardly unique in this.
Several mothers, particularly urban, educated ones, are loath to feeling helpless when giving birth. Especially in a country where rampant overmedicalisation has seen C-section rates soar. Today, around [six in 10](https://www.nfhsiips.in/nfhsuser/assets/National%20Family%20Health%20Survey%20(NFHS-6)%202023-2024%20Fact%20Sheets.pdf) births in urban private hospitals end in surgery. At Aastrika, that number more than halves…
