Good Morning Ishan,
Investors, from time to time, have to weather market shocks. It’s the nature of the beast.
Just as markets were settling from the volatility set off by tariffs this past year, and Covid a few years before that, the war in West Asia is sending stocks crashing. Indian indices have already suffered a 10% shave, and with hostilities escalating, things could get worse.
This pain to the piggy bank will be felt more acutely by retirees. A sharp hit to their retirement corpus—on which they depend for regular expenses—will hurt, as they may not have the time to wait for the stock market to recoup.
The just-retired and soon-to-retire also face a nasty, often overlooked shock: the sequence-of-returns risk.
Put simply, the order in which returns arrive matters as much as the returns themselves. Returns from equity vary widely year to year. So, losses early on during retirement, when withdrawals have already begun, can shrink a portfolio disproportionately. That’s because it leaves less capital to participate in any recovery. The case is no better, even if long-term average returns look healthy.
The result is an unpleasant possibility of outliving one’s retirement corpus.
