Adani pays Rs 3,000 crore to outwit electricity-stealing Indians

After Adani Energy’s acquisition of smart-meter company Intellismart, the combined entity now manages a book of 47 million smart meters or 23% of the total sanctioned rollout

India’s Rs 3 lakh crore RDSS scheme has made smart meters one of the country’s biggest infrastructure rollouts, with private AMISPs financing, installing and operating them for state DISCOMs. Adani Energy’s Rs 3,050 crore acquisition of IntelliSmart is less about manufacturing smart meters and more about owning the long-term contracts that run them. The rollout is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore because financially stretched DISCOMs need accurate billing, lower losses and better visibility over electricity consumption. Power theft remains one of the biggest drains on the sector. Smart meters won’t eliminate it overnight, but they make it far harder to steal electricity undetected, turning leakage into a business opportunity. [Read the full story]

The mission picked companies to build AI for farmers, schools, and hospitals. Two years later, they are selling mostly to everyone else

India’s elite fight back against C-sections. One midwife at a time

The midwifery-led model ensures more personalisation and natural births. But it comes with higher expenses and difficulty scaling

To combat accent-based discrimination, BPOs have adopted accent translators like Sanas, so even an Indian can sound American. The ultimate goal is to improve representatives’ net promoter score, which ensures clients don’t shift calls to better-performing geographies elsewhere. The flipside, though, has been that these accent translators risk muting out “human” emotives such as laughter, whispers, and tones of voice. All this is happening as AI agents are getting better and cheaper by the day, threatening to replace humans at call centres. [Read the full story]

SBI Funds’ dream IPO run is headed for a halt

The stumbles of a smaller, superior competitor don’t bode well for the country’s largest asset manager

SBI Funds, the country’s largest asset manager, just had a blockbuster IPO. Its OFS issue was subscribed nearly 41 times. But potential new investors may not make much from the newly minted stock. How BSE’s Saatvik 100 and NSE’s Ahimsa ended up with missile makers and coal miners

One tracks 100 companies, the other 327. Yet they share 71, showing how India’s listed universe bends even the most niche investment themes towards familiar blue chips

The rules behind Saatvik and Ahimsa are more nuanced than their names suggest, allowing companies as varied as L&T, Bharat Forge, and Coal India to make the cut. Even after those screens, India’s concentrated stock market keeps pulling the indices back towards familiar giants like HDFC Bank, Reliance Industries, and Infosys. A nearly two-decade-old Shariah index shows that values-based investing has found a loyal niche in India, but never a mass audience. For BSE, NSE and fund houses, the bigger opportunity may lie in launching new investing narratives rather than radically different portfolios. [Read the full story]
