The interest-rate cycle is turning north, and “smart loans” are being used to beat at least some of the blues.
Take a recent curious case from Amritsar, which was narrated to me, of what one might call a “reverse money multiplier”, or a deposit multiplier, except from the customer’s side.
The customer had Rs 1 crore in a fixed deposit (FD) at a bank. And he wanted to show business contacts he was trying to impress that he had Rs 5 crore in the bank. The conventional route was to borrow a loan of Rs 4 crore, but he found a more offbeat one.
He borrowed Rs 90 lakh against that FD and put the money into a fresh FD in a different bank account. Then he borrowed Rs 81 lakh against the new one and deposited that too. He kept repeating the process, and after seven rounds, he held more than Rs 5.2 crore in fixed deposits in his name.
...
This businessman’s workaround to pay less rests on a family of products loosely called “smart” loans—credit lines backed by money you already have. “Smart loan” is not a product category as such, but the label is often used for a few related products.
... Whether they work out that way depends on how smart the borrower is.
