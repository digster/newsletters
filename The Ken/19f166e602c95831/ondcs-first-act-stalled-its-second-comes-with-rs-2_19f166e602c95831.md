Good Morning Ishan,
Vibhor Jain and Rohit Lohia have had a busy month.
The CEO and COO of ONDC travelled to Hyderabad to review the e-commerce facilitator’s new offering, Digidukaan; returned to the capital to pitch the platform to HUL, ITC, and Nestlé; and drove to Jaipur to launch it with Rajasthan’s chief minister.
They are, at once, reassuring startups that they can build sustainable businesses on ONDC, seeking to convince FMCG companies to plug into it, and telling sundry governments it remains worth supporting.
And they cannot afford to tarry. Digidukaan, designed for retailers to digitise stock procurement, is one of their levers to prise ONDC out of a rut before it sinks for good.
ONDC—Open Network for Digital Commerce—was launched by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade in 2022 as the “UPI for commerce”, enabling every buyer app to connect with every seller app on the platform, ultimately preventing oligopolies. The state-backed network is open as opposed to a closed platform like Amazon or Zomato.
But where UPI shook the duopoly of Visa and Mastercard, ONDC didn’t so much as nibble at the dominance of the likes of Amazon.
So, ONDC is trying to reinvent itself.

My colleague, Suprit, has a second story for you today. Let’s jump right into it.
“How many of you use Cred for…?”
Give him a crowd and Kunal Shah, the founder of the fintech Cred, will immediately turn it into an impromptu business survey. “Gathering feedback has continually shaped his conviction at Cred,” said a senior executive at the company.
But on the morning of 22 June, Shah called it a day by doing what he does best—[announcing](https://the-ken.com/newsletters/two-by-two/the-meta-cred-kunal-shah-deal-stripped-to-its-fewest-assumptions/?utm_source=web&utm_medium=homepage&utm_campaign=con_1_unit_8) a major deal. Global giant Meta would invest Rs 8,550 crore in Cred. This was accompanied by the fifth Esop buy, delighting both employees and investors through a combination of primary and secondary sales.
“By the end of FY26, we had cash and cash equivalents of around Rs 1,400 crore. Of the Rs 8,500 crore ($900 million) Meta-Cred deal, about Rs 5,100 crore ($540 million) went to Cred, while the remainder was distributed to investors through the secondary sale. As a result, our cash and cash equivalents have now risen to Rs 6,500 crore,” confirmed the senior executive, speaking on condition of anonymity.
The deal also handed Shah the [keys to](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c0my4n38myjo) the world’s largest messaging app, Whatsapp. After eight years of answering the same question over and over again—what was Cred all about?—it let him walk away from the very thing he built.
There couldn’t have been a more opportune moment for this shake-up…
