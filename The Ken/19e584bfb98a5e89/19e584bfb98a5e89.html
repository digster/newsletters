NEET’s switch from pen-and-paper to computer: damned if you do, damned if you don’t

The government is moving NEET online to fight question paper leaks, but the real problem might not have to do with the mode of exam

The NEET exam this year was cancelled within a week amid concerns of a question paper leak. This has put the focus back on the NTA, which was already in hot water when NEET question papers leaked two years ago. As a quick response, the education ministry announced that NEET will be a computer-based test from now on. But the question remains on how far NTA has followed up on the Radhakrishnan Commission’s suggestions, which were put forth in 2024. [Read the full story]

Zepto uses drones for groceries. Narayana Health for blood samples. Mamaearth wants them to fight Amazon

As drones shrink delivery times and logistics costs, D2C brands are beginning to see a way around e-commerce giants

Drone delivery in India is moving past the pilot stage, with operators like Skye Air and Airbound now running daily commercial routes. Drone delivery unit-economics tend to be sensitive to demand volumes and are more likely to struggle in quick commerce, while mid-mile use cases have seen meaningful adoption. Among notable buyers of mid-mile drone delivery are D2C brands, for whom drones offer one way to approach marketplace delivery speeds without investing as much in road-bound infrastructure. However, government regulations around drone usage are hindering the scaling of these services, especially as large airspaces are marked as no-fly zones in cities. [Read full story here]

Power shared, influence intact

LIC has lost its throne to SIPs. It’s still the smartest investor in the room

Mutual funds have grown faster as domestic institutional investors in Indian stocks than LIC of late. Yet, in moments of market stress, the insurer shows up in ways that others don’t

LIC, the market leader in insurance, dwarfs its rivals as one of the largest domestic institutional investors. Its role in the past was to be the sole pillar to support the market during a downturn. Sustained SIP inflows over the years have ensured that LIC no longer has a monopoly over the market now. Mutual funds are also shouldering the volatility. Yet, LIC’s contrarian investing style, guided by market cycles, makes it the smartest in the room. [Read the full story]

Two ex-Tesla engineers show what is waste for Reliance or Praj is gold for them

By making high-value chemicals from biomass, at mouth-watering gross margins, and replacing petroleum-derived products

India is a biomass-rich country but it has struggled to make biofuels or other products at scale. Alternate materials startup AltM has shown it can take a fraction of the biomass and produce high-value products. First to replace some petrochemicals-derived products in personal-care goods, then to create novel applications like batteries. If it scales to what its business plan shows and protects its competitive advantage, then a sizeable specialty-biochemicals company is in the making. [Read full story here]

Airtel and Jio treated AI like an OTT bundle. Was OpenAI right to stay out?

The onus of bankrolling the partnership has fallen on AI companies. All telcos have to do in return is leverage their existing ecosystem

Telecom companies like Airtel and Jio are vying to reach high-paying users, and bundling AI services was a part of that plan. But what happens when the barter is lopsided? Telcos have barely any skin in the game, while AI companies burn cash chasing scale. Gemini’s deal with Jio is going strong, but Perplexity couldn’t make it work with Airtel for a simple reason: you need more than telecom reach. OpenAI is wary and for the right reasons, staying away from telco deals despite missed revenue and user-growth targets. [Read the full story]

Maharashtra lured Amazon, NTT with cheap green power. Then it changed the data-centre deal

The state built India’s largest captive solar market for its data centres. Now it’s dismantling the economics that made it work

Maharashtra offered data-centre companies a deal, giving them a special framework to build captive solar projects for themselves. Months later, the state discom changed tariff rules, making it more expensive than before to rely on solar power. The discom, which was bleeding revenue as customers left the grid, saw this as a way of making up the shortfall. While data centres can’t up and leave their existing projects, they may look to other states for future expansion. [Read the full story]
