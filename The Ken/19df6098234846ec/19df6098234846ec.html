The government has given BSNL more than $33 billion, in multiple tranches since 2019, to effect a turnaround, but it has kept the state telecom company’s leadership on a three-month clock. A gig leader, then, for a magic trick of an undertaking.

Robert Jerard Ravi got his sixth extension as interim chairman and managing director (CMD) in April and may receive another to oversee the company’s 5G rollout, expected around August. For a manager tasked with raising the lumbering giant back on its feet, such precarity of tenure cannot but be a limiting factor.

Sure, BSNL has added 100,000 mobile towers, expanded its fixed broadband presence, and trebled its Ebitda since Ravi was given “additional charge” of the company two years ago. But achieving sustained success will entail, to begin with, improving network, brand, and distribution. Each is a project requiring substantial investments of money, effort, and time.

But time—and, to an extent, effort—is what an ad-hoc leadership can’t afford to supply.
