India’s chip ambitions have moved beyond budget-speech promises and are now producing steel, glass, and shipped units.

CG Power and Industrial Solutions is the latest to add an Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT) unit in Sanand, Gujarat. Inaugurated by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 4 July, it is India’s third such facility, after Micron and Kaynes. 

The next tangible step, India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) 2.0, is also around the corner. 

Two weeks ago, the finance ministry’s expenditure finance committee cleared https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/cons-products/electronics/india-semiconductor-mission-2-0-gets-efc-nod-with-1-2-lakh-crore-outlay/articleshow/132100897.cms a Rs 1.25 lakh crore incentive framework, nearly double ISM 1.0’s Rs 76,000 crore. Even as the cabinet approval is pending, the phase’s focus is already clear: equipment, specialty materials, gases, packaging, and design services. 

As Amitesh Kumar Sinha, ISM’s CEO, said in an interview https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/india-in-final-stages-of-rolling-out-ism-2-0-amitesh-sinha/, they’re shifting from a fab-centric approach to a full-stack semiconductor ecosystem. In essence, this phase, too, has an almost complete focus on supply-side gaps.

What it doesn’t address directly is whether India’s chip-design startups will find buyers at home. Even design startups who’ve spent years building capability under the Design-Linked Incentive (DLI) scheme are now saying https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/dli-backed-chip-companies-call-for-government-procurement-support/articleshow/131690611.cms?from=mdr that without demand visibility from the government, they may have no option but to sell out, relocate abroad, or shut down. 

The stakes are real.
