While across the world, there is a rise in marine insurance for ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, with surging premiums of more than 60%. India, meanwhile, is witnessing a quieter signal emerging from the insurance sector, one that has everything to do with the anxieties of the Indian middle class.

Recently, a Bengaluru man lost his Rs 1.2 Crore home after missing three EMIs. The bank invoked the Sarfaesi (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest) Act, 2002, issued a default notice, and auctioned the property 60 days later for Rs 95 lakh, leaving the borrower with just Rs 15 lakh after eight years of payments.

Now, if he had a home loan insurance plan, the insurer might have covered him when he missed those EMIs, and he wouldn’t have lost the house at all. 

In the last five months alone, adoption of home loan insurance has surged nearly 7X, according to an analysis by Policybazaar (shared with The Ken). 

This data highlights how far-reaching the goal of owning a house is, but also points towards the need for an update of a decade-old act and re-emphasises the ever-growing agonies of the middle class.

Salaried professionals’ current fear…

…is not having a house to live in. 

“For many families, a home loan is their single largest financial commitment, and customers increasingly want to ensure that an unforeseen event does not transfer that burden onto dependents.”

Vivek Jain, CBO of life insurance, Policybazaar

Salaried professionals make up around 80–85% of home loan insurance buyers, and 70% of them are between 31 and 45 years old. Of the total, people aged 36 to 40 account for the largest share at more than a quarter, followed by 41–45 at 23%, and 31–35 at 22%. 

Borrowers with home loans between Rs 50 lakh and Rs 1 crore account for nearly half of the insured customers, while around a quarter cross the single-crore threshold, and the average sits at Rs 50–75 lakh.

Delhi NCR leads with 8–10%, followed by Mumbai at 5–7%, and Bengaluru, Lucknow, and Pune at 3–5% each. Interestingly, demand from non-metro cities has accelerated over the last two months, the report observes. 

The latest economic slowdown, particularly in the IT industry in India, is having a ripple effect. 

“Based on the feedback we receive from point-of-sale persons, one possible reason behind this is AI-triggered job losses, as IT companies have been laying off many employees lately, and the uncertainty has pushed people to go for dedicated home loan insurance adoption, as term insurance won’t solve this uncertainty,” said an insurance broker registered with Irdai (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority). 

Term insurance protects your family from losing the house if you pass away. Home insurance covers the physical property once the loan is cleared. Home loan insurance, however, ensures you don’t lose the house while you are still repaying the loan. Given the possibility of losing your job unexpectedly someday, understand the different types of insurance coverage to increase your financial security. 

However, maintaining a home loan can be unsustainable when banks hold significant power under the Sarfaesi Act, 2002.

This act allows the government to auction any non-performing assets if you miss three EMI’s and fail to pay the cost within the next 60 days, such as what happened with the Bengaluru man above. 

Currently, over 50,000 such residential properties are listed for e-auction by banks on Baanknet.

Speaking on the trend of growing home loan insurance, Varun Agarwal, head of term insurance at Policybazaar, told The Ken that large financial commitments are becoming a bigger priority for borrowers.

“Customers view home loan insurance as an additional layer of protection rather than a substitute for term insurance. As awareness around financial planning grows, adoption of both products can rise together because they address different aspects of a household’s financial security.”

Varun Agarwal, head of term insurance, Policybazaar

He also added that the introduction of online loan insurance plans by insurers, which are significantly cheaper compared to existing offline plans, is also a factor. 

India’s insurance industry has become competitive and user-friendly too. Global consulting firm Aon’s 2026 global insurance insights report cites reforms like higher FDI limits, relaxed governance, higher policy limits, and more favourable terms.

There is also talk of housing prices plateauing between 2026 and 2028, according to a report published by real estate consultancy CBRE, because for the first time since 2021, “household income growth is now anticipated to outpace property price appreciation”. 

This is what the ratio looked like when we wrote about it last year.

Clearly, there is a shift. 

By law, home loan insurance is not mandatory for home loans. However, for years, loan executives have been trying hard to mis-sell the narrative that home loan insurance is “almost” compulsory. Or that you will get a home loan only if you buy insurance.

However, only a small percentage of buyers usually subscribe to this narrative, partly because they see it as an additional burden over the loan, and secondly, because not all areas are prone to natural calamities as advertised. Now, with more clarity, people explore online options. 

And to offset risks associated with home loans, people usually buy term and endowment insurance policies. While they are notorious for being heavy on the pocket, Policybazaar reports that zero GST on online policies, combined with more efficient pricing, now makes them significantly more affordable. 

Amid increasing uncertainties, insurance policies have become the way to double down on security. 

Though if an unforeseen event does befall you, good luck trying to dodge the insurers who at times deny benefits through technicalities.
