India’s Rs 1 lakh crore R&D fund: speed was the signal, but Rs 5 lakh crore is the point

The Technology Development Board ran through its budget in weeks. Approved VCs are next. The harder ask, persuading one more stakeholder to respond to the big bet, falls to Saurabh Srivastava and Kris Gopalakrishnan

India’s Rs 1 lakh crore Research, Development, and Innovation (RDI) Fund has been writing large cheques for companies and VC funds in “strategic sunrise” fields. Why Bharti, Birla are betting on a food business that doesn’t scale like fast food

Bharti’s dining arm, Gourmet Investments, wants to more than double its restaurant footprint to 125 in three years. But casual dining chains don’t grow like fast-food outlets

Corporates like Bhartis are betting on wealthy Indians to splurge even more on casual dining. The group plans to scale global casual-dining concepts such as Chili’s, Olive Garden, and Pizza Express in India. This shows in its actions: the group recently hired ex Pizza Hut, Dominos executives in leadership roles at Gourmet Investments, and have set goals to double the store count. The catch is scalability in casual dining is a herculean task. [Read the full story]

Playing on both sides

Heads, founders win. Tails, platforms win. AI fund Activate wins either way

The country’s latest AI fund, founded by a former IndiaAI Mission advisor, is courting the next crop of Indian AI founders, while also backing Elevenlabs, the platform many of them are likely to build on

Activate, the new Indian AI fund, likes to invest in founders before they even have a company, finding them through events, referrals, and conversations. The fund has also invested in Elevenlabs, the voice-AI firm whose technology most Indian voice-AI startups already build on. This creates an unusual position: if the startups Activate backs succeed, the fund wins; if Elevenlabs expands into their categories instead, the fund still wins. Founders and analysts say building on top of a platform that can absorb your product at any time is the central risk of Indian AI—and one that Activate’s structure does not resolve. AI cites a hospital for health advice, but the hospital uses AI to write the advice. And both are wrong

The vicious loop poses real-world health risks

AI platforms and search engines present health-related information that can be wrong or misleading. That’s partly because they rely for sourcing on big-name hospitals that publish copious volumes of medical content on their websites.
