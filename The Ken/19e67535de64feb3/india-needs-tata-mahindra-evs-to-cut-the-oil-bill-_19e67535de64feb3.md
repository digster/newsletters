Good Morning Ishan,
India’s EV dream is souring. At the most inopportune time, too.
As the US-Israeli war against Iran disrupted crude supplies from West Asia, which fed the bulk of India’s oil demand, Prime Minister Narendra Modi urged citizens to reduce fossil fuel consumption and adopt EVs. The 11 May appeal rallied EV stocks—pushing Ather Energy’s to an all-time high and JBM Auto’s to a six-month peak—because it made intuitive sense.
India’s import bill for oil runs to over $10 billion per month, but it’s only about $500 million for lithium-ion batteries, which fuel EVs. Switching from oil to lithium, the logic goes, sizeably cuts the crude bill and spares the country from the vagaries of geopolitical tensions.
Except, there’s a glitch in the circuit: consumer interest in EVs is fraying.
Tata Motors aimed for 50% EV sales by 2030, but recently slashed that target to 30%. Mahindra is quietly hedging with hybrids, retrofitting born-electric cars with combustion engines. Honda has scaled back its EV plans for India.
