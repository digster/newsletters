“The raja has to decide when he wants to meet the praja. How will I decide when he has to pay me?”

That’s what Kushagra Bajaj said in 2007, when a fight over the family empire was underway. He was referring to his uncle Rahul, then patriarch of the Bajaj Group.

One side wanted factories, sugar mills, and consumer brands—businesses that would surely thrive as standards of living improved and Indians spent more.

The other got a lending business tied to scooter sales. It was a modest part of Bajaj Auto’s market cap at the time, but soon became one of the most efficient financial machines in India.

Intermission’s second episode is out. This time, Seetharaman and Rohin unpack Bajaj Finance. And as Seetha puts it, “You’d expect Bajaj Finance to be a story about finance. It’s actually history, politics, and family.”

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jhgvdo9rb0w

Within that story, and in the exchange between Intermission’s two hosts, you’ll discover the company’s brilliance. 

Anyone who bought a TV five years ago, for example, could be identified by the company as someone who might start looking for a replacement soon. Every purchase was a data point, and the more you bought, the better Bajaj Finance could predict what you’ll need next and when to offer a loan for it. 

By 2025-end, the company was cross-selling to 71 million of its 110 million customers. Its assets under management have already crossed Rs 5 lakh crore.
