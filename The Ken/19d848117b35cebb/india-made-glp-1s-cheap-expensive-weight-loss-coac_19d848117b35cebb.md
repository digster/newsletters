“How much weight do you want to lose?”
Not at a gym. Not at a doctor-ordered dietitian’s. But even before you step into a GLP-1 weight-management program, this is the question they throw at you.
Last month, semaglutide—the molecule behind drugs like Ozempic and Wegovy—went off-patent in India, and over 50 pharma companies flooded the market with their generics. Mounjaro, the other GLP-1 drug that had dominated the segment with sales growing steadily, dipped by about 15% in the last month.
But abundance has a way of creating its own grey markets. Even before the dust settled, newer GLP-1 drugs, like Eli Lilly’s Retatrutide, which is still under clinical trial, began showing up in Whatsapp groups and closed communities, imported for “research use” but boldly sold for weight loss. The promise of weight loss had already outpaced the systems meant to contain it.
Back in the open market, the effects have been just as dramatic. Everyone from brides-to-be to ordinary people looking to lose just a few kilograms has taken to it. Endocrinologists, who are needed to both prescribe the drug and monitor its effects, have shot up their fees. AI-generated before-and-after weight loss photos have been aggressively deployed to lure more customers. And personally, it’s been hard to scroll through Instagram without every other post turning out to be an ad for a new kind of business on the other end of the spectrum: weight-loss “management programs”.
Healthtech platforms like Healthifyme and Tata 1mg, along with a long tail of clinics and startups, are already selling something adjacent to the drug: the [process](https://the-ken.com/podcasts/two-by-two/healthify-swallowed-its-disruptors-but-can-it-digest-them/) of taking it.
GLP-1 drugs don’t work in isolation. You can’t just prick yourself and sit back, waiting for your waistline to shrink.
“The drug only works when you consider diet, strength training, building up the dosage, and a number of other lifestyle changes”, said Sourabh Devardekar, a Pune-based healthcare recruitment manager who has lost nine kilograms in the last three weeks on Mounjaro, a GLP-1 drug.
“They’re not a magic fat burner,” said Devardekar. “They are just appetite suppressants.”
But even that is an understatement.
