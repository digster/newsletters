Good Morning Ishan,
At 4 a.m. every Monday, Rohit Rohilla leaves his home in Noida to catch a 7.30 a.m. flight to Bengaluru.
His routine is set in stone. “I tend to sleep earlier than usual on Sunday,” said the 27-year-old consultant. His driver, Imran, knows it well. Like clockwork, week after week, he shows up 15 minutes before Rohilla has to start.
On a good day, it takes them 90 minutes to get to Delhi’s Indira Gandhi International Airport. But with the Noida International Airport now open for business, that routine is about to change.
For Rohilla, it means a shorter commute and a little more weekend at home. For Imran, it means his routes and customers could start splitting.
But for Delhi airport, the stakes are higher. The clockwork it once relied on is breaking; so is its grip on the skies.
The airport’s dominance didn’t come from handling traffic alone.
