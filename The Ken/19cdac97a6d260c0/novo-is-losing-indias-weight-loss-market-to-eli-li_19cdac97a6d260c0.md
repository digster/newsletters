Good Morning Ishan,
Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound, Mounjaro. Some of these drugs are labelled for type 2 diabetes patients or people with cardiovascular risk. All of them are used by people who want to lose weight.
The first two drugs are made by Novo Nordisk, which basically created the prescription weight-management market. But Eli Lilly has been eating into its market share, and Indians will soon have affordable generics to choose from, given that Novo Nordisk’s patent for semaglutide, the active ingredient in Wegovy and Ozempic, will lapse in a little over a week.
Go up one level in the company structure. If Novo Nordisk can’t sell its drugs, then maybe its biggest shareholder can absorb part of the infrastructure that delivers medical care.
That’s precisely what’s going on: Novo Holdings acquired stakes in Manipal Hospitals in 2024 and Surya Hospitals in January this year. After all, “hospitals are sure-shot bets to make money in Indian healthcare,” as one analyst told The Ken.
Manipal is the second-largest hospital chain in India. Surya, although smaller, is a specialist network that serves women and children. Sudeshna makes sense of the moves by the Novo Group in today’s [story](https://the-ken.com/story/novo-is-losing-indias-weight-loss-market-to-eli-lilly-how-does-it-redeem-itself/?utm_source=daily_story&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=daily_newsletter). If you care about how you or a loved one may receive care the next time a hospital visit is warranted, then you shouldn’t miss it.
IIT-Bombay is set to make good money from Sedemac’s listing today. So will most of its new investors, who came in during the $100-million secondary round two years ago. But no money is flowing into the company; the Rs 1,087 crore IPO is entirely an offer for sale. That, in itself, is not unusual. What is, however, is the academic-founder’s math on why a company growing at more than 25% doesn’t need capital. With extreme customer concentration and a pricey IPO, the markets are watching closely.
