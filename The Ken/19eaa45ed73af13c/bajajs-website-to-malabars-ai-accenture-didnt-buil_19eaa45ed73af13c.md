Good Morning Ishan,
Relationships matter much in advertising. But they no longer decide everything.
In May, cosmetics and beauty firm L’Oréal India ended its 16-year partnership with WPP’s Wavemaker and handed its Rs 900 crore media account to Publicis Groupe’s Zenith.
The short reason: Publicis was better prepared. The communications firm’s data and AI capabilities, built through acquisitions of tech-first companies like Epsilon, Sapient, and Performics, aligned better with L’Oréal’s growing focus on digital commerce.
It isn’t merely a public battle between two advertising giants. In fact, it shows a broader shift in the industry. Technology matters more than familiarity in modern marketing. In other words, those who bet on technology early are pulling ahead.
One of that shift’s less visible beneficiaries is Accenture Song. And that’s by choice.
The creative arm of consulting and technology giant Accenture has worked on projects from Bajaj Chetak’s website revamp to even training AI models on Malabar Gold’s celebrity ambassadors to automate promotional material. Globally, its client roster cuts across industries. “From luxury brands like Chanel, to airlines, banks, and fast-food chains like Mcdonald’s, you name it, and Accenture Song is there,” Azmina Poddar, a former managing director and chief creative officer with the company, told The Ken.
In FY25, it made $20 billion in annual revenue globally, making it the world’s largest advertising agency. Yet, Song barely identifies as one. The vertical, responsible for nearly a third of Accenture’s overall revenue, sits alongside the latter’s consulting and tech businesses.
…
Song’s DNA is technology-first, the opposite of creative-led traditional agencies. In that sense, its closest competitors are not advertising groups, but the likes of Deloitte Digital.
Despite not being an apples-to-apples comparison, they’re fighting in the same field. It’s the sign of the times.
