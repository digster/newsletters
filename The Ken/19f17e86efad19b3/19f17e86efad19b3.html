By now, you’re probably aware of how Anthropic, the world’s most valued AI company, cried wolf about its all-powerful, much-too-dangerous model, Mythos. (Sorry, OpenAI. We’re long past the point of caring for your explanations and protests.)

It was then taken at face value by the Trump administration, leading to Anthropic rolling back its launch, cutting off access to paying customers, and losing an incredible amount of trust all over the world.

Mythos was then diluted, repackaged, and renamed as Fable for the masses, before that was also disabled for similar reasons.

It doesn’t matter what Anthropic’s intent was, because the effect was that the US government forced it to discriminate against its customers from everywhere else in the world. 

That includes India. 

I briefly used Fable for a few queries before I became a “foreigner” who could not be trusted to pay for and use it.

As I write this, Anthropic has been “allowed” to release Fable to over 100 American companies and government agencies.

It’s the same story with the latest model from OpenAI, GPT 5.6. That, too, will be made available only to a select set of American companies approved by the Trump administration.

No access yet for India or Indians. 

This is despite India being part of Pax Silica, a US-led “strategic counter-narrative to the rising global trend of restrictive, fear-based regulatory regimes.”

“The shift in the administration’s stance to AI companies has sparked fear in other countries that their citizens, companies, and government agencies could be cut off from top technology,” said an article in The Washington Post.

You don’t say.

Now, these few weeks of intense self-sabotage by American AI model companies like Anthropic and OpenAI have also been mirrored by the emergence of promising non-US alternatives.

GLM-5.2 from China’s Z.ai (also known as Zhipu) has had an incredible run up the charts since launch and is widely considered to be almost as good as Anthropic’s best models, but at one-eighth the cost. The best part is that it’s open source (open-weight, in the context of AI models), meaning customers can download, tune, and run it on their own servers.

Mat Velloso, a former VP at Google Deepmind, called it the first open model that passes the bar as a daily driver. “Things are not going to be the same,” he said.

“Genuinely impressed, almost shocked, at how good GLM-5.2 by @Zai_org is at coding. This changes things,” said Guillermo Rauch, CEO of Vercel, a cloud infrastructure and development company, last valued at close to $10 billion.

There was also Fugu, a novel model-of-models from Japan-based Sakana. 

You’d think this is great news for Indian businesses and startups that were feeling chafed at being treated like second-class customers by Anthropic and OpenAI, even after paying the exact same prices since everyone is billed in US dollars. They could now consider diversifying their AI usage to, say, GLM-5.2 or Fugu. 

But no. Few Indian cloud providers, which include Yotta and E2E Networks, offer these models on a billable, token-based system. Most of them are focused on large enterprise users who will rent “bare metal” Nvidia H100 and B200 GPUs from their data centres and figure out what to do with the capacity on their own.

In a truly competitive and innovative market, we’d have Indian AI cloud providers offering the best models from around the world with per-token pricing, monthly billing, and clear privacy, data-retention, latency, and throughput commitments.

But Indian cloud providers are content to merely rent out their data centres to enterprises and governments who offer better margins and predictability than SMEs, startups, or individual founders and developers.

So, if you’re an Indian startup or founder, tough luck. 

Sure, Sarvam exists on paper and in the papers as “India’s sovereign foundational model”. But when was the last time you saw the Sarvam founders talk about the performance of their models in public? When was the last time you remember Sarvam even figuring on global benchmarks for the most common tasks that businesses want, like coding, knowledge management, or content generation?

How can you build your future on a company that won’t show public confidence or ambition in its own products?

India doesn’t have great AI models of its own, and we may not for a while or ever. We must accept that.

Once we do, it frees us to think about the near- to medium-term future dispassionately. Should we stop innovating using cutting-edge AI? Because it won’t be made available to us, or is not lucrative enough for local cloud providers to offer?

Ironically, it has been a global brokerage—Bernstein—that has been one of the most vocal critics of where Indian industry, businesses, and professionals are headed.

It argues in a recent report that India might “wake up and find that the most powerful applications in finance or military are no longer working, and the APIs and weights they rely on have vanished overnight,” and that over time, this will entrench India’s dependence on foreign AI models for decades.

It’s easy to feel despondent about where we find ourselves. But, to paraphrase a great adage, “The best time to do something about this was four years ago; the second best time is today.”

Why four years? Take a guess. 

Building intelligence independence in the age of AI

At The Ken, we’ve always believed that the most important and pressing questions have no quick or easy answers. 

Which is why we’re announcing our sixth live event on the topic of “intelligence independence” on Saturday, 1 August, at the Bangalore International Centre.

Unlike prior waves of technology, the AI wave doesn’t just impact all of us—be it as a country, a company, or a professional—it also extracts what is most valuable from us too. 

Our knowledge. Our intelligence.

It’s the part of the AI equation we talk about the least. It’s also the one that generates the most leverage, usually for others.

It’s tempting to ignore this as a problem that isn’t ours to solve. But the incentives have aligned and concentrated around AI that it’s no longer a reasonable assumption.

If it were, would we be where we are today?

It is also tempting to think that this won’t directly affect us as professionals, investors, founders, or operators. 

But that’s not true. Your country’s geopolitical AI strategy decides the AI ecosystem that you are in. Your employer’s AI strategy dictates what tools you will use at work. Everything is connected and must be factored in when we think about our respective future plans.

It is important to understand the respective stances, stacks, and memories at all levels before we can create our own strategies, whether as individuals or organisations.

In our events, we have always attempted to break down complex and important topics by bringing in intelligent, experienced, and opinionated panellists. We then use the collective curiosity and experience of our attendees to engage in stimulating discussions. 

Discussions that aren’t scripted or glib. 

This will be our third AI-focused event, after “The Great Unbundling—Chaos as the ladder for agentic AI in India” and “How AI is breaking and remaking the way products are built”.

If you’ve attended either of those, or other ones before them, you know that you’ll leave at the end with a sharp, multi-perspective idea of the possibilities ahead. It will be the same this time too. I promise. 

You could be a curious professional thinking about how you can build your own approach to AI tools. You could be looking for models that are not merely what’s been made available to you. You could be a founder who wants to know how others are diversifying their dependence by using a few models. You could be a stock investor who wants to understand the incentives and systems that will play out in the near future. You could be the builder behind an AI model or service who wants to understand which way the wind is blowing. Or you could be someone smart who just wants to be part of a great, unfiltered, and intelligent discussion that leaves you smarter.

At The Ken, we create events that we’d love to attend ourselves because they make us sharper as well.
