Plucky founders, not Indian pharma giants, could reshape drug discovery in its new ‘golden age’

Even without betting the farm like Glenmark and Wockhardt, it’s possible to punt on the drug India won’t make

AI is helping discover and develop new drugs at unprecedented speed. 173 new AI-discovered drugs have entered clinical studies in five years. Generics-focused Indian pharma faces a reckoning, as does global big pharma with a patent cliff. But unlike the West, drug discovery biotechs in India have few private funders. Founders spend more time raising paltry sums than doing science. So why are they even attempting drug discovery where the outcome is binary? [Read the full story]

Catch-22

There’s a ‘borrowed revolution’ on our roads. And India has no control over it

The recent spate of e-rickshaws stalling in the middle of the road, due to Chinese BMS apps like BAT BMS and Epoch Li-ion, is yet another symptom of a larger problem

Indian electric-vehicle numbers have been rising, but a lot of this “revolution” is powered by Chinese tech. The security risks of this are manifold, one of them being the recent trend of people using Chinese battery-management systems (BMS) apps to stall e-rickshaws in the middle of the road. Chinese automakers are increasingly buying up stakes in auto companies across the world, from Benelli to Aston Martin. India now faces a trilemma: should it ape the US’ blanket ban on Chinese-backed firms which it can’t, follow Japan’s detour into hybrids, or, much like Europe, embrace an uneasy collaboration with its northern neighbour? [Read full story here]

Tata 1mg refused to get carried away in the e-pharmacy battle. It’s in no mood to change its mind

For over a decade, Tata 1mg has resisted following trends other e-pharmacies did. It wants to be a healthcare company

The e-pharmacy sector has seen many players change their moat, crash, or get absorbed in the last decade. Tata 1mg has somehow managed to be a part of a bigger conglomerate while keeping its original moat—putting its consumers first. It has also grown from being an e-pharmacy alone to offering diagnostics, teleconsultation, and retail stores, which were always part of the original plan. Tata 1mg has also shown restraint against trends such as massive discounting and quick-commerce. It’s managed to not become just another name in the Tata Digital umbrella. [Read the full story]

India’s space college broke its bond with ISRO. Not its dependence

Two years after ISRO did away with its hiring bond with IIST, the institute is struggling to adapt to private-industry requirements—continuing to place over half its students at the space agency

ISRO ended its hiring bond with the Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST) in 2024, but most graduates still want to join the space agency. IIST, for its part, is trying to align itself with the needs of India’s growing private-spacetech sector. The 2020 space-sector reforms are realigning ISRO towards frontier research while private industry takes up the commercial work. IIST’s future hinges on how it evolves beyond a single employer [Read full story here]

Changing of the guard

TCS and Infosys think they can profit from AI paranoia

IT companies are building new tools and repacking existing services to serve as the mediation layer between enterprises and AI models

As concerns grow around AI security and data safety, IT-services firms are sensing a new business opportunity. They want to be the middle layer between AI-makers and enterprises, promising security that most AI labs don’t sell directly. But AI security procedures are not so different from cybersecurity practices that have been on offer for decades. So what’s new? The real attraction for enterprise clients may be the peace of mind that, if all goes south, someone else can be held responsible. [Read the full story]
