Brands would kill to have the kind of stickiness milk-makers have with customers. Most of India buys milk every day, or at least every other day, invariably in plastic pouches from the neighbourhood grocer.

And depending on where they live, shoppers are inextricably bound to specific brands: Amul in the west, Mother Dairy in the north, and Nandini and Milma in the south. 

Dodla Dairy has earned a large enough loyal following in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. The problem is, Dodla, or any milk brand for that matter, can’t charge consumers a lot more for the milk than what they buy it for. It’s nothing but a commodity, unlike cheese, with a label stuck on the packaging. And commodities don’t command a premium. The affinity to brands in milk is more to do with availability or habit than with any specific attribute of the product itself. 

But dairy producers can’t kick the addiction to good old milk as their flagship product, as I wrote https://the-ken.com/story/milky-mist-is-anti-milk-its-still-a-rs-20000-cr-dairy-giant/ last year. 

It’s easy to see why dairy companies yearn to sell milk by the truckload. The average urban Indian household spends roughly Rs 2,500 a month on milk and only Rs 500 on other dairy products, according to Yatish Talvadia, co-founder and former chief executive of Milkbasket, an e-grocer Reliance Retail bought in 2021.

Dairy producers also expect to be paid a day in advance for the milk they dispatch to stores. In contrast, they distribute value-added products with a credit period of 15–20 days. So milk is great for a company’s sales and cash flow even if it doesn’t buoy up its bottom line much.
