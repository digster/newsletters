Good Morning Ishan,
A Blinkit executive walking around Colaba market in South Mumbai stopped in his tracks. In front of him, right in the heart of this jam-packed market, was an Amazon Now dark store about 2,000–3,000 sq ft big.
“In order to cover [an important pocket of] South Mumbai, Amazon has opened the store in a place the expansion head of Blinkit could only dream of,” said the executive.
Flanked by landmarks like the Kala Ghoda art district and the BSE, this is a densely populated part of Mumbai and houses the target audience for quick-commerce apps: urban, affluent professionals with little time for mundanities like grocery shopping.
Blinkit had tried to set up a dark store in this area too. “But it didn’t work out because none of the sites we saw were feasible,” said the executive.
Parking space is a must for dark stores. A massive player like Blinkit, for instance, needs parking space for at least 50–60 bikes at once. “Meanwhile, Amazon went ahead with a spot with only seven to eight parking spaces. That’s how aggressive it is,” said the Blinkit executive. “Only time will tell how smart of a move this is, but at least it gets things going.”
Since it piloted its quick-commerce service, Amazon Now, in September 2025, the company has been on a tear. And understandably so, considering…
