Good Morning Ishan,
At first glance, Zepto’s IPO filing reads like a victory lap.
The pure-play quick-commerce challenger, not yet six years old, has sprinted to the very edge of Swiggy’s total income, reporting over Rs 23,000 crore ($ 2.7 billion) in FY26 against the food delivery giant’s almost Rs 23,600 crore ($2.8 billion) during the same period.
Its co-founder Aadit Palicha is slated to become the youngest CEO of a listed company, and Zepto itself would be among the youngest startups ever to attempt a mainboard IPO.
The trailblazer of the 10-minute delivery model has raised about $2.5 billion over the past five years and is now heading to the public markets on the back of a fivefold revenue increase since FY24. By that measure alone, Zepto appears to have cracked a growth code that has eluded even [Instamart](https://the-ken.com/story/is-swiggy-snatching-defeat-from-the-jaws-of-victory/), Swiggy’s quick-commerce arm.
But is growth alone enough to inspire investor confidence? Perhaps not. And a closer look at Zepto’s draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) suggests the company knows it.
Zepto has chosen not to disclose several key performance indicators, most notably metrics that both Eternal’s Blinkit and Swiggy’s Instamart report to investors every quarter.
And behind the headline revenue surge, losses have swollen nearly fivefold, from Rs 1,200 crore in FY24 to nearly Rs 6,000 crore in FY26. Zepto’s liquid reserves are also depleting fast, with cash and cash equivalents dropping 23% to Rs 5,680 crore in FY26 from Rs 7,440 crore a year earlier.
It’s against this backdrop that Zepto is now turning to public investors, asking they put in Rs 8,100 crore through a fresh issue, even as the updated DRHP’s fine print admits that this amount may not be enough. “Alternative forms of funding”—much like in Swiggy’s case—may eventually be required.
This is the nut of Zepto’s IPO pitch: a growth story so seductive it almost successfully distracts from the fundamental flaws beneath it…
