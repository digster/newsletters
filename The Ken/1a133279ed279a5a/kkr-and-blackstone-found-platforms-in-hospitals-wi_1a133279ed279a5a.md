Good Morning Ishan,
Even if you stand at a stolid degree of remove from anything subjective, the pull of the private equity and private healthcare tango in India today is palpable. Not a quarter goes by without a sizable deal.
“It’s a potent combination; insurance is the least insidious player now—to give the devil its due—but it won’t be for long,” said the co-founder of a 150-bed hospital in one of the southern cities. Despite building a standalone hospital, considered “financially and reputationally successful”, the founders had to sell out. It had become untenable to manage the wage bill as PE-backed hospitals came with “blank cheques” for their doctors.
It doesn’t matter what the poaching costs are; hospitals are paying.
“You can’t say it’s one and not the other; all major hospitals are PE-backed and there’s more capital than ever to attract the person they want…And you can’t really blame the doctors when they get offers they can’t refuse,” said the chief executive of a mid-sized hospital in Bengaluru. He bowed out of the business when a budding chain acquired his hospital in 2025.
India’s hospital industry is, depending on your vantage point, either the most mouth-watering investment story in Asia or the most stomach-churning experiment in healthcare. The country has never been here before…
