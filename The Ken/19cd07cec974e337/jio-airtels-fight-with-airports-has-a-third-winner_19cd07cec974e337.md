Good Morning Ishan,
India’s newest, flashiest infrastructure showcase has a conspicuous problem—one that users passing through its portals vociferously complain about. Navi Mumbai International Airport (NMIA) lacks the most basic amenity: telecom connectivity. It’s something most people in India now take for granted. Did NMIA miss the memo?
Nope. Rather, this unseemly state of affairs flows from a standoff among corporate India’s biggest names: the Adani Group on one side, and Reliance Jio, Bharti Airtel, and Vodafone Idea on the other. Adani Airport Holdings, which runs NMIA, wants the telecom operators to cough up a cool Rs 92 lakh a month to access the airport’s built-in 5G network and provide connectivity to their customers.
Telecom operators—masters of their spectrum universe so far—aren’t used to such demands.
But there’s always a first time, no? You see, a deep change is underway in India’s telecom infrastructure, and there’s a new kid on the block. Say hello to neutral-host firms. Ever heard of Ibus and Cloudextel? Me neither.
But as Priyal writes in her eye-opener today, these near-unknown entities pose a mighty threat to the telecom biggies. Landlords like NMIA love them, of course. The balance of power is changing, aided by 5G and a proposed policy tweak. The implications go much beyond flashy, new airports.
The government, meanwhile, is running with the hare and hunting with the hound.
