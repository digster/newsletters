Good Morning Ishan,
Saloni, 26, works at a call centre in Delhi that serves ride-hailing companies. She currently onboards new drivers, verifies their documents, and familiarises them with the companies’ code of conduct.
Until recently, she also handled calls from disgruntled Uber, Rapido, and Ola customers, a function now mostly relegated to voice-AI bots. For a few weeks, Saloni was even the human in the loop, stepping in when the bot couldn’t resolve a customer’s problem.
“By the time the voice bot transferred a call to me [over a refund or lost belongings, for instance], the customer was already so frustrated that they would just start screaming,” Saloni said.
Voice-AI bots have increasingly become the first line of defence for customer complaints, while access to a human agent is being recast as a premium, paid perk.
Gnani.ai, Corover, Skit, Bolna, Haptik, Navana, Ringg AI, among countless others, are making it easier for businesses to automate customer calls, replacing human agents with bots that can handle thousands of conversations at a fraction of the cost.
This has also attracted significant investor interest.
…
But new rules on application-to-person (A2P) communication introduced by the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (Trai) last month could mess up voice-AI companies’ fragile unit economics.
