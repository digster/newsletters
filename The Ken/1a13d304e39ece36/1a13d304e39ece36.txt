Last week, reports https://www.indiatoday.in/business/story/irdai-insurance-commission-caps-january-april-2027-report-3013151-2026-10-09 indicated that Irdai, the insurance regulator, might have to extend the deadline for comments on its consultation paper, “Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution” beyond 25 October. That could push implementation beyond the early 2027 timeline the regulator had envisaged. 

Indian stretchable time is a known devil, but a delay on what Irdai has proposed could cost many people a high premium with a bad cover. That’s because the regulator’s dripping-in-detail papers cover much more than commission caps that brokers and agents have been pushing back hard against. They also talk of an ill that has stalked India’s insurance industry for long—mis-selling. A longer consultation timeline risks pushing back the regulator’s fixes for mis-selling, something that can least afford to wait. 

Mis-selling, rampant in Indian insurance, has only been on the rise. Irdai’s 2024–25 annual report shows that complaints about unfair business practices (UFBP), mostly mis-selling, rose 14% year-on-year. The regulator’s consultation papers have identified the sources and symptoms of the malady, and prescribed remedies. These need to be administered pronto.
