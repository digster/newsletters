Good Morning Ishan,
Meesho held its quarterly panchayat at its offices in Helios Business Park in Bengaluru’s Kadubeesanahalli last week. Inside the town hall, Valmo, the company’s in-house logistics arm, was one of the hot topics.
“[Vidit] Aatrey (Meesho’s CEO) spoke about wanting to expand Valmo,” said a manager present at the panchayat. “[He] even mentioned how its share has increased compared to the previous year.”
With almost 2.7 billion orders placed in FY26, Meesho ships more parcels than any other e-commerce platform in India, and Valmo has become central to how the company thinks about growth, costs, and survival in Indian e-commerce.
“Valmo is super strategic for us,” said a person close to the company. “We started it because there wasn’t enough low-cost innovation happening in the overall logistics ecosystem.”
Launched in 2024, it was built as an asset-light logistics-orchestration platform meant to lower shipping costs and reduce dependence on India’s third-party logistics (3PL) players. And when Meesho filed to go public in late 2025, Valmo was positioned as one of its defining competitive advantages.
The scale-up was rapid. From handling 2% of Meesho’s shipments in FY23, Valmo grew to handle more than 60%, surpassing Delhivery, India’s largest logistics company.
But that was two quarters ago. In Q4, Valmo was fulfilling less than 50% of the company’s orders. Meesho’s growth trajectory had placed immense operational pressure on Valmo, and scale had come at a cost.
