On 20 July, people in Haryana woke up to yet another “monsoon” day stripped of the scent of fresh rain. Instead, they were subjected to scorching heat and soaring mercury. Angry locals took to the streets in five districts, protesting the ensuing power cuts. 

Interestingly, Haryana’s energy shortages have historically been close to zero, and its power-distribution companies are the healthiest in the country. 

India’s installed power-generation capacity has also been moving at a clip, largely due to rising solar-power generation. Over the last decade, installed solar-power capacity has jumped 58X to touch 162 GW, as the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy told the Rajya Sabha in July.

So why the power cuts?

The answer, as it turns out, lies in the net load. This is the total demand for electricity minus the share fulfilled by solar power. As solar-power generation rises, this net load, as a recent report by the Economic Advisory Council to the Prime Minister found, undergoes wild fluctuations throughout the day.
