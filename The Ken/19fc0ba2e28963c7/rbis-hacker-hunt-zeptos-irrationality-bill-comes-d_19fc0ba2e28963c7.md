[RBI’s pipeline for hackers now starts at IIT Kanpur. It isn’t where they want to end up](https://the-ken.com/columns/zero-shot/kimi-k3s-maker-is-what-openai-was-supposed-to-be/)
The country is losing its world-class hackers to GCCs and foreign firms, just as government bodies are realising they need them most
India produces more young ethical hackers than any country in the world, and a rising wave of AI-powered cyberattacks has pushed the government to start recruiting them. India’s challenge is not a shortage of talent but retention, as many top researchers move to higher-paying GCCs and foreign firms. The government has created new cybersecurity roles, but these tend to offer either competitive pay on short-term contracts or permanent employment at lower salaries, rather than both together. The industry has largely stopped grooming fresh talent, demanding years of experience upfront, and placing junior staff in senior roles, leaving India to address an ongoing threat with an employment model built for temporary work. [India should stop adding solar farms. Now](https://the-ken.com/story/india-should-stop-adding-solar-farms-now/?utm_source=weekly_email+&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
Such is the obsession with renewables that no one has bothered to ask how much solar energy is too much without battery storage
India has witnessed an explosion in installed solar-power capacity since 2014, but even then, we continue to face power cuts. The main solution to the grid instability caused by solar power is more battery storage, and the government has indeed incentivised companies to set up battery storage plants. A lot of the time, solar capacity remains stranded due to a lack of co-located storage as well as not enough transmission lines. What we need is a recalibration of priorities: a tighter focus on surrounding infrastructure, along with storage for existing solar plants. Almost every major industrial state is giving billions in discounted land, subsidised power, and exempted taxes to attract data centres. What exactly are they buying?
Indian states are offering subsidies worth billions in a race for data centres. Unlike automobile plants or semiconductor fabs, though, data centres don’t create many jobs. Jobs peak during the construction stage and few remain by the time the facility is online. So, India may end up trading cheap land, subsidised power and scarce water for minuscule data-centre capacity and negligible job-creation. Operation Sindoor made drones the next military obsession. Now, as India prepares to spend $2 billion on them, a 2022 ban has spawned an ecosystem of smugglers, shell companies, and procurement loopholes
The first battlefield for drone warfare was where anti-Maoist operations turned DJI into the surveillance drone of choice years before Operation Sindoor.
