Good Morning Ishan,
Inox Clean Energy has been learning to handle big numbers in the two years it’s been around.
When it filed its draft papers for a Rs 10,000 crore initial public offering (IPO) last week, the clean-power producer and solar-panel maker was set to be as big a share sale as the IPO of state-owned giant NTPC’s renewables arm in 2024.
But the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi), the market regulator, swiftly put Inox Clean’s IPO plan on hold to investigate the company’s prior transactions with other entities under its parent, the InoxGFL Group.
Since June 2025, Inox Clean Energy has made 11 acquisitions, sending its revenue skyrocketing to Rs 2,000 crore in FY26 from just Rs 47 crore the year before. The IPO also comes just six weeks after the company acquired Blackrock-owned renewable-energy platform, Vena Energy, for Rs 6,000 crore in its largest-ever purchase.
“As a group, we do not like debt,” Devansh Jain, Inox Clean Energy’s current promoter, had said in a 2016 interview, five years before InoxGFL had broken away from the century-old “chemicals-to-celluloid” Inox Group. “Second, we are focused on hard profitability. We like to build businesses that are [the top number] one or two in the sector.”
But the intervening decade seems to have softened Jain’s stance on debt-fuelled, heavily loss-making growth. Inox Clean Energy has racked up nearly Rs 16,000 crore in outstanding borrowings, up from Rs 350 crore before its buying spree.
