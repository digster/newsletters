India is running out of gas. Why is Adani Total Gas running up?

The Strait of Hormuz is nearly shut due to the war between US-Israel and Iran. Gas imports into India have been squeezed, with the cascade effect felt by city gas distributors. Yet, Adani Total Gas’s stock was on a tear in a couple of recent trading sessions, shooting up almost 30%. No woe for Novo?

Novo is losing India’s weight-loss market to Eli Lilly. How does it redeem itself?

As Novo prepares to lose market exclusivity in India, it sets its sights on the healthcare system, partnering with Healthifyme, buying hospitals, and investing in biotech

Come 21 March, pharma multinational Novo Nordisk will lose exclusivity to sell its blockbuster weight-loss drug in India. As it anticipates cheaper generics to flood the market, Nordisk is pushing for partnerships to keep its edge. Separately, the group’s investment arm, Novo Holdings, is on a hospital-buying spree; quite in contrast to its global pattern of tapping firms associated with life sciences only. Turns out, the Indian hospital market is a one-of-a-kind investment. [Read the full story]

AI just broke brands’ Google ad strategy. The answer is the ad

As the way people search evolves, companies like Flipkart, Mamaearth, and The Whole Truth must go from getting clicks to being the answer—which is both harder and more expensive

With AI increasingly becoming the go-to platform for queries, Google Ads are no longer making sense for companies in general and D2C brands in particular. The visibility game has now moved from SEO to SEO+AEO+GEO. Not only are keywords getting replaced by personalised queries and prompts, but the medium is also changing from text to voice AI and images. Amid multiple startups shutting down due to poor online visibility plus poor demand, brands are now focusing on building AI visibility. Unlike Google Search that features hundreds of links for every keyword, AI just offers the top three or four options, rendering the remaining brands practically invisible. [Read the full story]

Jio, Airtel’s fight with airports has a third winner

As airports, malls, and office towers build their own 5G networks, telecom operators are losing control of indoor connectivity

5G networks are transferring the control of indoor connectivity from telecom companies to landlords. The latter is increasingly handing shared infra tenders to neutral-host firms like Ibus and Cloudextel for multiple telcos to plug into. While the government sympathises with telcos on one hand and bats for their “right of way”, it’s also backing these neutral hosts. The legal ground beneath neutral hosts is unstable, though. Telcos are directly contesting their ability to run active infrastructure. [Read the full story]

IBM, Infosys, and Wipro entered Kochi. Only one emerged unscathed

IBM’s focus on AI-related products shows that tier-2 cities can go from low-cost delivery hubs to global software frontiers

Tech companies across India are expanding into tier-2 cities like Kochi. In Kochi’s Infopark, Infosys and Wipro run large delivery centres built on the traditional outsourcing model.
