JSW One perhaps wanted no confusion around the kind of company it wants to be seen as. What began as JSW Retail in 2018 became JSW One Platforms two years later. Last week, it filed to go public in a Rs 3,000 crore share sale. 

The last word in its name is essential to the initial public offering. “Platform” is so irresistible to an increasing number of companies that it features in their one-line self-description if not in their name. 

What The Economist said https://www.economist.com/business/2016/05/21/the-emporium-strikes-back a decade ago now seems to be a gross understatement. 

“Interesting thesis, but don’t use the word ‘platform’ in the title. No one knows what it means.” That was the reaction of a professor at MIT in 2000 upon reading the dissertation of Annabelle Gawer, now co-director of the Centre for Digital Economy at the University of Surrey. She ignored the advice and kept the title.

If anyone were to counsel Ms Gawer against using the word today, it would be for the opposite reason: overuse. Academic papers on the concept are now legion. Books are multiplying, too … A rapidly growing number of incumbent firms, too, are striving to build platforms. Yet the professor’s problem remains pertinent: confusion still reigns over what exactly platforms are. And this, combined with the hype, hides the fact that they are not for everyone.

Platforms are different kinds of marketplaces (Amazon, Uber, Zomato, Instagram, iOS), where users beget users of the same kind, as in social media, or those that want to sell them a product or service, as in e-commerce. Each of these platforms has a company that acts as a gatekeeper and makes money off commissions or advertisements. JSW One, in which billionaire Sajjan Jindal’s JSW Steel owns almost two-thirds, operates a marketplace for building materials, and could hence qualify as a platform.
