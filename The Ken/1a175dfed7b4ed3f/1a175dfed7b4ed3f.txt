On 8 September, Gurugram-based Rays of Belief, the company that runs the Mom’s Belief chain of neurodevelopmental care clinics, listed on the bourses as a “for-profit social enterprise”. The company is micro-cap, with a market capitalisation of nearly Rs 600 crore and just over Rs 80 crore in revenue in FY26. Given the share size of recent initial public offerings (IPO) in healthcare—Manipal Hospitals went public https://the-ken.com/story/ranjan-pais-grand-unified-ipo-theory-for-manipal-hospitals-small-is-boring/ in August for over Rs 9,000 crore—it’s easy for this to go unnoticed.

Founded in 2017, Mom’s Belief runs 136 centres in 57 cities, offering therapy programs for autism, ADHD, cerebral palsy, and other neurodevelopmental disorders (NDD). By number of centres, it is India’s largest such chain, according to Careedge’s research report on neuro-developmental disorders cited in its red herring prerospectus (RHP). 

But most of its money no longer comes from those centres. In June 2025, the parent bought an allergy, asthma, and immunology practice in Virginia, US, with three clinics in Salem, Lynchburg, and Roanoke. In FY26, the US business brought in about two-thirds of its revenue. Revenue more than doubled from about Rs 36 crore the year before. Profit fell slightly from Rs 5.9 crore to about Rs 5 crore. 

Why would India’s biggest autism-care chain need allergy clinics in America? Because NDD therapy in India is very hard to make money from.
