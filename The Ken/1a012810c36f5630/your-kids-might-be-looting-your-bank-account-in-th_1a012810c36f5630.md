Arush, my neighbour’s 12-year-old, doesn’t own a phone or even a credit card. He has two working parents, doting grandparents, and an abiding love for mobile games.
His parents’ phones are mostly off-limits. His grandparents’ phones aren’t.
That’s how he plays Free Fire Max, Battlegrounds Mobile India (BGMI), Call of Duty: Mobile, Efootball, Clash Royale—you name it, he has probably played it. The games range from battle royales and football simulations to shooters and strategy games, each with its own in-game economies designed to keep players spending.
And his favourite part across these games? Loot boxes. Also called lucky chests, they are in-game mystery boxes that let players spend real or virtual currency for a chance at skins, outfits, weapons, and other virtual “rewards.”
“Loot boxes are a big deal,” he said, telling me about the little rush of opening one, “like a Pokémon card.”
So I asked him the obvious question: how do you pay for all this?
“I know Nani’s (grandmother’s) UPI pin,” Arush said, parroting it back to me.
This, it turns out, is not especially unusual.
