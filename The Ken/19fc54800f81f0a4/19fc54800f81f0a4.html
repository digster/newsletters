In late 2017, Satsure almost didn’t apply for the Andhra Pradesh Agtech Summit. Founder Prateep Basu was in Switzerland chasing customers when he learnt there were only a few hours left before applications closed. He filed one anyway. A few weeks later, the Bengaluru-based space-tech startup walked away with a Rs 5 crore purchase order from the Andhra Pradesh government.

The money mattered as much as the credibility.

Until then, Satsure had been stuck in a well-known trap for deep-tech startups. Insurance companies wanted proof that governments trusted satellite-based crop assessments. Governments wanted proof that insurers did. The order broke the deadlock, opening the doors to insurers, banks, and eventually some of India’s largest private lenders.

India’s space story usually gets told through launch vehicles and moon missions, whether it’s Vikram 1 or Mangalyaan. But Satsure’s rise hinges on something else.  It’s a bellwether of the larger commercial opportunity, which lies not in launching more satellites, but in owning the Earth Observation stack. This includes everything from sensors to analytics that help governments, banks, and utilities make everyday decisions. 

Years later, ICICI, HDFC, and Kotak Mahindra are Satsure’s customers and also investors.
