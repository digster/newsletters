Good Morning Ishan,
There’s a shift underway in how farm advice is being built, and who controls it. Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are using AI to influence what gets grown and how, raising concerns that the “food system” itself is being nudged by algorithms. At the same time, those tools don’t always travel well. Models trained on Western farms can fail badly in different soils and climates, making “universal” AI advice far less reliable than advertised.
So you end up in an odd place. On one side, AI-driven farm advisory is becoming powerful enough to shape decisions at scale. On the other, it is increasingly seen as something that should be open, local, and not entirely in corporate hands. Which is what makes India’s agritech story feel both familiar and a little precarious.
Agritech startups here, in fashion for a while a couple of years back, have essentially been trying to solve a simple problem: helping farmers decide what to do on their fields. Increasingly, that advice has been powered by AI. There was just one hitch. Farmers don’t really pay for advice.
So companies like Dehaat and Plantix had a workaround. Advice became a gateway to commerce. A diagnosis led to a product recommendation, and a recommendation led to a sale on a platform. It worked. Dehaat clocked roughly Rs 3,000 crore in revenue. But that model may be running into a new kind of competition.
Across India, governments are building their own AI-powered advisory systems. The Centre is rolling out Bharatvistaar. Maharashtra has Mahavistaar. And in Bihar, an app called Bihar Krishi is stitching together soil data, weather feeds, mandi prices, and satellite imagery to offer farmers tailored guidance. This advice doesn’t come with a marketplace attached.
That also means, the layer agritechs built their businesses on could start to thin out. But agriculture is not the same as UPI and fintech. Data is complex and trust is hard to earn, especially from a chatbot. Inderpal dug into Bihar’s experiment and what it says about the future of AI in Indian agriculture.
