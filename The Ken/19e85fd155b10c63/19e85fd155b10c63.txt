Last week went well for the investors of Varun Beverages, Pepsico’s largest bottler in the country. After all, the stock had jumped to a 12-month high of Rs 545 a piece on May 25, a big jump after hitting a 12-month low in March this year. 

It’s rare for stocks to react so much when no results or acquisitions are in the news.  

What drove up the price was a seemingly  “regular” update it issued to the stock exchanges on 21 May. In the release, Varun Beverages said it had extended its partnership with the American beverage giant for another ten years until 30 April, 2049.

There was more to it. Varun Beverages also said https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachHis/d3d68843-f503-42b1-b22d-6d0e30c058b2.pdf:

“The earlier EBA [exclusive bottling appointment and trademark license agreement] restricted Varun Beverages  from carrying out any activity other than to act as an SPV [special purpose vehicle]  for PepsiCo business, now this requirement is deleted in the revised EBA.”

This meant that restrictions imposed on the bottler by the brand to function only as a bottler for them were now “deleted”.  

So as things stand now, it can launch its own brands in India and capitalise on the distribution might it has gained from the 34-year-long partnership with Pepsico. It could potentially also expand its services as a bottler to other brands (just as it has been doing in international markets).

But if it’s all good, why has the stock corrected from the 25 May highs? It’s down over 3.5% as of Monday’s close.
