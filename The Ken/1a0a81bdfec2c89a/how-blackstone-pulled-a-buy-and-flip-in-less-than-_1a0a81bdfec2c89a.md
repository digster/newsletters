Good Morning Ishan,
Across all the businesses Blackstone has ushered into India’s public markets as majority owner, three of every four rupees of fresh money raised from the public ends up retiring the very debt the private-equity firm put on their books.
This is against a market baseline where Indian IPOs, on average, use 29% of fresh capital for debt repayment. Blackstone-backed listings routinely push that figure to around 80–90%. This template resurfaced in June, when The Ken [examined](https://the-ken.com/story/blackstones-next-big-ipo-looks-built-more-for-its-own-exit-than-the-publics-entry/) Horizon Industrial Parks, which Blackstone had assembled, in record time, into India’s largest collection of warehouses.
Again, debt is what paid for both the scale and the speed. So much so that at the time of filing its prospectus, interest payments on Horizon’s Rs 6,900-crore debt swallowed up close to 80% of its total revenue. The company is now on the bourses, and the public market will retire the borrowings on its books.
Now, Blackstone is back with another IPO. This time, it’s bringing a US-based healthcare-services firm, AGS Health, to the Indian public in its second-largest domestic stake sale to date. The Rs 4,800 crore offering combines a Rs 1,800 crore fresh issue with a Rs 3,000 crore offer for sale by Blackstone.
The deal appears to push private equity’s affection for scale and speed to the limit…
