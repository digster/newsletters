Good Morning Ishan,
May was an unusually big month for the AI industry.
Here’s some of what happened:
1. Anthropic said it’s [on track](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/20/anthropic-revenue-explosive-growth-ipo-profitable-quarter.html) for its first-ever profitable quarter, with $10.9 billion revenue for Q2. A big piece of this was Claude Code enterprise deployments alongside contracts to create agents for PwC and JPMorgan. But part of that is presumably because of tokenmaxxing, which may [not](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/harder-justify-uber-coo-says-225800850.html?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAKDsQO_YpX46jQVbD7Yp36LKFP_HHUQgROZPwTfl-w5zHT6RXd9F598KHRHb4UEErP76spYr42GmQ6gR28ZCy019ZGo6kn6FcP_UZPcgFfR6bzZJqeCdVFfHEbAe_zpyPSQlYappgubL1rXZy2nyqBdNtLi800DIUxjBHihtKG8x) [persist](https://www.theverge.com/tech/930447/microsoft-claude-code-discontinued-notepad). And there’s also the nagging part of SpaceX conveniently offering [discounts](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm?ref=wheresyoured.at) in May and June for compute.
2. OpenAI reportedly [filed](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/20/openai-ipo-filing.html) a confidential IPO prospectus with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. It’s aiming to go public as early as September with a valuation above $1 trillion.
3. Anthropic [bought](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/18/anthropic-has-acquired-the-dev-tools-startup-used-by-openai-google-and-cloudflare/) Stainless, which makes SDK/MCP tooling. The deal itself was not huge, but it’s a manifestation of some changes in how AI majors move.
4. Google Deepmind [absorbed](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/google-deepmind-hires-staff-contextual-ai-licensing-deal-bloomberg-news-reports-2026-05-19/) the Contextual AI team in what it frames as an $80–90 million licensing deal, a relatively small exchange in the grand scheme of things.
Zoom out a bit, and there are a couple of ways to read this. The first is that we’ve reached the beginning of the end of the AI industry’s venture-capital era and are entering its public-markets era. That’s exciting if you’re an early investor, but for now, not so meaningful for everyone else.
Another read is that there is consolidation taking place, but it is buried beneath deal structures that are designed to look very much not like consolidation. The two paths will converge if you keep reading…

Hi! This is Vidhatri, the producer of Zero Shot.
For as long as I can remember, one version of success around me primarily looked like this: join an engineering college, get recruited by an IT-services company, get trained, work for clients abroad, maybe even go to the US for one of these projects, and settle down.
And for years, this path paid off, changing the lives of many families. But with AI existentially cracking this model, all eyes are on how IT-services companies will reinvent themselves.
The recent news about HCLTech’s $150 million bet on Sarvam is one answer to that major question. As per reports, no VC led this round. But HCLTech, one of India’s largest IT-services firms, is looking to write the biggest check in Sarvam’s $300 million raise, which valued the company at $1.5 billion. This is a 7X markup on Sarvam’s last fundraise from December 2023.
But what is HCL buying and what is Sarvam selling? What does this signal about the IT-services industry?
On this week’s episode of Zero Shot, host Rohin Dharmakumar debated these questions with Abhishek Pathak, the lead IT services and internet analyst from Motilal Oswal, and Kashyap Kompella, the founder of RPA2AI Research, an advisory firm focused on AI governance, risks, due diligence, and policy.
