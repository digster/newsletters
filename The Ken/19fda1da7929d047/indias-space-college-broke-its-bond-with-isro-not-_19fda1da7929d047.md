Good Morning Ishan,
Gaurav Seth, founder and CEO of Piersight, thought he had found an effective way to recruit from the Indian Institute of Space Sciences and Technology (IIST).
Students at IIST have to undertake a six-month industry project as part of their final semester, and Seth offered them the opportunity to develop synthetic aperture radar systems for maritime surveillance at his company. They’d be given a monthly stipend of Rs 25,000, along with the possibility of pre-placement offers for strong performers.
Between December 2025 and June 2026, six students completed their industry project at Piersight. By the end of their stint, the company was ready to hire all six.
None of them joined.
The reason had little to do with Piersight and everything to do with the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Turns out, the space agency released its recruitment offers in April and May. All six students who interned at Piersight received an offer, and every one of them accepted.
“It completely screwed up our plans,” said Seth…
