If a Ministry of Railways employee opens her inbox today, everything looks the same.

Her email address still ends in the usual gov (dot) in. The login page still sports the credentials of the government’s technology partner, National Informatics Centre (NIC). Even the helpdesk number hasn’t changed.

But what’s decidedly different is who manages the government’s email stack.

By 1 April, 1.7 million of the 3.4 million email accounts NIC managed had been moved to the servers of Zoho Corporation, a 30-year-old technology company specialising in cloud-based business software. This was announced in Parliament by Jitin Prasada, the Minister of State for the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (Meity). The remaining accounts will likely transition to Zoho by mid-2027, The Ken has learnt.

The government is spending around Rs 180 crore for the migration. But for Zoho, which posted a profit of around Rs 3,200 crore on a revenue of Rs 12,300 crore in FY25, that amount may as well be loose change. What it gets instead is far more valuable.

…

And as the government trades in a 40-year-long relationship with NIC’s open-source software for a seven-year one with Zoho’s proprietary stack, it may be replacing one dependency with another.
