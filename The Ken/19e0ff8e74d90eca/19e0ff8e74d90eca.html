TVS Capital spent 20 years learning what it’s good at. It wants to undo it now

After staying conservative for 20 years, this homegrown PE firm now wants to be an institutionalised one, with bigger bets and a wider portfolio. Where does that leave its legacy?

TVS Capital is trying to move from a conservative, family-backed investor into a larger institutional private-equity platform. Its fourth fund marks a strategic shift into larger cheques, larger ownership stakes, and expansion beyond financial services into wealth management, enterprise tech, and manufacturing. The firm’s first fund struggled because it was too scattered, while its second and third funds performed better after TVS narrowed its focus. Gopal Srinivasan remains central to TVS Capital’s identity, even as the firm is trying to build a platform that can outlast its original leadership. [Read the full story]

Jio Blackrock and Zerodha took the same path. Only one stayed on it

Even though direct plans have jumped from nothing to half the mutual-fund industry’s AUM, Jio Blackrock’s dependence on active schemes needs something regular—a distributor’s push

In April, Jio Blackrock Asset Management reversed course and signalled its plan to engage distributors to sell its mutual-fund schemes. That leaves Zerodha Fund House as the only asset management company in the direct-only ring. On the face of it, Jio Blackrock and Zerodha seem similar. Dig deeper, and they are chalk and cheese. From the kind of schemes to the type of investor, there’s much that lets Zerodha stay the course while forcing Jio Blackrock to change tack and bend to distributors. [Read the full story]

Why did India give BSNL $33 billion but not a full-time boss?

The cash-infusion has enabled the public-sector telecom company to improve its financials, but not, so far, its market position. The lack of a full-time leader isn’t helping

BSNL’s ongoing attempt at a turnaround has become a cash-guzzling exercise, thanks to multiple taxpayer-funded revival packages. The revival looks real in some aspects but it is being overseen by an interim leadership appointed over two years ago. Given that major decisions, like on 5G rollout, are still pending, the ad-hoc nature of the PSU’s leadership is turning out to be a problem. Government-imposed operational constraints on leadership and business expansion put the state-run telco in an awkward position. It doesn’t yet have a lane or a leader. [Read the full story]
