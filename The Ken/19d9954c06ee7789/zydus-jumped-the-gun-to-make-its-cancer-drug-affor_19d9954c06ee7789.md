Good Morning Ishan,
In India, a six-month regimen of Keytruda, a breakthrough cancer drug made by Merck, costs 80 months of a school teacher’s average salary. For the vast majority of patients in the country, that’s well, nigh unaffordable.
The hospitals and pharmacies they get the drug from, though, buy it for far less.
That discrepancy tells the story of how opaque cancer-drug pricing is in India. And it was put into stark relief earlier this year when Zydus launched Tishtha, a biosimilar for the blockbuster cancer medication Nivolumab. This led to a patent-infringement claim from the original drugmaker, Bristol Myers Squibb, but the Supreme Court said the drug was too critical and too costly to persist under patent protection, which was expiring on 2 May.
Before Zydus launched Tishtha in January, Bristol Myers Squibb’s Opdivo was the world’s only Nivolumab. It costs upwards of Rs 1 lakh a unit. Tishtha’s MRP is about a quarter of that. To a patient in India, it should actually sell for less than one-tenth the cost of Opdivo.
Why doesn’t it?
