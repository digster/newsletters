Double trouble

New-age venture capital would rather manufacture founders than find them

From Entrepreneurs First’s co-founder speed dates to The Foundery’s idea vault, a new wave of venture studios believe that plug-and-play founders are safer for their portfolios

The venture studio model is in vogue. Instead of backing founders who arrive with their own ideas, studios build companies in-house to cut early-stage risk. The formats vary widely, from EF’s 8% stake to The Foundery’s 75%. In each case, the aim is to de-risk the portfolio by turning company-building into a systematic, repeatable process. The model rewards speed, which pulls studios towards businesses that can be built and validated fast, such as AI products for the US market. But speed can cost what organic company-building allows time for, like real chemistry between co-founders. Some studios have dropped the outside founder altogether, and hire executives to run the companies they create. Studios pitch themselves as an asset class in their own right, but investor appetite in India is yet to catch up. [Read full story here]

‘Winner’ to patsy: what the radical insurance reforms could mean for the patient

Neither cutting broker commissions nor mandating patient co-pay is a sure cure for increasing medical costs. But that’s about the only choice right now

The health insurance sector has resumed the “passing the parcel” of rising medical costs. Last year it was hospitals and insurers battling it out over premiums and cashless services. India needs about 2 million caregivers. The govt has trained fewer than 40,000

Senior-care companies offer hospital-level pay, food, and housing, yet can’t find enough caregivers. Without a training pipeline, the silver economy has no one to staff it

India currently has around 150 million people above 60, a number that is increasing rapidly. This has attracted investments in the senior-care sector. While the capital is flowing in to build the infrastructure, there is a huge gap in the caregiving workforce needed to sustain it. The government and businesses alike are trying to scale the skilled workforce, but are met with challenges like high attrition and preference for hospital-based practices. What will it take to attract people to pick a career in geriatric caregiving? [Read the full story]
