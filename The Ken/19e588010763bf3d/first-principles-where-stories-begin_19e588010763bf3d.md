Do you like reading children’s books?
Now, these come in a range. To keep it not too limited but also not too broad, let’s say ‘children’s books’ here means books for kids up to six years old.
Toddlers and pre-schoolers are the main audience, so think colours and simple words. Occasionally, a little value lesson here and there.
Goodnight, Moon is a popular choice in Bengaluru. I (Tanim) have heard many new parents talk about it. Maybe it holds up across the rest of the country too. But aside from the crowd favourite, what would you recommend? You can share it [here](https://theken.typeform.com/FPBooks?typeform-source=the-ken.com).
Maybe it’s something you read to your kids, or to someone else’s. Or maybe you just love picture books because they are very cute and make a great alternative to adult coffee table books.
I see them as something I admire from afar, but once in a while, something catches my eye, and then the price baffles me.
Colours are costly in India. This is one of the few times I have realised that. The other times are when I need to get something printed in colour, and it is five or ten times the price of a simple black-and-white printout.
I have read a lot about how picture books are important for a child’s healthy development. I can’t justify that with my own experience yet. But with so many Youtube and Ipad babies out there, we need “book baby” back in business.
Eventually, no matter what we do in the 21st century, we will end up watching screens for hours. So protecting and encouraging non-screen hours, along with no tobacco, no social media (some parents even go for no sugar and no inorganic food) until a certain age, is a luxury when you can afford it.
But beyond luxury, there’s also the question of how you present these restrictions to children so that they don’t see it as ‘forbidden fruit’.
That’s one of the trickiest parts.
Just as we are learning to balance screens and paper, a new layer has arrived. One that we aren’t fully ready for.
If you haven’t guessed it, yes, I mean AI.
Udemy has courses for people to learn how to make picture books with AI. On the other hand, so many posts call AI-written children’s books “soulless”.
As I read back, I realise this has become a cautionary tale in picture books, and there’s no colour in this post except for red.

Vishnu here, with the movie/TV shows section for this week.
A couple of days, I tagged along with Rohin and PGK on their post-lunch coffee walk to Plume, in Indiranagar.
On the way, the discussion somehow turned to recent Malayalam movies (no surprises there) and then to Mohanlal (again, to be expected in a conversation with me).
As always, I started passionately talking about his recent movies, his slump after Odiyan (2018), and how Lijo Jose Pellissery’s Malaikottai Vaaliban (2024) is the biggest ‘what if?’ story in recent Malayalam movie history.
(Special note: There’s an excellent video essay on the movie that I urge you to watch. I will link it [here.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p1Mq5aYTZnY))
Then, as a parallel, PGK spoke about how he loved LJP’s Nanpakal Nerathu Mayakkam (2023) and Mammootty’s performance.
My forever collector-of-random-trivia head could not help but add that the movie was, in fact, inspired by a 40-second [advertisement](https://youtu.be/B7EvtzB0V9k?si=NmtXYwOTdSzHyRO_) made for Greenply!
It was written by Rupesh Kashyap for Lowe Lintas and directed by Prakash Varma for Nirvana Films.
His thanks note in the opening credits of the movie (translated to English): “To the old advertisement that gave the seed of thought.”
Pretty impressive how he managed to distil an idea from a plywood advertisement into a full-length, award-winning movie!
Which got me to thinking about any other instances of this happening, i.e., a non-movie origin of a character or thread that led to the creation of an independent movie or TV show later on.
So here are some that came to my mind:
Ted Lasso (2020–Present)
The now-popular show about the American college football coach hired to manage an English football team had roots in a [promotional skit](https://youtu.be/6KeG_i8CWE8?si=M8Nf3UW3quR8xti5) that Jason Sudeikis acted in for NBC in 2013.
Upon being suggested by his partner, he then reworked and expanded the character in 2015 into a more sympathetic portrayal, and the first season was commissioned by Apple TV in 2019.
What a journey—barbeque sauce!
MacGruber (2010)
Probably one of my favourite movies in the ‘smart-dumb’ genre is this relentlessly funny 2010 movie.
It’s basically based on the recurring SNL skit series of the same name, which in turn is a spoof of MacGyver, a mid-80s to early ‘90s action television show:
| MacGruber has had 32 distinct sketches on Saturday Night Live, btw! |
It’s an extremely quotable movie with a lot of ‘illogical’, plain stupid plot points and characters, but it does a very good job of keeping you entertained.
No wonder Michael Scott is a fan:

Borat (2006, 2020)
Speaking of stupid, hilarious characters, another one of my favourites—Borat Sagdiyev!
Its creator, Sacha Baron Cohen, wrote the first version of the character for a short-lived youth chat show series in the UK. It was then called Alexi Krickler.
But later on, Sacha brought it back for his Da Ali G Show. Renaming him Borat, he was now a Kazakh television journalist.
Borat went on to achieve cult status with his 2006 standalone movie:
So much so that he got a sequel in 2020 that was released on Prime Video.

Seven Psychopaths (2012) – Black Comedy/Crime/Action
An alcoholic writer with an unfinished script. An eccentric duo of a struggling actor and a devoutly religious dog-kidnapper who collects rewards for their safe return. They end up getting entangled after they kidnap a ruthless gangster’s prized Shih Tzu.
Yup, it’s as random and silly as it sounds. But trust me, it’s an experience and a half. Immaculate writing (the writer and director made Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri and The Banshees of Inisherin), jokes that land hard, solid acting, and editing that keeps you hooked.
I mean, if you still need a reason to watch it, just look at that cast!
