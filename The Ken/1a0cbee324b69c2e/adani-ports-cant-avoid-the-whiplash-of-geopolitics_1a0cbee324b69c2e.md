Some of the wildest swings in the share price of Adani Ports and Special Economic Zone in the past couple of years have been in response to, or in anticipation of, developments that weren’t of its own making.
US President Donald Trump’s trade salvos, the Iran war, and the flip-flops in both have led to visceral reactions among Adani Ports investors. You can hardly blame them. More than [half](https://www.economist.com/interactive/briefing/2026/03/27/the-nightmare-scenario-for-global-trade) the global trade by value is seaborne. By volume, the share rises to 85%. And Adani Ports is no bit player in this business.
In fact, at Rs 4.1 lakh crore, Adani Ports’ market capitalisation is higher than that of any other port operator in the world. The company is also India’s largest private logistics company by volume. Over a quarter of India’s cargo passes through its 15 domestic ports. (It also operates four overseas, including one in Israel.)
In June, Adani Ports [said](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-adani-ports-quarterly-profit-rises-cargo-volumes-grow-2026-04-30/) its profits could only grow 9–14% in FY27, thanks to America’s tariffs and the war in Iran, compared with 20% last fiscal. Last week, Jefferies, a broker, said Adani Ports’ cargo volume will grow at a compounded annual rate of 13% until 2030, while the company’s target is 16%.
There’s little a country like India, much less a company like Adani Ports, can do when the gulf between “free” and “trade” is only widening.
