Good Morning Ishan,
Economists have a term for situations where the usual rules seem to break: Giffen goods. When the prices of some products go up, people buy more.
Markets occasionally have their own version of this, albeit for different reasons. A company’s fundamentals worsen, the environment around it deteriorates, and yet, the stock goes up.
Which brings us to Adani Total Gas.
The past week has been rough for global energy markets. The war in West Asia has disrupted flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial artery for India’s LNG imports. That should worry companies downstream, especially city gas distributors supplying to vehicles, households, and industries.
Yet… on 11 March, Adani Total Gas’s share price jumped nearly 20%. The next day, it climbed another 7%, even as the broader market continued to slide. The rally has now pushed the stock above where it traded before the conflict escalated.
The good news is that the government has stepped in with emergency rules to prioritise gas supply for households, transport, and fertiliser plants. The less-good news: even if those measures work perfectly, city gas distributors are still likely staring at lower volumes and higher costs.
Anand’s [story today](https://the-ken.com/story/india-is-running-out-of-gas-why-is-adani-total-gas-running-up/?utm_source=daily_story&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=daily_newsletter) digs into this puzzling burst of investor enthusiasm. Hint: it may have less to do with gas supply and more with short covering and momentum trades.
