Good Morning Ishan,
Find out before you get really sick, then start treatment or lifestyle changes so you don’t end up with a serious medical condition. That’s the pitch of Cent, a startup founded by Shashank ND (of digital-healthcare platform Practo), Arpit Garg, and Anshul Khandelwal.
As you’d expect in 2026, the process involves artificial intelligence. Cent uses an orchestration layer that aggregates data from Siemens medical equipment and software modules for MRI, CT, and Dexa scans. For Rs 20,000–30,000, it provides results but doesn’t offer any diagnoses or treatment plans.
The question, then, is what follows the costly service offered by Cent. If general practitioners lack the incentive to treat patients who aren’t suffering from serious medical issues—as my colleagues say is the norm in India—then a systemic gap exists for patients who use services like Cent’s.
Sudeshna maps out this problem in the broader medical-care landscape in today’s [story](https://the-ken.com/story/why-the-man-who-built-practo-to-find-doctors-is-now-using-ai-to-find-disease-first/?utm_source=daily_story&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=daily_newsletter). If you or anyone close to you has ever wondered how to stay healthy, it’s worth a read.
