If there is one geography that has defined both the ambition and anxiety of telecom czar Sunil Mittal’s career, it is Africa.

Back in 2010, when Bharti Airtel bought Zain Africa’s assets in a $ 10.7 billion deal, Mittal was deeply bullish on the continent’s potential. He had said https://www.financialexpress.com/india-news/africa-buy-is-sound-says-mittal/584577/ Africa offered “more potential than other emerging markets”. 

By 2017, it “was one of [his] biggest regrets https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/entry-into-africa-one-of-my-biggest-regrets/articleshow/62091626.cms”. 

And on every analyst call, there was really only one thing people wanted to talk about: the other guy, Jio. 

But on Airtel’s latest earnings call, as Mittal laid out his succession plan, the mood around Africa felt completely different.

He described Africa as “probably where India was 10–20 years ago” and said he wanted “more income flowing back to the mothership”. 

The comments came alongside a Rs 28,200-crore no-cash share swap between Bharti Airtel and promoter group entity Indian Continent Investment Limited (ICIL), which will raise the over 30-year-old company’s stake in Airtel Africa from 62.73% to almost 80%. 

The chairs are changing too. The African counterpart recently named https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/boards-policy-regulation/airtel-africa-names-gopal-vittal-chair-succeed-mittal-2026-03-25/ Gopal Vittal as chairman, the man who turned the parent company around during the Reliance Jio disruption. 

What is different now than in 2017?
