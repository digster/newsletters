Good Morning Ishan,
Shravan Kumar Vishwakarma’s first job was driving a tempo between Kanpur and Unnao in Uttar Pradesh. He later moved into cement trading, steel rebaring, and sand mining, before returning to transport, this time as an owner. The high-school dropout who once earned about Rs 200 a day now runs a fleet of 400 vehicles.
At 35, he wants to add an airline to that fleet.
Vishwakarma’s Shankh Air plans to start with three Airbus A320s on wet leases, with two A321s to follow, according to two executives working with the company. It has spent the past two years preparing for takeoff.
In March 2024, Shankh announced plans to make Noida International Airport its primary hub. Two months later, it opened a counter at Lucknow airport. By February 2025, its ground-operations team began meeting airport officials and preparing to handle its own check-in, baggage handling, and ramp services.
“There was just one thing missing,” as an executive put it, “an airline to operate them.”
Shankh Air is hardly alone. Al Hind Air, Air Kerala, Spirit Air, and Flyexpress are among a new crop of budget carriers whose launch dates have proved similarly elastic.
The government, on the other hand, has been making the bet on regional aviation bigger.
