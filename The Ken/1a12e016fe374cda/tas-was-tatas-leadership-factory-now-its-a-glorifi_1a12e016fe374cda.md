Good Morning Ishan,
Among the many things that the chairmanship feud at Tata Sons is surfacing, contentious succession planning is the most glaring.
In September, Tata Sons incumbent chairman N Chandrasekaran was reappointed, with Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata being the sole opposer. This followed months of internal tensions. Now Tata Trusts—a 66% shareholder of Tata Sons—has labelled the reappointment unlawful, with the group’s apex leadership stuck in a stalemate.
Ironically, this has coincided with the 70th anniversary of Tata Administrative Services (TAS), the group’s general-management programme inspired by the Indian Administrative Services to internally groom future leaders of the Tata group. TAS was meant to bridge succession gaps just like these by investing in India’s “finest brains”, including people such as Tata Son’s Harish Bhat and Titan’s Ajoy Chawla.
But over the years, TAS’s relevance—and place in the group—has diminished.
As the Tata group has expanded into semiconductors, electronics, EVs, and e-commerce, TAS has been unable to keep pace with the expertise this demands. Instead, it has been reduced to one of several entry points into the group, at times finding it difficult to get group companies to return its calls. For instance, earlier this year when a TAS officer wanted to explore openings at Air India, he claimed the group company’s HR completely ignored TAS’s enquiries…
