Imagine taking a loan for a home renovation because you know it’ll add to your apartment’s value—but a long list of expenses already awaits. You just bought a car and haven’t finished paying for it, while a long family vacation is due next month, even though nobody at home asked for one. And your salary hasn’t grown in two years. The kitchen will probably be worth the investment, maybe even increase the value of the house, but can your bank account handle it?
Cyient’s acquisition of US-based Tao Digital Solutions, its largest so far, is that very renovation, costing the company a little over $200 million.
The company will pay 60% upfront, partly from cash on its balance sheet and a larger chunk through debt, though a large portion of that debt will be serviced by Tao’s free cash flows. The remaining 40% is performance-based, meaning the transaction is structured to maintain margin discipline at Tao post-acquisition.
The Hyderabad-based engineering R&D company, which operates across aerospace, defence, rail, utilities, and telecom, has been on a degrowth-to-flattish trajectory for two years. Between FY24 and FY26, its consolidated revenue barely moved—from Rs 7,147 crore to Rs 7,268 crore, a growth of less than 2% over two full years.
Margins tell a starker story: Ebitda fell from about 18% to 12% in the same period. That explains why the management has stopped giving hard guidance in recent quarters. Its consolidated PAT has also declined—from Rs 703 crore in FY24 to Rs 463 crore in the last fiscal.
The market has registered this slowdown, too. On the NSE, its share price has declined by over 55% in the last two years.
This $200 million bet doesn’t exist in a vacuum. Its previous arrangements make this recent deal all the more burdensome on its wallet.
