Roughly a month ago, I opened Anthropic’s Claude AI tool on my work laptop and was greeted with an invitation to use its newest state-of-the-art (SOTA) model: Claude Fable.

And so, I did. I’d been reading Anthropic’s bombastic PR about Fable’s “all-powerful” capabilities for weeks and was eager to put it to the test. I ran a few tasks with it, then went about my day.

The next day, when I opened Claude, my sessions from the previous day were still tied to Fable. But the moment I typed additional queries, the model “degraded” to Opus 4.8, the older model.

I had been bucketed and categorised as a “risky foreign customer” who could not be trusted with the powers of Fable. The Donald Trump-led US administration had ordered Fable to be withheld from all non-US citizens till Anthropic convinced his government that it had modified its AI models enough to offer a weaker, less-capable version of Fable to all users and companies that weren’t American.

It would be 18 days till Anthropic was allowed to re-release Fable to global customers. I presume they convinced the US government of whatever they had done to differentiate Fable’s capabilities and responses between US and foreign users.

Over the past week and a half, I’ve been sent multiple emails and alerts from Anthropic, urging me to use Fable while it was still bundled in our team plan, before it would be charged separately.

I haven’t bothered to use Fable even once.

I no longer feel the need to. In fact, I don’t want to. I’m satisfied with Opus 4.8.

Something has changed in the last month.

I still use Claude. I even like it. But I’m not sure if I love it anymore. And I certainly don’t trust it.

My Anthropic fever has broken.

Hi everyone! This is Vidhatri, the producer of Zero Shot.

A week ago, our host Praveen, technical producer and resident sound genius Rajiv, and I drove down to Koramangala for a special recording, armed with all our equipment.

We did our security checks, got visitor tags, and entered the Razorpay office to meet CEO Harshil Mathur. He sat down with us for an hour-long fascinating chat on what he thinks is the next frontier after UPI.

Razorpay is the infrastructure on which payments run and Harshil is now betting on agentic commerce.

In the last eight months alone, the company has built a UPI-OpenAI-Razorpay pilot for ordering groceries in chat, launched agentic payments across Zomato, Swiggy, and Zepto, rolled out a UPI Reserve Pay flow that practically kills the PIN, and built an agent studio on Claude for merchants to automate disputes, recoveries, and reconciliation.

As this future unfolds, we wanted to ask Harshil some questions. What does agentic commerce actually look like end to end? Can India make a mark here like it did with UPI? Can the habits of consumers actually shift? If they do, who loses and who wins? Does this genuinely level the playing field for small businesses, or does it just entrench the platforms that already have scale? And what does any of this mean for the tens of millions of people who haven’t touched e-commerce yet?

Praveen and Brady unpacked this future layer by layer with the leader who is taking the biggest swings in the agentic commerce space.
