Good Morning Ishan,
Can India pull off another UPI with agentic commerce? Smooth, stunning, successful, and at scale—something much of the world didn’t consider possible or worthwhile.
Well, Razorpay is wagering on it. Here’s what Harshil Mathur, chief executive of the payment solutions provider, proclaimed at the company’s annual meet a week back: “I’ll bet my money on this: India will do the same with agentic commerce.” My colleagues Mutasim and Valli write that the room erupted in applause at Razorpay’s AI-powered agentic-commerce showcase “like it had just witnessed a magic trick.”
After all, two spoken sentences were all it took for the AI agent to order and pay for grub. No checkout page, UPI pin, or payment app. And if Razorpay is to be believed, agents are now only weeks away from going mainstream in commerce.
The proof of this pudding, of course, will be in the eating—and not just food. Use cases can be far more complex than ordering chai and samosa.
But if this brave new world truly manifests, many entrenched entities have much to lose and the pecking order could be upended. Expect them to push back hard, similar to Amazon targeting Perplexity in the US. A lot is at stake in the battle for eyeballs. Besides, can agents really make the right choice, or even a real choice for consumers? Even if they can, will users bite? Old habits die hard, as OpenAI found out the hard way. And in this fast-shifting paradigm, can regulators ensure the consumer remains king?
Mutasim and Valli break down all this and more in today’s solid [Friday read](https://the-ken.com/story/what-does-razorpay-know-about-agentic-ai-that-openai-amazon-ebay-dont/?utm_source=daily_story&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=daily_newsletter). Let’s dive in.
