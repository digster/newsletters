Trai vs Truecaller is a fight over 0.2% of a problem neither has solved. It’s a turf war over 74 million calls, out of an unsolved 41-billion-call problem

By Sumit Chakraborty https://the-ken.com/writers/sumit_368/

I need you to sit with a number for a second, because reports covering this story are skipping past it to get to the fun part.

Indians received roughly 42 billion spam calls in 2025. That makes it nearly 30 unwanted calls per subscriber per year in a country with over a billion phones. Then, a survey found that 77% of Indian mobile users get three or more of these calls every single day. Niche annoyance but not a new annoyance.

This is a functioning shadow economy that has been harassing an entire country for two decades, and every institution nominally responsible for stopping it has, at some point, failed to.

Now here is what the telecom regulator, or Trai, and Truecaller, the Swedish call-screening company, are actually screaming at each other about this week: whether an app is allowed to slap a spam label on calls from two specific number series. 1400 for telemarketing, 1600 for transactional messages. Which, by Truecaller CEO’s own numbers, generate about 525,000 manually blocked calls a day between them. Seventy-four million over eight months. 

Now, compare that to the 42 billion figure above. Not even a rounding error (as AI-written Linkedin posts will tell you). It would be the digit after the rounding error. 

Trai’s own enforcement record this year (731,000 notices to unregistered telemarketers, Rs 150 crore in fines against telcos) is bigger than this dispute by orders of magnitude, and none of that enforcement has anything to do with the fight happening on social media right now.

So why is this the fight? Why, out of a menace 800X its size, did Trai and Truecaller pick this exact hill? Simple. It is a turf war over who gets to be the trust layer on top of Indian phone calls, and I need you to understand that both parties involved have spent four straight years failing at this job before either of them decided to fight about who is failing worse.

Start with Trai, because Trai’s record here is a bit embarrassing and nobody’s saying so out loud. Back in 2022, we reported https://the-ken.com/story/spam-gushes-through-the-cracks-in-trais-do-not-disturb-dam/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection that the National Do Not Call Registry—Trai’s original fix, launched almost 15 years earlier—had failed so completely that it spawned its own parasitic industry: data brokers selling subscriber contact details straight to the telemarketers the registry existed to stop. 

Trai’s answer to that failure was to roll out a blockchain-based anti-spam framework in 2018, marketed as a silver bullet against what was then a Rs 6,000 crore spam industry. We checked back four years from the diktat, and there was no headway. This is the same regulator now telling the country its next big idea, CNAP, or the Calling Name Presentation service, is the trustworthy one.

And the anti-spam failures aren’t even the worst of Trai’s résumé.  https://the-ken.com/story/trai-try-again-indias-toothless-telecom-regulator-fights-for-more-powers/

We reported https://the-ken.com/story/trai-try-again-indias-toothless-telecom-regulator-fights-for-more-powers/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection in 2021 that five different chairpersons, across two decades, had asked the government for the powers a regulator is supposed to already have. The ability to fine the telcos it polices, control of its own budget, and a say in its own hiring. It got none of it. Three of its five leadership seats were empty when we wrote that, one of them since 2018, despite a law requiring it to be filled within three months. This is the regulator now demanding legal authority over a brand-new category, caller-ID apps, after 20 years of not being able to secure authority over the industry it already runs. 

And the one time Trai did flex the power it has, we found https://the-ken.com/story/trai-telecom-five-year-legacy/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection in 2020 it wasn’t neutral about it: Airtel and Vodafone Idea got blocked from launching premium services the moment Jio complained, while Jio’s own nine-month stretch of free pricing sailed through, competitors’ CCI complaint and all. Trust-us-to-referee-this-fairly is a weird thing for this regulator to be saying with a straight face.

Truecaller, unsurprisingly, spent that entire stretch eating Trai’s lunch. In 2022, we covered https://the-ken.com/story/truecaller-may-lose-india-connect-if-trais-national-caller-id-has-its-way/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection the exact moment DoT told Trai to go build a national KYC-linked caller ID system, explicitly as a response to Truecaller’s dominance. A company that had just IPO’d on Nasdaq Stockholm, doubled its revenue while turning a profit and was sourcing 70% of its global user base from a country with, and I want to say this plainly, no privacy law stopping any of it.

Trai said the fix was coming “in a few months”. Two years later, in 2024, we checked https://the-ken.com/story/truecaller-beat-trai-to-the-punch-with-spam-call-fix/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection in again: Trai still hadn’t shipped anything, and Truecaller had already launched a paid “verified” business badge, lifting call pick-up rates by nearly 30%, monetising the exact problem the government promised, in writing, to solve for free. 

Our own headline at the time put it as simply as possible: “Why is Truecaller protecting you from spam calls instead of Trai? https://the-ken.com/podcasts/daybreak/why-is-truecaller-protecting-you-from-spam-calls-instead-of-trai/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection” 

And go back even further. In 2021, we were already writing https://the-ken.com/bfo/indians-are-getting-fewer-spam-calls-but-they-still-cant-get-enough-of-truecaller/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection that Indians had learned to lean on a foreign-owned private app with, in our own words, “a questionable track record on privacy” to do a sovereign regulator’s job. Ironically, that’s Trai’s entire justification for this week’s crackdown.

Airtel, for what it’s worth, looked at both of these institutions failing in public and decided to build its own thing instead of picking a side. In March 2025, we covered https://the-ken.com/newsletters/two-by-two/airtel-fights-spammers-and-truecallers-business-model/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection its free, AI-driven, network-level spam tagging system, which had already flagged more than 8 billion calls within months.  https://the-ken.com/the-nutgraf/airtel-finds-the-gap-between-truecaller-and-trai/

A companion piece https://the-ken.com/the-nutgraf/airtel-finds-the-gap-between-truecaller-and-trai/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection the same week nailed the actual problem: every single one of these systems, including Truecaller’s crowdsourcing, Airtel’s proprietary black box, Trai’s coming KYC system, is run by a different party with a different commercial motive, and not one of them answers to the person actually receiving the call. We polled five Airtel users live on air. Two trusted the network’s judgment.

And Truecaller is not the innocent party in this story either, which is presumably why Trai feels like it’s allowed to swing now. In 2019, we reported https://the-ken.com/story/whats-bugging-truecaller/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection on a bug that auto-enrolled users into UPI payments without their consent, using the exact SMS-read permissions the app needs to catch spam callers in the first place.  https://the-ken.com/kaching/if-your-bank-transaction-alerts-are-coming-through-truecaller-you-have-a-problem/

Five years after that, in 2024, we found https://the-ken.com/kaching/if-your-bank-transaction-alerts-are-coming-through-truecaller-you-have-a-problem/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection bank transaction alerts landing exclusively in Truecaller’s business-chat inbox, even for users who had reset their default SMS app—meaning a bank had, at some point, decided a third-party spam-blocking app was a fine place to route financial alerts.

Here’s the part that should actually worry you, and it’s the part Trai is hoping nobody connects to its own reporting on the subject. CNAP’s entire sales pitch is that a name pulled from a telecom KYC database is inherently more trustworthy than a name crowdsourced from users. That is the stated justification for stripping Truecaller’s labels off 1400 and 1600.

But we’ve already reported https://the-ken.com/podcasts/daybreak/what-a-cyber-criminal-told-us-about-kyc-frauds/?utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=collection, separately, that KYC-linked scams account for more than 70% of all fraudulent banking transfers in India, costing roughly Rs 900 crore a year, and that a source inside India’s own Financial Intelligence Unit told us fraudsters specifically exploit the public’s blind trust in KYC as a stamp of legitimacy. 

Trai is about to put that exact, already-compromised trust signal on every incoming call screen in the country, as the default, government-endorsed answer, at the exact moment it’s telling everyone that crowdsourced labels are the untrustworthy option. This is the same regulator whose last two big anti-spam ideas, including a do-not-call list and a blockchain framework, both failed in ways we documented in real time. 

There is no reason, on the current evidence, to assume the third one just works because it finally shipped.
