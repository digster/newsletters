India built a Rs 50,000 crore MSME fund to fight VCs’ tech bias. It still became a tech backer

Of the 750 companies the fund has invested in, seven out of 10 are tech-heavy—defying the fund’s intention of backing traditional manufacturers

SRI Fund has invested upwards of Rs 16,000 crore in 750 MSMEs so far, but with a heavy tech bias. This is in contrast to its original mandate of funding companies that VCs usually refrain from investing in. Nearly 70% of SRI-backed companies operate in technology-linked sectors, while only about 30% are engaged in manufacturing. The fund may have exposed a deeper problem: traditional MSMEs often need patient capital, while venture funds are built around a small number of outsized winners. [Read full story here]

Tesla and Figure AI are building robots to act like humans. Indian workers are teaching them how

…for a few hundred rupees. It may be a Faustian bargain, once again

India is becoming a supplier of one of physical AI’s scarcest resources: human behaviour. Unlike chatbots, robots cannot learn from the internet alone and need vast amounts of real-world data on how humans move, grip, navigate and interact with objects. A new gig economy is emerging, paying workers to turn everyday chores into training data for physical AI systems. Workers generating this data are rarely told who buys it or what it helps build, raising questions about consent, privacy and whether India is once again supplying labour while others capture the value.
