Running a restaurant group is a nightmare. What happens when you have a dozen investors for each eatery? A Bengaluru-based F&B investment platform wants to find out

There is no winning formula in the food-and-beverage (F&B) industry, so to say.

Even as global quick-service and casual dining chains throng to India, the struggle of successfully running a dining spot in the country has affected establishments big and small, restaurants and bars. Shilpa Shetty-backed Bastian had to shut its Bandra location and pivot its format, while Farzi Cafe by Massive Restaurants had to revamp itself.

But Bengaluru-based Investorant, an F&B investment platform, wants to change that. 

“The earlier model involved an operator and investor,” said Amrit Hemdev, one of its co-founders, introducing the company’s fractional ownership model. “The way we define it is that there’s an operator, an investor, and separate talent which only focuses on building a team, mixing up drinks, or taking care of the menu.”

For every bar and restaurant it starts, Investorant raises money from 10–12 investors, hires the right talent, gives them the reins and a couple of months of runway to set it all up and become functional. Then they do this all over again. 

The issue with the traditional model was also that before, the operator and the investor were at odds with each other, looking out for themselves. So there was usually a clash of expectations.

Take Bar Spirit Forward, where Hemdev, Guru Shivaram, and Salman Sait sat down with The Ken. Their first-ever establishment set up in late 2023, the 50-seater cocktail-forward bar sits on Lavelle Road, one of Bengaluru’s upscale commercial hubs. The founders raised Rs 2.8 crore from 10 investors, onboarded bar consultant Arijit Bose, set it up with Japanese technology, and let the staff take care of the rest.

But operating a traditional cocktail bar in India isn’t as glamorous as this sounds. It involves navigating a complex, compliance-heavy environment, balancing exorbitant state-specific liquor license fees, while ensuring high volume within the set strict operating hours.

“You could do everything right, but one day have no access to gas or a road goes into construction right outside blocking the way,” said CEO Shivaram. “If there’s one space opening, there are ten shutting down.”

Apart from Bar Spirit Forward, Investorant has six other concept-driven establishments. Bistro Cameo, Bar Cameo, Bunker Room, Project Grain, and Una Hacienda are all in Bengaluru, while Whisky Samba, their only outpost, is in Hyderabad. 

Their overall FY26 revenue was nearly Rs 40 crore, and to date, the overall money invested in the restaurants is Rs 18 crore.

“When we pitch our bars [to investors], we tell them that this is a risky investment. It’s unsecured. There’s no guarantee of success,” said Sait, who is the designated finance head. “Treat it the way you would any alternative investment.”

Shivaram added that F&B has an 80% failure rate. And it is this failure rate that Investorant’s entire model is designed and built to reduce. But how does it ensure not ending up being a failure itself?

Getting in the spirit

At Bar Spirit Forward, a classic cocktail takes four to seven minutes to prepare, while one from their separate seasonal drink menu could require around 20 minutes. 

Unlike fast-food chains that run on fixed menus and standardised operations, conceptual bars and dining experiences depend on larger kitchens and longer service times. There is also a lesser scope for expansion.

“When it’s run well and performs well, F&B is a great cash flow business. The bars make money month-on-month, and you don’t typically put money back into it,” said Sait. “A good concept has a decent runway where if it goes on for three to five years and you can make a reasonable amount of return.”

But what happens when it doesn’t or if investors want an exit?

Investorant assumes that the life of a bar is four to five years and its model is built around the framework that its investors should start seeing money similar to their invested capital in 24–30 months.

“People like to invest in F&B. You could say it’s a vanity project of sorts. But some are genuine enthusiasts too”

Salman Sait, co-founder, Investorant

But Investorant’s bars are also not bigger than about 50–60 seats, which makes the margins tighter. 

“Our team costs and rent are higher because we are in a central location. So 15% margin is typical,” said Sait. “We try to optimise and get to a little more here and there, depending on various other factors.”

A liquor license is itself a huge cost in Bengaluru, with each costing upwards of Rs 4.5 crore. Added to it is rent and its deposit, which can run into multiple lakhs. 

Investorant, however, also works on a revenue share model with its landlords, which means they share a decent upside with the owner and have a minimum guarantee. This hybrid agreement allows the company to pay a fixed base amount to the landlord while also giving them a share in their earnings.

“If you would like us to put a finger on what we are trying to solve, it’s problems [in the industry] at every level,” said Shivaram. This includes real estate, awareness of the business, and the right product to build at the right time.

“What’s your drink of choice?”

A buffet of different investors for each project can become a classic case of too many cooks in the same broth. It can do the opposite of what the platform set out to do. 

“We say no a lot,” said Shivaram, on accepting certain investor profiles. “Everybody has Rs 25–30 lakh to invest. It’s not a big amount, but the first thing we look for is whether they understand what we are trying to do.”

They shouldn’t question why we have no paneer on the menu, Sait added. 

They also move quickly.

Bistro Cameo, their fourth bar, situated on top of the Museum of Art and Photography in Bengaluru, had to be constructed without shutting the museum down. After the deck and investor presentation last September, the fundraising, which brought in nearly Rs 3 crore, closed in a week. And the rooftop bar and dining room started operations in February. 

Before accepting any investment, the co-founders send a concept note that needs to be bought into by all investors. 

“We are very careful about the profile of the people coming in because we have access to funds. That’s not an issue. We care about who we do business with”

Amrit Hemdev, co-founder, Investorant

In the case of Bar Spirit Forward, the investors only got to see the final establishment a day before it was set to open. “Otherwise it leads to added stress, in the midst of all the legwork we are already doing,” Sait said.

Investorant’s Rs 18 crore funding across seven establishments with about 160 employees is still too small to revolutionise the F&B business. The model needs to work with more bars and across multiple cities before it can be deemed a success.

But that doesn’t mean the founders are afraid to try.

This entire venture was an opportunistic gamble on their part, said Sait, because F&B is itself that way. 

After Bar Spirit Forward, Investorant didn’t open anything for two years and then opened three bars in 2025. It depends on getting the right space and partner, whether the market is ready for a concept like that, and then hitting it at the right time.

“The idea is to organically grow to a certain level, where we are making a decent amount of money that gets sustained in the long run,” said Sait. “We always want to be operator-focused in the sense that we want to manage and control our operations as much as possible.” 

Which is why they are not looking at external capital in the form of institutional investment. The founders have also been asking their Bar Spirit Forward investors if they can buy back some of the shares, but the answer has been no.

“It is just a byproduct of what we’ve been doing for a long time,” said Hemdev. “We’ve been at this since 2008.”

Hemdev and Shivaram were the duo behind Black Rabbit, a now-shuttered bar in Indiranagar due to business transitions and the changing real estate pressures. This, along with other reasons, made them realise the limitations of a plain operator-investor model.

Investorant was the answer they came up with after seeing those failures.

But as Sait added, “Our advice always is that if you have a crore to invest in F&B, invest 25 lakhs into four projects. Because you or we don’t know what can be successful.”
