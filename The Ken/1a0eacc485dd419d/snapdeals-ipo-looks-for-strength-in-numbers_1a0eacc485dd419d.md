Rohit Bansal and Kunal Bahl finally have something to celebrate.
Since its failed attempt in 2021, their co-founded company, Snapdeal Ltd, now renamed Acevector, has completed its IPO mission by offering almost the same content in a bigger pack. Like a new packet of chips.
On the second day of the much-anticipated mission yesterday, Acevector’s Rs 420 crore IPO was fully subscribed. The issue closes today (29 September) and lists on 5 October.
By a short-term account, it looks like a success. But as I read the 575-page RHP carefully, it’s not much different from the 2021 one. It’s just packaged differently to make it more saleable. And they have done it.
Snapdeal also faces other business-model concerns. While its “free delivery in 3–5 days” may be a USP for its users, it is a liability for the company.
