If anyone can pull this off, it’s Dilip Shanghvi.

That is the refrain across Indian pharma since 27 April, when Sun Pharmaceutical announced its $11.75 billion all-cash acquisition of Organon, the largest outbound deal by any Indian drugmaker ever.

It shows rare ambition for a $6.9 billion-revenue company to buy a $6.2 billion target. The scope is rarer still. In a single stroke, Sun will now rank in the global top three in women’s health, be among the top 1o biosimilars players, and enter pharma’s global top 25.

“It’s a fantastic deal. There are so many good things, but it’s also seen as very risky,” said the healthcare-practice head of a global consulting firm.

Some of those risks became apparent three days later when Organon reported Q1 2026 results. Revenue from women’s health, the strategic pillar of the transaction, fell 19% year-on-year, excluding the impact of foreign currency (ex-FX, as the company reports). Sales of its market-leading product, Nexplanon, plunged 28%.

But this wasn’t a one-off bad quarter.

…

On the post-deal investor call, founder and chairman Shanghvi cited Ranbaxy, Sun’s last transformational acquisition, as the comparison. But industry watchers know that the Ranbaxy integration was complex, even messy, and Sun came through.

Even then, for every parallel between the two deals, there is a sharper difference.
