Zepto’s ‘generational wealth’ narrative has started to crumble. A deferred IPO now pushes the momentum of confidence about the company in the other direction

On Thursday, when news broke about Zepto’s decision to pause its IPO, I read a report about the behind-the-scenes build-up about the company that had a specific detail which I found interesting.

Three weeks ago, one of Dalal Street’s most respected investors gathered a select group of portfolio management service (PMS) and alternative investment fund (AIF) managers for an unusual presentation.

The pitch wasn’t about quarterly earnings, valuation multiples or the next market cycle. It was about what he believed could become one of India’s biggest wealth-creation stories over the next decade.

Quick commerce, he argued, represented one of the largest value migration opportunities Indian business has ever seen. Kirana stores would steadily give way to dark stores. And among all the contenders, Zepto was one of the best placed to capture that migration.

It wasn’t merely a theoretical argument. He had already backed that conviction with capital, participating in Zepto’s funding rounds at both the $5 billion and $7 billion valuations.

Around the room, there was broad agreement on the opportunity. 

There was far less agreement on the vehicle.

Who Blinks First? Inside Zepto’s High-Stakes IPO Gamble, Moneycontrol https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/ipo/who-blinks-first-inside-zepto-s-high-stakes-ipo-gamble-13988552.html

Instantly, I couldn’t help but recall a story my colleague Suprita Anupam wrote 15 months ago, when Zepto was at its strongest position yet, about a similar pitch the company was making to prospective employees. 

“Would you like to create generational wealth?”

That was the pitch a Zepto HR manager made to a vice-president (VP)-level candidate during a recruitment call in 2024. “Think Rs 50 crore to Rs 100 crore in just four years,” the HR added, with the tone of someone offering a once-in-a-lifetime lottery ticket. The candidate, who declined to be named for professional reasons, didn’t even show up for the interview. But the audacity of the pitch speaks volumes about the quick-commerce darling’s ambitions.

Zepto, which promises to deliver groceries in the blink of an eye, wants to make millionaires out of its employees.

Zepto to recruits from rivals: We give you ‘generational wealth’, you give us long hours, The Ken https://the-ken.com/story/zepto-to-recruits-from-rivals-we-give-you-generational-wealth-you-give-us-long-hours/

For me, these stories come in the same continuum. 

Last week, Zepto’s investors were pitching wealth to other mutual fund managers and investors. Last year, Zepto was selling a story of generational wealth during hiring. The year before that, Zepto’s founders were making loud claims https://www.livemint.com/companies/zepto-likely-to-become-bigger-than-d-mart-in-18-24-months-ceo-aadit-palicha-on-growth-plan-11720316606408.html of how they could become bigger than Dmart. At the time, while discussing its plans to go public, Zepto’s co-founder said https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/zepto-ceo-sees-clear-path-to-ipo-in-2025-with-profitability-in-focus-124120200661_1.html, “In the next financial year, we will be PAT [profit-after-tax] positive. We are quite confident, and that hopefully will be a positive signal to the markets.”

Zepto closed the next financial year with an adjusted Ebitda loss of Rs 5,040 crore, larger than the year before. 

The market remembered. 

I don’t think most people have grasped the real implications of Zepto deciding to put its IPO on pause. For years, Zepto has told one story—about its potential: for growth, for profitability, for making money for everyone from investors to employees. And to be fair, Zepto executed at a blistering pace. But the story always ran on momentum, on a sense of inevitability. And the reason why Zepto told that story again and again, with bluster, is because the company always knew the truth: there was exactly one destination. 

For Zepto, going public wasn’t a milestone. It was the endgame. 

And it came within inches of pulling it off. 

Now, Zepto and everyone in and around its massive orbit, from shareholders, vendors, delivery partners, and employees, will acutely feel the gravitational pull of the same momentum that propelled it up. And the way down, like the way up, will be driven by narrative. Except it’ll be a counter-narrative this time, and perhaps just as irrational.

The confidence narrative has two sides: thus far, Zepto has been climbing ladders, and now it’s going to encounter snakes one after another.
