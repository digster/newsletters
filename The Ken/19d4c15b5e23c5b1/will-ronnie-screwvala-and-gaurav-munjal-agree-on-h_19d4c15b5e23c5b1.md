Good Morning Ishan,
Ronnie Screwvala loves asking people to rate things.
“How would you rate this course on a scale of one to 10?” is a staple at meetings at Upgrad, his Mumbai-based edtech company. And the answer better not teeter at five, because Screwvala does not believe in midway averages.
“Everything has to either be a two or a nine,” said a former executive at Upgrad. “If it’s a two, you better back it up. If it’s a nine, you have to explain why it’s not a 10.”
This intolerance for the middle ground extends beyond internal reviews. It shaped Screwvala’s engagement with Gaurav Munjal, the chief executive of Unacademy, when the two met last September to discuss a potential acquisition.
Upgrad, whose main focus is on upskilling, wanted to acquire Munjal’s test-prep company. But the road to get there wasn’t the smoothest. The acquisition was called off just five months later, in January, over valuation differences.
Now, two months since then, Screwvala and Munjal are back at the negotiating table and have signed a term sheet for an all-stock transaction, complete with a break fee. With the Competition Commission of India in the mix, they are looking to seal the deal by July. Munjal will continue to lead Unacademy.
The outcome marks a reversal of fortunes. At the height of the pandemic-era edtech boom in 2021, Unacademy commanded a valuation of $3.4 billion—more than double that of Upgrad at the time. Now, it is set to be folded into its smaller rival.
