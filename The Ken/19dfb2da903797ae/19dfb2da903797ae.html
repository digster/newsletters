Gopal Srinivasan doesn’t have many idle days.

As the chief executive and founder of Chennai-based private-equity firm TVS Capital Funds, his week is split into two halves. Mondays and Tuesdays are fixed for investment committees, deal decisions, and portfolio reviews. The rest of the week is less orderly, involving travel and meetings.

In late April alone, Srinivasan hopped from potential client meetings in Mumbai to a discussion with the government on growth capital for the country.

This is how he likes it. Stillness, to him, is useless. “If I’m not moving around, meeting people, and getting their thoughts and ideas, then I’m wasting time,” he told The Ken over a phone call.

By any standards, it seems like an exciting way to run a firm. But not for Srinivasan—so much so that he’s “banned” the word from both his personal vocabulary and TVS Capital’s office. “We are never excited. Enthusiastic, but not excited.”

It’s an ironic rule. Especially for a two-decade-old firm at an “inflection point”, as an employee termed it.

In March, the company made its first investment in wealth management, putting Rs 500 crore into Mumbai-based Neo Group. The family-backed, sector-agnostic investor, which once wrote Rs 50–60 crore cheques, is now getting bigger and bolder.
