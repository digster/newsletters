On 3 August, the old 30-minute volume-weighted average price (VWAP) method was retired and replaced with the closing auction session (CAS) for stocks with derivatives—futures and options (F&O). The intent was impeccable.

The old one calculated the official closing price across continuous trades between 3:00 p.m. and 3:30 p.m., which still allowed players with high capital to nudge the final settlement number simply by driving heavy buying or selling right into the closing bell. So it had to go.

In its place, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi) introduced a dedicated 20-minute batch auction running from 3:15 p.m. to 3:35 p.m., bringing the Indian markets in line with global exchanges like the NYSE and LSE, where buyers and sellers converge at the end of the day to find a single, non-distorted equilibrium price.

But the new method was compromised almost immediately.
