It is a busy time for anyone with a skin in copyright law—lawyers, policy experts, government folks, AI developers, and industries ranging from publishing to music labels.
On 15 March, the office of the Principal Scientific Advisor (PSA) entered the copyright debate with a [white paper](https://www.psa.gov.in/CMS/web/sites/default/files/psa_custom_files/WP-Foundation%20Model%20New%20Edited%20version%20%281%29.pdf) that endorsed what the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade ([DPIIT](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/ff266bbeed10c48e3479c941484f3525.pdf)) had proposed in December. Meanwhile, a committee set up by the commerce ministry to shape copyright policy around AI has been working tirelessly. Across the capital, conference halls and policy [roundtables](https://x.com/_DialogueIndia/status/2033428397297381706) are filling up with the same cast of stakeholders, all trying to build a consensus on complex questions surrounding AI and content rights.
The Ken has learned from two sources close to the committee that it will soon begin consultations with the industry before releasing its final recommendations and then going silent. According to these sources, a formal law is unlikely to see the light of day anytime soon. The government is already starting to press the brakes, preferring to wait for the space to mature.
The closer they get to stitching a framework, the more the threads from before come undone.
