Good Morning Ishan,
SBI Funds Management is fresh off a blockbuster IPO.
The country’s largest asset manager, which lists on the bourses today, saw investors bid 41X for its Rs 9,800 crore ($1 billion) offer-for-sale issue earlier in July. If the grey-market punters are right, the stock should debut at Rs 670, about a 17% premium over its IPO price.
That’s a great exit point for SBI Funds’ backers—the State Bank of India and Amundi, Europe’s largest asset manager. But for investors looking to buy the listed stock, the euphoria may not last long.
For one, asset management is a business where “big” doesn’t always mean “better”. SBI Funds manages assets worth Rs 29 lakh crore, more than double those of ICICI Prudential AMC, the next-largest asset manager. Yet, the latter generates more revenue and profits. That explains why SBI Funds was valued lower than many of its peers.
But then of late, ICICI Prudential AMC, the better of the two franchises, hasn’t been having a good run itself. And when the industry leader struggles, others are expected to feel the heat sooner rather than later.

Rohin started interviewing founders for First Principles, starting with Kabeer Biswas of Dunzo in August 2022. Over the years, he’s spoken with several CEOs, chairpersons, and operators, but no venture capitalists. Kanwaljit Singh of Fireside Ventures is his first exception, because the most interesting thing about him, as Rohin writes, is the bet he made with the fund itself.
“I have six or seven people with fifteen to twenty years of experience, so I’m investing my own resources in a team like that. Then there’s the culture, which is an overhead. From a traditional VC’s point of view, the legends are individual glories,” Singh tells Rohin. “Here, we have multiple people touching a company, so there can’t be a sense of ‘this is my deal, this is your deal’. It is culturally a complex deviation from the typical model.”
