Bain Capital bet Rs 4,000 crore on gold. The RBI just walked all over it

The regulator’s new rules that came into effect on 1 April undercut the country’s largest private-equity bet on gold-lending to date

Bain Capital is completing a Rs 4,385 crore bet on Manappuram Finance just as new RBI regulations threaten to reshape gold-lending economics for NBFCs. Manappuram’s gold-loan book grew 51% in three quarters, but its customer base stayed flat. The expansion was driven largely by rising gold prices, not new borrowers. The new RBI rules affect the bullet repayment loans that form a big chunk of NBFCs’ gold-loan AUM, and might end up squeezing their margins. Banks are rapidly gaining market share, new NBFCs are flooding in with 3,000 branches planned, and gold itself has corrected 20% from its January high. [Read full story here]

A royal family’s billion-dollar bet on Indian startups—without a winner

VC firm Lightrock used the Liechtenstein dynasty’s fortune to prop up Waycool, Pharmeasy, and Dunzo. Then it slid into irrelevance

Lightrock deployed nearly $1 billion in India across around 40 growth-stage bets in just a few years. It doubled down on existing investments more than most peers, allocating a larger share of capital to follow-ons. The firm scaled aggressively during the zero-interest-rate era, when deploying large sums was both easy and expected. As the cycle turned, it slowed sharply—shifting focus from investing to managing its existing portfolio [Read the full story]

India has two sovereign-AI programmes. One is official, the other isn’t

Neysa took on a $600 million debt to build sovereign AI even as 22% of India’s chips are lying unused, and the market is running into Big Tech’s arms

IndiaAI Mission’s sovereign infrastructure is running at 22% capacity. Neysa, the country’s newest unicorn, raised $600 million each from Blackstone and debt financing to build sovereign compute ahead of enough demand. The enterprises the compute was built for are already on hyperscaler platforms such as Azure, AWS, and GCP, thanks to VCs bundling credits with funding. Now, hyperscalers, too, are marketing their own compute infrastructure as “sovereign”. [Read full story here]

Google, ITC Hotels, Titan have a newfound love for their alumni

It’s not just to do with their search for talent

Corporate alumni networks have always played second fiddle to university ones, often overlooked and taken for granted. But companies have lately been realising their potential—for hiring, skill building, and industry influence. In an effort to formalise these networks, companies are stepping up to engage former employees with perks.
