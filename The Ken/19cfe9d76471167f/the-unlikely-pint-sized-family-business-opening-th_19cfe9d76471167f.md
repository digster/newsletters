Long and Short
Wed, 18 Mar 26
The story behind the numbers and motivations driving India's stock market.
Good Morning Ishan,
After pushing its IPO from 6 March to 16 March, XED has gone ahead and listed on the NSE IX despite the ongoing crisis in the Middle East that has seen major players like Phonepe pause their IPOs.
This means the first IPO from Gift city, India’s first international financial services centre in Gujarat, is a small company, unlike what one might have expected from a debut listing.
When finance minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced in 2024 that companies can now directly list in Gift city, she likely had very specific companies in mind.
Startups in the “sunrise and technology sector” would now have access to global capital, she said. These are typically high-growth, venture-capital-funded technology firms.
This Monday, two years after Sitharaman’s announcement, Gift city saw its first IPO. But it’s not a VC-funded company.
The first step was taken by a home-grown family business known as XED Executive Development, an executive education provider with both B2B and B2C verticals. Established in 2015, the company has raised only $5 million so far and has no institutional investors on board. Nearly 82% of the stakes are held by founder John Kalleli and Meenu John, with the rest held by individual shareholders, including CFO Piyush Agrawal.
This could paint a picture of what’s to come for India’s bet in international finance.
