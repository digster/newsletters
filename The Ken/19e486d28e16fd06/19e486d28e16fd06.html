In the years Apoorv Garg worked at Tesla, he tanked up on Elon Musk’s carbon creed. To the extent that when he moved back to India five years ago, he couldn’t shake off the belief that “carbon and energy will drive future progress”.

The oil shock induced by the war in West Asia may have sent governments scrambling to provision for fossil-fuel alternatives, but Garg and his co-founder, Yugal Raj Jain, are emerging from their biomass bet more prepared than ever.

India produces the same amount, roughly 1,000 million tonnes, of biomass and coal every year, but the former has a much lower calorific value (heat or energy released when burned) than the latter.

…

At the four-year-old AltM, Garg and his team seem to have cracked the science and engineering of using agri waste to make high-value materials. Made from cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin in the biomass (rice husk and sugarcane bagasse for now), these compounds provide a replacement for petroleum-derived chemicals in personal-care products.

But just a few years ago, the founders wondered why on earth nobody was doing so.

They asked that question to Harshad Velankar, too, who joined them as head of R&D in mid-2023. Before that, Velankar led the bioprocessing lab at Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd (HPCL) and, one day, fed up with building technologies that never got commercialised, applied for an intern’s role at AltM. “Until a minute before the call, we were pretty sure someone was just trying to have fun. Why would someone with this experience [at Reliance, Praj Industries, and HPCL] apply for this position?” said Jain.
