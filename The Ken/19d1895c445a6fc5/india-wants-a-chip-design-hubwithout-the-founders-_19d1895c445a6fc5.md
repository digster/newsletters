Good Morning Ishan,
Once again, the global chip supply is under threat. The Middle East is a key supplier of some materials used to produce semiconductors, and a protracted war could cut off their supply.
But India’s semiconductor plans have a supply-side problem of a different kind.
The country is all set to announce its Semiconductor Mission 2.0, adding roughly $10 billion to the subsidy kitty. And yet one of the main cogs in the semiconductor value chain, design-linked incentives, is caught in a curious paradox.
Instead of filtering for the best, the scheme deters some of the most promising companies from participating at all.
On the one hand, the very founders it hopes to attract—ones with decades of experience in Silicon Valley and networks and capital—are finding themselves ineligible. On the other hand, companies backed by foreign venture capital are shut out before they even begin.
This is quite unlike India’s successful PLI scheme in electronics, which attracted big brands such as Samsung, Foxconn, and Tata Electronics. Priyal spoke to founders and investors who want to design and tape-out chips from India but are left wringing their hands.
