Every day at 3:20 p.m., India opens its gates to a casino, one that, in a mere 10-minute window, manages to dwarf the largest ones in Las Vegas.
Welcome to the closing auction session (CAS)–a unique setup which, in its short 30-day journey, has already achieved feats that the Indian capital markets couldn’t in their entire history.
There is the roulette table. On 31 August, 60 of India’s largest 210 stocks moved ±3% from their 3:15 price within a 10-minute interval, a supernatural-ish phenomenon without precedent in Indian markets. Investors in TCS, Sunpharma, Divislab, and Asian Paints celebrated jubilantly, while those holding ITC, Tata Motors, Bharti Airtel, and Adani Enterprises went to sleep with a frown.
Then there is the slot machine. On 27 August, from 3:15 p.m. onwards, the Sensex performed a ‘hips don’t lie’ style gyration, dropping 3% from its prevailing price, keeping the pose for five minutes before recovering 2.7% to close just 0.3% below its 3:15 level. In the process, it briefly allowed traders to emulate in 10 minutes what Rakesh Jhunjhunwala’s Titan investment took 10 years to achieve.
The casino also houses a couple of jokers...
Now, CAS is a globally-accepted system, but hardly appropriate for the Indian context. Global acceptance by itself doesn’t warrant implementation. The benefits need to be weighed against the costs.
