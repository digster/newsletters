Few companies know the weight of improbable, outsize success as well as Trent.
In an ideal world, investors would have treated the Tata Group fashion retailer differently on Friday. Trent grew its profits in the June quarter by over a fifth from a year ago, beating analyst expectations.
The growth in profits was no anomaly. In the first three months of 2026, Trent had put on a similar show. But investors were clearly in no mood to celebrate the latest quarterly numbers.
They expected Trent’s revenue to rise at a similar clip as its profits, which it didn’t. What was even less acceptable to them was the anaemic sales growth in stores that existed over a year ago, one of the top metrics for retailers. That number was in the low single digits for two consecutive quarters. In other words, much of Trent’s sales growth came from new stores.
As has been true at every single point in the past five years, Trent’s fast-fashion chain Zudio is the only lens through which investors are willing to look at Trent.
The company added roughly 20 Zudio stores in the three months to June. I remember a time when investors, while cheering on Zudio’s breathless expansion, worried about the impact of Zudio’s sub-Rs 1,000 T-shirts and denim on Trent’s profitability, which had been the best in the industry.
