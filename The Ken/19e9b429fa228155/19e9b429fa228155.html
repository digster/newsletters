“You’re going to hear a giant sucking sound of jobs being pulled out of this country,” said Ross Perot, US Presidential candidate, during the 1992 presidential debate.

He was referring to the impending mass exodus of manufacturing jobs from the US to neighbouring Mexico.

There’s a sucking sound that’s getting louder in India right now. It’s not jobs (yet), but money.

Even as the Indian government beseeches its citizens to avoid buying gold, vacationing abroad, or even using too much oil in their daily cooking to conserve valuable foreign exchange, Indian businesses have been doing something spectacularly different. 

They’re spending more of the same valuable foreign exchange to buy foreign companies. Last year, they spent $18 billion on such acquisitions, an increase of 34% over 2024. 

And this year, that figure has already crossed $15 billion!

Why would India, “a country that requires close to $90 billion in net foreign capital annually to create jobs, build productive capacity, and sustain rapid growth permit $30 billion of capital to flow abroad, thereby contributing to pressure on the rupee?” asks Debashis Basu, the editor of Moneylife. 

Why indeed.

This week on the Zero Shot podcast

Hi everyone! This is Vidhatri, the producer of Zero Shot. Hope you are enjoying your Saturday morning! 

I want to tell you about a recording from last month. Like all Zero Shot discussions, it lasted an hour. The setup, the sound check, and the banter—it was all pretty standard. Yet, something was different. 

Here are some snippets from the conversation to illustrate my point: 

  • “You can break down a word like love into different family resemblances and love changes meaning depending on the context it’s used in.”
  • “There’s a straight line from this to final awareness and consciousness.”
  • “The world is just full of mysteries. We cease to exist when there are no more mysteries.”

You might ask me why we are talking philosophy and asking existential questions on an AI podcast. Sure, at face value, that might be the right question to ask. However, it all makes sense when we look at the guest of our latest episode: Paras Chopra. After wrapping up his stint at Wingify, Chopra took a break. And reflected.

That reflection pushed him down the path of chasing his curiosities in science and setting up Lossfunk, a lab that wants to be a “cosy home for researchers”. Lossfunk is not concerned with products or running the dizzying AI race. They want to write papers. And ask questions. All for research. Research for research’s sake. 

After hearing the conversation, I felt energised and wanted to chase down more stories like this. It made me pause and listen. When you are tracking AI day in and day out, that pause often seems unimaginable. 

Which is why this is among my favourite conversations on Zero Shot. 

The refreshingly quiet and deliberative nature of this episode makes it perfect for your weekend. I’d urge you to listen to it after your run, during a meal, or on a walk when you want something that will make you pause and reflect.
