Good Morning Ishan,
Beauty brands buying other beauty brands is fairly common.
For instance, Estée Lauder recently bought the Indian Ayurvedic brand Forest Essentials last month, and E.l.f Beauty made a blockbuster $1 billion [deal](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/28/elf-beauty-to-acquire-hailey-bieber-brand-rhode-in-1-billion-deal.html) with model Hailey Bieber’s brand, Rhode.
Similarly, large conglomerates scooping up smaller brands is not unusual either. Minimalist’s [acquisition](https://the-ken.com/story/four-year-old-minimalist-does-for-skincare-what-loreal-couldnt/) by Hindustan Unilever comes to mind.
So when an Indian newspaper reported that Nykaa, a beauty retailer, was looking to acquire a brand, that did not really surprise me.
What surprised me was the target brand itself.
On Monday, The Economic Times reported that Nykaa, the country’s largest beauty retailer, is in talks to buy a majority stake in 82°E, a skincare brand founded by actress Deepika Padukone. The catch? The celebrity-backed brand has not taken off at all.
82°E, founded in 2021, falls in the prestige category, with products averaging between Rs 2,000–2,500. Its sales declined by 30% in fiscal year 2025, dropping to [Rs 14.7 crore](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/bollywood/deepika-padukones-82-e-declares-rs-12-cr-loss-2025-katrina-kaif-kay-beauty-to-hit-rs-100-cr-in-revenue-10387092/). In fact, its co-founder Jigar Shah, who also ran Padukone’s family office, left the brand last year to take on a private equity and venture capital role at a wealth management firm.
Separately, Nykaa released its fourth-quarter [update](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachLive/9d4e2828-0139-4a94-b044-6ee7482e2cd8.pdf) yesterday, reporting that its full-year revenue is expected to improve to the “upper end of mid-twenties”, its highest growth in the last three years. The market rewarded the stock, which rose 3% by the close on Monday.
But the question remains: why would Nykaa want to buy a withering brand at all?
Nykaa has long pursued a “house of brands” strategy.
Its portfolio includes Dot & Key (skincare), Earth Rhythm (skin and hair care), Kay Beauty (colour cosmetics), Nykaa Fragrances, and Nykaa Wonderland (body and bath care). Basically covering the entire beauty spectrum.
All of these brands fall in the mass-premium to premium category, with products priced below Rs 1,000 on an average.
However, that’s where Nykaa’s reach ends. It still lacks a brand in any category above premium. 82°E, which nestles between premium and luxury would serve as a good bridge to begin moving into that higher tier. Historically, India has seen few homegrown luxury beauty brands. Forest Essentials or Kama Ayurveda are good examples, but they are not dominant. The bulk of this market is controlled by foreign players like L’oreal or Estée Lauder.
As India’s luxury beauty market becomes noteworthy, it’s only natural that Nykaa wants to grow with it.
What’s also interesting is that Padukone’s nearly five-year-old brand exclusively partnered with [Reliance’s Tira](https://retail.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/health-and-beauty/cosmetics-and-fragrances/deepika-padukones-82e-announces-offline-retail-debut-and-multi-channel-partnership-with-reliance-retails-tira/109313002) two years ago, when it first expanded offline. But today, its products are neither available on Nykaa or Tira.
If this deal goes through, it’s expected to be a “distress sale” for the skincare brand, two analysts who cover internet stocks told The Ken.
“So you have a 2X2 fit. One, the brand is cheap from a valuation perspective, and two, the visibility you get from having a prominent face like Padukone is decent,” added another analyst. “Deepika Padukone is easily among the top three women influencers in India, after all.”
If one looks at Nykaa’s track record, the retailer has struggled on two fronts: fending off competition from [quick-commerce platforms](https://the-ken.com/tradetricks/nykaa-is-doing-all-it-can-to-protect-its-turf/) on one hand, while its fashion vertical has [done little](https://the-ken.com/tradetricks/nykaa-still-cant-get-a-handle-on-fashion/) for the top or bottom line on the other.
But what Nykaa has done well is scale its acquisitions, especially in beauty. For instance, Dot & Key, which it first bought a majority stake in 2021, has seen sales grow by 100% year-on-year, with an annualised GMV (gross merchandise value) of Rs 1,900 crore. Similarly, Kay Beauty, a celebrity beauty brand co-owned by Katrina Kaif and Nykaa, saw a 60% jump in annualised GMV to about Rs 500 crore, the company said in its third-quarter [analyst call](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachHis/132004f5-80ca-47e1-91ec-36190368640f.pdf) in February.
This strategy is what differentiates Nykaa from other retailers like Tira, Sephora, or even Shoppers Stop Beauty.
Between fiscal year 2020 and 2025, Nykaa’s in-house brands scaled about ninefold to over Rs 2,100 crore in GMV. The company plans to scale this to Rs 6,000 crore by fiscal year 2030.
And unlike rival brands such as Mamaearth that are focusing on slower-growth segments like oral care, Nykaa is targeting “faster-growing beauty categories such as skincare, makeup, fragrance, and haircare,” according to a January [report](https://ventura1.com/calltracking/TechnicalCalls/stockideas/FSN%20E-Commerce%20Ventures%20Ltd_639052689482586452.pdf) by Ventura Securities, a brokerage firm.
On Monday, Nykaa also said that top-line growth during FY26 was boosted by the “House of Nykaa” (its in-house brands portfolio) which is scaling well and “contributing meaningfully to overall performance”.
Now with 82°E, high-end skincare seems to be the next high-growth category that the “House of Nykaa” wants to target.
And given the expertise it has gained in scaling its own brands, along with the distribution advantage of its own stores—which, at the latest count, stands at 313, far higher than Shoppers Stop Beauty (79), Sephora (27), and Tira (25)—Nykaa’s strategy seems well thought out.
That way, the deal with Padukone, whom Nykaa signed as a brand ambassador last year, is probably too sweet to refuse, with Nykaa betting that the frog will turn into a prince with its kiss.
