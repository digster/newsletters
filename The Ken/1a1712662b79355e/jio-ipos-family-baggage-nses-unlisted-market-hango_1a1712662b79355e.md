Jio Platforms’ Rs 30,200 crore IPO, India’s biggest so far, romped home in a day. But challenges beckon the giant. A key one is big and familial. More than 70% of Akash Ambani-managed Jio Platforms’ revenue comes from its arrangement with Jio Retail, which his sister Isha Ambani heads. The dependence runs deep, with Reliance Retail handling much of JioPlatforms’ logistics, from distribution to new customer acquisition. The companies have so far worked as family entities, but will their ties and tie-ups weather the public scrutiny a listing brings? India’s digital infrastructure for the physical world is being built city by city, with no common framework
Indian cities have been commissioning digital twin models of themselves—for disaster management, traffic control, and urban planning. So far, these models have largely been used to track down unpaid property tax cases. While there is a national policy for building digital twins, the execution has been left to urban local bodies, which is limiting their use. Genesys, the company behind most of these models, is turning to the private sector as its next big customers. NSE’s listing was so underwhelming that it has shaken faith even in safe-sounding names that are next in line to list, like Zepto and OYO
NSE listed at just above its Rs 1,785 issue price but it was still less than what many retail investors had paid in the unlisted market. Most of the retail shareholders arrived between April and June 2025, when their number jumped from about 34,000 to 150,000. NSE is the latest, and so far biggest, in a series of unlisted market favourites that have let retail down, following Reliance Retail, HDB Financial, Tata Capital, and SBI Funds Management. With NSE gone, the unlisted market has lost its most liquid stock. And its underwhelming listing has cooled the demand for unlisted shares of other major companies. [L&T Tech took a Rs 350 cr haircut for the AI tag. Now it must prove the moat isn’t rented](https://the-ken.com/story/lt-tech-took-a-rs-350-cr-haircut-for-the-ai-tag-now-it-must-prove-the-moat-isnt-rented/?utm_source=weekly_recap&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
The engineering services provider is undertaking a root-and-branch restructuring in a quest to become an AI-led company
After seeing its Ebitda margin slide from just under 20% in FY22 to 16% in FY26, LTTS pivoted to offering AI-centred solutions. CEO Amit Chadha says AI-focused deals now account for over 50% of the company’s projects. To hop onto the AI bus, the company sold its low-margin SWC business at a Rs 350 crore loss. Pivoting to AI, the firm says, has cut project timelines by 3-4X and reduced costs and headcounts by up to 30%.
