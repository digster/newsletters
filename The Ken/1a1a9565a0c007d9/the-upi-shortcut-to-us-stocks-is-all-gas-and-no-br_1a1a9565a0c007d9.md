I met my quintessentially fin-bro friend last week and, as is customary of our weekend meetups, indulged him in a recap of his latest market misadventures.
“Bro!” he began, eyes glinting with the same optimism that forever outshines the bright red of his portfolio, “I bought some Tesla and Nvidia shares!”
“Oh, you mean through Gift City?”
“Nah, man. Takes forever, and they don’t offer leverage.”
“So… how?”
“Through my UPI”
“Through your UPI!!??”
Fair enough, he walked me through the process until I nodded in alignment. Except, as I found out later, he was not “buying” Tesla shares in the sense of becoming a recognised shareholder in the company. What he owned, through a crypto exchange called Mudrex, was a “[perpetual future](https://www.investopedia.com/what-are-perpetual-futures-7494870)” of the Tesla stock.
That is, roughly speaking, a futures contract without an expiration date, structured to closely mirror the movements of its underlying asset. You don’t own any Tesla stock, or receive dividends, or get voting rights, but your money moves with Tesla’s share price, just as a shareholder’s would. He was using UPI to move money into one of the many crypto platforms that now sell these contracts.
