Good Morning Ishan,
Vimal Kaw likes to joke that he can’t go into every one of his 18 data centres in India.
“Just because I work at NTT doesn’t mean I have access everywhere,” the India head of Japanese IT-services and consulting company NTT Data says.
A modern data centre is, by design, resentful of human presence. It’s a building that safety and security dictate be sealed, monitored, and, ideally, deserted. Fewer the badges handed out, fewer the breaches.
It’s also the kind of facility that almost every Indian state is eager to build. So much so that they are pouring their purses out. Gujarat unveiled its data-centre policy earlier in July, chasing up to Rs 600,000 crore in investment. That same week, HCL Tech inaugurated a new AI data centre in Gandhinagar’s GIFT city, the Noida-based IT company’s board having approved Rs 3,500 crore to enter the sector just four days earlier.
Andhra Pradesh has brought in Reliance and Google by offering Rs 41,000 crore in incentives. That’s 84% of what the state plans to spend on capital works—roads, schools, hospitals, housing, and power—in FY26. And Andhra doesn’t even have cash to burn. Its revenue deficit crossed 110% of the budgeted estimate in just the first quarter of FY25.
So what are Gujarat and Andhra, and other states signing data-centre deals, paying so exorbitantly for?
