Good Morning Ishan,
The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority’s (IRDAI) proposed commission cuts have come at an unusually good time for Go Digit.
They come just weeks after the digital general insurer deliberately sacrificed growth for profitability in the June quarter.
Last week, IRDAI set the cat among the pigeons with its radical proposals in a public consultation paper titled ‘Recalibrating economics of insurance distribution’.
The proposals introduce sharp cuts in commissions for distributors and tighten expense caps for insurers.
Many stocks joined at the hip with the sector are still reeling, while the biggest beneficiaries of the move could be some of its more under-the-radar players such as Go Digit and ICICI Lombard. Both insurers have found a sweet spot in the regulator’s proposals: a sizable motor-insurance business.
Given the bigger share of motor insurance in Go Digit’s business mix, the company stands to gain more by saving more on commissions.
The company has been feeling the strain of high commissions for some time now. Go Digit paid out about 36% commission on motor third-party (TP) policies and about 44% on motor own-damage (OD) policies in FY26. ICICI Lombard paid out 19% and 44%.
Kamesh Goyal, Go Digit’s founder, seems to have anticipated the shake-up by IRDAI after the company’s difficult June quarter, when it slowed down its business.
On the other hand, several competitors might see their business models being upended and profits being squeezed.
