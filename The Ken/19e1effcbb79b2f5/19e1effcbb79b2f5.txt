My early memories of Hotel Leela are of the landmark five-star property close to Mumbai airport—spoken of in the same breath as Taj and Oberoi, its luxury peers in downtown Mumbai. 

Over the years, though, the hospitality chain founded by the redoubtable Captain CP Krishnan Nair was more in the news for its deep debt troubles that eventually led to the 2019 sale of most of its assets to Canadian investment manager Brookfield. The erstwhile Hotel Leelaventures, now a shadow of its former self but retaining the Mumbai property, continues to be listed as HLV. 

Meanwhile, the pain and restructuring during the pandemic years and beyond led to an IPO of the Brookfield-controlled Leela Palaces Hotels & Resorts about a year ago. The Rs 3,500 crore public issue sailed through with nearly 5X oversubscription.

But that didn’t stop the stock from listing at a discount. The market seemed wary of its debt-ridden past, meagre profit in the year before the issue, and its losses before that. Also, the asking price looked very high optically—the price-to-earnings was in the triple digits.

A year on, Leela Palaces Hotels & Resorts still trades below its IPO price. But a closer look shows something that might warrant a more favourable treatment by the market. A sharp turnaround.
