We used to have these analogy questions in school. The Sun is to the solar system as the nucleus is to ___. Our answer, the atom, didn’t really depend on our knowledge of astronomy or chemistry. Such questions were a test of whether we could identify relationships between seemingly unrelated things.

My headline today is one for Indian e-commerce. Anyone who has followed the sector for more than five minutes already knows what Instamart is to Swiggy, Myntra is to Flipkart.

Both are businesses their parent companies can’t afford to get wrong. Quick commerce is where Swiggy’s actual future sits as food delivery slows into a side gig, and fashion, the second-largest e-commerce vertical after electronics, is where Myntra sits at the very centre of Flipkart’s own ambitions. Both businesses still burned through leadership and needed an outsider to walk in and fix things. 

That relationship would just be a fun bit for a pub quiz in Bengaluru, except that as of this week, the person who just solved one side of it is now being asked to solve the other. 

Nandita Sinha fixed Myntra, then got handed Flipkart Fashion too. Eight months later, she resigned from both to take over Swiggy Instamart, replacing Amitesh Jha, who, as it happens, had built much of Instamart’s leadership bench from his own former Flipkart colleagues, almost all of whom have now left too. She takes over a business whose own standalone app, launched in January, is still pulling in only a sixth of Blinkit’s daily users five months later. And she leaves a Flipkart whose CEO, just last week, told people not to expect an IPO timeline any time soon.

There is a lot going on. So, instead of just narrating it, I thought I would make you work a little for it. Six questions below, and every answer is sitting inside something we have already reported on Swiggy, Instamart, Flipkart, or Myntra over the past two years. Whether you have been with us for years or just found us today, this one tests how closely you actually read a story.

Scroll to the bottom for answers, but you will enjoy this more if you figure them out first.

Answers:

A. Part of Myntra’s jump to a Rs 550 crore FY25 profit, from Rs 31 crore in FY24, was helped along by a one-time favourable tax adjustment and lower employee costs. It was not quite the clean operational miracle the headline number suggests.

B. Krishnamurthy’s category-expansion playbook leaned on Sastasundar for health and Cleartrip for travel. Neither delivered as expected, part of a broader pattern that saw a dozen-plus senior executives, including the CFO, leave within two years.

B. An investor told us as early as August 2025 that Swiggy was a couple of quarters away from raising money again, right as Jha was publicly framing restraint on discounting as Instamart’s edge. A month later, JM Financial was recommending Swiggy raise Rs 4,400 crore just to stay competitive.

B. Our profile of Majety opens with him calling a senior executive at 9.30 am on a Wednesday, before most of Swiggy’s office had clocked in, wanting to know why the orders dashboard was blinking red. He now spends roughly 80% of his day in meetings. One former executive summed him up as “very polite, but extremely demanding, too”.

B. Instamart’s Ebitda margin sat at -109.5% against Blinkit’s -22.4%, despite launching in 2020, years ahead of Blinkit as we know it today.

C. Noice scaled from 200 to 350-plus SKUs in four to five months, a pace even Instamart’s own team admitted was outrunning its supply chain, leading to inventory wastage.

C. Tracked on Two by Two: Blinkit, with roughly 700 more stores and about 30% more daily orders, lost only about 1% of order value per order. Instamart lost roughly 12%. The gap barely moved through three different pivots.
