Their share of the GDP is equally high, compared to other advanced or similar economies. In the last five years, all groups have expanded into newer businesses far more aggressively and comfortably than in the previous years. Libas wants to sell fashion like FMCG. Most brands aren’t sold

This Indian-wear fast-fashion brand built a Rs 1,000 crore ARR business by being everywhere its shoppers are

Libas, a D2C-first apparel brand, has made selling fashion like FMCG its next goal post. Its founder, Sidhant Keshwani, believes that his products should be available everywhere—be it at a mall or on a quick-commerce app—whenever the customer demands them. Other challenger apparel brands like The Souled Store, Fable Street, and Koskii are also “cautiously optimistic” about the idea of quick commerce intersecting fashion. India spends billions on drones it doesn’t build. Its own are sold abroad

For homegrown drone startups, matching global capabilities is one thing. Navigating India’s defence procurement is another

Indian drone startups offer tech-first capabilities, but their sale to the defence forces are stuck in a mirage of delayed orders, unclear specs. The government has large allocations for defence procurements. But for local companies, military orders are limited to tactical and surveillance drones. Most big-ticket and high-priority combat orders by the Indian military are taken through an inconsistent procurement method. As a result, even well-funded startups are relying on exports and civilian markets to sustain growth. [Read the full story]
