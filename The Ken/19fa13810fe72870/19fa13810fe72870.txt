A bachelor’s in science degree is a common choice for millions in India. Yet the job market has been largely unwelcoming to these graduates.

Degrees like BSc in life sciences, zoology, Ayurveda, homeopathy, dental surgery, and even physiotherapy sit awkwardly between technical white-collar jobs—the likes of engineers and coders—and full-fledged clinical practice. Fresh graduates in these subjects usually earn around Rs 15–20,000 per month in lab and sales roles. 

But finding reliable work isn’t easy.

This 36-year-old BSc graduate found a way out. Fourteen years ago, he was hired as a fresher by a Mumbai-based company that processed hospital bills for US hospitals and insurers (remotely, of course).

“I spent about six months learning human anatomy in depth, including over 1000s of codes that doctors and insurance companies in the US use,” he said. A US exam for so-called “medical coders” required him to register with https://www.aapc.com/certifications/cpc the American Academy of Professional Coders to become a certified coder in this Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) company.

Put simply, RCM companies are specialised healthcare-service providers that handle the entire administrative and financial process of medical billing for doctors, hospitals, and clinics.
