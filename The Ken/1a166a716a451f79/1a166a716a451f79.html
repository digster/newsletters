After almost 10 years of waiting, the National Stock Exchange finally listed on 24 September. It underwhelmed. 

Institutions bid for 13X the shares on offer to them, retail buyers 1.4X, and HNIs 6.5X, and the stock opened at Rs 1,800, just above its issue price of Rs 1,785. Yet, many shareholders already knew they wouldn’t make money.

That’s because they had paid more than Rs 1,785 for NSE in the unlisted market. In a frenzy between April and June 2025, retail shareholders soared in number from 34,000 to about 150,000, while the price rose from Rs 1,600 to over Rs 2,200. Nearly 70,000 of them bought too late to sell in the IPO, becoming locked in until March 2027 due to Sebi’s six-month lock-in period for pre-IPO investors. Now the shares are trading at Rs 1,695.

India’s most hyped unlisted stock flattered to deceive. 

And the impact is already cascading through the unlisted market, less than a month on.
