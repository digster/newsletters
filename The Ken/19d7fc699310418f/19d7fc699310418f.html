Meta to get the world’s longest internet cable to India. It’s 100% exposed

India’s internet runs on cables it cannot protect, repair, or regulate—in spite of knowing exactly how to go about it

India has 17 international undersea cables, with no protection zones, no repair ships, and no law classifying them as critical infrastructure. For the first time in internet history, both primary cable routes between Asia and Europe are simultaneously inaccessible, caught in the crossfire of the Middle East war. A government study proposing Rs 4,000 crore to fix this has sat unactioned on the PMO’s review portal since 2023. Meta’s Waterworth cable, the world’s longest and designed to bypass every active conflict zone, will land at Rushikonda beach in 2030. There will be nothing to protect it. [Read full story here]

Buying time with gas

Maruti, Tata caught between war, EV delays, and emission rules, find an unlikely fix

A fuel that emits 19% less CO₂ and costs far less than oil imports is buying carmakers time

Amid slower EV adoption and stricter Cafe-3 norms, companies like Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, and Hyundai are doubling down on CNG variants. The government is doing its part, too, by guaranteeing CNG stations with 100% supply of their average past six-month consumption. Automakers have addressed key drawbacks of CNG vehicles—low bootspace and power—even as India continues to scale its existing infrastructure. But with the country heavily reliant on gas imports, the government is also pushing blending compressed biogas as an alternative. [Read the full story]

Adani’s think tank flexes its muscles in Delhi

Chintan Research Foundation, just two years old, is already claiming a role in shaping policy—while staying tethered to a powerful patron

Chintan Research Foundation is a new think tank in Delhi. It’s bankrolled by the Adani Group, which is committed to funding it to the tune of Rs 100 crore. Chintan’s recommendations found their way into the Shanti Act of 2025, which opened up India’s nuclear sector to private players. To be taken seriously in the long run, the think tank will need to walk the tightrope between its patron’s interests and its editorial independence. [Read full story here]

If VCs won’t fund defence, India’s wealth and asset managers will

360 One’s defence fund, among the first of its kind in the country, is backing the makers of modern war’s eyes and brains. But too much capital can steer companies away from innovation

360 One has launched one of India’s first dedicated private defence and aerospace funds, raising Rs 1,000 crore with a Rs 500-crore greenshoe, and has already backed four companies. Only 20% capital is for VC-style bets, while the rest is aimed at growth, late-stage, and pre-IPO companies better suited to defence’s long timelines. Its strategy is to back “brains of warfare” tech like radar, avionics, and space surveillance—areas with dual-use potential—rather than capital-heavy platforms. That kind of capital could reshape India’s defence ecosystem by scaling proven companies faster. It may also starve riskier early innovation. [Read the full story]
