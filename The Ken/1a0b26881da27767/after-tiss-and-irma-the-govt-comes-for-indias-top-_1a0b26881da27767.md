Good Morning Ishan,
Partha Majumder has spent the better part of 72 years on Barrackpore Trunk Road in Kolkata—first as a student, then as a researcher, then as faculty, and now as an emeritus professor at the Indian Statistical Institute (ISI). Of late, though, things have been pretty different.
In one of the meetings of the Ministry of Statistics and Program Implementation (Mospi), Majumdar was invited to discuss the future of the country’s leading statistical institute. Out of India at the time, he couldn’t make the meeting at Bengaluru’s Jawaharlal Nehru Centre. So he asked them to send him the meeting’s agenda and minutes. The ministry replied that it had neither.
For a career statistician, there are few things more disappointing than being told there are no records.
Majumder is a lead signatory to one of the petitions against the new ISI Bill introduced in the Lok Sabha this August, urging the government to consult the institute before the bill is passed. Its 637 signatories include academics from across the country, including PC Mohanan, former acting chairman of the National Statistical Commission (NSC), former directors of ISI, SB Rao and BLS Prakasa Rao, historian Ramachandra Guha, and Jadavpur University emeritus professor Supriya Chaudhary.
“I have signed the petition precisely due to a lack of clarity about the motives of the bill,” Mohanan told The Ken.
Incidentally, it’s not the easiest time for India’s statistical system…
