“Yes, we are arguably the first pure-play alternative asset management company (AMC) to go public in India,” says Gopal Jain, cofounder and managing partner of Gaja Capital.

After 27 years of operating quietly in the shadows of India’s private markets—the way PE funds typically do—Gaja Alternative Asset Management is now getting listed on the NSE and BSE through a mainboard initial public offering (IPO). 

The interest so far has been decent. On 19 August, the opening IPO day saw 1.12X and 1.27X subscriptions from non-institutional and retail investors. The company lists on the bourses on 26 August this year.

Earlier this week, it raised over Rs 165 crore in anchor investments from mutual funds, insurers, and high-net-worth individuals, including Nippon India Mutual Fund, HDFC Life, and SBI Life. 

Jain sees the listing as a natural progression, drawing a parallel with the mutual fund industry, where fund managers that were private and unlisted for years too, before gradually going public over the last decade. 

Now, he thinks, is the time for alternative fund managers to do the same.
