“One cigarette, please… Classic mild.”
“Sir, that will cost Rs 17 plus Rs 1 extra if you’re paying by UPI.”
Before 2020, this was a familiar scene. Panwallahs in Bengaluru and South Delhi routinely charged an extra rupee for UPI payments.
That changed when the government set the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) to zero on UPI transactions in 2020. Awareness campaigns gradually helped small merchants drop the surcharge. ( MDR is the fee banks charge merchants for accepting digital payments via cards, UPI, Bhim, Aadhaar Pay, etc.)
Now, the government is reluctant to keep subsidising UPI. Reports cite “Trump factor”, though officials deny it.
Whatever the case, UPI won’t remain free. At least in some instances.
Is this the only way forward with the seamless payments system? There’s potential here, but there are also other ways to reimagine it.
