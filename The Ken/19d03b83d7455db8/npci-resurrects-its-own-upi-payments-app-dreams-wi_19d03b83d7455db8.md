Good Morning Ishan,
The National Payments Corporation of India (NCPI) made a curious announcement yesterday.
Former cricket star MS Dhoni will be the brand ambassador for Bhim, the mobile payments app developed by the not-for-profit retail payments body that runs the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) system. But why does Bhim need an ambassador now, a good 10 years after its launch?
Bhim, after all, was meant to fade into the background and let privately-funded payments apps like Phonepe, Google Pay, Paytm*, and others take the lead.
Bhim was born during the 2016 demonetisation, when Prime Minister Narendra Modi wanted to promote UPI adoption through a government-backed app.
But the government could not just pick any existing app, even one made by a government-owned bank, as it would have given it an unfair market advantage over the others. So NPCI decided to come up with a starter UPI app that the government could put its face to and promote and educate users with. And on 30 December Modi urged citizens to use Bhim.
The unlikely story of BHIM, the upsetter of plans, [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/unlikely-story-of-bhim/)
This is from our 2017 story. That intent appears to be shifting.
Over the last two years, NPCI has wanted Bhim to play a bigger role. It envisions the app as a “sovereign alternative to UPI”. One that is always maintained by NPCI as “a neutral party to the ecosystem”, NPCI’s CEO Dilip Asbe said at the 2025 annual general meeting.
It’s not just an alternative, but an alternative with market share ambitions.
NPCI wants Bhim to be among the top five UPI apps, with around 5% market share over the next three years, Business Standard [reported](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/bhim-targets-5-share-to-break-into-top-five-challenge-upi-duopoly-126031701368_1.html) on Wednesday, citing sources aware of the development.
Currently, it is the sixth-largest app, recording over 175 million transactions and holding a 0.86% market share as of February 2026. A year ago, it had only about 36 million transactions.
The likes of Phonepe, Google Pay, and Paytm, which together make for 80% of the market, cannot rest easy knowing there is a more profitable, competitive, and government-backed alternative quietly building muscle. In FY25, NPCI’s operating income rose 19% to Rs 3,270 crore, with surplus after tax rising 42% to over Rs 1,552 crore.
Such profit means that when NPCI has big dreams, it has the staying power to see them through.
Take Rupay, for instance. NPCI built the Rupay card network as an alternative to Visa and Mastercard, which dominated India’s credit and debit card market. But over the last 15 years, the Rupay credit card has not been able to grab more share from the two giants; in 2024, it was just around 10%.
However, more recently, the Rupay credit card and transaction volumes have seen an uptick. And that is thanks to Rupay’s special, structural privileges: it is now the only credit card that can be linked to UPI for payments.
Rupay credit card transactions on UPI currently account for 38% of all credit card transactions in India by volume and about 8% by value, according to data from a 2025 Bernstein Securities [report](https://www.livemint.com/industry/banking/rupay-upi-visa-mastercard-amex-credit-card-volumes-india-npci-11761635955496.html). The brokerage did not mention a time period for this data.
Here is another example of things NPCI can do that others simply can’t.
In 2024, NPCI set up a new arm called NPCI Bhim Services Limited (NBSL) to hive off Bhim and enhance the adoption of digital payments, especially in areas that other apps were not prioritising. As a result of this internal restructuring, this arm acquired the Bhim app for just Rs 59 crore from NPCI, the holding company, according to NBSL’s 2025 annual report.
Then, to get Bhim to reach a target of Rs 20,000 crore in total transaction volume in FY25, the government approved a Rs 1,500 crore “Incentive Scheme for promotion of low-value Bhim-UPI transactions (person to merchant)”.
With UPI growth [moderating](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/digital-india/npci-counts-on-bharat-northeast-to-accelerate-upi-adoption/129650928), NPCI wants payments apps to push for growth in rural areas, but it will be hard for others to double down on that.
“Fintechs that drove the first wave of UPI adoption are now rationalising spending amid profitability pressures. NPCI is stepping in with limited incentives through BHIM,” the banker said.
NPCI counts on Bharat, North East to accelerate adoption, [The Economic Times](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/digital-india/npci-counts-on-bharat-northeast-to-accelerate-upi-adoption/129650928)
That’s why Bhim’s comeback is something those with payments app ambitions cannot ignore.
In 2016, when PM Modi promoted Bhim, it hit 10 million downloads in just 10 days, a feat which took Phonepe three months to achieve. NPCI may now be hoping Dhoni will help do the rest for Bhim.
