Good Morning Ishan,
In mid-August every year, as Himachal Pradesh’s apple harvest peaks, trucks begin lining up outside Adani Agri Fresh’s controlled-atmosphere storage facilities in Rohru in Shimla district of Himachal Pradesh. Farmers arrive with blue crates stacked high with apples, hoping to secure a slot before the warehouses fill up.
Inside, the fruits enter a near-hibernation state. Oxygen levels are lowered, temperatures hover around freezing, and the nearly 25,000 metric tonnes of apples can remain fresh for up to 10 months before being released back into the market when prices are higher.
For nearly two decades, this has been the business of Adani Enterprise’s agricultural arm: buying apples, storing them, and selling them during off-season.
That ambit just widened. On 9 May, the company announced its expansion into cherries, plums, and peaches—fruits that are more perishable, more visually graded, and more dependent on refrigeration and fast logistics than apples.
Other conglomerates have their own fruits of choice.
Reliance Industries has built a 600-acre mango-orchard ecosystem in Jamnagar with over 130,000 trees, reportedly contributing over $1.2 billion a year to export earnings. Mahindra, meanwhile, has its own grape-export supply chains through its subsidiary Mahindra Agri Solutions. Even ITC has been expanding its fresh-fruits business as part of a Rs 20,000-crore push across FMCG and agri-businesses.
But that’s the thing. For Adani, fruits themselves were never the main play…
