Chasing the silver lining

L&T’s Rs 15,000 cr bet with Nvidia is a gut punch to a struggling E2E

E2E is buying chips, splitting its stock, spinning up subsidiaries—anything to sell more compute. L&T’s data-centre unit has already moved on

L&T bought 21% of Indian cloud provider E2E in 2024 for its AI platform, but has since built its own AI infrastructure directly with Nvidia. E2E is buying new GPUs, while its old ones depreciate with just 40% utilisation. The GPU cloud provider is burning cash in the process, and has taken out bank loans to fill the shortfall. Nvidia, with a new financing agreement, backstops depreciation losses for select cloud providers. But E2E isn’t one of them [Read full story here]

Orders up

Inside Rapido’s plan to upend Zomato and Swiggy’s hard-won food-delivery math

Live in Bengaluru, Ownly wants to take food delivery beyond the top 10% of earners and is already doing over 50,000 orders a day

Rapido’s food-delivery app, Ownly, is barely six months old and has already gained 7% market share in Bengaluru. It relies on a zero-commission model, giving restaurants relief from high take rates. Because they aren’t paying high commissions, restaurants list dishes at lower, dine-in-menu prices. The challenge is whether delivery fees alone can achieve long-term sustainability and profitability for Ownly.  [Read full story here]

The RBI’s revolving- credit rule treats every NBFC like a bad lender

Bajaj Finance and Tata Capital could lose a lucrative lending tool. But the central bank has another option

The RBI’s concern is that reusable credit can mask a borrower’s deteriorating finances, especially when lenders cannot see the full picture of their cash flows. But the regulator’s own framework recognises that a large, well-supervised NBFC does not carry the same risks as a small one, while new data-sharing tools have changed how lenders can assess borrowers. The RBI has already shown with credit cards that NBFCs can enter traditionally bank-dominated products when they meet stricter conditions, offering a template for a more targeted approach. Pushing all such lending towards banks could leave the smallest borrowers with fewer regulated options, even as larger NBFCs are capable of managing the risks. [Read the full story]

Stories you don’t want to miss

UPI is the Trojan horse carrying small banks into HDFC, Axis’s credit-card empire

Once touted as the next big disruptor, the credit-line-on-UPI spent two years in the shadows. Not anymore

After two muted years, Credit-Line-on-UPI (CLOU) is finally taking off, led by small banks and fintechs rather than the big incumbents. Big commercial banks reigning over the credit-card empire face a predicament. Competing on CLOU risks cannibalising their own cards, but scaling it means routing through the fintechs they are trying to outflank. A matured tech stack has turned CLOU from a small-ticket experiment into a full-strength credit-card equivalent, with real-time underwriting and many benefits over physical cards. The promised financial inclusion has flipped. CLOU has democratised lending for smaller banks, not borrowing for the credit-starved. [Read full story here]

Selling out

Jio Financial signals defeat by selling its crown jewel cheap

By giving up half of Jio Credit to Bank of America for a song, Mukesh Ambani makes an uncharacteristic move

Bank of America has entered into a joint venture with Jio Financial Services, acquiring a 49.9% stake in Jio Credit. Jio Credit gets capital, while Bank of America gets access to a big lending market. Win-win? Not quite, think the market and sector experts who are looking askance at an unequal deal. Jio Financial Services has given up its crown jewel too soon and for too little. [Read the full story]

Worker-owned Uber and Snabbit? India faces up to the grim reality of gig labour

The country can’t afford to leave the fate of the ever-expanding gig workforce entirely to market forces

Bharat Taxi, the government’s answer to Uber and Rapido, promises to share profits with drivers, much like Amul and Nandini. It’s one of several market segments where the government is testing a cooperative model. But a majority of existing cooperatives continue to struggle. A cooperative model in segments dominated by gig work will need to justify not just its effectiveness but also its existence. [Read the full story]
