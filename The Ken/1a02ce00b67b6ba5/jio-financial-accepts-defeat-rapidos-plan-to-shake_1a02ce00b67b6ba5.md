[The RBI’s revolving- credit rule treats every NBFC like a bad lender](https://the-ken.com/story/the-rbis-revolving-credit-rule-treats-every-nbfc-like-a-bad-lender/?utm_source=weekly_email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
Bajaj Finance and Tata Capital could lose a lucrative lending tool. But the central bank has another option
The RBI’s concern is that reusable credit can mask a borrower’s deteriorating finances, especially when lenders cannot see the full picture of their cash flows. But the regulator’s own framework recognises that a large, well-supervised NBFC does not carry the same risks as a small one, while new data-sharing tools have changed how lenders can assess borrowers. The RBI has already shown with credit cards that NBFCs can enter traditionally bank-dominated products when they meet stricter conditions, offering a template for a more targeted approach. Pushing all such lending towards banks could leave the smallest borrowers with fewer regulated options, even as larger NBFCs are capable of managing the risks. [UPI is the Trojan horse carrying small banks into HDFC, Axis’s credit-card empire](https://the-ken.com/story/upi-is-the-trojan-horse-carrying-small-banks-into-hdfc-axiss-credit-card-empire/?utm_source=weekly_email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly_recap_card)
Once touted as the next big disruptor, the credit-line-on-UPI spent two years in the shadows. Not anymore
After two muted years, Credit-Line-on-UPI (CLOU) is finally taking off, led by small banks and fintechs rather than the big incumbents. Big commercial banks reigning over the credit-card empire face a predicament. Competing on CLOU risks cannibalising their own cards, but scaling it means routing through the fintechs they are trying to outflank. A matured tech stack has turned CLOU from a small-ticket experiment into a full-strength credit-card equivalent, with real-time underwriting and many benefits over physical cards. The promised financial inclusion has flipped. CLOU has democratised lending for smaller banks, not borrowing for the credit-starved.
