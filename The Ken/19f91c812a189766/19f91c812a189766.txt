“DMart is a $30 billion company and only 4.5 times our size in sales. If we keep executing, we grow 2–3X every year and potentially surpass them in the next 18–24 months.”

That was Aadit Palicha, Zepto’s CEO. Around two years ago. 

Today, DMart is about Rs 70,000 crore in revenue and makes Rs 3,000 crore in profit. Meanwhile, Zepto is about a third of DMart’s size in revenue at ~ Rs 22,000 crore in revenue, but losing nearly Rs 6,000 crore in the past year. 

The “we want to surpass Dmart” story hasn’t aged too well. 

So Zepto has moved on to the next one, and the next one, and the next. 

But back to where we began.

For four years, the narrative about two of India’s quick-commerce companies has been more or less fixed. 

Zepto is the lean one, the insurgent, the disciplined one. It has fewer dark stores, a leaner, tighter catalogue of products, and focuses heavily on speed. Sure, it did the odd experiment, but for the most part, everyone saw Zepto as a challenger. 

Swiggy, on the other hand, was the sprawler: it expanded and went all over the place, spinning up and then spinning down businesses—meat, cloud kitchens, premium grocery, a mall, an errands app, a storefront for D2C brands… the list goes on and on. 

In one of our earlier stories, just six months before Swiggy went public, my colleague Gaurav wrote https://the-ken.com/story/swiggy-used-to-be-a-playground-for-innovation-now-its-a-graveyard/, “̌Swiggy used to be a playground for innovation. Now, it’s a graveyard.”

Now, in the weeks before Zepto’s IPO, the roles appear to have switched. Swiggy has discovered a focus and discipline it last had in the previous decade. It’s made difficult decisions and has an eye on the long term, in a bid to conserve capital. 

And Zepto is the one that’s running amok, launching and killing products, and fighting for a narrative. One day it’s about subscriptions. Another day it’s about AI. The next day it’s about launching premium products. It all sounds too familiar, because it is. 

Both Swiggy and Zepto have swapped places. As Bob Dylan famously (quite appropriate for the current moment) wrote: 

The slow one now

Will later be fast

As the present now

Will later be past

The order is rapidly fadin’

And the first one now

Will later be last
