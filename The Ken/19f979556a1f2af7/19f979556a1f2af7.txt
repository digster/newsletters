If there were such a thing as the Dead Sea Scrolls of artificial intelligence, they’d record events of a distant past that seem no longer imaginable: OpenAI was set up as a non-profit and much of its work was open-source. 

In hindsight, those flags were flown more to recruit people with great minds and lofty ideals to go with their brilliance. These weren’t grounding principles that the organisation would truly stick to in the long run.

I’m sure that among those who still roam the halls of OpenAI’s offices, there are some who would feel embarrassed to read those scriptures. The company is no longer “unconstrained by a need to generate financial return”, as was broadcast in its 2015 founding announcement https://openai.com/index/introducing-openai/, and few could still argue that it “avoid[s] enabling uses of AI or AGI that harm humanity or unduly concentrate power” to the extent intended in its 2018 charter https://openai.com/charter/. Some old-timers have lamented https://x.com/gothburz/status/2023764119791562897 how OpenAI has changed dramatically for the worse.

But the ideals that OpenAI’s founders once cloaked their organisation in are alive and well across the Pacific. That abandoned ethos is precisely what now gives Chinese labs an edge.

This week on the Zero Shot podcast

Hi everyone! This is Vidhatri, the producer of Zero Shot. 

Once in a while on this podcast, we step back, pause, and look inward to really understand our relationship with AI. 

Earlier this month, hosts Rohin, Praveen, and Brady audited their AI use. Here are some questions they answered: What did you use AI for in the last 24 hours? On a scale of one to 10, how proficient are you with AI? Have you ever thought about where your conversations and data actually go? If you lost access tomorrow, what would you actually lose? Have you taught the AIs you use about yourself? Could you take that to a different tool?

These simple questions revealed a lot and led the hosts to make a case for “intelligence independence”. Put simply, it is about learning “how to protect and compound our intelligence in an AI world”. This thesis is the basis for The Ken’s next live event on 1 August, at the Bangalore International Centre. You can read more about it here https://the-ken.com/events/intelligence-independence/ and get your tickets too. 

This week, the hosts did another audit. It was a simpler exercise—defining the AI buzzwords swirling around. Forcing yourself to define something lets you get to the root of a concept and tests the relevance of the words in your own life. That’s exactly what happened. 

They picked from the following options:

A. Never heard of it

B. Heard of it, can’t define it

C. Can define it

D. It changed how I work

They ended up unpacking these terms: frontier models, open weights, tokens, context window, fine-tuning, distillation, RAG, prompt/context engineering, context store, prompt library, context rot, voice and style, harness, loops, and evals. 

It was a whole lot of fun. I’d recommend you try this exercise too to scan your own relationship with AI.
