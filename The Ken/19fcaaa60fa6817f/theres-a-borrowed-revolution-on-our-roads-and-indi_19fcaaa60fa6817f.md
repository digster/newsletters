Good Morning Ishan,
India’s electric-vehicle numbers have been looking up. Over 65% of three-wheelers being sold are now EVs, while electric passenger vehicles have gone from 5% to 8% of the total in just the last few months.
But the reality is, India’s EV revolution is largely powered by China. Case in point: around 80% of heavy-duty battery-management systems (BMS) in the country—what’s popularly called the EV brain—are imported, mostly from China.
And the ripple effects are already visible.
Hackers have recently been using Chinese BMS apps, such as Bat BMS and Epoch, to connect to e-rickshaws’ batteries via Bluetooth and remotely disable them in the middle of the road.
The country’s response has been swift. It banned these BMS apps, even as the government mulls a new set of rules, meant to further cybersecurity management and ensure safety during software updates. British defence firms have long been raising alarm bells, too. EV vehicles are rolling spyware, crammed with cameras, mics, and cloud links, they warned the country’s defence department in 2025.
