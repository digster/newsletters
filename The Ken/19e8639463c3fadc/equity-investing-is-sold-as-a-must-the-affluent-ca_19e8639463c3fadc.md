Good Morning Ishan,
Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C Joseph Vijay might have stunned the hustings with his victory. But the former actor’s assets declaration in the run-up to the state elections left another group with its jaws open.
Vijay was worth Rs 624 crore, and more than half of it was with banks—in savings accounts and fixed deposits. Over Rs 200 crore sat in a single savings bank account. Real estate formed the next largest chunk.
More than the sum, what really raised eyebrows was the near-absence of equity in the portfolio. Less than Rs 20 lakh, barely 0.05% of his assets, was in stocks and equity.
A financial blasphemy to many.
Equity has long been sold as indispensable to any portfolio worth its salt by the financial-services ecosystem. Not having a tidy part of your money in stocks or equity mutual funds is considered outdated, even scoffed at, in India.
But as it turns out, an investor with a no-equity portfolio can, well, have the last laugh. Because if you are really rich, run-of-the-mill wisdom is often optional, even avoidable.
And if you’re only modestly wealthy, say, just rich, it turns out, fixed income can be your friend, too.
