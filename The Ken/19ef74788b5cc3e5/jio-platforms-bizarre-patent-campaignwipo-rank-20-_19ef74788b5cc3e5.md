For a conglomerate that has reported static R&D headcount—“1000+” for four consecutive years through FY26—a 320-rank jump in global patent filings should have raised questions. It didn’t.
It appears Reliance Industries, which owns 66.4% of Jio Platforms Ltd (currently filing its IPO papers to raise around $4 billion) and reported 6,817 cumulative patent filings in its latest annual report, has consolidated patents across subsidiaries. Of these, 1,037 are applications under the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT), the metric World Intellectual Property Organisation (Wipo) uses for its annual rankings. That’s a nearly 800% jump from 120 PCT filings the previous year, against unchanged R&D spending, which has stayed at roughly 0.4% of revenue for years.
On 14 June, the company said:
…Most notably, Jio’s 320-rank jump to the 20th place comes in a year when global PCT filings grew by less than 1% globally. The WIPO ranking is an important external recognition of Jio Platforms’ R&D productivity and intellectual property depth. It firmly places India-origin innovation in the global technology creation landscape across multiple advanced technologies…making it one of the fastest-rising innovators globally and placing it among an elite group of global technology leaders, including Huawei, Samsung, Qualcomm, LG, Panasonic, Nokia, Google, Apple and Microsoft.
It requires some naivete to make such statements. Patent count means legal filings, not distinct inventions.
[...]
The shift is conspicuous enough that other divisions within Reliance and across the group have been scrutinising the applications, wondering “why they were filed in the manner they did with little internal review”. One Reliance executive called it “for bragging rights”.
The haste shows in the numbers.
