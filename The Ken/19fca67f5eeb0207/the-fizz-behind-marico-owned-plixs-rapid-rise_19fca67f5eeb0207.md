If you drop by a pharmacy in a metro city’s neighbourhood, you’ll notice a curious shift on the front counter. Along with the usual Vicks lozenges and hand sanitizers, sit brightly packaged, neon-coloured tubes of fizzy supplements claiming to offer a tasty, over-the-counter shortcut for weight loss. Pick one up, and it’s likely to be by the Marico-acquired nutraceutical and personal care brand, Plix.
When Marico first bought a 58% stake in Plix in 2023, its revenue was Rs 100 crore. This quadrupled in two years, and then doubled to Rs 864 crore in just a single year, according to Marico’s FY26 annual report. Profit rose in step, from Rs 4.77 crore to Rs 25.9 crore.
Numbers like these are usually unheard of in Indian direct-to-consumer (D2C) acquisitions.
Usually, getting bought by an FMCG brand is typically where a D2C brand story ends. As The Ken reported back when FMCG majors first started bringing D2C brands into their fold, these legacy companies had “a huge blind spot when it comes to figuring out how to sell to Instagram- and Youtube-influenced twenty-somethings, who are more comfortable browsing an online catalogue than walking into a store”.
