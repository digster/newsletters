Hi Ishan,
You couldn’t make up the origin story of Aashirvaad atta if you tried.
Around the turn of the millennium, ITC’s then CEO Yogesh Chander Deveshwar wanted the decades-old cigarette maker to sell packaged foods to a growing middle class in a liberalised economy.
He chose a particularly tricky product to prove his mettle. At that time, Indians were particular about getting their atta, or wheat flour, freshly ground. Even the mightiest of FMCG companies, Hindustan Unilever, hadn’t had much luck persuading them to buy branded atta.
The odds were clearly stacked against ITC. And Deveshwar knew that a marketing blitzkrieg alone wouldn’t cut it.
ITC had to get the product just right for consumers’ varied tastes. So it turned to its three major businesses. ITC bought 18 grades of wheat through its “e-Choupal” agri-procurement initiative; asked the chefs in its hotels across the country about regional preferences in atta; and then brought to bear its tobacco-blending chops in mixing different kinds of wheat.
Lo and behold, within a few years, ITC had not just defied sceptics and become the market leader; it had managed to make Aashirvaad synonymous with atta.
The story of ITC is one of improbability. And that’s the story Rohin and I tell, warts and all, in the fourth episode of Intermission.
