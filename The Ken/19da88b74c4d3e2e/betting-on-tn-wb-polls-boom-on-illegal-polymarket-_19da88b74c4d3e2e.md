Prediction markets by any name, or under any garb, are banned in India. The country officially disallowed even global platforms like Polymarket and Kalshi in 2025, under the new Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act (PROGA).
Yet, some people are getting around the ban by using virtual private networks (VPNs) and trading by converting rupees into USDC (a stablecoin). On Polymarket, users are already choosing winners and losers in the upcoming West Bengal and Tamil Nadu legislative assembly elections.
According to Polymarket, the ruling TMC and DMK are winning in West Bengal and Tamil Nadu, respectively. Both states go to polls later this week.
For a brief period last week, I could access the platform on my phone without a VPN, but it’s otherwise inaccessible. My colleagues on the podcast team, who recently ran a [Daybreak episode](https://the-ken.com/podcasts/daybreak/india-banned-online-betting-polymarket-is-wagering-on-our-elections-anyway/), could still access the site via VPN as of 17 April.
The volume of trade is low; the contract value is rather modest—$1.9 million for West Bengal and $370,000 for Tamil Nadu. But even then, these contracts can apparently influence people.
A new policy paper [published](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aee3932?utm_source=sfmc&utm_medium=email&utm_content=alert&utm_campaign=SCIeToc&et_rid=34849577&et_cid=5931445) on 16 April in the journal Science argues that the rapid institutionalisation of these commercial platforms has raised three grave concerns: democratic manipulation, gambling-like design, and public health hazards. It contends that while these platforms—based on years of academic and corporate [research and experimentation](https://tower-research.com/the-prediction-markets-opportunity-part-1-from-experiments-to-exchanges/)—advocate scientific credibility, they flout the “very principles and evidence” on which that credibility depends.
When I asked the lead author, Nizan Geslevich Packin, if the low volume or value of contracts on Indian state elections were harmless and could just pass as play, she disagreed.
