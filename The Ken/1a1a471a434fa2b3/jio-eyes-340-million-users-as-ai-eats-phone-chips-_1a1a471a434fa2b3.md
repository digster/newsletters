Good Morning Dear Reader,
For nearly a decade, Tallil has sat in the same dimly lit mobile-sales-and-repair shop in Madanpur Khadar in Delhi. The walls are covered with advertisements and the shelves with a range of devices.
Behind him sits a considerable collection of “dumb” phones, i.e., feature phones priced anywhere below Rs 10,000. Amid this pile lies what Reliance Jio Infocomm—the telecom subsidiary of the recently listed Jio Platforms—calls a Rs 1.3 lakh crore opportunity: moving India’s remaining 2G users to 4G through its latest phone Jiobharat.
Launched in 2023 for Rs 999, this is a smart feature phone with 4G internet access, UPI payments, music streaming, and video entertainment. The price has since increased to Rs 1,499, which still makes it the cheapest phone on the market.
In October, Jio even offered a 10th-anniversary deal on the phone, bundling six months of recharge benefits and bringing the effective price of the handset down to Rs 299.
The company’s reasoning is simple. It wants to add 340 million customers to its 4G and 5G base, of which 240 million are existing 2G users, while another 100 million are expected to enter the market over the next five years as they come of age, Pankaj Pawar, CEO of Jio Platforms, said during the company’s Q2 FY27 earnings call on 30 October.
At various points, Jio has dubbed these 2G users as “trapped” in an older era and one of the “most exploited customers”, who’re charged Rs 180 for just 2GB of data, most of which they can barely consume.
This has enabled the chairman of Reliance Industries, Mukesh Ambani to pitch the Jiobharat phone as a way to make India “2G-mukt” and eventually, move all phone users to 4G, or better yet, 5G, by the end of this decade.
