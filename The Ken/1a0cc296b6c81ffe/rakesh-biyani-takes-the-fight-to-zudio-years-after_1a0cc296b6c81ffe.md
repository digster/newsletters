Good Morning Ishan,
“We never wanted to be a Zudio.”
Odd thing for Rakesh Biyani to tell The Ken when the Tata-owned fast-fashion retailer’s influence on Style Union is inarguable.
Since 2022, when Rakesh founded Style Union with Ekta Biyani, the value-retail chain has been expanding, adding 180 outlets. In FY25, its revenue rose two-thirds to Rs 588 crore from a year earlier, while net losses shrank by a third to Rs 24 crore. The company claims that revenue has since doubled in each of the last two years, with FY26 at around Rs 1,000 crore and FY27 expected to close at “a little more than Rs 2,000 crore”. The company hasn’t filed its FY26 financials yet. And it is now in the process of raising Rs 800 crore from Venturi Partners and Elev8 Venture Partners.
Few expected Zudio, which counts over 1,000 stores across 300-plus cities in India and more than $1 billion in sales, to have a worthy rival, and fewer still expected the Biyanis to be behind that rival.
For most of the 2010s, if an Indian consumer wanted cheap and decent clothes from a chain, they went to Fashion at Big Bazaar (FBB). FBB had grown to a Rs 5,600 crore business operating more than 335 stores at its peak. Biyani’s cousin Kishore persuaded millions of Indians that shopping in a large, air-conditioned store with more options than they were used to needn’t be an expensive proposition, setting up Pantaloons in 1997 under the Future Group, followed by several other chains. For two decades, the Biyani name and Future Group were synonymous with modern Indian retail.
But by 2020, the group was deep in debt and ran into insolvency soon after.
