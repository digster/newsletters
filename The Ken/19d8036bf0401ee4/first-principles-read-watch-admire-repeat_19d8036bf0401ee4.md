I love the fact that we understand we don’t always need to read non-fiction to learn something new. For those of you who don’t, there’s still time. Pick up something unreal. Actually, even with non-fiction, how much of it is even non?
Computer books are labelled as non-fiction. Philosophy books are non-fiction. But are these books really devoid of imagination? Or fantasy? Computers were a fantasy in the 14th century! Think about it.
Which is why I am not surprised that we have a healthy mix of the two. Working with hard numbers and outcome-driven jobs can sometimes make us forget that not everything can be measured and quantified. Some things are just a feeling.
But a list of nearly 300 books? That is pretty cool, I would say.
Congratulations to all of us for collating this wonderful curation of books. And thank you, as always, for your active participation.
It’s Vishnu, with the Movies, TV, and Music section.
Growing up, my first Hollywood wasn’t really Hollywood at all.
It was Stuart Little dubbed in Malayalam on Kiran TV and Surya TV (regional Malayalam channels). Then came Spy Kids, Home Alone, Anaconda, Jurassic Park, and Titanic—all delivered through the warm, slightly off-kilter voices of Malayalam dubbing artists.
Soon enough, I got “promoted” to the likes of Movies Now and HBO. These days, the only time I catch a dubbed film is whatever’s playing at my barber’s in Bengaluru. Almost always from the Dangerous Khiladi cinematic universe.
But dubbing as a post-release afterthought does feel like it’s quietly fading.
At the 2020 Golden Globes, while accepting the award for Best Foreign Language film, Bong Joon Ho said:
The line landed because, well, it was true. But also because a lot of people were already halfway there. Remember how Dark, Money Heist, and Squid Game were embraced and loved across the world?
Sure, we had to shuttle our eyes between the screen and the subtitles at first, but soon enough, it was never a problem. It came naturally. (Though, there’s the other extreme now too. Where we NEED subtitles with everything. There’s a [Vox video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYJtb2YXae8) on that. Worth watching.)
Take my industry, for example: Vaazha 2—a Malayalam film made on a shoestring budget of an estimated Rs 10 crore, has crossed 1,000% returns on investment and breached the Rs 100 crore worldwide collection mark in just seven days.
What makes this even more impressive? It also happens to be the debut feature from a group of Instagram [content creators](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/malayalam/vaazha-2-box-office-hashir-h-gang-social-media-success-story-10623445/)!
I’ve had non-Malayalam-speaking friends asking me if the movie is worth a watch, in a language they don’t speak, from an industry they barely follow, all because of how comfortable they have become with subtitles.
They also wanted to know how to pronounce the title, but the zha in Vaazha is doing something quintessentially Malayali. So I just let it be, sat back, and enjoyed them taking a shot at it 🙂
Fingernails (2023) – Romance/Sci-fi.
What if there were a test to tell you whether you and your partner were in love. Would you take it?
Oh, and the test also involves pulling out one fingernail each, to be tested in a machine built on “science”. No, the gore ends there. Unless you categorise mental turmoil as hard to watch. In which case, yes, it is violent.
This Jessie Buckley, Riz Ahmed, and Jeremy Allen White starrer is streaming on Apple TV.
Worth giving it a go, with some cold coffee, and hopefully a strong stomach for about a minute and a half’s worth of nail pulls in total 🙂
