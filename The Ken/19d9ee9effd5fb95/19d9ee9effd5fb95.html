Virtuosos like to speak to themselves, simultaneously playing the role of inquirer and subject.

The eccentric, erudite pianist and broadcaster Glenn Gould did this and waxed poetic—to himself, to the world—about performance, isolation, and morality. One of my favourite composers, György Ligeti, also did this to express how he turned raw musical ideas into more structured compositions.

I don’t know exactly why figures like Gould and Ligeti choose to do this. Maybe it’s lonely at the top of the mountain, and a choreographed conversation involving one person in two roles is a quick and easy way to get messy notions ironed out.

Jia Zhangke, one of the most important contemporary Chinese filmmakers, joined their ranks a couple of months ago:

Hello, everyone. For the Lunar New Year this year, I worked with Doubao’s video generation model Seedance 2.0, and collaboratively made a special short film called Chief Jia’s Dance.

As the producer, I did not act in the film. The two Jia Zhangkes shown on screen were both created by Seedance 2.0. We let one of them maintain a distinct “AI feel”, while the other is practically just like the real me.

— Jia Zhangke on Weibo, via @FrankYan2 on X (translation adjusted)

Sure, it was a major PR stunt, one that was designed to gain traction during the Lunar New Year holiday, when everyone in China was looking for ways to entertain themselves. But it was actually good; you can feel how the auteur’s touch comes through.

In the process, some essence was extracted from the director, encoded, and made reproducible. It was a couple of months ahead of what’s now taking place across industries, across borders: expertise is being separated from the body that holds it.

This week on the Zero Shot podcast

Hi! I am Vidhatri, the producer of Zero Shot.

My favourite part of this job happens before the recording. We have a “Zero Shot ideas” Slack channel, where Rohin, PGK, Brady, other colleagues, and I share newspaper articles, Substack posts, Mastodon posts, and really any media on AI. We’ve got everything on there, from people’s personal Claude projects to the similarities between China’s AI plans and The Great Leap Forward. It basically captures our collective attention and is honestly a gold mine of interesting—sometimes spicy—takes, with everyone’s personality coming through.

Like with all things AI, the future and what will happen with this rate of progress is something we constantly talk about—with great passion. And Rohin had been asking something for a while: which Indian companies will actually survive when agentic AI arrives?

We took that question to 180 people last Sunday (12 April) in our first-ever Zero Shot live event!

And they answered in real time through polls and questions that were then debated, dissected, and discussed by Muralikrishnan B, former president of Xiaomi India; Amod Malviya, co-founder of Udaan and former CTO of Flipkart; Anant Choubey, co-founder of Capillary; and Thomas Fenn, co-founder of Mahabelly and joint secretary of the National Restaurant Association of India.

One of the most invigorating sections of the event was when we put Cred through a live stress test in the agentic AI era.

That’s what the latest episode of Zero Shot is based on.
