Rapido broke the Uber-Ola duopoly. Can it now break the Swiggy-Zomato one? The bike is the same, but the battle might not be 
 By Uddantika Kashyap https://the-ken.com/writers/uddantikathe-ken-com/

This is either the most useful thing I have done this week or the most embarrassing. Possibly both, but I was committed to the research. I opened three food delivery apps, found the same restaurant on all three, and ordered the same meal to see what would happen. I chose Sharief Bhai Biryani, which falls under the umbrella of the house-of-brands food-tech company Curefoods. (We just published a First Principles episode with Ankit Nagori https://the-ken.com/podcasts/first-principles/curefoods-ankit-nagori-on-building-indias-house-of-food-brands-why-healthy-food-will-never-win-on-a-friday-night-and-what-flipkart-taught-him-about-talent-density/?utm_source=web&utm_medium=homepage&utm_campaign=horizontal_layout_First%20Principles, Curefoods’ CEO and co-founder.) My order was chicken boneless special biryani, al faham chicken wings, and one 330ml Coke. 

In spite of this being the same dish from the same restaurant, I got three very different bills:

Zomato: Rs 847
Swiggy: Rs 807
Ownly: Rs 567

For the exact same biryani.

I have always opened both Zomato and Swiggy every time I order food, compared which is cheaper that day, and switched based on which one has the better deal. It genuinely changes day to day, and I have zero loyalty to either of them (a reference to our Gen Z episode https://the-ken.com/podcasts/two-by-two/is-gen-z-tip-of-the-spear-for-consumer-brands-today/).

Last week, Rapido launched Ownly, a standalone food delivery app in Bengaluru that charges zero commission. So we asked a simpler question: Rapido destroyed one duopoly. Can it destroy another? 

Praveen Gopal Krishnan and Rahel Philipose sat down with Kunal Khattar https://www.linkedin.com/in/kkhattar/, one of Rapido’s earliest investors, and Gautam Balijepalli https://www.linkedin.com/in/balli/, founder of cloud-kitchen company Kouzina Food Tech, to discuss and debate the answer.

The same playbook, a different battlefield

Rapido now has 74 million https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/rapido-clocks-nearly-74-million-monthly-users-races-ahead-of-uber-and-ola-13858308.html monthly active users, more than Uber and Ola combined. This is a company that focused only on bike taxis, staying exclusively in Bengaluru for three years. Even Uber’s CEO publicly admitted that Rapido is their toughest competitor.

Kunal invested in Rapido in 2016, when nobody thought this was possible, and his fund has since returned 88X on that bet. When PGK asked him why he is confident about Ownly, his answer was grounded in something more than just optimism:

“Food delivery is a three-sided marketplace. Zomato and Swiggy had to solve for delivery, the customer, and the restaurant from ground zero. Rapido has already solved two of those three. All they have to do now is add restaurants. And adding B2B is significantly cheaper than acquiring supply and demand.“

How I see Rapido and Ownly’s strategy to take over my dabba

The most interesting insight I got was about the logistical advantage. Ride-hailing peaks in the morning and evening, while food delivery peaks at lunch and dinner. The same captain who drops you off at the office at 9 a.m. can deliver your biryani at 1 p.m. And so Rapido can keep its riders earning all day because of its pre-existing fleet.

Lightning never strikes twice. Or does it?

Ola and Uber were caught off guard because neither took the two-wheeler outlook seriously until it was too late. But Zomato and Swiggy are not going to be surprised. They are publicly listed companies with quarterly numbers, aggressive leadership, deep pockets, and every reason to fight back hard. PGK does not let Kunal off easy. “Swiggy and Zomato are not Ola and Uber. They will aggressively respond,” he says.

And then there is the consumer-behaviour problem, which is the most honest—and funniest—part of this episode.

Gautam, who runs over 400 cloud kitchens across 125 cities and is already listed on Ownly, set up the app, tried it, and then that same evening, opened Swiggy out of habit to place an order. His own daughter had to remind him that he had just downloaded Ownly.

It goes to show that consumer behaviour is a real wall. When you think food delivery, you think Swiggy or Zomato. Here, muscle memory is the barrier, not downloading a new app.

“That shift of the consumer is very important,” Gautam says. “It is going to be a little harder in this market for Rapido than it has been in ride hailing.“

Plot twist: Rapido does not need to win

Kunal does not say that Rapido will take over, only that it needs 20% market share to double its valuation. It does not need to beat Zomato and Swiggy; all it needs is the price-sensitive, repeat-order, same-restaurant-every-time customer.

And that person is me, or it might be you, too.

As a restaurateur, Gautam puts a number on when he will know this is serious: “When Ownly gets to about 20% of my business, I cannot ignore it.“

And while we were recording this episode, Zomato and Swiggy were busy making Ownly’s argument for it. Both platforms hiked their platform fees https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/after-zomato-swiggy-hikes-platform-fee-by-17-to-17-58-per-order-126032400903_1.html this week, Zomato by 19%, Swiggy by 17%. Both now sit at Rs 17.58 per order on top of everything else you’re already paying. Just consider this: Zomato’s platform fee has gone up 645% in under three years. 

It’s either a very bold move or a very dumb one. We do not know which yet. But it does mean they are very clearly positioning themselves as premium options. Which means they either have a plan, or they just handed Ownly exactly the customer they were looking for. 

Until then, I have a Rs 567 biryani order to place.

Will you switch apps to save Rs 280? And if not, what would it take?
