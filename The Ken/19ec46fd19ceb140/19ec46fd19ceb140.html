Zepto wants Rs 8,100 crore—and investors to trust the numbers it won’t disclose

Revenue is up 5X since FY24. So are losses. Yet the IPO filing leaves investors without key customer metrics and answers on FDI risks

Zepto is entering the public market with a top line matching Swiggy’s and nearly 50 million annual transacting users. However, its revenue also matches its losses, both up 5X since FY24. Despite tweaking its model slightly in 2025, experts believe that Zepto might still invite regulatory scrutiny under FEMA and FDI rules. The company is also not disclosing some of its key performance metrics, such as customer acquisition costs and monthly transacting users. [Read the full story]

Blackstone’s next big IPO looks built more for its own exit than the public’s entry

Horizon Industrial Parks is set to be India’s largest warehousing platform. But it’s saddled with Rs 6,700 crore of debt and over half of its 58 million square feet are still under construction

Blackstone is listing warehousing platform Horizon Industrial Parks in India as a regular equity stock. This is in spite of the fact that it gets 90% of its income from rent, a quintessential Reit trait. This is because Horizon is too unfinished and debt-laden to qualify as a Reit. Blackstone could have sliced off a part of its assets and listed them as a Reit separately; but it wanted the higher returns that come with an all-inclusive listing. [Read the full story]

Lendingkart’s last stand: a founder, a Temasek unit, an outdated deal, and a $600M wipeout

Harshvardhan Lunia and family have filed an NCLT petition alleging foul play by Temasek subsidiary Fullerton. The case will be heard on 18 June

The founders of Uwards, the fintech company Lendingkart acquired in 2024, are now pursuing legal proceedings against Lendingkart, seeking compensation of Rs 80–90 crore. Between Q1 and Q3 of FY25, Lendingkart’s valuation plummeted from $700 million to less than $100 million. As a result, Fullerton, its largest investor, ousted founder and then-CEO Harshvardhan Lunia and proceeded to acquire the company. The company had prioritised scale above quality of lending during its rapid expansion during Covid. Founder Harshvardhan Lunia in a petition filed with NCLT has requested the tribunal to hand over control to a court-appointed committee, alleging Fullerton’s motive to be “mala fide” and “oppressive”. Accenture Song’s biggest competitors are no longer just WPP, Publicis, and Dentsu, but everyone trying to blur the lines between marketing, technology, and consulting

As tech transforms ads and marketing, Accenture’s creative arm has prospered, raking in $20 billion in FY25, even as global ad agencies ramp up tech adoption. Despite a different DNA from traditional media groups like Publicis and WPP, it’s fighting for the same pie with different weapons. Song’s focus is skewed towards global clients, and rightfully so, as budget constraints and shorter contracts plague Indian brands. A creative expansion into India may be imminent, and Song is armed with the right tools for it, but so are its competitors. [Read the full story]
