Good Morning Ishan,
About 100 senior wealth managers last year agreed to both buy equity in Anirudha Taparia’s new wealth-management venture, Nexedge Capital, and work for it.
That was an unusual proposition in an industry where the next career move is often decided by the salary hike or bonus on offer. Taparia, a former 360 One executive, was asking bankers to become shareholders in the business they were joining.
He was also entering a market where there is increasingly more money to fight over. India’s ultra-high-net-worth population has grown 63% in five years, making it the world’s sixth-largest. Deloitte estimates that India’s wealth-management industry has a $1.6 trillion AUM growth opportunity by FY29.
The big ones are expanding through acquisitions. Kotak Mahindra agreed to acquire Deutsche Bank’s India wealth and retail business in June, while 360 One bought UBS’s onshore wealth arm last year. Newer firms are joining too. And all of them need bankers who can bring clients with them.
That makes the relationship manager a valuable bit of corporate property, especially in an industry where the attrition rate hovers around 40%. A banker can spend years building a book of wealthy clients and then move to another firm with some of that business in tow. Sachin Jain, co-founder of Mumbai-based Trigen Wealth, says moving even 20–30% of a banker’s AUM to a new platform is considered a win.
Taparia sees another problem with the conventional model, particularly at wealth managers within larger financial institutions…
