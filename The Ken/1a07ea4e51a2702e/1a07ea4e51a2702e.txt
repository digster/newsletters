How many times can you click “not interested” before your social media algorithm stops showing you AI-animated microdrama advertisements? Apparently not enough. 

It’s been my (ongoing) battle for the past few months to keep tales of rich CEOs chasing damsels in distress at bay. Presumably, the volume consumed by less-resistant viewers is substantially higher. Indians watch microdramas as much as they watch Reels, which is 80% of smartphone users, according to a report https://ads.sharechat.com/research-and-insights/report-on-state-of-microdrama-in-india by Sharechat, Moj, and Kantar. 

It got me thinking—where do the stories come from? With all the spotlight on distribution and consumption, the “supply chain” remains a mystery. 

To understand the answer, you first have to understand the mess. India’s microdrama supply chain is highly fragmented. Independent production houses pitch stories to distributors, but they lack budgets and time. Quality, of course, is not their main concern. 

This scattered ecosystem is where Pratilipi saw an opportunity to build a different kind of advantage.

A digital storytelling and self-publishing company, Pratilipi recently became the first Indian supplier for microdramas to Tiktok in May. Domestically, it has distributed to platforms like Jiohotstar’s Tadka and Amazon MX Player’s Fatafat. 

... 

As the industry shifts towards AI-generated microdramas, making a home on mainstream OTT platforms, the quality of source material matters more than ever. 

With Rs 53 crore revenue in Q1 FY27, up 86% year-on-year, Pratilipi is turning the licensing game into its golden goose.
