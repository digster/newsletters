As the crowds began to leave British rock band Coldplay’s concert in Mumbai last year, the crew had four hours to dismantle the production. The band’s next show was four days later in Ahmedabad, a 36-hour drive away in normal traffic, and much of the rig equipment was common for both shows. 

The odds were unfavourable. But Bookmyshow, the nearly 20-year-old ticketing app, which produced the event was prepared. It had conducted two GPS-tracked runs and convinced the police for a dedicated transport corridor to make the feat possible in 18 hours. 

Long story short, there’s a major problem in India’s live-entertainment business. Putting on a large-scale event means building the infrastructure from the ground up. Every single time.

And the economics are naturally hard when the cost of production is so high. 

“Ticket sales alone hardly ever cover this,” said Ishan Chokhawala, consulting partner at Atomx, a company that handles cashless payments and entry control for live events. “Out of 100 events, 10% probably make money.” Significant money only comes from sponsorships, as food and beverage income is also not sizable, he added. 

But Bookmyshow Live, the ticketing app’s live-entertainment arm, seems to be coming closer to cracking this code. 

In August, international private-equity giant KKR announced that it will be buying a minority stake in Bookmyshow; 6% for about $50 million, Reuters reports. KKR’s interest comes in light of the strong numbers the company recently posted.
