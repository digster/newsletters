NSE launched a great new gold product. Or did it? The Electronic Gold Receipts, announced on 4 May, are not being sold by brokers or quoted on the NSE, at least not yet.

Gold is back in the news again; it’s rarely been out of it for a while now.

Earlier this week, Prime Minister Narendra Modi urged Indians to stop buying the shiny yellow metal for a year. As if on cue, the government raised the import duty on gold from 6% to 15%, making it pricier. In any case, the massive rally in gold prices over the past couple of years has already made it mighty expensive. Whether more taxes will curb demand now is a multi-billion-dollar question. 

Interestingly, just days before the government moved to curb gold demand, the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE)—the country’s predominant bourse—launched an interesting new product. One that could change the way Indians buy gold. On 4 May, it introduced Electronic Gold Receipts (EGRs) to, as it announced https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-05/PR_cc_04052026_20260504160349.pdf?ref=finshots.in, “deepen India’s gold ecosystem”.

The new shiny thing

That Indians love gold is no secret. We own the largest stash in the world, with an estimated 25,000–35,000 tonnes stocked up in households, far more than many countries combined. We keep adding 700–800 tonnes each year, importing almost all of it. 

Gold is an evolutionary bug https://the-ken.com/kaching/all-that-glitter-are-gold-bonds-and-app-stores/ in the human brain, I wrote a few years back. More so in the Indian one.

It’s costing us now. The more we import, the more we spend dollars. That could further weaken the rupee, which has already been slipping badly, thanks to the war in West Asia. Gold remains among India’s biggest imports after crude oil. So the government is doing what it has to do. It could tighten the screws even more in the coming days to curb non-essential imports. Oil is essential. Gold, not so much. At least, not now, or so the government thinks.

Essential or not, we need better ways to organise the household stash. And EGRs may come in handy. 

Electronic gold receipts, as the full form suggests, are basically gold receipts in electronic form. These dematerialised securities https://finshots.in/markets/electronic-gold-receipts-explained/ represent ownership of physical gold stored in vaults accredited by the market regulator Sebi (Securities and Exchange Board of India). 

Each EGR is backed by physical gold and is tradable on the exchange. 

Those two factors make them a neat third option to invest in the yellow metal—beyond physical gold and paper gold (gold exchange-traded funds and gold mutual funds). 

The hitherto best option for paper gold—sovereign gold bonds https://the-ken.com/kaching/the-sovereign-gold-bond-tax-change-violates-a-golden-rule/—is no longer available for the most part (the government stopped issuing them in 2024, burdened by high payouts). Also, digital gold remains under a regulatory https://the-ken.com/kaching/digital-gold-in-regulatory-no-mans-land/ cloud.

In this context, can EGRs become the next best option? They definitely have potential. 

Unlike physical gold, investors in EGRs don’t have to worry about purity or safe storage. 

And unlike gold ETFs and funds, investors can exchange their EGRs for physical gold if they wish. Like ETFs, EGRs can be bought and sold on the stock exchange through brokers using demat accounts. The tax rules are also similar to ETFs. 

However, if you choose to convert an EGR into physical gold, a 3% GST (currently) will apply, similar to buying physical gold. 

On the flip side, EGRs come with additional costs for vault charges and delivery logistics. As Basunivesh puts it https://www.basunivesh.com/egr-gold-investment-india/?ref=finshots.in:

Its true target audience is investors who want the best of both worlds — digital safety with a physical delivery option — and who are comfortable with a slightly higher cost structure compared to pure financial gold products.

EGRs also allow conversion of physical gold lying idle in homes and temples into electronic receipts that can be traded on stock exchanges, and eventually converted into physical gold if the EGR holder so desires.  

The NSE has big ambitions for EGR as stated in the press release:

A transformative initiative designed to bring transparency, efficiency, and formalization to India’s massive, deeply tradition-rooted gold market.

[…]

[This] is expected to bridge the age-old gap between physical gold and the financial markets by offering a regulated, secure, and technologically advanced platform for trading in the precious commodity.

[…]

With this launch, NSE aims to create a robust and transparent ecosystem for gold trading, enabling efficient price discovery, improved market participation, and enhanced trust across stakeholders including jewellers, refiners, traders, and institutional investors.

The exchange also said that it witnessed a successful dematerialisation of a gold bar (1000 grams) into an Electronic Gold Receipt, confirming “the operational readiness of the NSE EGR framework and its ability to facilitate efficient, transparent, and delivery-backed trading in gold”.

…Is not shiny enough

Yet, even ten days after the grand announcement, there is no sign of EGRs on the NSE website or on brokerage platforms. While NSE documents https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/about-electronic-gold-receipts lay out various EGR options—from as low as 100 milligrams to 1 kg, in both 999 and 995 gold purity offerings—there are no quotes for the related tickers https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/electronic-gold-receipts-999-product-specifications on the exchange portal. 

Four leading stock brokers confirmed to The Ken that they have not enabled EGRs for investors.

One attributed this to the NSE not rolling out the product yet. Another said the NSE is offering it only through one depository (NSDL), so they cannot offer it. A third broker said there are dependencies on their order management system vendor, and they cannot give a timeline for launch. The fourth broker has no plan to launch EGRs at all. 

An NSE spokesperson said the onboarding of brokers is underway and that trading will start from 18 May, the coming Monday. 

The proof of the pudding will be in the eating. 

And history says it might not be eaten. The NSE is not the first to launch EGRs in the country. That badge goes to its smaller rival, BSE, which has had its own EGRs since 2022. The idea is not really novel. Sebi set the ball rolling on spot gold exchanges and EGRs in 2021. The smaller exchange was the first to pick up the ball and run.

But check the ticker on BSE’s portal. The traded quantity is a grand total of zero. No broker seems to be offering BSE EGRs even more than three years after launch. 

Could NSE EGRs have better luck if and when they really launch? Maybe, if they manage to increase awareness, get many brokers on board, and ensure enough liquidity for smooth and seamless transactions. Indian gold investors are looking for choices, options that offer more flexibility with the ability to convert digital gold into physical gold. 

The cherry on top? If NSE EGRs eventually manage to draw out even a fraction of the gold hoard from household lockers onto the stock exchange, it could be a win-win-win. Lesser imports, continued investments, and the government easing off on directives and taxes.

Golden age or gilded mirage?
