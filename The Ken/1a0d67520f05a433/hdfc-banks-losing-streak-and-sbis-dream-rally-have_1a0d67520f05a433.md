Good Morning Ishan,
HDFC Bank will soon have a new chief executive, and the market is loving it. The stock of India’s largest private-sector bank is up 6% over the fortnight since it began homing in on names to replace Sashidhar Jagdishan.
But plot this gain against the nearly 30% hammering the stock has taken since January, and the pop pales. Zoom out further, and HDFC Bank—once an eternal market favourite—has delivered negative returns to investors over three and five years, of 5% and 9%, respectively.
Meanwhile, State Bank of India (SBI), the country’s largest public-sector bank—and once a market laggard—has been on a roll. The stock has more than doubled over five years, risen over 60% over three years, and delivered positive returns across most periods, despite giving up a fifth of its gains since February.
Their divergent paths have also brought HDFC Bank and SBI closer in terms of market capitalisation, sharply shrinking the gap to Rs 2.2 lakh crore now, from over Rs 4 lakh crore in 2021. This even as HDFC, the lending entity, merged with HDFC Bank in 2023, propelling the latter’s valuation and increasing the gap with SBI to nearly Rs 6 lakh crore then.
The public-sector banking giant gaining more ground and stealing a march over its private-sector counterpart would be a pivotal moment for investors in Indian banking stocks. But as alluring as this appears, the tide is set to turn.
