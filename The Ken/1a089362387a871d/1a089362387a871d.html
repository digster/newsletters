In the middle of her pitch to get me to switch to Vodafone Idea (Vi) as a postpaid subscriber, a sales representative in West Delhi paused. And changed tack. 

“Did you know that even SRK has joined us now?” she asked, flashing the Shah Rukh Khan trump card she’d been carrying the whole time. After spending years fronting campaigns for Vi’s two biggest rivals, the Bollywood superstar had finally crossed over to Vi’s side. And that, the sales representative believed, had to count for something.

This isn’t all that’s new at the telecom operator. After years of hemorrhaging subscribers, Vi finally stopped the bleeding in February. The count has grown every month since, and currently sits at around 195 million. 

Competitors Airtel and Jio have been adding subscribers too—at a much faster clip. For instance, in the six months to July, Vi added about 700,000 subscribers whereas Airtel and Jio added 22 million and 14 million respectively. BSNL, meanwhile, added 250,000. It explains why Vi’s subscriber- and revenue-based market share has continued to fall.

That doesn’t take away from the fact that Vi seems to be going through a larger shift. 

In the annual general meeting in August, Kumar Mangalam Birla, Vi’s chairman, made it clear that FY27 would be the year of execution and delivery. The plan is to address “legacy challenges, restore financial flexibility, invest in network infrastructure, and rebuild momentum in the business”. And it’s visible on the ground.
