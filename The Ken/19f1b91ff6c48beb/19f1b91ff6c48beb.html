Sangita, a 36-year-old Asha worker (government-employed community health volunteer) in Delhi, has been spending her days conducting the 2027 Census on her smartphone, going door-to-door in temperatures exceeding 40°C. Every household visit requires completing a 33-question survey digitally, but the heat often causes phones to overheat.

“The census app doesn’t work properly on our phones,” said Sangita, who, like a couple of others quoted in the story, didn’t want her last name identified due to professional reasons. “It keeps glitching, and in this heat, the phone often hangs or goes blank.” 

She needs to replace it, but she’ll be entering a smartphone market where affordable phones are becoming harder to find.

It would be easy to blame the heat or an ageing handset. But something far larger is unfolding inside the smartphone industry. The memory chips that once kept budget phones affordable are becoming scarce as AI companies outbid consumer-electronics manufacturers for them, leaving phones either more expensive or stripped of features.

Sangita’s predicament comes at a time when the economics of affordable technology are being rewritten.
