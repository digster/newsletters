Good Morning Ishan,
On 7 August, a high-powered delegation from Swiggy landed in Bengaluru for an urgent discussion.
The 15 people in it ran from Swiggy’s CEO of food marketplace, Rohit Kapoor, to product heads, to city leads. They had all come for a sit-down with the National Restaurant Association of India (NRAI), which represents over 500,000 restaurants across the country.
The NRAI’s list of gripes against Swiggy was familiar. It included commissions running as high as 35–40% after accounting for ads and discounts, non-consensual discounting, and account managers who went missing when disputes arose. What was new, though, was that the restaurant body was now demanding specific operational changes. Mandatory OTP-based consent for every promotional discount. Refunds on unauthorised deductions. And a capped, written commission structure.
Significantly, this was accompanied by an ultimatum: if Swiggy didn’t resolve these issues by 1 September, kitchens across the country would start dropping off the platform.
The NRAI had been asking for this meeting for over a year—without much luck. What made Swiggy listen this time around was a new player in the game.
