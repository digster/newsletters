Never has one class of drugs moved markets, medical literature, companies, and country fates as GLP-1 has in the last two years. The drug, semaglutide, which works like magic for many in weight reduction and diabetes control, lost its patent protection on 20 March. More than 40 brands from as many companies in India will sell their generic version starting this week.
Chennai-based physician-scientist V Mohan calls this “unprecedented”.
“Even when drugs go off patent, there aren’t 50 companies anywhere in the world to produce them. You can imagine the cutthroat competition that’s going to come,” he said. About 10 generics makers have already approached him.
Originally meant for controlling sugar, the drug is now marketed to be effective in other metabolic conditions such as obesity, cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, fatty liver, [substance abuse](https://www.bmj.com/content/392/bmj-2025-086886), and polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS). A few other indications are under clinical trials. Looking at the drug makers’ response, it seems the Venn diagram of eligible users could cover nearly a third of the Indian population.
It’s like soon there will be people taking semaglutide, and there will be people looking at people taking semaglutide and wondering why they are not on it. The thin-fat Indian phenotype—even those with low body mass index have high body fat—studied for decades by the Pune-based clinician Chittaranjan Yajnik, is at the core of the initial pharma promotion.
Put simply, pharma companies believe most people could do with shedding a few kilograms.
One pharma company [says](https://www.cnbctv18.com/videos/business/companies/indian-pharma-weight-loss-drugs-onesource-eris-lifesciences-shaily-engineering-19868331.htm) it’ll bring down the price, from the innovators’ range of Rs 10,000–26,000 for a month to Rs 400–500/week (or Rs 2000/month) within a year.
Some doctors are calling it a “statin” moment for India. A drug that the then-pharma industry believed was good enough, metaphorically speaking, to be mixed in water and given for the prevention of cardiovascular issues.
