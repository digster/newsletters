On a lane in Gurugram’s Udyog Vihar industrial area sits a two-storey office of Optimist, the startup that’s positioning itself as a challenger to India’s air-conditioner giants.

The building doesn’t quite resemble an R&D unit. Inside it, however, two rooms are devoted solely to research and development. In one room, the company’s engineers are monitoring the readings of the internal unit of an Optimist AC. In the other, they’re tracking its cooling efficiency and the effect of temperature changes on its external unit.

For a company that launched its first product just a few weeks ago, in the second half of May, this may seem too nerdy a project.

But it’s not without reason. Optimist is trying to build an air-conditioner brand from scratch in a market dominated by companies with decades of manufacturing expertise, dealer relationships, financing partnerships, and service networks.

Its debut product has solid credentials. The 1.4-tonne split AC has the highest energy-efficiency rating among household ACs currently sold in India, according to the company. More than 90% of its components are sourced locally, too. It also uses a heat exchanger that’s uncommon in other air-conditioners.

Its founders, Ashish Goel of Urban Ladder-fame and Pranav Chopra, believe these engineering decisions matter because India is facing a cooling conundrum.
