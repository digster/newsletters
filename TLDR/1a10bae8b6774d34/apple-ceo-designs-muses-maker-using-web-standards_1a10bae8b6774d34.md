Pretraining on 50,000 Hours of Unlabeled Brain Data (10 minute read)

These decoders are unique to each user - all users have to perform calibration prior to using a Neuralink device. Neuralink has collected over 50,000 hours of unlabeled, freeform neural data since its clinical trials began. This post details how the company used this data to improve its models, making its decoders more accurate and long-lived.

Things that Apparently Cause Cancer (12 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.breakthroughjournal.org%2Fp%2Fthings-that-apparently-cause-cancer%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a10bae895f-eb84173b-27cc-421b-8258-274cb7abfc9e-000000/VjaCvxz9AIGLlt7sMRMVV_YMDlUppWs90jzrunUtTDk=452)
The series of Harvard studies that showed that nuclear power plants were associated with higher rates of cancer and cancer mortality are nonsense. The methodology used in the studies will always show an increased cancer risk and mortality no matter what landmark is used. The studies simply do not prove anything about cancer incidence. Sophisticated statistics can produce extremely precise nonsense when the design fails to identify the causal mechanism.

e2e (Website)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Ftester.army%2Fe2e%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a10bae895f-eb84173b-27cc-421b-8258-274cb7abfc9e-000000/1tfw06z1VF-PRvSZnINWPwwxnP7wzQ52hApoAXs8Kk0=452)
e2e is an open-source AI testing framework. It provides customizable testing for web, mobile apps, and more. e2e remembers agents' actions and replays them when possible without calling the model again. Less repeated reasoning results in fewer tokens and lower costs. Developers can choose their preferred AI model and customize the testing agent.

Why don't more developers “use the platform”? (11 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fnolanlawson.com%2F2026%2F10%2F03%2Fwhy-dont-more-developers-use-the-platform%2F%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a10bae895f-eb84173b-27cc-421b-8258-274cb7abfc9e-000000/ZMa7Z5dZkxNQnhbL8hH1jfah6l_BKShRPhoyNTk7KfY=452)
Advocates for web standards, performance, and accessibility have implored web developers for years to 'use the platform', but despite how obvious the advice seems to be, many people seem to still need convincing. One reason for this is that, for the longest time, browsers were playing catch-up with the ecosystem on top of them. Another is due to the familiarity with tools they've used in the past. Often, even with a robust standard, libraries fill a useful gap between framework ergonomics and the platform underneath it.

David Robinson resigned from OpenAI this week, joining a parade of former colleagues who have decided that the company's current path is unacceptable. Robinson headed the writing of the safety reports that OpenAI publishes with each major launch. OpenAI has thrived by trial and error, but this approach guarantees periodic failures, and the scale of those failures is growing. Achieving something much closer to perfect the first time is becoming more essential because iteration after a mistake may not be possible.
