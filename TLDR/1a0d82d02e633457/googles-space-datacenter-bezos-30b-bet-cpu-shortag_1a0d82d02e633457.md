Google's first Suncatcher orbital data center test launches October 1 (3 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Farstechnica.com%2Fgoogle%2F2026%2F09%2Fgoogles-first-suncatcher-orbital-data-center-test-launches-october-1%2F%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0d82d00da-ebcace3a-abe2-4e82-a156-558c4e5427ac-000000/-5EAGspwb6n_9v6-2lHA54drDRnmkDfevwy8Aj1obLs=452)
Project Suncatcher is a moonshot effort by Google to design orbital AI data centers. Its first experimental satellite will launch on October 1. Its aim will be to validate Google's vision for a constellation of AI satellites. The satellite, about the size of a refrigerator, has four custom TPU AI accelerators and solar panels that supply about one kilowatt of power. It will serve as a test of several technologies over its few months of operation.

The commitment has helped Blue Origin become a large space company with around 15,000 employees and an ambitious growth plan. Blue Origin recently raised capital from outside investors for the first time. The company expects to produce about $1.4 billion in revenue this year, growing that number to more than $30 billion in 2030.

A new trend of CPU shortages (10 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fblog.pragmaticengineer.com%2Fthe-pulse-a-new-trend-of-cpu-shortages%2F%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0d82d00da-ebcace3a-abe2-4e82-a156-558c4e5427ac-000000/hnl2QGjPckEgpEj2IPBA9YUVTQxRMWKUgIxQ2LsA7gI=452)
Many companies are struggling to source CPUs. CPU spot pricing has disappeared because there's no longer any lack of demand for CPUs. It's now nearly impossible to get CPUs on spot instances without long-running connections with cloud providers. Reserving specific CPUs now needs to be done months in advance. Cloud providers will even turn down certain reservations because they don't have enough CPUs or the right type of CPUs.

Thinking in Systems, Shipping in Loops (3 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Ftomtunguz.com%2Fthinking-in-systems%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0d82d00da-ebcace3a-abe2-4e82-a156-558c4e5427ac-000000/bVpB6wgD-lyYLyS_tkgkXn9tf62mZVcsxcdboNNz6ws=452)
AI has removed the typing from software engineering but left the system design. What's left is designing the loop that decides whether the output is right. Great system design is defined by resilience, self-organization, and hierarchy. Resilience is AI checking its own work, self-organization is the loop learning, and hierarchy is the layering of skills, tools, and sandboxes into composable components that are reused once verified to work.

The Specter Of Neuralese (20 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.astralcodexten.com%2Fp%2Fthe-specter-of-neuralese%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0d82d00da-ebcace3a-abe2-4e82-a156-558c4e5427ac-000000/wMi-KLH9cCaVQmxveKjfB2HInUv7jBS5HJFNumF59y8=452)
Neuralese recurrence is a hypothetical technology that would allow AIs to think dangerous thoughts without getting detected. OpenAI's new Astra model uses a reasoning technique called recurrent depth that makes the model's chain of thought more difficult to monitor by allowing it to operate outside of the sequential thinking that characterizes most reasoning models. There is an emerging taboo on creating dangerous neuralese AIs. It is unclear whether OpenAI broke that taboo, as a clear line has yet to be drawn.
