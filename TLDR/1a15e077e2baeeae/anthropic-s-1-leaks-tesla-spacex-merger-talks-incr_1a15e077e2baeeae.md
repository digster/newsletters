How Claude designed protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins (16 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.anthropic.com%2Fresearch%2Fclaude-scorpion-toxin-binder-design%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a15e077ded-fcbbeadc-364a-4dba-a4eb-542fd2ce70ed-000000/BUZPJye24mt7VCI9sO_k5yFJdF0Jrxm6VqxAw7EWynU=452)
Anthropic asked an internal research model to design mini binders, small proteins that attach tightly to a target, against scorpion neurotoxins as part of its work in experimenting with how Claude can accelerate drug discovery. 17 of the 211 designs tested met Anthropic's criteria. Anthropic has not confirmed whether these binders neutralize scorpion venom in a cellular environment, so it is not claiming to have produced an antivenom. However, the study is a promising first step towards using AI to design credible binders against hard-to-target toxins in days, with lab confirmation within weeks. The challenge, as always, will be turning those binders into safe, affordable, and widely accessible drugs.

How To Use Decision Models for Evals (23 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fhamel.dev%2Fblog%2Fposts%2Fdecision-model-evals%2F%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a15e077ded-fcbbeadc-364a-4dba-a4eb-542fd2ce70ed-000000/T9usZJaikjR7mja_DlmAndVDZ6wVQeHK4K1RHc5w5GM=452)
Decision models are an appealing alternative to LLM judges for automated evals. However, whether they're the right choice depends on the cost, performance, and latency requirements of the use case. Many people leave the default decision threshold at 0.50 without checking whether that's appropriate for the task, leaving a lot of performance on the table. This post shows how to tune thresholds to get the best results from decision models, compares cost and performance between models, and explores how to interpret model scores and how calibration can make them more useful.
