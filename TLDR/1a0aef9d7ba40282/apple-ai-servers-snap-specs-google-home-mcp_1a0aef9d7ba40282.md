Apple reportedly building server packed with M-series Ultra chips for AI (2 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Farstechnica.com%2Fai%2F2026%2F09%2Fapple-reportedly-building-server-packed-with-m-series-ultra-chips-for-ai%2F%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0aef9d5ee-f3bc99fa-fb66-4138-8c9f-52dc9f85ace6-000000/dPwqZ3bm2usLi1ivRVb_ew9Hyh7wsKiQu2ftsjeV30k=452)
Apple is working on an AI server powered by its M-series Ultra chips. It is expected to be released in 2029. The enterprise server will come with either two or four M8 Ultra chips. The project, which received support from John Ternus when it began a year ago, back when Ternus led Apple's hardware engineering efforts, will be Apple's first server to hit the market in nearly two decades.

Mice with human brain cells offer a tool to study disease. Ethicists ask: What's next? (7 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.npr.org%2F2026%2F09%2F16%2Fnx-s1-5968873%2Flab-mouse-human-brain-cells-organoid-disease-ethics%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0aef9d5ee-f3bc99fa-fb66-4138-8c9f-52dc9f85ace6-000000/VOVkxsItPoLRR5V4uL5td6oAvI3J1HtidpVYTIsthak=452)
Researchers have developed a technique for installing millions of human brain cells into mice. The scientists bred mice that were missing most of their cerebral cortexes and then replaced a large part of those missing mouse cells with lab-grown human brain cells. This resulted in mice with human neurons that could be altered to approximate human brain conditions. The technique could potentially create better scientific models for studying neurodegenerative disease.

Introducing Home MCP: enabling your agent to interact with your home (2 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fsupport.google.com%2Fgooglehome%2Fthread%2F467705013%2Fintroducing-home-mcp-enabling-your-agent-to-interact-with-your-home%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0aef9d5ee-f3bc99fa-fb66-4138-8c9f-52dc9f85ace6-000000/jJhPnX_yVosB6dHto8EFLufSE5HRvpEyCrAjwgifnWc=452)
Google has opened early access to Model Context Protocol for Google Home in English for Google Home Premium Advanced users in the US. Any agent that supports calling MCP tools can now interact with all of the devices and event history in the Google Home ecosystem. The MCP allows agents to analyze and answer questions about home activity, summarize camera footage across rooms, manage devices, and create smart home dashboards using everyday language. It contains safety protections, such as prohibiting sensitive actions like unlocking doors, but depending on the agent, using the MCP can result in unexpected or even undesired behavior.

Training a 4B model to produce 81% faster query plans than Postgres (66 minute read)
](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Frohanbansal.com%2Fqorl%3Futm_source=tldrnewsletter/1/010001a0aef9d5ee-f3bc99fa-fb66-4138-8c9f-52dc9f85ace6-000000/wl3CvpxyLX8VMDfLSKQduv2Vj1_MqgULFe_ib81WZZU=452)
Query optimizers continue to leave much to be desired despite an enormous body of research. A Postgres database should know everything about the stuff that lives in its tables, but the problem is enormously hard. What's not as hard is verifying whether a query plan an optimizer picks is good or not. Language models are particularly good at learning how to do tasks with easily verifiable outputs. A small, open-weights model can be post-trained via supervised fine-tuning and agentic reinforcement learning to produce Postgres query plans that beat Postgres' default plans.
