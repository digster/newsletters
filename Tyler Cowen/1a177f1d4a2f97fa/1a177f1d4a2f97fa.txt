Cowen’s strongest recommendations include 1491 ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/1400032059?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , The Power Broker ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0394720245?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , Knausgaard’s first two My Struggle ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0374534144?tag=marginalrevol-20 )  volumes, Fuchsia Dunlop’s Chinese cookbooks ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/1324004835?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , and Meetings with Remarkable Manuscripts ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/1594206112?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) . These stand out through unusually strong praise, repeated endorsement, or both. The analysis uses qualitative, book-specific sentiment analysis: explicit superlatives, strength of recommendation, repeat endorsements, and evidence that a book changed his thinking or behavior. Qualifications and discussion context also matter. The result is a top tier, not a definitive ordinal ranking—Cowen rarely compares these books directly.

Charles C. Mann, 1491 ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/1400032059?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of my favorite books ever, in any field.” May 22, 2011 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2011/05/1493.html ) .
Among his clearest unrestricted endorsements. The post reviews 1493 ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0307278247?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , but this particular praise explicitly concerns 1491.

Robert Caro, The Power Broker ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0394720245?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of the best non-fiction books ever.” April 15, 2012 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2012/04/the-economics-of-robert-caro.html ) .
An enduring benchmark: in 2026, he uses Caro’s biography of Moses as the standard against which to praise Skidelsky.

Karl Ove Knausgaard, My Struggle, volumes 1 ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0374534144?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) –2 ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0374534152?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
He calls the second volume “one of the greatest novels ever written” and selects it as his fiction book of the year. November 16, 2013 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2013/11/best-fiction-books-of-2013-2.html ) .
Particularly strong evidence of durability: both volumes return in his 2022 favorites ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2022/04/the-best-fiction-in-recent-times.html )  and 2024 best-since-2000 selections ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2024/07/the-ten-best-books-since-2000.html ) .

Fuchsia Dunlop, Land of Plenty ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0393051773?tag=marginalrevol-20 )  / its expanded successor The Food of Sichuan ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/1324004835?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of the best and most valuable books ever produced.” July 2, 2019 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/07/the-food-of-sichuan-by-fuchsia-dunlop.html ) .
Exceptionally strong praise across genres, reinforced by years of use and discussion. His enthusiasm extends to Every Grain of Rice ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0393089045?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) : “I will buy everything she writes, forever.” February 3, 2013 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2013/02/every-grain-of-rice.html ) .

Christopher de Hamel, Meetings with Remarkable Manuscripts ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/1594206112?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
A possible number-one book of 2016 becomes “one of the very greatest books of the last twenty years.” November 5, 2016 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2016/11/meetings-remarkable-manuscripts.html ) ; June 22, 2026 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/06/the-migrants-a-memoir-with-manuscripts.html ) .
One of the strongest examples of an enthusiastic initial judgment surviving a decade.

Robert Skidelsky, three-volume biography of Keynes ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/014023554X?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of the very best books ever written.” April 16, 2026 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/04/robert-skidelsky-rip.html ) .
An explicit all-time endorsement. He specifically prefers the three-volume biography to the one-volume condensation.

John Stuart Mill, The Subjection of Women ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0486296016?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of the very best books ever written, on any topic.” September 11, 2022 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2022/09/the-new-bryan-caplan-book.html ) .
Strong recommendation plus intellectual influence: he says he drew his views from Mill and urges everyone to read it.

Tony Judt, Postwar ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0143037757?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of my favorite books ever.” February 6, 2012 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2012/02/tony-judts-new-book-thinking-the-twentieth-century.html ) .
Important attribution trap: the surrounding post is a mixed review of a different Judt book ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0143123041?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) . A whole-post sentiment score would understate his enthusiasm for Postwar.

Robert Cialdini, Influence ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/006124189X?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of my all-time favorite books.” February 26, 2007 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2007/02/affiliation.html ) .
A particularly direct early recommendation, embedded in a discussion of affiliation and persuasion.

M. A. Orthofer, The Complete Review Guide to Contemporary World Fiction ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0231146752?tag=marginalrevol-20 )
“one of the best books written, ever”—conditional on its practical value to the reader. April 10, 2016 ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2016/04/the-complete-review-guide-to-contemporary-world-fiction.html ) .
His behavioral evidence is striking: the manuscript prompted him to order about forty books. Best suited to avid fiction readers.

What the sentiment and discussion reveal
Repeated enthusiasm is more persuasive than a spectacular adjective. Knausgaard, Dunlop, and de Hamel have particularly strong longitudinal evidence. But precision matters: Cowen’s enthusiasm for Knausgaard’s first two volumes should not be transferred uniformly to the whole series. His September 8, 2018 discussion ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/09/sixth-final-volume-knausgaards-struggle.html )  describes volumes 3–5 as often unsatisfying. He values books that change how he sees or does things. Orthofer changes his reading purchases; Dunlop changes his cooking; The Conflict Shoreline changes his conception of what a book can accomplish. His strongest praise often concerns explanatory power and intellectual usefulness. Reader discussion is useful context, but a poor ranking instrument. In a spot-check of ten recommendation threads, the post praising Mill had 163 archived comments, while the Cialdini endorsement had four. The former centered on a controversial contemporary debate. Comment volume measures attention to the post, not necessarily enthusiasm for the recommended book. Influence deserves its own category. His March 16, 2010 list of formative books ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2010/03/books-which-have-influenced-me-most.html )  includes Hayek’s Individualism and Economic Order ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0226320936?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , Keynes’s General Theory ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0230004768?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , Parfit’s Reasons and Persons ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/019824908X?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , Paglia’s Sexual Personae ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0679735798?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) , and Proust’s Remembrance of Things Past ( https://www.amazon.com/dp/0812969642?tag=marginalrevol-20 ) . For understanding Cowen’s intellectual development, that list is more directly relevant than his annual favorites; for finding books he recommends with exceptional enthusiasm, the top-tier table above is the stronger starting point.
