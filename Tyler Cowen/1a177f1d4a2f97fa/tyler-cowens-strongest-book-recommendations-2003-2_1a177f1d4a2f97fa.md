What the sentiment and discussion reveal
Repeated enthusiasm is more persuasive than a spectacular adjective. Knausgaard, Dunlop, and de Hamel have particularly strong longitudinal evidence. But precision matters: Cowen’s enthusiasm for Knausgaard’s first two volumes should not be transferred uniformly to the whole series. His [September 8, 2018 discussion](https://marginalrevolution.com?action=user_content_redirect&uuid=b39a0e5b09ec654c8af752ce0fdd2719383a47dea5d0ef709aab726d9652ddcc&blog_id=42693868&post_id=94049&user_id=262258391&subs_id=225460721&signature=a23b05bb32b8a3e8b54581f615add06e&email_name=new-post&user_email=ishan.mail@gmail.com&encoded_url=aHR0cHM6Ly9tYXJnaW5hbHJldm9sdXRpb24uY29tL21hcmdpbmFscmV2b2x1dGlvbi8yMDE4LzA5L3NpeHRoLWZpbmFsLXZvbHVtZS1rbmF1c2dhYXJkcy1zdHJ1Z2dsZS5odG1s&email_id=b19908addd9aa035c24cae4d5055bedb) describes volumes 3–5 as often unsatisfying.
