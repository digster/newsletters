Post       : Michael Woodford, Nobel Laureate

Author     : Tyler Cowen
Categories : Economics

I think of Michael Woodford as the kind of economist who studies something very specific very intensively and tries to get it just right.  But in his case he is not studying something small and irrelevant, he is studying "monetary policy."

I think of him as the economist who put monetary policy and "monetarism" -- broadly construed -- back on the map by melding it with modern theory and also modern approaches to expectations.  Think of his monetarism as for a world where interest rates and not the quantity of money are central for central bank policy.  In his world there is some nominal stickiness, and central banks can help to stabilize output without running afoul of the Lucas critique or the other problems with earlier Keynesian approaches.  He helped to enable and flesh out what macroeconomics call "the neoclassical-Keynesian synthesis."

He wrote a massive 2003 book Interest and Prices: Foundations of a Theory of Monetary Policy ( https://www.amazon.com/Interest-Prices-Foundations-Theory-Monetary/dp/0691010498/ref=sr_1_1?crid=IA3UFO9B8VRA&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.0tqpXNw9Z_MtIReZn3yhrFMpRz3DsPybbRfdohY3WKWS6uGC7ocIYsj0b_dozQIVIA3Lh1R2XwJC3AW76BQqB6LMcDg942Aliy2wXdliuA1sA5lzVj4uZimFrAOkuMuXKAsPwVlYys2Pet_gBdsxjg.qG3AincG-yiyfKkdVjDKIlkqQFYnusk5PVIr_KZYebg&dib_tag=se&keywords=Interest+and+Prices%3A+Foundations+of+a+Theory+of+Monetary+Policy&nsdOptOutParam=true&qid=1791807617&sprefix=interest+and+prices+foundations+of+a+theory+of+monetary+policy%2Caps%2C187&sr=8-1 ) , note the title is taken from Wicksell.
