The author is Omri Ben-Shahar, and the subtitle is How Privacy Panic Led Tech Regulation Astray -- and How to Fix It ( https://www.amazon.com/Fear-Data-Privacy-Regulation-Astray_and/dp/0674297865/ref=sr_1_1?crid=B5HHB3F7Y8I5&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.78uNZS_3eBwDup6cnldbOJ4DNcCOzWmuWKkaJWeEGUi7JgMuFpgG26B0dk7NOHATcnt7GMaJ9QF0DpUsk98d7H8IJPSMy8pr27wlR5M8pg3CLs_-G7Bz1aobtxg_HTeizJBCqJC7G0GWrjPaxoP7dw.Jj3PZMGvW1p3XOPhM-lj0QU7lSHAP9wKfk4fHtDAZTo&dib_tag=se&keywords=Omri+Ben-Shahar&qid=1790086971&sprefix=%2Caps%2C215&sr=8-1 ) .  I would describe this book as bracing, and full of substantive engagement.  Basically the author wishes to give privacy considerations less weight in social decisions.  Excerpt:

> What is the concrete evidence for the benefits of facial recognition technology in investigation of  human-trafficking crimes?  I would love to have found global estimates of the magnitude -- of the trafficking victims rescued through the most advanced facial recognition methods -- but all I have is a collage of reports [reports are then described].

One chapter is entitled "The Futility of Personal Rights."  Agree or not, this book is full of actual arguments, so I approve.
