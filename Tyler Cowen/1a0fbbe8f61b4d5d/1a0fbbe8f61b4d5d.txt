Yes I will be doing a Conversation with him.  Looking at Wikipedia ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Griffiths_(cognitive_scientist) ) :

> Thomas L. Griffiths (born c. 1978) is an Australian academic who is the Henry R. Luce Professor of Information Technology, Consciousness, and Culture at Princeton University ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University ) . He studies human decision-making and its connection to problem-solving methods in computation. His book with Brian Christian ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Christian ) , Algorithms to Live By: The Computer Science of Human Decisions ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms_to_Live_By:_The_Computer_Science_of_Human_Decisions ) , was named one of the "Best Books of 2016" by MIT Technology Review ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Technology_Review ) ...
>
> Griffiths released The Laws of Thought: The Quest for a Mathematical Theory of the Mind in 2026. Siobhan Roberts describes it as "a rigorous and captivating account of how cognition can be modeled via three mathematical frameworks: logic ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic ) , artificial neural networks ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network )  (“mathematical systems that emulate the operation of the brain”) and probability theory ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_theory ) ."

Here is his research page ( https://cocosci.princeton.edu/tom/index.php ) , here is his new book on Amazon ( https://www.amazon.com/Laws-Thought-Quest-Mathematical-Theory/dp/1250358353/ref=sr_1_1?crid=H4XWV4ZGD3V8&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.XDyJOz9yvJk5VYesu6YS_EdOxq1ZescrkH0p2WZthmKU_7_EoREoxrs0-kC5DqvpCmMJlT1_TPeEZtaCymi8Fw.wZKxDeChmL0j3brmax15eGgzsbSSrHyIeCsH0QezdX4&dib_tag=se&keywords=tom+griffiths+laws+of+thought&qid=1790818578&sprefix=tom+griffiths%2Caps%2C615&sr=8-1 ) , I thought the book was excellent.
