Post       : Pro-Development Environmentalists
URL        : https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/04/pro-development-environmentalists.html
Posted     : April 30, 2026 at 7:18 am
Author     : Alex Tabarrok
Categories : Current Affairs, Economics, Law

> The Breakthrough Institute (BTI) found that “just 10 organizations initiated 35% of the total NEPA cases brought by NGOs.” The Sierra Club and its local chapters alone were responsible for more than 14% of these lawsuits. The dominance of a small number of groups is more pronounced in forest management and energy cases; only 10 groups filed 67% and 48% of these cases, respectively. In BTI’s “The Procedural Hangover: How NEPA Litigation Obstructs Critical Projects” ( https://thebreakthrough.org/issues/energy/the-procedural-hangover )  follow-up, which expanded the analysis to district and circuit court NEPA cases, Alliance for the Wild Rockies and the Center for Biological Diversity were responsible for 24% of all litigation against public lands management decisions.
>
> To paraphrase Alex Tabarrok, federal environmental agencies seem to exist to manage the obsessions of a tiny number of neurotic—and possibly malicious—environmental NGOs.

Grant Mulligan's excellent post ( https://www.grantmulligan.com/p/environmentalists-arent-anti-abundance?utm_source=post-email-title&publication_id=2780167&post_id=195701197&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=70gnqy&triedRedirect=true&utm_medium=email )  confirms in detail what critics have long suspected: yes, environmental groups do use the courts to block projects—including environmental projects. But Mulligan finds that a disproportionate share of the lawsuits come from a small handful of not especially well-funded organizations. A textbook case of the tyranny of the complainers ( https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/01/the-tyranny-of-the-complainers.html ) .

Many environmental groups are not reflexively anti-development.

> What are the largest environmental groups doing with their money if not suing to stop development? Two of the three biggest, the Wildlife Conservation Society and San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, primarily operate zoos. Land trusts ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_trust )  like TNC, The Conservation Fund, and Ducks Unlimited protect land directly. Many also work on research and policy to varying degrees. Contrary to the typical narrative, many operate pro-market, abundance-style projects.
>
> TNC has several programs that align with the abundance agenda. TNC’s Power of Place ( https://www.nature.org/en-us/what-we-do/our-priorities/tackle-climate-change/climate-change-stories/power-of-place/ )  research and policy work is aimed at facilitating the build-out of renewable energy and transmission infrastructure. The idea behind the research is to identify and speed the permitting and development of renewable energy projects that won’t interfere with important conservation areas. The Bureau of Land Management (BLM) used the research as part of its Western Solar Plan, which aims to promote solar development on public land. TNC also wants permitting reform ( https://www.nature.org/en-us/about-us/who-we-are/how-we-work/policy/permitting-reform-policy/ ) , and their mapping ( https://www.nature.org/en-us/what-we-do/our-priorities/tackle-climate-change/climate-change-stories/power-of-place/ )  efforts are an example of what environmentalism that builds could look like — identify critical habitats that need protecting and guard them closely while unleashing building everywhere else.4 ( https://www.grantmulligan.com/p/environmentalists-arent-anti-abundance?utm_source=post-email-title&publication_id=2780167&post_id=195701197&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=70gnqy&triedRedirect=true&utm_medium=email#footnote-4 )
>
> While the tyrannical minority has held up forest management projects, TNC has been an advocate and practitioner of forest thinning and prescribed burns to prevent catastrophic wildfires for more than 60 years ( https://www.nature.org/en-us/what-we-do/our-priorities/protect-water-and-land/land-and-water-stories/why-we-work-with-fire/ ) . In California, they’re part of a coalition working to thin millions of acres ( https://www.wsj.com/articles/facing-deadlier-fires-california-tries-something-new-more-logging-1542390642 )  of overgrown forests.
>
> TNC isn’t alone. Audubon’s renewables siting ( https://www.audubon.org/our-work/climate/clean-energy )  work, Ducks Unlimited’s water infrastructure ( https://www.ducks.org/newsroom/ducks-unlimited-completes-critical-renovation-of-infrastructure-key-for-healthy-great-salt-lake-wetlands )  projects, and the Conservation Fund’s Working Lands programs ( https://www.conservationfund.org/focus-areas/working-lands/ )  all follow the same pattern of balancing environmental protections with economic imperatives. Plenty of green groups agree, as Larry Selzer, Conservation Fund’s President and CEO, says in Abundance by Ezra Klein ( https://open.substack.com/users/113351-ezra-klein?utm_source=mentions )  and Derek Thompson ( https://open.substack.com/users/157561-derek-thompson?utm_source=mentions ) , “we have to build, and build, and build.”
>
> I’m not trying to defend all the choices of TNC or suggest that the big environmental NGOs don’t promote their share of bad policies. I had plenty of discussions with degrowthers when I worked at TNC that made me want to pull my hair out. I’ve also written about the need for environmentalism to be more positive-sum ( https://www.grantmulligan.com/p/positive-sum-environmentalism )  in frustration over zero-sum environmental positions. But on the whole, environmentalists have been made too convenient a villain by abundance advocates. Environmentalists aren’t as uniformly obstructionist, degrowth, and misanthropic as commonly believed.5 ( https://www.grantmulligan.com/p/environmentalists-arent-anti-abundance?utm_source=post-email-title&publication_id=2780167&post_id=195701197&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=70gnqy&triedRedirect=true&utm_medium=email#footnote-5 )

Understanding that only a vocal minority of environmentalists are anti-progress procedural complainers is important because abundance advocates and environmentalists aren't natural enemies—and assuming they are serves neither side.
