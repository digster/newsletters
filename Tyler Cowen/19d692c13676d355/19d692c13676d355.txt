Post       : Andy Hall advice on AI and economic research

Author     : Tyler Cowen

Here is the document ( https://docs.google.com/document/d/1fyQ5brVo3AUo0epoL-cYubYw7WayDYQBXQNNqnTckmE/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.ofuc1efn78la ) , excerpt:

> In January, I released the results of an experiment ( https://x.com/ahall_research/status/2009658499030991172?s=20 )  showing how Claude Code could helpfully extend old papers “automagically.” It was pretty astonishing to me. Claude was able to come up with a plan, scrape the web, write code, run regressions, create tables and figures, and write a whole memo on what it had found—all in about 45 minutes.
>
> Are AI tools perfect? No. Claude made some interesting mistakes in that extension, and since then, I’ve seen it make a whole bunch more. Are human researchers perfect, though? Hell no. 
>
> The evidence that AI tools should now be an essential part of your toolkit is overwhelming—look at the recent work that my Stanford colleague Yiqing Xu has put out ( https://www.leoyang.org/publication/ai_reproducibility/ ) , for example, which allows for the automated verification of empirical research. This is so clearly valuable. When it comes to empirical work, we’re never going back to the pre-AI world.

Here is a thread on the paper ( https://x.com/ahall_research/status/2041170325917094323?s=20 ) , heedworthy throughout.  If you do not have some kind of decent plan here, other economists will leave you in the dust.  Even if it is only a minority of "other economists" their total leverage and impact will be extreme.
