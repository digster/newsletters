In October 1982, then-Burlington Mayor Bernie Sanders wrote a letter [ https://substack.com/redirect/bf518959-18fe-44b8-a77f-591413f7dfc9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] to President Ronald Reagan warning him that nuclear weapons could “destroy all life on the planet” and imploring him to “think courageously and boldly” toward ridding the world of these weapons. Now, in what is likely his last term in public office, Sanders is reprising the existential rhetoric he once brought to nuclear war — this time, for artificial intelligence.
Sanders recently announced plans to introduce legislation for a federal moratorium [ https://substack.com/redirect/8a13fff8-1761-4f0c-82b8-f85fb02525eb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] on data center construction. Data center moratoriums have been introduced [ https://substack.com/redirect/cb190733-cd41-4224-b15b-a93976b40692?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] in at least 12 state legislatures, so he’s not the first person to do so. But he’s one of the few to ground such a proposal in AI safety terms, and I don’t think that framing is spurious. Sanders’ recent work [ https://substack.com/redirect/4999ea08-64cc-4d0d-bd1d-70af91a90595?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] on AI labor displacement — and his conversations with AI thinkers such as Geoffrey Hinton [ https://substack.com/redirect/be379374-ef58-4f44-a752-d2d5efb9ae84?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] and Eliezer Yudkowsky [ https://substack.com/redirect/34914e66-477b-4a8d-9845-67d85126d3ba?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] — evince a sincere concern for the immediate and long-term risks of AI.
In data centers, Sanders imagines he’s found a vehicle for broader AI governance ambitions. These are visible, literally concrete structures that play on a trifecta of grievances: rising electric bills, anxiety over AI and its corporate creators, and environmental concerns.
I share many of Sanders’ concerns about unchecked AI development. But while the political calculus guiding this move is comprehensible, the policy prescription itself is not.
There’s no plausible path to data center non-proliferation
A data center moratorium is an attempted double-bank shot at AI safety — regulating buildings, in order to constrain compute, in order to limit capabilities. Unfortunately, it would not succeed on its own terms.
Nuclear nonproliferation has been fairly effective because weapons programs depend on rare materials and other controllable chokepoints. Data centers merely require land, electricity, cooling, fiber, and chips. Some of those inputs can be limited at the margin, especially the most advanced chips. But the basic physical infrastructure of compute just isn’t scarce in the way fissile material is scarce. Even Eliezer Yudkowsky noted [ https://substack.com/redirect/c3f17939-25a3-4ae8-90d5-d633e27a61dd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] that while a domestic data center moratorium can protect communities from electricity rate hikes, one would need an international treaty to actually stymie development.
Singapore offers us an instructive precedent. When Singapore paused [ https://substack.com/redirect/511b8a00-2226-4564-af96-bd4fb785cc4d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] new data center development between 2019 and 2022 over energy and land use concerns, it avoided those specific costs, but it did not halt the regional buildout of compute. Microsoft, Google, and AWS have collectively committed billions across Malaysia [ https://substack.com/redirect/a3e69abe-62ab-4883-92c9-24b563b1aeea?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], Thailand [ https://substack.com/redirect/481d5b31-62ac-402f-a8ad-de8b56d9fa9d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], and Indonesia [ https://substack.com/redirect/f982206f-e28a-4e91-8ef1-efcea74d9b1f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ].
Meanwhile, the Gulf states are treating AI infrastructure as their post-oil economic strategy. Middle East data center capacity is projected [ https://substack.com/redirect/f87c3f74-5bdb-483e-a714-743fd642f14e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] to triple from 1 gigawatt to 3.3 gigawatts in five years. Saudi Arabia just earmarked [ https://substack.com/redirect/5cc4933f-f716-4989-9445-19c7ac6e4212?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] $40 billion for AI investments and declared [ https://substack.com/redirect/778ce0bc-39f1-4067-9691-30010ad91eb2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] 2026 the “Year of Artificial Intelligence.”
This is the likely outcome of a US moratorium as well. Facilities would simply move to friendlier jurisdictions with weaker environmental standards, and we’d lose the leverage that local ownership confers.
Many data centers have nothing to do with AI
Data centers facilitate all kinds of digital activity: Netflix streaming, retail shopping, Instagram scrolling, online banking. AI represented about a quarter [ https://substack.com/redirect/bd37e2ff-ec07-4bd4-b974-75161f195c91?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] of all data center workloads in 2025, though that percentage stands to grow significantly. A blanket moratorium on data centers wouldn’t distinguish between facilities contributing to frontier AI capabilities and enterprise data centers hosting a hospital’s patient records system. And the average voter won’t either.
Even if Sanders’ proposal only targets AI data centers, the distinction isn’t as clean as it sounds. Many data centers have multipurpose functionality [ https://substack.com/redirect/61965e70-9016-41e4-adc3-41bf8ac40ed4?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]; the same infrastructure is used for an evolving mix of cloud, enterprise, and AI tasks over time.
Most people who oppose data centers in their communities aren’t worried about x-risk; they’re worried about problems like electric rate hikes or fan noise. These are understandable concerns. But a moratorium framed around AI safety collapses these concerns into one narrative — and could instill anxiety in people that any nearby data center supports advanced AI.
It’s supply skepticism all the way down
Read Sanders’ statement [ https://substack.com/redirect/8a13fff8-1761-4f0c-82b8-f85fb02525eb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] carefully and you’ll notice that he doesn’t lead with the AI safety argument. He opens with environmental concerns and rising electricity costs, then he covers job loss, and finally he mentions AI safety risks.
That ordering reveals the real center of gravity here: general opposition to local construction. Sanders cites Denver’s moratorium as vindication of his proposal, but Denver’s pause was driven [ https://substack.com/redirect/6980c6d9-7eab-4428-9149-bc76164d718c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] by more prosaic land use and electricity concerns, not AGI anxiety. Framing it as a win for AI safety retroactively imports existential framing onto what really amounts to a normal infrastructure fight. That same reactivity has helped produce the housing crisis and obstruct our clean energy buildout — but data centers offer an opportunity to break this pattern.
Data centers aren’t the only new loads coming onto the grid. Electric vehicles and electrified manufacturing are also driving demand that requires more generation, more transmission, and long-overdue grid modernization. Many data centers are leaning on gas for near-term power, because gas-powered projects can be brought online quickly. But data centers could serve as anchor tenants for new clean generation, battery storage, and transmission. Many companies [ https://substack.com/redirect/de31e80c-3a69-46e0-a575-c58c458bd7eb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] are moving in that direction.
Industrial projects like these are also prompting pragmatic shifts [ https://substack.com/redirect/04678da1-b695-45fb-9ea8-bfd0e1315715?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] on decarbonization from environmental groups. The Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC), for instance, just supported its first nuclear project ever, to power a data center.
A moratorium forecloses exactly the kind of creative thinking these projects are beginning to generate.
Simply govern the actual technology
Even if a federal moratorium were feasible, the question remains: What do you plan to do with the time you’ve bought? A pause is not a substitute for actual AI governance.
There are many ways elected officials could approach this. Congress could fund [ https://substack.com/redirect/adfcf0cf-03cf-4311-a176-515df7ba976a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] an empowered AI Safety Institute with the authority to conduct risk assessments, interpretability research, and mandatory pre-release testing of frontier models (going further than the current US CAISI). As AI researchers like Narayanan and Kapoor have argued [ https://substack.com/redirect/bf3ba586-dd6b-4887-b00f-ef297b918993?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], such institutions must assess risks not only at the model level, but in the context of deployment, making state capacity even more essential.
Congress could also instate aggressive chip export controls [ https://substack.com/redirect/95c9f01d-58ff-4a7d-85c4-104a62a2c658?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], or pass whistleblower protections [ https://substack.com/redirect/1bb14b8b-283c-47c0-8d27-bcb2fe4ff693?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] to support workers who notice specific dangers to public health or national security. All of these initiatives would do more to address the risks Sanders is worried about.
Congress is a blinkered institution; its limited capacity and political incentives generally keep members focused on immediate harms. For that reason, lawmakers like Sanders deserve commendation for grappling with more diffuse and long-term risks. But lawmakers must honor that seriousness with remedies that actually address those risks.
Sanders was right in 1982 that the threat of nuclear annihilation demanded a strong response, and he’s right that AI demands one now. ​​But AI demands a distinct approach, one that apprehends the political and technical realities of AI development. A federal moratorium on data centers does not.
