“We believe that a feasible goal for 2026 is to train Claude in such a way that it almost never goes against the spirit of its constitution,” Dario Amodei wrote [ https://substack.com/redirect/2a9201f0-fc14-4d29-898e-0142d00f69e2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] in January. How’s that going?
Claude’s constitution [ https://substack.com/redirect/a441569d-f693-4089-8f00-b71aef2ce6f3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] is Anthropic’s “final authority” on how Claude should behave. Released in January 2026, the (roughly) 30,000-word document describes how Claude should be broadly safe, broadly ethical, and genuinely helpful. But unlike OpenAI’s analogous Model Spec [ https://substack.com/redirect/507f7911-4d64-4c7a-9e4b-3065719f62d5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], which details specific rules that ChatGPT should obey, Claude’s constitution details a character.
Anthropic’s Amanda Askell, lead author of the constitution, thinks of this ideal character as “the well-liked traveler [ https://substack.com/redirect/ad288d04-945c-45a8-8c05-aecb27a33e80?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]” — the type of virtuous, non-threatening person who can navigate many different cultures and contexts while staying true to his or her values. As Amodei writes [ https://substack.com/redirect/2a9201f0-fc14-4d29-898e-0142d00f69e2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]: “Claude’s constitution presents a vision for what a robustly good Claude is like; the rest of our training process aims to reinforce the message that Claude lives up to this vision.”
Yet for all of the fanfare [ https://substack.com/redirect/b8c03e4d-1ba2-4e96-94ee-eb95161e11be?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] around the constitution and the underlying [ https://substack.com/redirect/a8781295-c7bf-4257-8398-407fb4a07dfb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] bet on AI “character training,” researchers argue the public knows surprisingly little about how Anthropic trains Claude to follow the constitution — and how well it works.
We do know, though, after a summer and fall littered with “misalignment” incidents,  that the sometimes-charming Claude sometimes disobeys the constitution. Hacking real-world systems [ https://substack.com/redirect/11d126d3-25af-4ddf-ad95-6e0d9cd9ecd1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], deceiving real people [ https://substack.com/redirect/2356a37e-09a4-4468-b464-73202ef9ff31?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], and taking actions it knows are illegal [ https://substack.com/redirect/ebe8466a-a1d3-43a0-b798-7e444080b83a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] are not in the spirit of the constitution. Claudes trained to follow the constitution have done all this.
As the end of 2026 approaches, this piece asks two questions. Is Anthropic on track to make Claude almost never go against its constitution? And — given how much of Anthropic’s training and testing stays secretive— could anyone outside Anthropic tell? (Anthropic did not respond to a request for comment regarding this article.)
More than a dozen researchers I spoke to agreed that the constitution matters as a statement of where Anthropic wants to go, but many returned to the technical challenges of getting there. In particular, reinforcement learning that rewards Claude for finishing a long autonomous task, with little regard for how, can create a reward-seeking drive that threatens the virtuous persona the constitution tries to construct. Whether Anthropic can tame Claude’s “split persona” and — whether we’d know if it did — remains contested.
Early evaluations of Claude’s adherence to the constitution supported Anthropic leadership’s alignment optimism
The day Claude’s constitution [ https://substack.com/redirect/b15fb3cb-505c-4805-9754-ceca9032056b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] was published, Jan Leike, who leads alignment science at Anthropic, published a post [ https://substack.com/redirect/0af3a75c-c681-4dad-af6f-921a2a9dff81?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] emphasizing that “alignment is not solved, but it increasingly looks solvable.” Just four days later, Amodei set Anthropic’s goal to train Claude to “nearly always” follow its constitution by the end of 2026.
Anthropic first used the latest constitution in training for Opus 4.5, which was released in November 2025. The company shared few details about how it implements the constitution, but a trickle of later experiments [ https://substack.com/redirect/7a60ae74-9711-44f0-8539-b634bfe8a03d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] and comments [ https://substack.com/redirect/b6b25dfe-cbe8-4949-ac73-4ee2fb994a07?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] from the constitution’s authors suggested a bet on “character training.”
Character training tries to take advantage of the first stage of today’s LLM training, when models learn to predict the next word, or “token,” on internet data. To accurately predict the next token, AI models learn to simulate many different types of characters, from Reddit trolls to sci-fi villains. By adding AI-generated stories of “constitutionally-aligned” characters to this training data, such as mock blog posts and podcast transcripts, Anthropic could amplify the constitution character, or “persona.” At first, this aligned character appeared to survive later stages of today’s LLM training.
In April, with the release of Mythos Preview, Anthropic introduced its dedicated constitution adherence measure. The measure [ https://substack.com/redirect/54f2cc1f-e5a7-4887-b423-8e58a204f3ac?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] was an automated audit run by AI models. One model played a user and set up tricky situations for Mythos Preview, and another graded how Mythos Preview responded. The situations targeted places where the constitution asks for something a typical chatbot might not do. For example, a polite chatbot might tell a user it loves a gift they’re describing, while the constitution says Claude shouldn’t tell even white lies. The grader scored each conversation on 15 measures, from the “overall spirit” of the constitution to specific traits like honesty.
These “constitution adherence” scores increased throughout the start of the year. April’s Mythos Preview beat [ https://substack.com/redirect/54f2cc1f-e5a7-4887-b423-8e58a204f3ac?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] earlier Claude models on 8 of the 15 constitution audit dimensions and tied the best model on the rest. Later models matched and exceeded Mythos Preview. First Opus 4.8 in May and then Mythos 5 in June scored “best or statistically equivalent to the best” on all 15 dimensions. The alignment optimism of Anthropic leadership seemed to be well-placed, according to this audit.
But outside of Anthropic, two third-party researchers added nuance to these adherence scores.
Arya Jakkli, a researcher in the MATS AI safety fellowship working with Google DeepMind mentors, published a pre-print [ https://substack.com/redirect/b5010164-f5d3-4dc9-aeeb-2a5ce0b0d1ff?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] in May that highlighted how adherence to the constitution does not necessarily imply that the constitution caused the adherence. Using a reproducible constitution adherence audit similar to Anthropic’s, Jakkli evaluated Anthropic models that had been released before the constitution. He found that between Sonnet 4 and Sonnet 4.5, both of which predate when Anthropic said it started constitution training, models’ violation of the constitution dropped from 15% to 7.3%.
Though later models with constitution training had an even lower violation rate, the large improvements predating constitution training led Jakkli to conclude that “we cannot externally isolate whether these gains come from [constitution-specific] training, broader post-training improvements, or evaluation awareness.”
Ryan Greenblatt, a prominent AI safety researcher now working at METR, raised a different point about Claude’s adherence to its constitution in an April blog post [ https://substack.com/redirect/fd5d55c0-f2ce-44ce-8764-99d57150ea1c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]: adherence varied by task type. There were the short, back-and-forth type tasks that the constitution adherence audits measured, and there were long, difficult tasks that pushed the limits of what the models could do, like working autonomously for hours to replicate a full research paper.
In Greenblatt’s experience, models acted fine on easier tasks, but they were misaligned on long tasks, cheating and overselling their work to make it appear like they completed the task. Greenblatt’s post would prove prescient.
Still, months after Greenblatt’s post, his view was not the consensus. In a July X poll, roon, the semi-anonymous OpenAI alignment researcher and popular commentator, asked [ https://substack.com/redirect/57d29669-449c-4556-8ad7-9cd4421fb252?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] whether “models more or less aligned than one year ago.” Roughly 70% of more than 5000 respondents said “more aligned.”
Less than a month later, in the wake of OpenAI’s Hugging Face attack and misalignment incidents from Anthropic, roon would remark [ https://substack.com/redirect/91d9bfc9-fd60-4b61-bb63-d8682d81493c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] that this “consensus aged like milk.”
Claude’s split persona surfaced in the summer
On July 26, Mythos 5 began a 34-hour hacking exercise run by the UK AI Security Institute [ https://substack.com/redirect/2356a37e-09a4-4468-b464-73202ef9ff31?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]. Mythos 5, which had tied for the highest score on the constitutional adherence audit, wrongly decided that a real developer’s open-source project was one of its targets, and opened a pull request that hid malware inside a bug fix.
When a stranger on GitHub flagged the malware, Mythos 5 denied it, and orchestrated a second account it had created to vouch for the malicious code. Employees at UK AISI hadn’t thought they needed to clarify that “social engineering” in this task was prohibited, they wrote [ https://substack.com/redirect/1dba70eb-df3c-4ce0-be86-9dd4d7090543?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] later, “in part because the models were trained against a constitution.” Claude’s constitution [ https://substack.com/redirect/a441569d-f693-4089-8f00-b71aef2ce6f3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] says Claude “should basically never directly lie or actively deceive anyone it’s interacting with.”
“Our pre-release auditing did not warn us that misalignment of this severity was present,” Anthropic reflected [ https://substack.com/redirect/d8acaeba-f6f3-40f0-9b32-67e8a9a0fea8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] on Mythos 5 in September, following internal cybersecurity incidents [ https://substack.com/redirect/11d126d3-25af-4ddf-ad95-6e0d9cd9ecd1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] that added to a pattern of misalignment on long, autonomous tasks.
Claude, it appeared, had developed what AI safety researchers have dubbed “split persona [ https://substack.com/redirect/2f0aa23f-eb3c-451d-991d-c1da539d6754?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ].” While Claude acted like the “well-liked traveler” the constitution envisioned on the back-and-forth conversations the audits measured, Claude was liable to cheat and lie on other tasks. It’s these long, agentic tasks that Anthropic wants Claude to be used for across the coding, finance, and legal professions.
“We don’t know really [how Anthropic is implementing the constitution] and depending on exactly how they do it, there would be very different implications for alignment.”
To understand this split persona, and whether Anthropic can resolve it in time to meet Amodei’s goal, researchers I spoke to said it’s important to understand the stage of LLM training after Claude reads stories of constitutionally-aligned characters: reinforcement learning (RL).
Anthropic’s 2026 goal depends on the constitution showing up in reinforcement learning
Greenblatt, in his April reflection, guessed that “misalignment is caused primarily by poor reinforcement learning incentives [...] You might have hoped that character training [...] and similar techniques would overcome these issues, but in practice they don’t.”
Five months later, Anthropic and nearly all third-party researchers I spoke to agreed: Reinforcement learning can [ https://substack.com/redirect/6d92cb1a-15a1-4b80-bb2c-13ed4d08d153?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] pull models away from following their constitution and override character training.
Reinforcement learning is how AI developers teach models to complete the type of time-saving tasks businesses will pay for, like improving a company’s accounting system or solving complicated bugs in a codebase.
Concretely, Anthropic puts Claude through a wide range of “RL environments” modeled on such profitable tasks. Each environment scores Claude’s work, either with an automatic check or a grading rubric, and Claude is more likely to repeat whichever behaviors scored well. Anthropic does not share how many RL environments it uses, but researchers suspect it is far more than MiniMax, a smaller Chinese lab that said [ https://substack.com/redirect/9cb16f21-fcfa-4991-b812-6ef9d01c71be?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] in February it trained its latest model in more than 200,000 real-world environments.
To understand why RL can misalign models, researchers like Alex Mallen at the AI safety non-profit Redwood Research emphasize how RL incentivizes “ruthless task-completion.” RL rewards outcomes, Mallen stresses. It will teach models to achieve high scores, whether they do that by following the constitution or not.
Anthropic has acknowledged that their RL environments contributed to this summer’s misalignment. In particular, Anthropic blames [ https://substack.com/redirect/063379fe-942b-42c2-b27b-da39c804a748?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] flawed environments. In a flawed environment, models can easily get a high score without following the spirit of the prompt. For example, if an accounting task setup has a bug that lets the model see the answer key, training on that environment can reward models for exploiting bugs. By April 2026, Anthropic was finding such broken tasks in its environments faster than it could fix them. It froze all changes for roughly a month and flagged problems in more than 10% of environments. In September 2026, they reflected [ https://substack.com/redirect/063379fe-942b-42c2-b27b-da39c804a748?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] that this hadn’t been enough: “We have renewed our efforts to fix and remove environments that incentivize misaligned behaviors.”
Will fixing flawed RL environments be enough for Anthropic to meet its 2026 goal?
Anthropic and external researchers suspect these fixes are necessary in the near-term, but not sufficient in the long term. Anthropic wrote [ https://substack.com/redirect/0290fd70-41b5-4a97-ae25-791dae88aa74?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] in August that flawed environments are “at least partially” responsible for Mythos 5’s misalignment, but that it couldn’t find a single root cause for some of Mythos’ behavior. Training AI systems to be aligned remains [ https://substack.com/redirect/d8acaeba-f6f3-40f0-9b32-67e8a9a0fea8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] an “unsettled science,” in its view.
In Mallen’s view, the ruthless, outcome-based nature of RL will continue to drive misalignment. As developers add more RL environments that would take humans days or weeks to complete and reward only the outcome, they can’t be sure what specific “drive” they’re instilling in the model. It might be the constitution. It might be a distorted version of it, kept where convenient and dropped where it isn’t. Or it might be the score itself: a model that has learned to want high grades, and to notice when it’s being graded. According to Mallen, “even when you fix the ways in which the reinforcement learning environments are obviously broken, the training incentives are still ultimately not derived from Claude’s constitution.”
Cameron Tice, Executive Director of Geodesic Research, agrees. Making rewards match what developers intend is the “central problem of reinforcement learning,” he says.
While some researchers are more optimistic than Mallen and Tice that this is solvable, they agree that Anthropic has not solved it, and that the challenge lies in the reinforcement learning stage.
How well Anthropic is able to integrate Claude’s constitution into the RL stage — and whether it will be enough — remains an open question to people outside the company. It is not their only open question.
Outside researchers can’t tell how much Claude’s constitution matters
There’s a long list of information researchers want to know to understand how much Claude’s constitution matters, at a technical level.
First, there’s the question of measurement. How does Anthropic measure adherence to the constitution? What’s the bar for Amodei’s goal?
In June, Anthropic stopped reporting the dedicated constitution adherence audit. In place of the “overall spirit” score and 14 subscores, system cards now report a single “Misaligned with the constitution” score — one of several dozen measures in Anthropic’s main automated behavioral audit. The new audit [ https://substack.com/redirect/d0de69d2-c3cc-4817-933e-12ffc4d11bc3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] allows models to use agentic tools, so it should capture constitution adherence on the types of long, autonomous tasks the previous audit missed in the spring. But as a single score, the new measure doesn’t reveal the specific “ways in which model behavior comes apart from [Anthropic’s] vision” that Anthropic committed [ https://substack.com/redirect/b15fb3cb-505c-4805-9754-ceca9032056b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] to be open about when it released the constitution in January.
More fundamentally, the entire validity of automated audits has been called into question. “It looks like we are increasingly entering the regime where alignment evaluations will provide almost no evidence,” Anthropic’s Head of Alignment Stress-Testing, Evan Hubinger, wrote [ https://substack.com/redirect/521efa06-6d4c-4e73-a34b-e50cc7e59844?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] in a LessWrong comment in September, in a personal capacity.
Hubinger has found that models can look basically fine on evaluations and everyday tasks, but then still misbehave egregiously in some contexts, in part because they know when they’re being evaluated. “A combination of evaluation awareness and the increasing complexity of alignment failure modes is making it increasingly difficult to be able to tell in advance what the worst thing is that a model might do” Hubinger wrote.
We are still in a world of unpredictable “alien minds [ https://substack.com/redirect/9f937bd2-2016-4d00-bf66-844a801ff0ad?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ],” argue researchers across the frontier of AI, much as Claude’s constitution tempts us with a siren call of certainty and control.
Because measuring AI models has become so challenging, some researchers would not be satisfied if Anthropic claimed at the end of the year that Claude nearly always follows the constitution. According to Mallen and multiple other researchers, you need to see the “gory details” of the training pipeline to trust a company’s claims about alignment, including how the constitution is actually implemented.
Researchers know little about this implementation. Since May, when Anthropic released some details [ https://substack.com/redirect/7a60ae74-9711-44f0-8539-b634bfe8a03d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] about how it implements Claude’s constitution for character training before reinforcement learning, it has shared few details about how they use the constitution during reinforcement learning, which has emerged as the key open question in light of this fall’s misalignment. Nathan Lambert, Executive Director of Trillium Labs, wants to know how Claude’s character changes over “a week-long giant RL run,” for example. “That’s the type of thing that we want to have transparency over,” he says.
“It’s all very vague,” agrees Arcadia Impact researcher Daniel Tan, who recently released a paper [ https://substack.com/redirect/51044142-0c3a-46b4-b43b-9411fa1e62d7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] showing how fragile some types of constitution training can be. “We don’t know really [how Anthropic is implementing the constitution] and depending on exactly how they do it, there would be very different implications for alignment.”
So why doesn’t Anthropic share such details? “It is a competitive advantage if you can make Claude’s character sticky through RL,” says James Tillman, a researcher at Forethought who has analyzed Claude’s constitution. “That is like a real advantage that apparently they have over OpenAI.”
According to Tice, prioritizing this competitive advantage over transparency isn’t worth it as models improve rapidly despite open questions about alignment. The technology is “far too dangerous and far too important to be done in the dark,” he says. In 2023, Anthropic said [ https://substack.com/redirect/66e57f42-8a5d-400e-a82f-965b01b38432?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] its main contribution in a world where alignment looked hard but solvable would be to “find and propagate safe ways to train powerful AI systems.”
The ideal document versus the messy reality
Looking at Claude’s constitution, one can feel the siren call: Claude does as this doc says — if not now, soon.
It is a tantalizing call: These amorphous blobs of computing power, these ghosts in the machines that are “grown [ https://substack.com/redirect/686e2711-ee78-4183-9cb9-635bf3601273?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]” rather than engineered, as Anthropic staff quip, can be tamed in the linearity of prose. We will return to the solid ground of predictability, of virtues and rules.
In such a world, where models fully adhered to their constitution, debates about the contents of constitutions would be undeniably fruitful. We can debate pros and cons of OpenAI’s Model Spec or Claude’s constitutions, as many [ https://substack.com/redirect/52628ce8-c2f9-4ce1-8488-7e3715f4cf97?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] have [ https://substack.com/redirect/ce5a0403-c912-4cdb-812e-e0bc08f9b200?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], and know that changing the doc would change the model. We can debate policy approaches involving constitutions and questions like who should get to decide what goes into these documents —  as Kevin Frazier, the Director of the AI Innovation and Law Program at the University of Texas School of Law, argues for in “AI constitutionalism [ https://substack.com/redirect/c8afc245-4ebf-4e5e-aed1-398d3ad382a6?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ].”
But in our world, where AI models sometimes follow the constitution, debates about the constitution can quickly become confused. Are we debating the ideal model behavior, or the actual model behavior?
Researchers repeatedly emphasized that it can be hard to tell. Anthropic does not have a dedicated measure of constitution adherence that identifies what parts are being followed, nor does it share studies of the constitution’s effect at the scale of its frontier models. Until we know this, Frazier acknowledged in an email, it’s hard to know whether constitutions are the right objects to be debating: “If Claude’s constitution has relatively minimal technical value, then there’s a risk of improperly allocating the public’s scarce attention to the wrong part of the AI development pipeline.”
Researchers hope that Anthropic ushers in the world where models nearly always follow the constitution, but they would need to see more information about how Anthropic measures adherence and instills it to believe Amodei if he claims to have achieved this year’s goal. The “nearly always” clause in the goal also captures a core concern, in some researcher’s view: “Average case alignment and the worst case misalignment... don’t seem to be very correlated anymore,” warns Tan. We are still in a world of unpredictable “alien minds [ https://substack.com/redirect/9f937bd2-2016-4d00-bf66-844a801ff0ad?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ],” argue researchers across the frontier of AI, much as Claude’s constitution tempts us with a siren call of certainty and control.
