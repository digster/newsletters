Since the best way to predict the future is to understand the past, let’s do that with currencies to learn how we got here. Shells, Copper, Bronze, Gold, Silver, PaperYou can understand why Bitcoin has been successful when you consider the history of currencies, as we did in Scarce: eg, the market can be flooded by shells Stable: Its value can’t be changing all the time Divisible: for big or small transactions Durable: eg, shells or bad quality paper can break Portable: eg, copper was so cheap that you needed to carry a lot of it to make any payment Fungible: eg, two shells are unlike each other Acceptable: other people must recognize it as a valid currency
Some things work better than others as currency: These factors explain why Bitcoin is so valuable now, worth $1.7 trillion dollars, nearly as much as silver. Indeed, Bitcoin is: Available: You can easily get Bitcoin wherever you are Scarce: There will only ever be 21 million BTC Divisible: One BTC can be divided into one hundred million pieces (0.00000001 BTC, about 0.08 cents today) Durable: It’s going to be there as long as we see value in it, as BTC is the result of computation Portable: You can send it anywhere with a few clicks at very little cost Fungible: One BTC is exactly the same as another Acceptable: Everybody that is close to crypto knows BTC and accepts it as payment. This is the core reason why BTC is one of the biggest cryptocurrencies and why it will remain so for a long time: As the first one, and one that’s well built, everyone recognizes it as the most important, and that makes it so.
I skipped one of the properties of a good currency. Did you notice which one? Stability. The price of BTC is volatile. This makes it like gold, for the same reasons. The Dullness of GoldThis is the value of gold over time: Notice this is a logarithmic graph, so gold price swings are even wilder than this. Why? Gold is very scarce, so in moments of Scarcity and stability are connected: the scarcer a currency is, the less stable it will be. Bitcoin has the exact same problem, for the exact same reason. It has the ultimate scarcity (only 21M BTC will ever be minted), so when people think its value is high, they will hoard it, pushing its price up, and when they think its value is shrinking, they will dump it. This is why BTC prices swing so much.¹ This has catastrophic consequences for an economy whose currency is gold: When the economy expands, people need more gold, so it becomes more scarce, so its price increases, people start hoarding it, and the lack of currency makes the economy cool off. In other words, a lot of the economy’s productivity increases simply translate into higher gold prices. Shiny SilverThis is what made silver so valuable. In the 1400s, after the Black Death in Europe, the economy boomed. By the 1450s, this² had led to a dramatic shortage of silver, which spurred technology improvements in Central Europe, which helped them Since silver is more abundant than gold, it’s less scarce, which made it more stable as a currency. And stability is key for a currency, because that conserves the value of the signal of prices. We all know how bad inflation is for the economy. Deflation is, too: If a currency gains in value over time, people will reduce their consumption, dragging the economy into a recession.⁴ The global economy boomed, from Spain to Europe and even China. That’s why silver coins became legal tender around the world: Hence stablecoins. Get it? The Stability of StablecoinsStablecoins, like most cryptocurrencies, inherit the same benefits as a Bitcoin: availability, divisibility, durability… The main difference is the scarcity vs stability tradeoff. Since scarcity is what makes Bitcoin like gold—too valuable, which makes its value unstable—then the next generation of successful cryptocurrencies was always going to be the one to solve this problem: Eliminate scarcity, and instead target stability. That’s how a stablecoin works. A Radical Impact on the Global EconomyWith people worldwide buying USD, the entire world economy might dollarize. Lower US InflationThis would be valuable for the US because it can keep printing dollars while inflation stays low, since it’s distributed over many more dollars around the world. The competition will be good for customers—except when it prevents citizens from buying the competing stablecoins. Weaker Financial WeaponsBut of course, the more transactions run on stablecoins, the less they run on the US-led SWIFT system, undermining its influence on global transactions. The US would have fewer financial weapons against its enemies, but a more powerful monetary policy. All this is not a trivial side-effect. Eliminating the power of a central node is arguably the core purpose of cryptocurrencies. Undermine Local Currency PolicyAlong the same lines, most countries wouldn’t be able to influence the economy as much through monetary policy, since many more of their citizens’ and firms’ assets would be in another currency. The Advantages of Stablecoins over USDWe talked about the big needs of currencies in history before, but that doesn’t mean we’re done. There are still many pain points with payments today that new payment and currency systems can solve. 1. Access USD Where It’s Hard ToIf you’re in Venezuela, Turkey, Iran, or Syria right now, you’re losing 15–80% of your currency value every year you hold it. If your money is in the bank, you’re watching it evaporate. It would be better to hold USD, which lose their value much more slowly. But as a foreigner, you can’t easily access USD. You can’t get a US bank account, your local banks might not offer the service, or your government might simply ban it. Buying USD bills on the streets is at a deep discount, you need a place to store them, and people might steal them from you. But if you can log into your crypto account and with a few clicks buy a USDT with your own currency,⁷ you will not lose your life savings just because you happen to live in an inflation country. You’ll have access to this money anywhere in the world. 2. Fast International Payments“Oh I’m sorry, it’s 5:01pm on a Friday. You’ll have to wait 88h for us to process your payment—Monday is a bank holiday.” And then on top of that, you need 1–5 business days to get your money! Are we in the Middle Ages or what? Are we carrying silver ingots by horse-pulled cart? We’re not, but traditional payment systems like SWIFT can still take day transfer your money. 3. Working CapitalDuring that time, the money is neither in the sender’s account nor in the receiver’s, so both need to cover that money during that time. This working capital can add up to a lot, and some companies need huge, expensive loans in the interim. An immediate settlement—which stablecoins can do—bypasses that problem. 4. Cheap Fees & CommissionsTraditional payments are expensive, too. An international wire transfer frequently costs $20–$30, and remittances cost on average over 6% of the amount! If you send home $1,000, the intermediary can shave $100 off your meager income. Those are just fees. What about exchange commissions? Not great to lose 1-3% of the money in every transaction you make. That’s some medieval financier kind of stuff. 5. Convenient International PayoutsImagine a company that has thousands of employees in dozens of countries around the world. How do you manage all the currencies, national banking systems, bank accounts…? 6. ProgrammabilityA dollar is dumb. It just sits there waiting for you to do things with it. Cryptocurrencies are not. Imagine a homeowner paying a plumber €500 to replace a water heater. The homeowner sends the money into a digital escrow. The contract releases €250 when the plumber arrives,⁸ then releases the rest when the homeowner confirms the heater works. If they disagree, the money stays locked until a named mediator decides what happens. Cryptocurrencies can support smart contracts that settle and move money automatically. For example, they can release funds when certain conditions are met—subject to software, oracle, and governance risks. Trading, collateral, lending, prediction markets, decentralized exchanges, tokenization… These are all examples of things we can do with intelligent money that are hard to do with dumb money. TakeawaysAs you can see, while Bitcoin is a way to store value like gold was (and is), stablecoins are optimized specifically as currencies for payments, the way silver was in the past. All these new stablecoin-related currencies and payment processors are focusing on solving the pain points of our current ones: high costs, slow settlement, accessibility, inconvenience, and stupidity.
