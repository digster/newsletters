This week, let’s take a look at a map of the Biblical Sinai Peninsula and a question that bugged me during my research for my While I was researching, I found lots of maps like this: It’s a map, published in London in 1873, that shows the important ancient sites on the peninsula, especially those that show up in Biblical accounts. It’s actually an inset of this larger map for “Bible readers.” It makes sense that Christians in Europe would be really interested in mapping out the Holy Land, but why were there so many of these maps from the mid- and late-19th century, specifically? And why were most of them British? It turns out that there’s an interesting answer: these maps are an expression of a simultaneous fear and embrace of new scientific discoveries, with a sprinkle of imperialism for good measure. ...
