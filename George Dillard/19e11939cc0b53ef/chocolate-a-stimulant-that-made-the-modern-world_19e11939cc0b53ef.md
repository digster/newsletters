The Catholic Church didn’t quite know how to categorize the stuff. Was it a drink and therefore permissible during fasting periods, or did it provide sustenance and deserve to be banned? In the end, the Church realized it was fighting a losing battle. People enjoyed their morning chocolate so much that they were willing to risk eternal damnation for it. People fermented cacao beans, ground them up, and mixed them with all kinds of things. There was a relatively bitter mixture of chocolate, water, and achiote, a spice that gave it a reddish color. Some people mixed it with chili peppers to make it spicy. Some mixed it with masa or other forms of maize. Others put dried, aromatic flowers in it. The preparation closest to our own heavily-sweetened chocolate consumption contained honey. Whatever people mixed with chocolate, most of the time they prepared their drinks by pouring them between two containers.
