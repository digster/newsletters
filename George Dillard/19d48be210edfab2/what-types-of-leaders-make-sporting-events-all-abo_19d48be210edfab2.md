The Roman Emperor Nero fancied himself a skilled singer and athlete, and he knew that nobody could tell him otherwise. The historian Nero — who had become emperor as a spoiled teenager and never evolved much beyond that — dominated every competition he entered. When he entered a singing contest, no one was allowed to leave the theater while he was performing. Suetonius tells us that some people faked death so that they could be carried out rather than listen to the ruler’s caterwauling. Nero had statues of past winners removed so that they didn’t overshadow his glory. He won the Olympic chariot race even though he fell from his chariot and never finished the race.
