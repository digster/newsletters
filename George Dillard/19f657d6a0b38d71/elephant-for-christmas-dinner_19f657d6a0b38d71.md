The Battle of Sedan could not have gone much worse for France. In the late summer of 1870, Emperor Napoleon III marched the French Army out to meet an invading Prussian force at the eastern edge of France. The Prussians outmaneuvered his hapless force over and over again, and the French finally sought shelter in the hills near Sedan. French commanders believed the Prussians had only a small force nearby and that the hills would protect them. They were wrong on both counts. By September 1, the Prussians had surrounded the French forces. The French tried to break out, but failed. As Prussian artillery battered French positions, Napoleon III realized he had been beaten. He raised the white flag. By the end of the day on September 2, he and more than 100,000 other Frenchmen were prisoners of the Prussians. That could have been, probably should have been, the end of the war. Otto von Bismarck had baited France into declaring war on Prussia (his diplomatic provocations had, in his words, With Napoleon III imprisoned rather luxuriously in a German castle, leaders in Paris overthrew him, declaring a Third Republic and establishing a Government of National Defense. It wasn’t entirely clear for a while who the Prussians should even negotiate with. Eventually, the new French government proved surprisingly firm, refusing to surrender any territory to Prussia. So the Prussians tightened the noose. Their armies surrounded Paris and tried to starve it into surrender. With the city encircled by hostile troops, the two million people of Paris faced winter with no way to get more food or communicate with the outside world. Things were bad for Parisians, but arguably worse for the city’s animals. The French government was slow to set up a rationing system for food; people hoarded food, and desperation drove up prices. It became harder and harder to find meat, especially, which meant that people started looking for new kinds of animals to eat. One cartoon joked that the new place to look for meat is the sewers, where one might catch a rat: Rats, however, were a rather impractical source of meat. They were hard to catch and difficult to prepare.
