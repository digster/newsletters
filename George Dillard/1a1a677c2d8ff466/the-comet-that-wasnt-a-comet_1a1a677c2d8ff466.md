José Guadalupe Posada lived a depressingly familiar artist’s life. He struggled as a printmaker and illustrator, commenting on Mexican politics and society in magazines and posters. He often used the calavera, a skeletal figure associated with the Day of the Dead, to make his points, as in this 1903 example: Posada was one of those artists who only gained respect after his death. He died in squalor and was buried in an anonymous grave in 1913, but artists like Diego Rivera and Frida Kahlo rediscovered his work and used it as inspiration for some of the great art of the 1930s. But this image was recycled from 1899, when Posada had accompanied the picture with a far more alarming text. Here, he states that the whole world will be roasted like a pork rind, that a comet arriving in 1899 will be the end of the world. At first glance, this seems like a joke, and it probably is, on some level. What’s strange is that there was not even a comet.
