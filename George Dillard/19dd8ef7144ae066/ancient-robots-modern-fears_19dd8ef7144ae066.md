I find it fascinating that the ancient Greeks imagined all sorts of automata and robots even though they were far from being technologically capable of producing them. It’s even more fascinating that their tales anticipated many of the concerns we have with our own humanlike technologies today. Other societies in history indulged in similar feats of imagination. The ancient Chinese imagined an artificial man so lifelike that people couldn’t tell the difference. Ancient Buddhists told stories of humanlike machines that guarded sacred texts. Medieval Muslims and Christians spoke of humanoid automata that could answer any question or play music. It seems that people have been thinking about what it would mean to create convincing facsimiles of ourselves for thousands of years. Many of these tales contain a mixture of wonder and worry, not unlike what we’re experiencing today, as we stand on the cusp of actually producing the machines that our ancestors dreamed of for so long. Will they be cheerful servants like Hephaestus’ maidens? Bringers of violence like Talos? Or will they unleash chaos on the world like Pandora?
