The Monday Media Diet with Sanchita LaMore

On The Correspondent, Heartburn by Nora Ephron, and Eurotrash by Christian Kracht

Sanchita
Aug 24
∙
Guest post

Sanchita LaMore is an excellent writer and travel commentator. She writes an excellent Substack on hospitality design.

Tell us about yourself.

I was born in India, raised in the UK, and have lived in the US for the past two years. I knew from a young age that I’d end up working for myself, and I was right. I’m currently building HAFH, a media platform focused on hospitality design.

Prior to this, I started out interning at Condé Nast in London, then went on to PA Media, where I dipped my toes into news, found it depressing, and retreated to the more joyful side of travel at the FT.

Outside of work, I pride myself on being quite boring. I like staying in, and I like my family and friends.

Describe your media diet.

My diet switches up, but I keep a few staples. I’ve curated my Apple News into something I’ll browse every morning. I must confess, though, I only read the full news story if it’s a big one; otherwise, I tend to skim through headlines.

​For cultural commentary, I read a few different Substacks, which you can find on my profile. Beyond that (in no particular order):

  • Air Mail

  • HTSI

  • T Magazine

  • The Atlantic

  • The Drift

  • Puck

  • WARKITCHEN

  • Cultured

I also love Reddit, especially the subreddit r/MadeMeSmile. I don’t even care if half of it is fake or made up. It does what it says: it makes me smile.

What’s the last great book you read?

My definition of ‘great’, at least right now, is anything that manages to make me laugh out loud. Eurotrash by Christian Kracht did that. It’s about the author travelling with his difficult mother around Switzerland. It’s dark. But funny.

​Also, Heartburn by Nora Ephron. I can’t believe I hadn’t read it before. She somehow turns a tough life event into something amusing.

​I promise I’m not bleak.

What are you reading now?

The Correspondent by Virginia Evans. I started it, couldn’t get into it, left it, and came back to it. The letter format definitely makes it unique, but it’s also what’s throwing me off.

What’s your reading strategy when you pick up a print copy of your favorite publication?

If I’m buying print, it’s usually a magazine, and I read it twice. First, I’ll do a quick flip-through of every page, then I’ll go back to the beginning to read it properly—masthead, contents, ads, the lot. There’s so much that goes into a print issue, and this is my way of appreciating the behind-the-scenes work. It’s the same reason I’ve been conditioned to sit through the credits of a film or series.

Who should everyone be reading that they’re not?

I have a theory that people recommend books that flatter the version of themselves they want to present, not the one they actually finished. I’ve seen too many friends pretend to be knee-deep into some boring academic text when what they want, and what they’d be better off with, is a trashy beach read. Read that. And read it unapologetically.

​But the real answer, I suppose, is that everyone should be reading more of everything. The good and the bad, the broadsheet and the gossip rag, the thing you agree with and the thing that makes you furious. You can’t read culture if you only consume the parts that confirm what you already believe. Some of the brightest people I know are the ones who read widely and without snobbery.

What is the best non-famous app you love on your phone?

I don’t think I have a non-famous one. I love my Whoop app and 1stDibs—do they count?

Plane or train?

Depends on the train and how long I’ll be on it, so plane. I used to adore being airborne. I’d treat it as me time. But in the last two years, I’ve become a really nervous flier, which I can’t explain or bear, since I have to fly a lot.

What is one place everyone should visit?

If possible, you should go somewhere that has no resemblance to anything you know—not the language, not the food, not the manners, nor the sense of time. These should all be wrong by your standards, and you’re the one who has to adjust. It’s not comfortable. You’ll get things wrong, you’ll offend people without meaning to, and you’ll feel stupid for a few days. But you find out which of your habits are actually yours and which were just the water you were swimming in.

Tell us the story of a rabbit hole you fell deep into.

It started respectably. I have a long-standing thing for jewellery, and I enjoy learning about the craft and history. So I went looking into royal jewels, but it turned into royal gossip on Reddit, where I stayed far longer than is defensible. I now know who owns which tiara, what got broken up and reset, who inherited Diana’s jewels, what’s vaulted and never worn, and all the engagement ring designs. I can’t tell you the hours I’ve lost. But this isn’t nearly as bad as the Beckham family drama I fell into a few months ago.
