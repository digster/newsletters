The Monday Media Diet with Emma Näpänkangas

On McNally, hospitality, and the charms of Finland

Emma Näpänkangas
Sep 7
∙
Guest post
 
READ IN APP
 

Emma writes an excellent Substack on hospitality and works with EHL as a writer and editor. We always enjoy reading her stuff and are happy to have her with us this week.

Tell us about yourself.

I’m originally from Finland but have spent at least a third of my life living in Sweden, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and Spain. I’m a recent M.Sc. graduate from EHL Hospitality Business School, which is the hospitality institution, and I’ve been lucky enough to continue working with them as a writer and editor.

Before finding my place in hospitality, I studied global and comparative philosophy, where I got to immerse myself in not only the Western philosophical canon but also Indian, Chinese, Japanese, African, and Middle Eastern philosophies. While I love philosophy and it continues to be my favorite topic to dive into, I found myself wanting to practically apply what I’ve learned.

After a stint in private equity (it’s a long story), I finally found my way to the hospitality industry, where I get to combine all of these interests of mine, working in strategy, communications, and storytelling. One of these fun projects was to be part of PwC Middle East’s hospitality thought leadership team and write pieces such as this one.

Oh, and most importantly, why we are here, it was this mashup of interests that finally led me to start the Philosophy of Hospitality (tPH), my weekly newsletter on Substack.

TLDR; philosopher turned finance girl turned (hospitality) philosopher.

Describe your media diet.

I’m fully immersed in Substack at the moment, which is great because I feel like I found my people. From the hospitality and wellness scene (two of my main topics of interest), I always check The Other HAFH, which looks at hospitality from a really critical and fresh point of view, and it is precisely this critical aspect that I find myself missing in hospitality media.

AND you can tell it’s human-written and thought-through, which is unfortunately no longer a given. Another one I love is S.P.A, which is about bathing culture and saunas. I’m Finnish, so I’m like a moth to the flame in this case.

Outside of Substack, I’m a print media lover, and I will always get the new edition of Monocle or its design/hospitality/culture sister magazine Konfekt. I think the pieces are thoroughly researched and well-written, and contributors are often embedded in the industries they cover in one way or another.

My current magazine stack

For hospitality industry news, I always always always scour Skift, Financial Times, and now more increasingly Business of Fashion as the fashion industry has started to lean into hospitality more and more.

What’s the last great book you read?

I love this question!

I love biographies, and I had a period last year when I exclusively read rockstar biographies. Now, as I have exhausted that category, I’ve moved on to the rockstars of hospitality. I regret almost everything by Keith McNally, the man and the myth behind Balthazar and other iconic NYC institutions, was both brutally honest and an exciting read about the inner workings of legendary hospitality.

What are you reading now?

I’m currently reading Son of Nobody by Yann Martel (author of Life of Pi), which I picked up on a whim at a bookstore in Paris. Super new (came out in March 2026), and for some reason, I haven’t seen anyone talk about it yet?

It’s about a classics researcher who finds a lost Greek epic, and his life starts intertwining with the story. It’s like Rick Riordan’s Percy Jackson series but for grown-ups.

I have a Kindle, but highly prefer physical books

What’s your reading strategy when you pick up a print copy of your favorite publication?

I’m very diligent and go from cover to cover. Granted, I will skim over pieces that do not interest me whatsoever from the get-go, but that is rarely something I have to do with my go-to publications. I’m also not aboard the culture of quickly abandoning books or articles when they do not interest you - I think it’s good to be challenged, and I’ve found absolute gems of ideas when I’ve continued to read pieces with an open mind.

Who should everyone be reading that they’re not?

I want to start off by saying that LinkedIn is not a platform I gladly spend much time on.

That said, my friend, Rebecca Emsalem, writes a ridiculously good and fresh newsletter on there called the Playground, where she just pitches the quirkiest brand concepts ever. It’s one of the few LinkedIn publications I look forward to.

Food for thought from the most recent edition:

“Consumption becomes a continuous cycle of identity role-play, where brands act as symbols through which individuals express who they are, who they want to become, and where they believe they belong or not. Community is the touchpoint a brand controls the least, and that might shape perception the most.”

What is the best non-famous app you love on your phone?

I’m a proud member of Gen-Z, and like many of my peers, I have a toxic relationship to my phone. My (also Gen-Z) sister introduced me to OneSec, which forces you to take deep breaths before opening an app. I have set it to 4 deep breaths, which is enough time for me to contemplate if I really want to open LinkedIn to scroll on 99% AI generated content (the answer is almost always no!).

Plane or train?

I love a good train, and if I can, I always opt for it. When traveling domestically in Finland or Switzerland, for instance, it just makes the most sense. In Finland, I always upgrade the class because I get a comfier seat, silence, and “free” coffee.

What is one place everyone should visit?

I think everything I’ve said so far just sounds like a Visit Finland ad (which is not the case, although I wish it was), but I think everyone should experience the wilderness and immense peace of Finnish Lapland.

I have to say, it’s not for the weak, as in the winter it’s pitch black and you’ll freeze, and in the Summer, you’ll be faced with mosquitoes and also probably freeze. But the nature is still so beautiful and untouched, and you can just rent a little cabin with a sauna by a lake and be good.

Source: trust me bro.

Tell us the story of a rabbit hole you fell deep into.

As mentioned earlier, in my bachelor’s studies in philosophy, I also studied Indian philosophy and Buddhist traditions. I love finding ways to relate ancient wisdom to our current world and make connections that could (fairly) be deemed as far-fetched and obscure.

A recent rabbit hole happened when I wanted to brush up on my knowledge of mahayana Buddhism and the chakra system, and now I’m 3 books in (a great and approachable one is Wheels of Life by Anodea Judith) and writing a series on the chakra systems and hospitality.

If being on Substack has taught me anything, it’s that if I find it interesting, there’s probably at least one person (my mom) who will find it stimulating too and give me notes and thoughts. (EN)
