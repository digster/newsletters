Geordie is a man of impeccable style. He works at Berry Bros. & Rudd, the UK’s oldest wine and spirits merchant. We’re happy to have him with us this week. 
Tell us about yourself.
I joined Berry Bros. & Rudd, the UK’s oldest wine and spirits merchant, over 25 years ago and have been lucky enough to build a career that has taken me from London to Asia and the United States. Still family-owned and run today, BB&R has a rich heritage dating back to 1698, but what has always excited me is its willingness to look forward. In my current role, I spend my time exploring new opportunities for growth, whether that’s through new partnerships, products, or business models. It’s a bit like working for a 328-year-old start-up.
Describe your media diet.
I love print magazines, particularly about architecture and interior decoration, but those are mainly saved for the weekend. During the week I listen to endless podcasts on a variety of subjects. Although a lot seem to be on American politics; American Friction, Americast, and Pod Save America are all particularly good. I also listen to a few business history podcasts. Acquired is a fantastic deep dive into well-known businesses - fascinating stuff!
What’s the last great book you read?
I have just returned from a wonderful family holiday to Yosemite Valley. In preparation I read The Last Season, by Eric Blehm, which tells the true story of legendary backcountry ranger Randy Morgenson, and his mysterious disappearance in the Sierra Nevada. It is an extraordinary story of wilderness, solitude, and self-discovery.
What are you reading now?
I tend to have a few books on the go at the same time. I have just started Jon Ronson’s new book, The Castle, which explores the modern crisis of masculinity. I am enjoying The Artist, by Lucy Steeds, which feels like a holiday in postwar Provence, and I am almost finished restaurateur Jeremy King’s autobiography, Without Reservation, which is full of the best sort of name-dropping.
What’s your reading strategy when you pick up a print copy of your favorite publication?
I prefer to read print publications rather than the digital versions, which struggle to maintain my attention. A coffee is obligatory, and I quite like a bustling café for ambiance.
Who should everyone be reading that they’re not?
Papers with views that they don’t necessarily agree with. It’s important to avoid the echo chamber.
What is the best non-famous app you love on your phone?
Karl Weather, which visualises hyper-local weather and microclimate changes in San Francisco. I lived there a few ago and it was invaluable. I still check in from time to time to see what is happening.
Plane or train?
I spent half of my childhood on trains. Nothing beats a good train journey!
What is one place everyone should visit?
For some reason I had always avoided Venice, thinking is would be too hot, busy, and smelly (in fairness, all can be true). I went there for the first time in 2025 after a wonderful trip hiking in the Dolomites and have been back twice since.
Tell us the story of a rabbit hole you fell deep into.
There have been many! My girlfriend and I recently started beekeeping, and we now have two hives at home in the countryside. So, I have read an awful lot of books about bees recently.
