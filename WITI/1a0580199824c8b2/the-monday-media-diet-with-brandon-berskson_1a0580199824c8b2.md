Tell us about yourself.
I’m 31, from San Diego, and live in New York. I’m a hospitality-obsessed media junkie who runs two publications.[ Hotels Above Par](https://substack.com/redirect/d7f54353-19f5-4748-a095-ce186c481cfd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o), which I started in April 2020 after being furloughed from my travel PR job, covers boutique hotels plus the people and brands behind them for nearly 500,000 readers; my Substack,[ Hotel Espresso](https://substack.com/redirect/109d3f84-a96f-43a3-8b6d-245feebd3224?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o), goes behind the scenes of it all with hot takes, hotel recommendations, and notes on working as an entrepreneur in media.[ Mexzine](https://substack.com/redirect/97fce03d-aa73-4e8a-bd8c-52e0d232ba04?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o) launched on Substack two months ago, because no English-language publication existed for vetted, non-touristy, independent-leaning Mexico recommendations. It’s reader-supported and written by journalists who live there. My passion for Mexico runs deep—I’ve been more times than I can count. With both publications, I try to champion journalism as much as I can.
Describe your media diet.
I’m a Monocle loyalist. I absorb everything they say and collect every issue. The Thuma furniture I just ordered has a whole shelf dedicated to my treasure trove of back issues—I told my partner it was a non-negotiable when we moved in together. They aren’t going anywhere.
I read Air Mail with a publisher’s eye. From a business and media strategy perspective, the way they position their pieces magnetizes my curiosity, and it’s a steady dose of empirical inspiration for how I want to grow my own brand.
And I’m hooked on Substack, of course. Like most media people on the platform, I read every issue of Feed Me. Rich People Shit by Carson Griffith is another captivating one. After that, it’s mostly travel:[ The Other HAFH by Sanchita LaMore](https://substack.com/redirect/002ae009-69b5-40f7-83e7-297e336313da?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o) and[ MindHoliday by Bella Darden](https://substack.com/redirect/002ae009-69b5-40f7-83e7-297e336313da?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o) are two I adore.
What’s the last great book you read?
But You Did Not Come Back is the rawest memoir I’ve read. Most people don’t know this about me, but I’m insatiably drawn to Holocaust literature—a massive departure, obviously, from what I write about. I presume it’s because so much of my family perished in it, and being connected to those who came before me feels intrinsic. Marceline Loridan-Ivens, a French survivor, wrote the book as a letter to her father, who died in the camps. The way she recounts what Auschwitz did to her, and how the pain never ceased even after liberation, moved me to such a degree that I’ve read the paperback three times.
What are you reading now?
To a whole other groove—one that’s much lighter—I’m in the midst of reading Gary Janetti’s We Are Experiencing a Slight Delay. His writing is witty.
What’s your reading strategy when you pick up a print copy of your favorite publication?
Monocle. I head straight to the back, where the travel lives. Then I work my way forward to the front—but always starting from the back, because that’s where my favorite section begins.
Who should everyone be reading that they’re not?
[Italy Segreta](https://substack.com/redirect/cdda1d1b-e15d-45ba-bdcb-9d32453abbde?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o). An independent publication devoted entirely to Italy. I go a couple of times a year to “The Boot”—my favorite hotel in the world is[ Casa Angelina](https://substack.com/redirect/c830eaba-25ea-4563-a633-512e9ec9bb90?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o) in Praiano—and from sensational Sicily to metropolitan Milan, I heed their advice on where to stay, eat, drink, and visit. Their visual weight holds substance, too: cinematic, unposed, provenance-capturing photographs, with not a sliver of press-kit stock image anywhere in sight.
What is the best non-famous app you love on your phone?
[Opulist](https://substack.com/redirect/c1816099-f3f5-4efb-8d5d-bbca228b1979?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o). It’s an app and search site for beautiful places. The brand is gaining real momentum, so I can’t quite call it under-the-radar anymore—but I’ve been a fan since the beginning. I know the founder, and I consider her a visionary. She filled a genuine gap: a palatable resource for people who want to stay, eat, drink, and visit in aesthetically appealing spaces.
Plane or train?
Plane, all the way. If we are talking about ideal airborne situations, Etihad or Emirates first class.
What is one place everyone should visit?
Wow, so many come to mind. I’m going to have to say Baja California Sur. I’m not talking about Cabo’s touristy fray, where colossal mega-resorts wall off the shoreline. It’s everything outside that small radius I’m touting. Untamed miles of desert meet the sea, and dusty dirt roads lead unprepossessingly to standalone architectural marvels in the form of boutique hotels and villas. That’s especially true in Todos Santos, a surf town with a clutch of independent stays—my love language, of course. I always tell Baja-bound travelers to stay at[ Desierto Azul](https://substack.com/redirect/cd438a05-0b94-4556-8939-bba33ccbc6d1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o), the family-owned, four-key property that’s one of my happy places: unfussy, boho-chic, minimalist, with a photogenic vanishing-edge pool that disappears into the desert. And that’s only a fraction of it.
Tell us the story of a rabbit hole you fell deep into.
Hmmmm. My ADD spawns rabbit hole after rabbit hole. There are a lot of these in my quotidian life.
Ok, so my answer here is controversial and out of left field. About a year ago, I was scrolling Instagram when an influencer showed up on my feed documenting his time in Afghanistan, glamorizing his hangs with the Taliban and telling us they’re “actually really nice.” Flabbergasted, I went to YouTube to see if there were more like him. There were so many. Anyway: Western influencers doing Afghanistan tourism with the nefarious Taliban as their tour guides, and the moral weight of it, live in my mind rent-free. (BB)
