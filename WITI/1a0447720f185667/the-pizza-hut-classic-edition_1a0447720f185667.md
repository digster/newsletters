Colin here. Pizza Hut played a weird and outsized role for people of a certain age in certain parts of America. Though now most associated with fast food delivery, back in the day it was actually a gathering place for everything from the post-soccer tournament pizza party to a friend’s birthday. And it was oddly cozy and convivial: There would be tablecloths, wood, lots of larger tables, and of course the iconic lamp, recently called out by the New York Times as a These iconic, old Pizza Huts followed all the established rules of a brand positioning textbook. They had visually distinctive red roofs, tables with white and red checkered tablecloths. I also remember large red cups where you could put every available beverage in the self-serve machine into one drink, as well as the salad bars and actual video game consoles. Why is this interesting? Nostalgia is increasingly a lever that marketers use (to powerful effect) when trying to put the jumper cables on in a brand. So, unsurprisingly, Pizza Hut is now keen to evoke some of these memories. Recently, it’s been rolling out “Pizza Hut Classic” locations around the US. What is most interesting to me is this isn’t really a stunt, but something that cost some CapEx, and also likely required some serious crate digging into the archives. Naturally, these openings are resonating with everyone who lived through the first era of this design, and who loved eating their personal pan pizzas under the gentle glow of those iconic lamps.
