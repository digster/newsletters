Three OpenAI safety researchers, Those employees have published an open letter. The employees have said they believe they were fired for safety advocacy, and that the reasons given were pretextual. The letter calls upon OpenAI to take actions going forward on open discussion and safety auditing, which OpenAI says they strongly support. OpenAI responded, saying there was an additional breach of trust that was not mentioned in the employee statements, without claiming that the employees were (or were not) informed of what it was. As you would expect given OpenAI’s track record, most outside of OpenAI who discussed this believe the researchers and not OpenAI. Whereas those at OpenAI have not offered comment at all. No matter what happened, and how this is handled from here, this sucks bigly. OpenAI has three less good safety researchers. Trust will be lower. Speech will be chilled. Table of ContentsYou’re Fired

Chilling EffectThere was no world where OpenAI was going to get up and say they do not support an open and transparent culture of dialogue, or that they no longer care about monitorability, or that they are abandoning their public commitments on embedded evaluators. The only way to affirm an open and transparent culture of dialogue, and okay with full collaboration with outsiders and auditors and evaluators on safety issues, is to send costly or otherwise credible signals that you are truly okay with that, and not only will it not get you fired it will be at worst neutral for your career. That is not easy. OpenAI has done a lot of good work here over time exactly by having a bunch of people, including Roon, post a lot of things. If OpenAI wants to undo the damage and chilling effect, including at other labs, they have to convince their employees, and ideally everyone, that safety advocacy, both internal and external, had nothing to do with why these three employees were fired. OpenAI must give confidence that trust was lost for other good reasons, and that such fierce advocacy was and continues to be welcomed and celebrated. It is what it is. Choices are made. OpenAI Firing These Employees For Safety Advocacy Would Be Deeply StupidI don’t know the real reason these three employees were fired. Even the employees don’t fully know. They can guess, but the process is opaque. There are many reasons, good and bad, why the reason you are given may not be the real one, or the central one, or the whole story. What I do know, and what is being neglected in discussions over these events, is that ‘these three high level and highly valuable OpenAI safety researchers were fired for their safety advocacy’ would be deeply, deeply stupid. These were extremely valuable employees, whose loss will hurt. If you fire them without good cause, the world is going to notice this, and it is going to be an extremely bad look, and have a chilling effect and damage the safety ecosystem, and potentially cost you other current and potential employees. This was an expensive action to take even if the three employees had gone quietly. And if you fire them on pretexts, it is going to be even more expensive. Some will say that the chilling effect on safety advocacy and cooperation was the point, to cripple such efforts and avoid future exposure of future problems. I don’t buy that, and if it was true there were vastly better ways to go about getting the part of this that OpenAI finds valuable. The story that this was based centrally on any form of public communications or internal championing of various safety measures, and that everything else is pure pretext, never made sense to me. We also know that the reasons in the letter were not the central reason, because that is contradicted by OpenAI Newsroom’s Tweet (reproduced in full later) referring to a breach of trust, combined with the reasons otherwise looking like pretexts. A Significant Breach of TrustLet me repeat the bolded sentence:
There are two key implied claims here, although they are not logically forced. The underlying justification relied on key events not named by the employees. A key underlying justification is this ‘significant breach of trust,’ whatever that is.
I believe OpenAI on both counts. Nothing else makes sense. It was never likely that there were three top safety researchers who centrally broke trust in three distinct ways. Much more likely is one particular cause, and one investigation into one central cause, whatever it was. That does not mean that the researchers did anything wrong, or that what they did wrong rose to the level of ‘you need to be fired.’ It does mean that somehow things escalated to the point where trust had broken down, no matter who was at fault. What are the resulting possibilities? OpenAI’s core reasons (good or bad, true or false) went unshared. The employees were told the core reasons, and are withholding that.
Trust is not optional. If it is irrevocably broken, you don’t have a choice, no matter what caused that break in the first place. But nothing we know about seems like it should rise to a level that should irrevocably break trust. OpenAI also says that they do not anticipate that further information exchange would lead to a resolution. I believe them. OpenAI has an explicit request from the employees to share more information, but that does not make this wise. Sometimes you are doing your best and yet saying more things would only make things worse. That does not get OpenAI out of the consequences of being this avoidant. This Is Terrible But Not Maximally TerribleI do think many are jumping to a ‘maximally messed up’ interpretation that I find unlikely to be true, because of the incentives involved. Here are some things that make the situation less than maximally terrible: I believe that OpenAI did NOT fire these employees due to their publicly or internally expressed concerns about safety. I believe that OpenAI did NOT fire these employees due to their advocacy of 3P commitments or third party auditors or embedded evaluators. I believe that OpenAI did NOT fire these employees in order to create a chilling effect or reduce employee cooperation with third parties.
That still leaves us without a good explanation, probably permanently, and with the three employees claiming, I believe sincerely, that they were fired for their safety advocacy. Given the long and positive track record of all three of them, this was at best a huge unforced error, even if OpenAI was for whatever reason backed into taking an L on this.
