When we see widespread opposition to transmission lines, and Thesis: People Mostly Dislike Data Centers Because They Dislike and Distrust AI, Tech Companies, Big Money And Building ThingsThis is my central model. Building data centers combines: AI. Big tech. Other people having data, especially on you. Big money. Building things, especially ugly things. Not offering that many jobs.
People dislike and distrust these six things. So they hate data centers. They are also being asked to accept abstract arguments over concrete concerns, the reverse of the situation regarding things like existential risks. The rest, as they say, is commentary. It is what people latch onto. They mostly dislike these things for reasons that are wrong in their details, but highly understandable. Why is opposition rising so quickly? What changed over the last year? My answer is, mostly bandwagon effects, preference cascades and a rise in salience, combined with AI becoming vastly more visible and starting to impact the world including the job market, combined with a push to build vastly more data centers, which is forcing companies to push into communities that are less receptive. No It’s Mostly Not the Messaging About AI In General
Those same people blaming everything on bad marketing, of course, mostly also think the claims that they say are terrible marketing were intended as marketing. They think everything is marketing. That everything is vibes. That reality doesn’t matter. They are wrong. People very much are noticing things about underlying reality, especially locally as it impacts them and people they know. They are using empiricism and first principles reasoning, and picking up vibes from each other. It is possible to intentionally steer this process with the right words, but it is a lot harder than it looks.
They fear for their jobs because it is sensible to fear for your job given what AI can clearly do, and how people are planning for and reacting to AI. They fear that humanity will be usurped or wiped out because that is an obvious thing to worry about when building smarter artificial minds, as illustrated by every story and thought experiment about such minds ever, without any need to get into the more detailed and technical reasons for worry. They fear their culture and things they care about will be wiped out, because they’ve seen it before and can see it happening again in real time in some ways. They fear that big tech and big money are full of false promises and there will be a catch, because of the entire history of everything, as one would observe from the average person’s point of view. They don’t know economics, and never have. They do notice that in many cases communities allowing such construction look like they’re getting screwed out of their ‘fair share’ although in some cases not. They fear building and care about relative position. They are not abundance libertarians.
People’s concerns about tech are often coming from very basic places, you know?

No This Isn’t Luxury Belief or Moral PanicI absolutely believe that luxury beliefs and moral panics are common. But centrally, that isn’t what is going on here.
People understand that data centers power AI. That is why they are against it. They understand that AI is ‘good for the economy.’ They don’t care about an abstract economy. They care about their experiences, locally, and they fear for their futures. A luxury belief is when you are wilfully blind to the downsides, but use those beliefs as signaling and to seek positional goods. I don’t think either half is true here. A moral panic is when people have an unjustified and disproportionate fixation on the dangers of something, especially something that doesn’t exist or is extremely rare, that one would look back upon as rather silly. Water use by data centers, in particular, is a classic moral panic, but that’s a symptom, the same way that an endless stream of sexual or religious moral panics are a symptom, including today’s such panics. Do these people understand the full economic, strategic or service implications if everyone were to ban data centers? No, but they’re not being wilfully blind to it, and often would be fine with it on reflection. A Lot Of People Really Do Want To Stop AI

I am very much in the ‘more data centers in America would be good’ camp, because chips are highly economically valuable, the physical concerns are highly solvable when they’re not outright fake, and if we don’t put the chips here then someone will have to put them somewhere else. That would be worse. To the extent this opposition is successfully forcing chips overseas, it is very bad. One can route around the places that say no, but only up to a point. Lack of domestic placement has been used to justify putting massive numbers of chips in places like the UAE and KSA. That is way worse. It is not hard to imagine a world in which that bites us very hard, up to and including existentially. There are those I respect who are very worried about existential risk from AI and see that dynamic differently, and think the anti-data center movement is a good thing and want to support it. I get why, but I believe that they are wrong even on their own terms, because I think the dynamics of shutting down construction within America would shift construction elsewhere rather than reducing it overall. Solving these issues will not be easy. It is a lot easier to not lose trust and not trigger such a panic in the first place, than it is to repair the damage once it happens. The politics could easily turn into a runaway train here. But there are also a lot of marginal ways to make progress on the logistics or the politics, a lot of which are low-hanging fruit.
