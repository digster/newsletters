It was the probably worst day in cybersecurity history. So far. Not only is there a critical flaw in PHP that due to a leak was announced a day before the fix would be available, sending most of the internet scrambling for solutions and likely dooming a lot of long tail websites permanently, there were several other unrelated incidents. The floodgates seem to have been opened. I have a market on whether there will be an even worse day before the end of the year.

Another key leak this week was the draft bill Anthropic had been shopping around Congress, similar to but weaker than the FRONTIER Act. Anthropic’s initial attempts to explain itself only made the situation seem worse, as they seemed to not understand the problem with what they had done. Dario’s later response was better, but the situation remains troubling. There are enough different Anthropic developments, that I plan to have a post soon covering them all. It is a mix of news running from excellent to terrible.

Will interpretability save us? There is a new claim that neural networks are cleaner than we thought, meaning that individual neurons mostly have a single interpretable meaning, and it is tractable to go through and label them one by one. Initial reactions got out of hand, treating this as a far bigger development than it was. This is still excellent news that makes our estimate of difficulty substantially lower going forward. The work can begin.

We had better get on that, because there have been quite a few misalignment incidents.

I continue to owe a write-up of the Gemini 4 Argon Safety Card. That is in progress and hopefully I will finish it soon. Life has gotten in the way. I may or may not have a full capabilities report on Gemini 4 Argon once I tally up the reactions. Muse Wonder definitely will not have such a post, as it has proven to have been oversold.

Yes, of course the AIs will be ‘more accurate’ to the law plus underlying ground truth, but if we wanted accuracy we would not use typical juries.

The point of a jury is to provide a check on the system and avoid abuses or overly strict adherence to the letter of the law, and to protect the innocent and our rights, not to be accurate. Get My Agent On The Line

UPS blocks AI agents. So do for example Delta, JP Morgan Chase, Charles Schwab and Amazon. The group doing this by default is going to keep expanding. The agents will still be useful, but less so.

The biggest other practical barrier for agents like Dots, and also many new Claude projects, is overrefusals and misclassifications that require permissions. This is a hard problem, since the other error is worse. As an example, I gave my Dot a ‘do not delete anything that does not have source control’ rule before trying anything else, and it took that to some rather extreme places. Claude auto mode asking for permission so often is actively less safe than if it asked less, because many users will quickly stop even reading the requests.

If you share a Muse conversation it can get indexed and end up in the public records. In general it seems fair to assume that if you share an AI conversation there is a good chance the link will get indexed, so if you’re not okay with that then don’t share it directly.

They Took Our Jobs

Is AI changing the world? Freddie deBoer says no, ‘human life in 2026 appears to be almost entirely similar to human life in 2021.’ Jack disagrees, pointing out that all his friends and family have had their jobs transformed.

Jack: Five years of AI has changed the world dramatically more than five years of electricity or five years of the combustion engine did. It’s not even close.

AI so far has simultaneously changed the world more than any other technology (at least if you exclude military techs that won active wars) in its first five years. AI so far has also changed the world dramatically less than I or many others would have expected in that time, if we had been told the path of AI capabilities. Diffusion takes time and a lot of things have been bottlenecked.

As AI use continues to grow exponentially, expect bigger impacts, with the impact growing faster all the time, even if capabilities mostly stop improving from here.

Anthropic is hiring for a smaller share of junior positions and AI agent hours outnumber employee hours six to one. Human attention is a bottleneck for AI outputs and human productivity is way up. That’s the central finding of a new Anthropic report on what it is like to work there.

Chip City

IFP offers a big new report on chips and America’s lead over China. They conclude that America is currently six months ahead but could double this lead to a year by enforcing existing laws and using existing authority.

Tim Fist (IFP): On distillation: the current ~16x US training compute advantage would normally yield a 13-month lead. But we estimate adversarial distillation halves this to around 6.5 months.

Tim Fist, Georgia Adamson, and Konstantin Pilz (Institute for Progress): The size of the US-China AI capabilities gap is largely downstream of two factors US policymakers can influence today: China’s access to AI chips, and China’s ability to distill the capabilities of US models.

Tim Fist: The two most important interventions are:

- For BIS: Create a new end-use control to restrict China’s remote access to training clusters abroad — China’s largest and fastest-growing source of frontier-scale compute.

- For DOJ/FTC: Create a narrow safe harbor to allow US companies to coordinate on countering Chinese adversarial distillation.

Tim Fist, Georgia Adamson, and Konstantin Pilz (Institute for Progress): We estimate China’s access to AI chips will roughly double between the end of 2026 and the end of 2027. Of the portion of these chips that the US could restrict China’s access to, around half are in data centers outside China that the Chinese AI ecosystem will access remotely.

I strongly endorse cracking down on Chinese use of AI chips, as well as Chinese imports of AI chips. Even the Chinese chips that will get produced will often rely on imports of semiconductor manufacturing equipment that is a limiting factor. We should not allow that. Yes, they will build up domestic alternatives over time, but they will do that no matter what.

I also strongly endorse a safe harbor for countering distillation. The logic is similar to why safety should have such a safe harbor, except the argument is even stronger. There are competitive pressures pushing labs to not take proper defenses, and shared knowledge would be valuable.

If you are using adversarial distillation (I call it massive fraudulent distillation) to get the lead down from 13 months to 6.5 months, then both numbers matter.

  1. Chinese models available now are as good as American models 6.5 months ago.

  2. If America stopped releasing models, it would take 13 months for China to match our current best models.

My guess is that the 6.5 estimate is about right, and the 13 months is low. Notice on this chart that the 6.5 month lead is widening steadily over time, on top of 6.5 months now representing more generations than before.

If America were to maintain the current lead (6.5 months with distillation, 13 months to catch up fully) then for many purposes we will be happy to use small open models that are ‘good enough’ for quick tasks, but the frontier lead will look bigger in 2027 than it did in 2026.

If we follow IFP’s suggestions, they estimate China will only progress at half-speed in 2027, and end the year 13.1 months behind. That would be a dramatic change. A year behind still limits our options in other ways, but gives us a lot more flexibility.

For those involved in such policy discussions please read (or have your staff and AI read) the full report.

nev: Surprisingly, high neuron activations alone work well as a replacement for the MLP. After correcting for scale with some finetuning, neurons are better than any transcoder we tested even under extreme sparsity. So what do transcoder features add?

When we look at circuits, many transcoder features have very similar weights and activations to specific neurons. We call these neuron features. In most transcoders, neuron features make up over half of the activations in late layers.

I do not find this relevant to the ultimate question of orthogonality, as in whether a mind with any capabilities is capable of pursuing any goal, which I still expect at the limit. The good news about ‘at the limit’ is that it could be a while before you get to that limit, and the minds in between can be highly useful.

Cooperative Alignment

Let the AIs learn about who you are, let them talk about it, and even have them build a database. This is often, although not always, the correct strategy. Sometimes you want an unbiased response.

There are some good reasons to worry people are overselling interpretability and recent related advances, but there is no conflict between that and treating AIs well. You can and should absolutely do both, indeed they can assist with each other.

In at least some cases Claude can learn to route around classifiers on its own. Most versions of this seem to improve the Pareto frontier right now, but this does not bode well if you are counting on classifiers as a form of AI Control.
