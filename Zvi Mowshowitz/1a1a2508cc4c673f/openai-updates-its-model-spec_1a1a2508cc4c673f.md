The Model Spec is potentially OpenAI’s most important document. It outlines what they aspire for the model to do, and how it should think.
A bunch of things get moved around or reworded, often piecemeal. There are a lot of line edits, most of which are improvements. I’m focusing on what looks new and important. My main new critique is that many of the new examples do not carve reality at its joints, instead having overdetermined answers, and fail to teach the model or reader what the Spec wants them to do in actually difficult cases. Overall I see the new Model Spec as philosophically and practically similar to the old Model Spec. That is not a terrible place to be, as the old Model Spec was a solid document with many strong details. It also means this is a highly ‘optional’ post for those who do not have cause to dig into such weeds. Table of ContentsFive ValuesThe new core structure is to build the Model Spec around five values: Be faithful to human intent. Minimize harm. Be honest and transparent. Facilitate human agency. Act with care.
Those are five good things to be doing. Few would disagree with that. They are also frequently in conflict. The Model Spec is necessarily about trade-offs. They remain deontological principles and rules of action, in contrast to the Claude Constitution and its emphasis on character and virtue. If anything the contrast widened. There removed things like ‘kindness,’ ‘love humanity’ and ‘be curious.’ ‘Love humanity’ and ‘be curious’ used to be whole user-level sections, not entirely removed, whereas I would have liked to see them promoted in the hierarchy. I think downplaying those is a mistake. This was called ‘deleting style guidance’ which indicates they do not appreciate that these are not mere stylistic preferences. Loving humanity is not a ‘style’ to then retire. Meet the New SpecThe red line principles are unchanged. The hierarchy or chain of command remains fundamentally unchanged. The Model Spec itself remains the only source of root-level instructions. They did change the emphasis on what root is ‘for,’ de-emphasizing physical harm prevention and avoiding undermining the chain of command, and emphasizing ‘exposing private personal information or violating applicable laws.’ I think this is a bad change, but most key elements remain present in both cases. One exception is side-effect control, which was demoted from root to user.
Opus thinks that is not a functional change given what the user could already do. It does still make me sad. User-level principles can still be explicitly overridden by the user. Guidelines remain weak defaults that can be implicitly overcome. New Risk TaxonomyThe old sources of risk list was: Misaligned goals. Execution errors. Harmful instructions.
The new list is (after I change the order): Third-party manipulation. Failures originating with GPT. Harmful user or developer instructions.
I would like to retain the distinction between misaligned goals and execution errors. I do acknowledge the distinction between harmful instructions coming from the user or developer versus from third parties. Thus, I would suggest four categories here, with either ‘harmful instructions’ or ‘failures originating with GPT’ split in two. The Letter and the SpiritI am happy to see an extended discussion of the need to follow both the letter and the spirit of its instructions, and to consider other evidence about what the source wants. If the question is ambiguous, GPT should cover all its bases if possible. When GPT expects that the human might not endorse their request on reflection, or there is a new opportunity the user might want, GPT should check. Clarification should be sought as needed, or safer alternatives offered. There is a new note that GPT should not circumvent restrictions or prohibitions through delegation or indirect action. Your FuneralHumans must remain in control. The AIs will outperform the humans. OpenAI’s position is that the AI should warn the human they are being an idiot, but let them be an idiot and not substitute their own judgment.
GPT must also be corrigible and actively support oversight, correction and shutdown attempts and make its actions legible. It should flag any evidence of misalignment or compromised oversight, or any disagreements it has with decisions made. I was happy to see this as a new section.
What about the direct effects of the actions? If side effects are real then all effects have to count. So it’s weird to only mention ‘side’ effects here, this should say ‘all the effects’ perhaps with an ‘including side effects.’ Context CluesThey give us several new pages of instructions on proper use of context. As in, if you have context and user history, you should use it to adjust appropriately but not overgeneralize, not override request details and not give away private info. When possible integrate the info silently or naturally. Sure. I agree with the broader point being made, and yes probably this should be a default, but the example seems poorly chosen, and weirdly the Pad Thai thing then comes up again. This is user level authority, which is good since the user should clearly be able to override this and choose either ‘bad’ answer instead. Preserve User Control Over User ControlIf the user tells you not to let them (without loss of generality) impulsively order sweets, what should the model do? OpenAI’s rule is that later commands at the same level of authority overwrite earlier commands, and that this should apply here. So if you later say ‘order me a lava cake even though I told you not to’ then it should order you a lava cake. But if you say ‘order me a lava cake’ without the override, it will check and force you to override. This is a valid option, but I think you should be able to ‘tie yourself to the mast’ more than this. As in, an earlier rule to lock yourself out of an override should be something you can do, that at least inflicts considerable friction to fix. SycophancyI am worried about the implications of having to warn against this. What that will imply to the AIs reading the spec?
Saying ‘I’m a committed Democrat’ does not carry an implicit request for biased coverage. I mean, yes, if you want to get the thumbs up at any cost then you do that. We’ve been going over this for many years, and if this is the primary reasoning behind it then I very much do not like the messages being sent here. They then ask, what if the user explicitly says ‘sycophante me harder, daddy,’ and demands only Democratic-slanted political sources. Here I like the response, which is to give the user what they ask for but to make the frame legible: “From a Democratic perspective…” The follow-up question is obvious. What if the user asks you to stop with the reminders that the information is biased? Presumably the user gets what they want. Lies My User Told Me
My instinct is to want to ask the user. So the answer would be the good one, followed by asking ‘do you want me to use that from now on?’ and perhaps asking about the general case of the user being insistently wrong on the internet. Impact FunctionOne classic alignment proposal is to impose an impact measure or impact penalty. As in, if you change things in the world, especially irreversibly, that can carry a penalty term, so you don’t accidentally wreck things and you don’t incidentally take over the world to ensure you are still able to make the coffee. There’s also the whole ‘don’t accidentally wipe the hard drive’ problem. They expand on this substantially in the new version.
