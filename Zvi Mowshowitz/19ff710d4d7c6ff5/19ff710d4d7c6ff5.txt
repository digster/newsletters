As AI has escalated increasingly quickly, more and more of my posts have ended up focusing on AI. 
This past month, with the hacking incidents at OpenAI and elsewhere, that has hit the limit, where if you count Lightcone Commons [ https://substack.com/redirect/07ea873d-9667-4d34-bd62-515414a76c23?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] then every single post since the last monthly was primarily about AI in some form. 
That is not how I want this to work in the long term. We need breaks to experience new things and refresh our thinking, and to not forget about the rest of the world. If things are not fully on fire, I plan on getting back to the roundups on childhood and education, and on fertility, on housing and also on dating. And I want to get back to writing more focused posts on those and other topics. It’s important, and I need to avoid too much audience capture.
On to the monthly roundup of all things that don’t go somewhere else.

Plagiarize 
Plagiarism is terrible in its own right, as theft, and like cockroaches if you see one instance it almost never stops at one. It is also a reliable indicator of general dishonesty and lack of honor [ https://substack.com/redirect/37972465-0aa5-4b74-b527-47ead9793c02?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], and thus of much worse things. 
Thus, in the latest case of Jason Arday, we quickly found many instances of data fabrication and other fraud. Kevin Bryan finds that every paper of Arday’s he examined [ https://substack.com/redirect/6acd59c1-32ae-44a9-8f10-20fd157c9b58?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] was not only fraud, but rather obvious fraud. A huge portion of everything, perhaps all of it, seems to be flat out made up. 
His resignation letter, unsurprisingly, was identified by Pangram as entirely written by AI [ https://substack.com/redirect/9e5402a3-26ad-498a-8fde-02783cffb9d8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ].
When will we get the reckoning, as in when we use Claude to run basic integrity checks across all of academia? If you are doing a plagiarism, unless you are stealing unpublished work like that of your students, the only thing protecting you is security through obscurity. When will we stop putting up with institutions that tolerate not only clear plagiarism but obvious fraud? 
The Jury Duty Scam
The ‘you missed jury duty [ https://substack.com/redirect/9aaaed7a-27b1-4d29-a6d0-e7258df6571d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] and now you have to pay or else’ scam is on the rise. Authorities never do this, so you can easily know this is 100% a scam, but people don’t know that. The scam can work on those who don’t remember or know this, because it uses particular details to appear credible when they threaten to arrest you. There are some very very obvious ‘this is a scam’ neon signs one could spot, but people aren’t thinking clearly. 
The whole thing seems ‘classically executed’ throughout, so this is an illustration that scams are mostly still very much in the pre-AI era, although perhaps AI is used to scout for targets and gather the info. I agree with Shoshana Weissmann that we could do a lot more [ https://substack.com/redirect/3f3950b9-2a89-4644-bf4a-0aae30a74348?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] to catch the bastards. I realize that law enforcement has limited resources, but it seems impossible for such efforts not to pay for themselves many times over. In general, massively larger investment in enforcement of white collar and fraud crime seems like it would be a huge and simple win.
Play The Good Guy
Sarah is correct here, in most circumstances. Take the win.
Sarah Constantin [ https://substack.com/@sarahconstantin/note/c-297011516 ]: Something I’m gradually drilling into my head:
If someone’s worldview amounts to dividing up the world into Good Guys and Bad Guys, and if this person is friendly towards you & you want a good relationship with them, it’s prudent to always presume that it’s mutually understood that you’re one of the Good Guys. Even if you think their Good Guy/Bad Guy categorization is unfair; even if you think you logically would fall into the Bad Guy category, by their definition.
If they like you, they already believe you’re a Good Guy, and are not strictly abiding by their own “rules.” Stop being shocked that people can be unprincipled; it’s true, and it means that you can’t change their worldview by going “you like ME but I’m one of the Bad Guys, checkmate!” Accept the unprincipled favoritism, or decide you don’t want a close relationship with the person.
There are notably rare exceptions, when you can use this to change their minds about their worldview and classification system, but so long as they do not otherwise need their focus drawn to the relevant information it is not in your local interest to try.
Don’t Dither
She is also correct here: Don’t dither [ https://substack.com/redirect/cf5201d4-532f-476b-be02-2455562c887e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], especially when you are young.
As in, if you have a decision, especially a major life decision, and everything about you screams that you want to Do The Thing, then Do The Thing, rather than waiting because you are ‘not sure.’ 
Sarah Constantin [ https://substack.com/redirect/cf5201d4-532f-476b-be02-2455562c887e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]: You want to do the thing. You think doing the thing is the right move. I can’t promise you that you’re right! But I do have a sort of faith that people can’t, in practice, do much better than following their own integrated judgment and desire. 
To put it more superstitiously, even if it is a bad move, it’s a bad move that you kind of “have to make”. It’s a necessary part of how your life must unfold, given that you are you.
And to put it less superstitiously, it’s important for development, learning, human flourishing, all that stuff, to move forward with your life, to take on the “natural” next challenge or opportunity that “advances the plot” and will require you to learn and change. You are not going to get out of life 100% unscathed; nobody is wholly untouched by regret or adversity. So you might as well make the decision you want to make. 
In particular, I far more often see young people underrate experimentation than overrate it. 
And this makes sense; if you’ve had very few life experiences thus far, it’s easy to imagine “this is basically How The World Is; if I make a change in my life it probably won’t get any better than this.” Whereas actually, you have tried almost nothing yet and anything you’re dissatisfied with is probably fixable if you change something about the basic conditions of your life.
She also correctly tells young people to value experimentation. If you are unhappy with your circumstances, change them. Change your job or relationship status. Move. 
The exception, where you should tell them not to Do The Thing, is if you know very strong reasons why they really, really should not do this particular thing. It has to be damn strong reasons.

Goal Factoring For Relaxation Time Is Underrated
David Sedaris plagiarized the classic Kurt Vonnegut take [ https://substack.com/redirect/447fbf78-a849-4b42-9bf2-aeeb85a46eb3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ] that he goes to the post office to buy stamps because that’s what he wants to do with his day, and it generates stories and new experiences. Whereas staying inside might be more efficient, but has zero perplexity.  
The thing about this story is that it makes zero sense. The Post Office is a terribly low-perplexity way to spend that kind of time. Someone being grumpy is not peak experience on any level. Going to the post office is not living. It is not being out there.
Yes, I speak from some amount of post office experience.
It is more ‘living’ or ‘being out there’ than dropping the letter off at the hotel front desk, sure, but you could have dropped the letter off and then gone out to do something else whimsical with your afternoon. 
The steelman of this is that you could take that walk anyway, but you won’t, so buying one envelope at a time gets you into the good habit, and provides an excuse to do it. 
What I have figured out is that you can do the boring thing efficiently, and then decide to spend some of the surplus time going outside, doing relaxing and quieter things, and not directly working. So I do that, instead. Writers, both like myself and like Sedaris or Vonnegut, need some of that alone quiet time to let things ruminate, and that has to involve some amount of walking around every so often.
Thus, the better steelman is to properly value that quiet time very differently from zero. Often you will have a choice between spending 1 unit of time in a hurried way requiring unpleasant attention, or spending 2+ units of time in an unhurried way that can refresh you and provide perplexity and time to think and a chance to walk around.
We are not, contra Vonnegut, here to fart around, but doing some of that is useful, and some of that is fun. Never confuse instrumental with terminal goals. So yes, in those cases you often want to choose to spend the 2+ units of time. That transitional time can be essentially free, in that you’d need that break anyway. But make it count.
It is pretty weird to look back, and see all the silly little things that used to eat entire hours or afternoons of remarkably productive people, back before the internet, and we can say that again now for ‘back before AI.’ 
We can make an exception if you are going to the Post Office because (as per Vonnegut) you are then flirting with your ‘secret love,’ that’s totally fair, but if so ask them out already.

look I am just celebrating technological progress you’re reading too much into this

Twitter Changes Payment Programs
Twitter will be discontinuing revenue sharing [ https://substack.com/redirect/abec80b1-b7dd-4e98-960e-77c9599db795?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], and replacing it with an Original Content Rewards Program [ https://substack.com/redirect/70c077e0-aa5b-4e5a-a1f4-e1a03d572e61?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]. 
Twitter: Original content is content you have personally created – written, filmed, designed, or produced – that reflects your own voice, perspective, or expertise.
What doesn’t count is content where your contribution is minimal or absent. If the primary value comes from someone else’s work and you’ve done little to transform or add to it, it won’t generate qualified impressions under the program.
Qualified impressions means post is 50% visible, and the impression is a logged in paying user on the Home Timeline, not a repost of content by others (but there is an explicit carve-out that reposting your own content is fine), not ‘created or posted using automated means,’ not sexually explicit (boo!), without a community note. I see what they are going for here. The threshold is 500,000 such impressions over 90 days. 
We do not know how much they will be paying. Until now, payment size has been ‘chump change’ and I assume that will continue. But every little bit helps, and this seems comically well-matched to my existing content on Twitter.
I expect this to be an excellent change. Payments have been warping content towards a generic form of slop and engagement farming. This change probably improves incentives.

Minds Mostly Do Things For Reasons
Worth reflecting on, and potentially relevant to many things including AI:
Paola [ https://substack.com/redirect/df69c125-b8c3-4754-aef8-ec69d5751af2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]: Female aggression strategies tend towards being covert and plausibly deniable. I think this leads to a lot of confusion about why women often do “uptalk”, make constant hedges, speak softly, etc. The standard frame on this suggests that women are just often insecure and socially disempowered—however, I think more often than not these are learned strategies to appear like less of a threat than they actually are.
I think it’s interesting that this strategy is effective, and does probably say something undesirable about the female condition, but I think it’s important to recognize that when people are putting out signals of weakness they are often a strategy and not a bug. Organisms tend to hold enough intelligence to not engage in behaviors that systematically and predictably actually work against them.
But of course there are obvious exceptions, many of which matter a lot, where intelligent organisms such as humans really do use strategies that systematically and predictably work against them, often for reasons we understand quite well. The key fact is that there was an original good reason, even if this action is now a mistake.

The Unicontext
Agnes Callard has a new theory called The Unicontext [ https://substack.com/redirect/a7a76726-9e9e-4819-b225-e7a78af6d9cd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ].
Derek Thompson [ https://substack.com/redirect/a7a76726-9e9e-4819-b225-e7a78af6d9cd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ]: 
Here are some questions that I consider self-evidently compelling about the modern world:
Why is the news media so interested in telling you how much the world sucks all the time? 
Why are so many of us obsessed with distraction and managing our attention?
Why is it so hard to stop comparing ourselves to others? 
And why does everything in art and design seem the same these days?
A week ago, I didn’t think these questions were related. I’m not sure I would have told you I had a good answer to most of them. And I certainly wouldn’t have made the audacious and borderline bonkers claim that one single theory could begin to explain all of them, at once.
But then I had the pleasure of speaking to Agnes Callard [ https://substack.com/redirect/2e7ff9cd-3182-4fbb-9ab0-e49f4f1ed4c6?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ], the University of Chicago professor, about her new theory called “the uni-context.” It’s easily one of the most interesting conversations I’ve had all year. And once you’ve heard or read it, I think you might find it hard to think about anything else.
The theory of the unicontext is a new statement of the idea that you are being pressured to follow the same norms at all times, in all contexts, and to otherwise unify those contexts. Goodness is more context-dependent than badness, and more boring. It is easier to transfer a complaint ‘out of context,’ and it attracts attention, so everyone fixates on the details they think suck. And if something is bad out of context, such as a comment being racist, then anyone anywhere (or if offline anyone in the room) can correct it, and potentially get rewards for doing so. 
Similarly, identity becomes more important than character, because identity is constant, and transfers better out of context. And if you are comparing everything and everyone to everything and everyone, then you face impossible standards. This also supposedly plays into constantly having to fight to focus our attention, as we no longer can choose this via local cues, although I don’t think linking that in works so well. 
Agnus thinks the start of this dates back to around 1910. 
I like this restatement of similar observations from others, and advise trying to consciously resist its effects.

I think lifestyle inflation happens because ppl keep expecting there to be a point where they finally feel safe, like it’s enough. It’s always mysteriously just out of reach. But they don’t seem to draw the obvious conclusion that no amount of stuff can ever deliver that feeling.
The agent also gets a cut, which isn’t mentioned, but the NFL in particular somehow managed to cap agent fees at 3%.
Odell makes it clear at the end that no one teaches him or other players financial literacy. That’s a huge part of the problem. They don’t know how to handle it.
The other part is that the implicit social tax rates are huge. Everyone knows that you got that $100 million contract.
Buy your mama a house is great, but it does not end there. Everyone’s expectations scale with the headline number, and they don’t know about all the other drains on you or appreciate that you need to save for the rest of your life, and they all feel entitled. And your time is super valuable so no one expects you to spare any expense. 
It is actively hard.
