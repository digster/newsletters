Writers, both like myself and like Sedaris or Vonnegut, need some of that alone quiet time to let things ruminate, and that has to involve some amount of walking around every so often. Thus, the better steelman is to properly value that quiet time very differently from zero. Often you will have a choice between spending 1 unit of time in a hurried way requiring unpleasant attention, or spending 2+ units of time in an unhurried way that can refresh you and provide perplexity and time to think and a chance to walk around. We are not, contra Vonnegut, here to fart around, but doing some of that is useful, and some of that is fun. Never confuse instrumental with terminal goals. So yes, in those cases you often want to choose to spend the 2+ units of time. That transitional time can be essentially free, in that you’d need that break anyway. But make it count. The theory of the unicontext is a new statement of the idea that you are being pressured to follow the same norms at all times, in all contexts, and to otherwise unify those contexts. Goodness is more context-dependent than badness, and more boring. It is easier to transfer a complaint ‘out of context,’ and it attracts attention, so everyone fixates on the details they think suck. And if something is bad out of context, such as a comment being racist, then anyone anywhere (or if offline anyone in the room) can correct it, and potentially get rewards for doing so. Similarly, identity becomes more important than character, because identity is constant, and transfers better out of context. And if you are comparing everything and everyone to everything and everyone, then you face impossible standards. This also supposedly plays into constantly having to fight to focus our attention, as we no longer can choose this via local cues, although I don’t think linking that in works so well. Agnus thinks the start of this dates back to around 1910. I like this restatement of similar observations from others, and advise trying to consciously resist its effects.
