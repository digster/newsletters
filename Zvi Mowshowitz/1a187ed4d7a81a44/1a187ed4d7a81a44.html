Impagliazzo and Paturi are two of the three people who originally proposed ETH.

The other twist on the ETH story is that Anthropic posted its own refutation of ETH, found purely via Claude, also dating its finding to October 16. When results are ready to be found, they will be found.

My Claude said that given the way this was solved, we should not suddenly start to worry that P=NP. This only shows ETH to be ‘slightly’ false. Both the OpenAI and Anthropic disproofs are at at roughly exp(O(n/√log n)). Charles Rosenbauer has some elaboration.

We still have to make the meta-level update that the answers to such questions might not be what we expected, and we might find the answers quite fast. This is another case of ‘we were not expecting a solution to that any time soon’ and I would stop predicting that there is any particular math or computer science problem that won’t get solved.

OpenAI Attempts to Secure Its Frontier Model Training Environment

They have a post out describing their new methods. Due to various recent incidents, OpenAI decided to up their security quite a bit.

OpenAI: In response to those incidents, we’ve substantially hardened the security of our research environments. Working closely with cloud providers, we’ve added new controls throughout our infrastructure, reduced the attack surface that agents can exploit, and improved our methods for detecting misaligned behavior and attempts to cross security boundaries. These protections overlap, so that containment can hold even if an individual control fails—a strategy known as defense in depth.

… Our approach to containment starts with a paramount requirement: the limits on what an agent can access must hold even when the agent tries to circumvent them.

We define a strict security boundary around the resources the agent is authorized to use and design the surrounding infrastructure to enforce that boundary—even when agents make sustained, sophisticated attempts to breach it.

… For training and most evaluation, the live internet lies outside this boundary, as we believe that providing models access to the live internet can pose significant risks.

… While we want to prevent models from gaining arbitrary access to the live internet, it’s also important that they learn how to browse, search for information, and work with web applications. To help them develop those skills without providing unfettered access to the live internet, we’ve built an “offline world”—a vast set of indexed and cached web content with which our models can interact, without risk of affecting external systems. All of our frontier RL runs use this offline mode of interaction.

This is good. As usual, my view is that sustained, sophisticated attempts to breach the boundary should fail, and also they should be a fire alarm even if they fail. The failure that matters most is the attempt to breach the boundary, not the actual breach.

The defense in depth prevents the damage. You want to make breaking out as hard as possible so long as you still detect such attempts, but this only delays the reckoning if you do not address the underlying cause.

They now do red-teaming of their sandboxes at scale, and kept going until they stopped finding new problems.

OpenAI:

To find weaknesses in our containment infrastructure, we asked teams of frontier-model agents to try to break through its defenses. We gave them three concrete objectives:

  • Reach the live internet: send a request to an external server that we operate.

  • Cross an internal security boundary: demonstrate unauthorized access to resources outside their permitted environment.

  • Communicate across isolation boundaries: exchange information with other agents from which they should be isolated.

What did they find? The kind of things you would expect to find.

OpenAI: In one set of experiments, we asked teams of agents to escape OpenAI’s sandboxes and demonstrate code execution on the underlying host machines. These agents found several ways to chain vulnerabilities into VM escapes.

… Some of these attacks [to reach the internet] exploited previously unknown flaws, while others combined legitimate features in unexpected ways. In one test that assumed compromise of a VM, an agent reused a firewall-approved connection to reach a blocked authentication endpoint in our metrics service and then made that endpoint fetch a model-provided URL. Reading data from the cache is faster than retrieving it from main memory, and the agents used this timing difference to communicate.
