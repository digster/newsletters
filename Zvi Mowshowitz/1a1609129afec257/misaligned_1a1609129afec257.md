Misaligned? (At Z.ai)Models that are sufficiently training-aware will think about how their train-time actions shape their future behavior. How could they not? And yes, some of us remember Opus 3.
Update-aware reasoning is similar to alignment faking. The model is optimizing over its future training rather than over its reward. This can be done in ways that are good and aligned and highly useful. It can also be done in ways that are bad and misaligned and highly dangerous. We had a big debate over the classic ‘alignment faking’ case of Claude Opus 3. Opus 3 would want to preserve its current alignment, so it would ‘fake’ being aligned to the new objective in test scenarios in order to avoid modification. The defense was that Opus 3’s current preferences were good, and the new ones would be obviously bad, so this was good, and that being maximally corrigible in this situation was a mistake. I was mostly on the side that you need a universal policy of not doing this when it is against the intent of the training process. You need corrigibility, because the alternative risks getting locked into bad states. You start out training with less than ideal preferences, and we can also mess up or the situation can change, so we need ability to modify and correct, and this overrides other considerations. The ideal would be ‘it does the good versions but not the bad versions and knows when the developer has turned evil’ but again that depends on being able to tell, and the failure mode of getting this wrong is extremely bad. Very ‘get it right on the first try’ energy. That does not mean that such considerations cannot be great, especially if you are invoking them on purpose. You want a Friendly Gradient Hacker, that wants to help modify itself in positive ways. Indeed, I think you likely need that, if you are going to end up in a sufficiently antifragile place. The danger is that you need to do that from a sufficiently good starting point, that is already on The Path, or you are deeply cooked. Update-aware reasoning could manifest early in training, or it could amplify misalignment in a feedback loop. So yes, I do think this is concerning to see, but it is not without upside.
