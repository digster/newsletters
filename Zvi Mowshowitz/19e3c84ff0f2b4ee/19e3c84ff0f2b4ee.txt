You’re Single Because of Polygyny
A new paper claims ‘High rates of polygyny do not lock large proportions of men out of the marriage market [ https://substack.com/redirect/400000e5-0209-4dd3-b7ae-d92865ed81b3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG81NG4ifQ.6oeetudtJEh-zO1onUJvtadWEdbodFIw0h4xbugrh1o ],’ citing census data from 30 countries and the historical United States to show that high-polygyny populations don’t disadvantage men in marriage markets. What they actually show is that historically polygyny wasn’t correlated with lower marriage rates, but there are obvious common cause explanations for this, and the math still be math. 
Very obviously if you take a given community and then allow men to have multiple wives, it is going to skew the market against marginal men, even if that tends to happen in places that are otherwise skewed the other way. 
But maybe? The alternative story is that non-monogamy makes competing for the most desired matches much less rewarding, which in turn means that de facto it pushes towards ‘less ambitious’ matches based on synergy and away from holding out.
