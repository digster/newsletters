No, no, no. Occupational safety and chemical risks are ordinary amounts of danger, where you weigh harms against benefits. Frontier AI risks can involve mistakes that cannot be undone, including permanent loss of control over the future or very large catastrophic dangers. Even then, Under §13 the Secretary petitions a district court to restrain imminently dangerous conditions and can get a TRO without notice. This is the ‘take it down and we argue about it later’ plan with a fig leaf. Model blocking obviously needs to be a ‘take it down first and we argue about it later’ situation. Which it already is in practice, as observed with Fable 5, except in a terrible ad hoc fashion that when used the first time permanently damaged relationships with our allies and the reliability of American tech. It would be much better to have a formal mechanism, which then could be quickly challenged in court. This is especially true given that the evaluator picked by the lab likely provides the only eyes the government will have inside the lab in order to make its decisions. Anthropic has to know this. I take the potential for such abuse seriously, especially abuse of threats, but this looks like choosing to prioritize their business interests. Risk Thresholds
The risk thresholds were always a compromise and a tradeoff. The worried want the thresholds lower. Industry wants the thresholds higher. The complaints about the thresholds being too high are valid, and they were equally valid before, but previously opponents would make claims (both reasonable and unreasonable) about things that might count at lower thresholds and paint nightmare scenarios. The balance of power has changed, and we now have worked examples like Hugging Face that show us how the thresholds work in practice. Without $1 billion in damages or 50 deaths, it would not trigger anything, whereas any reasonable person accepts that this should have to have been reported. So it is disappointing to see these very high thresholds intact. Updates
I do acknowledge that halting internal use is a big improvement. Not having the ability to do that was a huge hole in previous rules. At the time, it would have been a complete non-starter, despite the correct abstract arguments in favor. Now, with Hugging Face, we have some very non-abstract reasons, and it is on the table. This still is FRONTIER with modifications in both directions. On net it is weaker. Cimino presents it as an attempt to strengthen FRONTIER, which it is not. Even if it was, why would we accept the central compromise now, when the case for acting is much stronger, and the political winds have changed, with a lame duck Congress where at least the House and probably the Senate are about to flip? Meanwhile: The Strongest Bill on the Table
Bullshit. This is not the strongest bill on the table. On reflection, is the bill actively weaker than the FRONTIER Act. It is clearly sculpted to optimize for Anthropic business interests. If you act like a normal profit maximizing business, except that your product could kill us all, then that is exactly how I will treat you. The strongest bill on the table was introduced by Senator Sanders. I am not asking Anthropic to support that bill or offer something similarly strong. I am asking them to be honest about the situation. Anthropic’s draft bill text was what actual Anthropic ‘regulatory capture’ efforts would look like in this context, if they were trying to do that. The ‘capture’ would be about preventing regulations that could impact Anthropic’s plans, including stopping the states. If you think it means anything else, then your definition falls under ‘remove Scooby Doo mask, find complaints about capitalism, then remove a second mask and find complaints about the human condition.’
In terms of preemption, the draft proposed to vest all power to deal with catastrophic and existential risks from AI exclusively in the Federal Government, and its preemption clause is stronger than the one in FRONTIER. That would be first best in theory. However, Anthropic is simultaneously refusing to trust the Federal Government to make decisions to deal with catastrophic and existential risk. We also obviously cannot trust Congress to be able to pass bills at all, let alone timely and good bills, or the White House to enforce anything consistently. On top of that, they are actively tying the hands of not only states but courts. Then, on other AI questions where preemption would be good for exactly the reasons a16z wants it, such as consumer protections and deepfakes, the bill has no preemption, and we will still face the sea of state laws that could plausibly disrupt AI diffusion for very little benefit. You cannot have it both ways. My central message is that Anthropic put out a bill that does not meet the moment and its needs, with details that actively reflect a step back, botched the execution, and Cimino regrets the botched execution but does not notice that Anthropic is not trying to meet the moment or its needs.
