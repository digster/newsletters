There is a newly leaked bill that Anthropic is purportedly shopping around is (if you assume its bugs get fixed). It is similar to the FRONTIER Act, with stronger preemption of state laws, stronger transparency provisions and weaker enforcement provisions. Having slept on this, I find it unacceptable that Anthropic is actively shopping exactly the type of bill I have warned we will need to fight against during the lame duck session. They are trying to use this last time with a Republican Congress to pass preemption of exactly the important state laws, and get some transparency but not much enforcement in exchange. This reads as intentional, that Anthropic is more afraid that the government might enforce rules against Anthropic, than they are worried about the ability of the government to enforce rules against everyone. Anthropic’s policy team seems to have, chosen protecting their business interests and guarding against their political risk, at the expense of safety considerations. There was no need for the company purportedly existing to promote safety to propose a bill that is not even actively stronger than the FRONTIER Act, when the FRONTIER Act was a compromise bill proposed back in June. Even if you were fine with what is being given up, what you get in return very obviously does not meet the moment. Nor is there any need for such compromises. It seems rather insane for Anthropic to be proposing it now. This seems like the bill that someone on the anti-regulatory side should be pushing. It is not obvious this makes us safer than doing nothing, even if the alternative is fully nothing at all levels. Say it isn't so, Anthropic, or at least that it was a mistake and is no longer so, or I am going to be rather cross. I am at a conference and cannot do an RTFB, but it feels important to get word of this out quickly, especially so Anthropic employees can look into this and ask questions.
Others RTFB (Read the Bill)I have not had the time to do so, but the other usual suspects quickly sprinted to RTFB (read the bill) and they did not like what they saw. You can try to call this a ‘pretty reasonable AI safety bill’ as Charlie Bullock does, but that is by the standards of people trying to protect the industry from more impactful regulation.
Why Would Anthropic Do This?The new Anthropic bill is weaker than their June framework, and offers both forward-looking and backward-looking preemption in exchange, despite months of movement to enable us to do more. What are we even doing?
Well, I know what it looks like Anthropic is prioritizing. The silly mistakes are a known and acceptable hazard, but there are things that really should not have made it into a bill that is being pitched to lawmakers.
Not Everything Here Is Bad But Oh BoyAs noted above, there are some good individual provisions. I like the broader coverage, that thresholds can move both ways, required risk reports and annual audits. The whistleblower provisions and security requirements seem strong. Putting automated AI R&D into the list of risks is very cool and necessary. The question is, even if you wanted to build on the FRONTIER framework, why treat this as a trade where you have to then give up other things? And why are we this deadly afraid of what the White House might do, when they clearly already can take down a model with things like export controls or otherwise order you around? Then we add a ‘kill switch’ provision that seems written mostly out of terror that it might get abused. We also get traditional Anthropic objections like ‘we don’t want to have to respond to safety incidents within 72 hours unless it is a true emergency.’ Why take out FRONTIER’s requirements for a cost-benefit analysis on catastrophic risks and substitute at best highly weak tea? The maximally charitable reading is that doing anything is a tough sell even now, and the White House wants no bill, so you have to give and take and this is as good as one can hope to do modulo fixing its bugs. Except this is backwards. The way to ‘pass nothing’ is to pass preemption, in the lame duck, in exchange for as little as possible. If the White House fails to realize this, that does not change facts on the ground. Anthropic needs to course correct, quickly. Earlier yesterday I already expressed, here at the conference I am at, that ‘what Anthropic would go for’ looks like a limiting factor in what we can achieve. And, well, here we are.
