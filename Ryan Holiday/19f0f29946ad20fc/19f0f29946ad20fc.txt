​Revolutionary Road ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/l2hehmhlm377z4s6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yZXZvbHV0aW9uYXJ5LXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTY4MGIyM2VmJl9zcz1y ) by Richard Yates
​Ok, so you’ve heard me rave about The Moviegoer ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/m2h7h5h368zz70amhq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tb3ZpZWdvZXI_X3Bvcz0zJl9zaWQ9YmE4NTUwMjU3Jl9zcz1y ) for years. I didn’t know that it narrowly beat out Revolutionary Road ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/l2hehmhlm377z4s6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yZXZvbHV0aW9uYXJ5LXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTY4MGIyM2VmJl9zcz1y ) for the National Book Award in 1962. Reading them both again this year, it struck me how similar they are. In Moviegoer ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/m2h7h5h368zz70amhq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tb3ZpZWdvZXI_X3Bvcz0zJl9zaWQ9YmE4NTUwMjU3Jl9zcz1y ), Binx is single and angsty. In Revolutionary Road ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/l2hehmhlm377z4s6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yZXZvbHV0aW9uYXJ5LXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTY4MGIyM2VmJl9zcz1y ), Frank Wheeler is married and angsty. Both are scarred by war, grappling with the emptiness of 50’s America and modernity. Both are problematic characters, but Frank much more so–certainly much more so than I remembered. I decided to re-read Revolutionary Road ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/l2hehmhlm377z4s6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yZXZvbHV0aW9uYXJ5LXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTY4MGIyM2VmJl9zcz1y ) after reading James Salter’s Light Years ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/dpheh0he90nnmmtmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9saWdodC15ZWFycz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD04YTZjZGE3MGEmX3NzPXI= ) (which I also loved). I was haunted by how dark and sad it was, how delusional and yet also how close to life–the way that when a relationship is struggling you can both fool yourself with flights of fantasies, the way that hurt people hurt people. I dunno, I feel like it’s an important book to read in your thirties or forties–as I have said about The Moviegoer ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/m2h7h5h368zz70amhq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tb3ZpZWdvZXI_X3Bvcz0zJl9zaWQ9YmE4NTUwMjU3Jl9zcz1y ), it’s sort of a second coming-of-age story but with cautionary elements. Fiction is, as always, a great mirror.

​Foster ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/e0hph7h730oo2ni8h2/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9mb3N0ZXI_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9M2NhMmI2YmZjJl9zcz1y ) by Claire Keegan
​As I’ve said, if you’re ever in a reading rut, go pick up a really short book ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/owhkhqhwmrdd7ravhr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vc2hvcnRzLzZVa1FTUXMyZXF3 ). It’s like swimming with fins on or something–look how fast I am. There is nobody better to do this with than Claire Keegan. You just rip through these books…and then the ending of each one just guts you. I loved Small Things Like These ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/z2hghnhe23xx8ebph0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zbWFsbC10aGluZ3MtbGlrZS10aGVzZT9fcG9zPTMmX3NpZD1lZThkZmNkMjMmX3NzPXI= ) (here’s why ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/p8heh9h4wzoolgiqh3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuaW5zdGFncmFtLmNvbS9yZWVsL0RZWDdKRWdoZllOLz9obD1lbg== )), I enjoyed So Late In The Day ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/x0hph6hevw00l3u5hl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zby1sYXRlLWluLXRoZS1kYXktc3Rvcmllcy1vZi13b21lbi1hbmQtbWVuP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWVlOGRmY2QyMyZfc3M9cg== ), and this one ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/e0hph7h730oo2ni8h2/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9mb3N0ZXI_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9M2NhMmI2YmZjJl9zcz1y ), I thought was the sweetest…until again, the ending. She is just a master of her craft and I’m not sure you can go wrong with any of her books (or short stories). The only reason I haven’t read them all yet is that, like I said, they are good to keep in your back pocket (literally and figuratively) until you need them.

​Don’t Call It Art: 10 Ways to Create Like a Kid Again ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/dpheh0he90nnmrimh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kb250LWNhbGwtaXQtYXJ0LTEwLXdheXMtdG8tY3JlYXRlLWxpa2UtYS1raWQtYWdhaW4tc2lnbmVkP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWZjZDRhY2MyMCZfc3M9cg== ) by Austin Kleon
​Austin Kleon told me he thought this would be my least favorite book of his. I don’t know why he said this–I love everything he does and have for years. In fact, I just had a lovely full-circle moment with my nine-year-old who has gotten really into poetry where I was telling him about Austin’s “black out” poems ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/e0hph7h730oo2mh8h2/aHR0cHM6Ly9hdXN0aW5rbGVvbi5jb20vMjAxNC8wNC8yOS9hLWJyaWVmLWhpc3Rvcnktb2YtbXktbmV3c3BhcGVyLWJsYWNrb3V0LXBvZW1zLw== ) (where he blacks out words from newspaper articles to create poetry). My son was amazed. You can do that? Yes, you can! And that’s exactly what Austin’s new book is about. It’s not just about the creative process (which he covers very well in Steal Like an Artist ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/7qh7h8h92o55mqizh6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zdGVhbC1saWtlLWFuLWFydGlzdC0xMC10aGluZ3Mtbm9ib2R5LXRvbGQteW91LWFib3V0LWJlaW5nLWNyZWF0aXZlLXNpZ25lZC1ieS1hdXRob3I_X3Bvcz01Jl9zaWQ9OWRiMjcyMjBhJl9zcz1y ) and Show Your Work ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/owhkhqhwmrdd79cvhr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zaG93LXlvdXItd29yay0xMC13YXlzLXRvLXNoYXJlLXlvdXItY3JlYXRpdml0eS1hbmQtZ2V0LWRpc2NvdmVyZWQtc2lnbmVkLWJ5LWF1c3Rpbi1rbGVvbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD05ZGIyNzIyMGEmX3NzPXI= ) and Keep Going ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/z2hghnhe23xx8xsph0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jb3B5LW9mLWtlZXAtZ29pbmctMTAtd2F5cy10by1zdGF5LWNyZWF0aXZlLWluLWdvb2QtdGltZXMtYW5kLWJhZD9fcG9zPTQmX3NpZD05ZGIyNzIyMGEmX3NzPXI= )) but a creative worldview, which children so naturally have and then we, as adults, crush in ourselves and in them. In Don’t Call It Art ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkqdro0tphwldrwk0i6h384w7/p8heh9h4wzool0hqh3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kb250LWNhbGwtaXQtYXJ0LTEwLXdheXMtdG8tY3JlYXRlLWxpa2UtYS1raWQtYWdhaW4tc2lnbmVkP19wb3M9MyZfc2lkPTlkYjI3MjIwYSZfc3M9cg== ), Kleon is drawing on kid-inspired insights to pull creatives out of a rut (and I would say to bring back your love of the game). As he does in all of his books, he communicates the essence of writing and creating art better than anyone else I can think of.
