When I graduated from high school, my aunt gave me a copy of [Man’s Search for Meaning](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/08hwh9h2redv86ul/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MTQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MjU2ODAxODE2Jl9zcz1y) by Viktor Frankl and it changed my life. I didn’t know there were books like that out there–I didn’t know there was advice like that out there, wisdom both that profound and practical, as well as hard-won. In a way, it was probably this book that set me up to be hit so hard, a few years later, by [Meditations](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/8ghqhohondlz4mtk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MTU_X3Bvcz0zJl9zaWQ9ZThjM2JhMGNjJl9zcz1y) and the Stoics. And this book has followed me since–just this week we lost the great Dr. Edith Eger (at 98) who I had gotten to know and who had studied under Viktor Frankl after she had survived Auschwitz (her book [The Choice](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/vqh3hrhor0pl5wcg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jaG9pY2UtZW1icmFjZS10aGUtcG9zc2libGU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9OGQ3MjY0ZTcyJl9zcz1y) is beautiful and powerful). I fell in love with Frankl again during the pandemic when his publisher brought out a collection of his writings called [Yes to Life: In Spite of Everything](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/l2hehmhl723egdh6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy95ZXMtdG8tbGlmZS1pbi1zcGl0ZS1vZi1ldmVyeXRoaW5nP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTZlYjlhMTQ4NSZfc3M9cg==). I am thinking about him now as I work on this book about Stockdale. My point is: The right book at the right time in someone’s life is an incredibly powerful thing. As I was trying to think about the theme for this month’s email, I thought that, with graduation season upon us–it’s the perfect time to talk about some other books that, if pressed in the right hands at the right time, could make all the difference.

[Mastery](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/e0hph7h7ov0v99c8/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDE1P19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTQzZGI1Njk5NiZfc3M9cg==) by Robert Greene
This is one of my favorite Robert Greene books. It’s also the first book I’d recommend to any young person trying to figure out what to do with this life. It’s about how to find your life’s task and realize it. It’s not going to be easy, it will take a long time–like 10,000 or 20,000 hours–but it’s a beautiful and important thing. There are so many good stories in here about apprenticeship, brilliance, and commitment that shaped Einstein, Martha Graham, Leonardo da Vinci, John Coltrane, Charles Darwin and more into the greats we know today. It’s the perfect book for anyone wrestling with what they want to do in life and where they want to go. The earlier someone reads this one in life, the better off they’ll be. I was very lucky to meet Robert at that point in my life and he helped me not only find what that task was but more importantly, showed me how to commit myself to mastering it.
[So Good They Can't Ignore You: Why Skills Trump Passion in the Quest for Work You Love](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/7qh7h8h95lolvkuz/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zby1nb29kLXRoZXktY2FudC1pZ25vcmUteW91LXdoeS1za2lsbHMtdHJ1bXAtcGFzc2lvbi1pbi10aGUtcXVlc3QtZm9yLXdvcmsteW91LWxvdmU_X3Bvcz00Jl9zaWQ9Y2QzYjQxYjI1Jl9zcz1y) by Cal Newport
There’s so much bad career advice out there. Cal Newport’s [So Good They Can’t Ignore You](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/7qh7h8h95lolvkuz/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zby1nb29kLXRoZXktY2FudC1pZ25vcmUteW91LXdoeS1za2lsbHMtdHJ1bXAtcGFzc2lvbi1pbi10aGUtcXVlc3QtZm9yLXdvcmsteW91LWxvdmU_X3Bvcz00Jl9zaWQ9Y2QzYjQxYjI1Jl9zcz1y) is not one of them. It pairs well with [Mastery](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/e0hph7h7ov0v99c8/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDE1P19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTQzZGI1Njk5NiZfc3M9cg==) because it reiterates the importance of honing in on your craft. Cal doesn’t tell you to “follow your passion” (which is usually naive), but instead argues that passion follows the act of getting so good at something that you can’t be ignored. This is also what gives you leverage in your career and lifestyle. I like to say that [this book](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/7qh7h8h95lolvkuz/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zby1nb29kLXRoZXktY2FudC1pZ25vcmUteW91LXdoeS1za2lsbHMtdHJ1bXAtcGFzc2lvbi1pbi10aGUtcXVlc3QtZm9yLXdvcmsteW91LWxvdmU_X3Bvcz00Jl9zaWQ9Y2QzYjQxYjI1Jl9zcz1y) teaches you to stop asking what the world will offer you and start focusing on what you can offer the world.
[On Character: The Choices That Define A Life](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/z2hghnhex63697ap/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9vbi1jaGFyYWN0ZXItY2hvaWNlcy10aGF0LWRlZmluZS1hLWxpZmU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NDEwNjg0YTVlJl9zcz1y) by General Stanley McChrystal
Graduating is, of course, a huge accomplishment. But this book reminds us that accomplishments and power matter little if a person does not hold themselves to high standards, if they don’t live by values–classical values like [courage](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/p8heh9h4o6z6k5tq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jb3VyYWdlLWlzLWNhbGxpbmctZm9ydHVuZS1mYXZvcnMtdGhlLWJyYXZlP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTkwMDRkNTlhNCZfc3M9cg==), [moderation](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/x0hph6he02w28lh5/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kaXNjaXBsaW5lLWlzLWRlc3RpbnktdGhlLXBvd2VyLW9mLXNlbGYtY29udHJvbC1wcmUtb3JkZXI_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTUwM2UyNDgwJl9zcz1y), [justice](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/6qheh8hler7rv2ho/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yaWdodC10aGluZy1yaWdodC1ub3ctcHJlLW9yZGVyLWRhdGVzLWFyZS10YmQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9ZTU1Mjk4NWVjJl9zcz1y), [wisdom](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/kkhmh6hnvwlwg8bl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93aXNkb20tdGFrZXMtd29yay1sZWFybi1hcHBseS1yZXBlYXQtdGhlLXN0b2ljLXZpcnR1ZXMtc2VyaWVzP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTkyM2NjMGNiNyZfc3M9ciZ2YXJpYW50PTQ2MjYwMTI2MDU2NzAx) ([someone should do a whole series on that! lol)](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/58hvh7hg2xmxpms6/aHR0cHM6Ly9zdG9yZS5kYWlseXN0b2ljLmNvbS9jb2xsZWN0aW9ucy90aGUtc3RvaWMtdmlydHVlcy1zZXJpZXM=). The ancients believed that character was fate–that character determines the trajectory of our lives. So if you want this person in your life to be successful, they need to know that it’s not just about investing in skills, but about developing their character. McChrystal’s [On Character](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/z2hghnhex63697ap/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9vbi1jaGFyYWN0ZXItY2hvaWNlcy10aGF0LWRlZmluZS1hLWxpZmU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NDEwNjg0YTVlJl9zcz1y) is a throwback to an older style of book, a meditation on a theme in the form of short essays and stories. It is also a throwback in terms of its earnestness and vulnerability. There’s no performance here. It’s not designed to get speaking gigs or consulting opportunities. It’s the thoughts of a man in the latter stages of his career and life, reflecting on what he’s learned, the mistakes he’s made (and there were some big ones!), the future he hopes to leave his children and grandchildren. [I love this book](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/z2hghnhex63697ap/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9vbi1jaGFyYWN0ZXItY2hvaWNlcy10aGF0LWRlZmluZS1hLWxpZmU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NDEwNjg0YTVlJl9zcz1y). I hope a lot of people read it and more than that, I hope more people live by it. He did a great episode of the Daily Stoic podcast last year [which you can listen to here](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/25h2hoh3w929r3h3/aHR0cHM6Ly9kYWlseXN0b2ljLmNvbS90aGUtaGFyZC10cnV0aC1hYm91dC1jaGFyYWN0ZXItZ2VuZXJhbC1zdGFubGV5LW1jY2hyeXN0YWwtcHQtMS8=).
[Memoirs of Hadrian](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/qvh8h7hdpwrwgobl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9maWN0aW9uMjI_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NzZiMzJjNjRjJl9zcz1y) by Marguerite Yourcenar
This novel takes the perspective of Emperor Hadrian speaking to his successor, Marcus Aurelius. Hadrian was a complicated figure, as all people drawn to power are. Yet somehow he managed to identify and cultivate not just one, but two heirs who were much better than he was. Hadrian’s example not only teaches us how to pass the torch to the next generation honorably, but also prepares those who are stepping forward into responsibility for the first time (in this case, adulthood). That’s where Marcus was when Hadrian first saw something in him, and this book seeks to answer how Hadrian instilled virtue in Marcus as he rose to absolute power. This is one of those novels that while not strictly true, gets at the truth in a way that only fiction can.
[Autobiography of Malcolm X](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/g3hnh5hmw9397pur/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hdXRvYmlvZ3JhcGh5LW9mLW1hbGNvbG0teD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD02MWJhYmUyMTgmX3NzPXI=) by Malcolm X
There are many great coming-of-age novels ([Catcher in the Rye](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/3ohphkh3gm7m6xtr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jYXRjaGVyLWluLXRoZS1yeWU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MDYyZWVjNGIwJl9zcz1y), [To Kill A Mockingbird](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/n2hohvhv0q3qwmb6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yaG0wMDU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9Njc2Njg5YWVjJl9zcz1y)) but I think the [Autobiography of Malcolm X](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/g3hnh5hmw9397pur/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hdXRvYmlvZ3JhcGh5LW9mLW1hbGNvbG0teD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD02MWJhYmUyMTgmX3NzPXI=) is the best coming of age biography. In 1946, Malcolm Little went to jail–he was a hardened criminal. Looking at a decade behind bars, he could have served his time simply counting the days. Instead, he started reading. He literally copied the dictionary word for word. Every minute he wasn’t in his bunk, he was in the library. That was how Malcolm Little was transformed into Malcolm X, one of the great civil rights leaders of the 20th century. He was once asked his alma mater. Books, he replied. There’s a lot to learn from his life and his choices. And then really, it’s a second coming of age novel at the end, when Malcolm X breaks with the Nation of Islam and turns away from the hatred and the conspiracy theories that had deformed his understanding of the world.
[Gates of Fire: An Epic Novel of the Battle of Thermopylae](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/48hvhehm8lrlzlcx/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXIxMT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD01NTM4MThjN2YmX3NzPXI=) by Steven Pressfield
Steven Pressfield’s Gates of Fire tells the story of the Battle of Thermopylae–the 300 Spartans who held the pass against the Persian army. What makes this book so powerful isn’t just the history, it’s the way Pressfield brings you inside the mindset of these warriors, unearthing what they believed, how they trained, how they thought about fear, discipline, brotherhood, and death. But why is it good for graduates? [This book](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/48hvhehm8lrlzlcx/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXIxMT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD01NTM4MThjN2YmX3NzPXI=) shows you what it means to dedicate your life to something bigger than yourself. You see what it means to stand your ground, even when the odds are impossible. And you realize that courage isn’t the absence of fear, but acting in spite of it. It’s a book about sacrifice. It’s about honor. It’s about what it takes to be great. And honestly, it’s one of those books that stays with you long after you finish it. I also recommend Pressfield’s [War of Art ](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/wnh2hghq67w796b7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXItb2YtYXJ0LWJyZWFrLXRocm91Z2gtdGhlLWJsb2Nrcy1hbmQtd2luLXlvdXItaW5uZXItY3JlYXRpdmUtYmF0dGxlcz9fcG9zPTImX3NpZD1lMmRlZjYyNDcmX3NzPXI=)and [Turning Pro](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/reh8hohmzd0d9ra2/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93cml0aW5nOD9fcG9zPTMmX3NpZD02ZjExMzY2ZDYmX3NzPXI=) for any young person who wants a creative life. Pressfield has also been on The Daily Stoic Podcast a handful of times, [you can check out his most recent episode here](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/08hwh9h2rede06al/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vd2F0Y2g_dj04eHpQY1ZTcW53dw==).

[A Calendar of Wisdom: Daily Thoughts to Nourish the Soul, Written and Selected from the World's Sacred Texts](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/vqh3hrhor0p02wcg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MjJkZTBkZWU1Jl9zcz1y) by Leo Tolstoy
We should all be reading every day. That doesn’t mean finishing a book every day, but reading something, anything, no matter how short, each day. That’s why I love recommending these page-a-day formats. Tolstoy’s [A Calendar of Wisdom](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/vqh3hrhor0p02wcg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MjJkZTBkZWU1Jl9zcz1y) is composed of “a wise thought for every day of the year, from the greatest philosophers of all times and all people…Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, Lao-Tzu, Buddha, Pascal.” I’ve read this one for years and still take something new out of it each day. It’s a great gift that anyone will love, but can be especially useful for a recent grad to incorporate into their new life and routines. They say youth is wasted on the young. That’s not fair, but it is hard to have a concept of time when you have so little experience with it, when you have not seen how quickly you can lose it. Which is why every young person has to read [On The Shortness Of Life](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/e0hph7h7ov0vp9a8/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MTk_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9ZTZlMThlMmU1Jl9zcz1y). Not only is it Seneca’s best work–and a great entrypoint into Stoicism–it’s also the best essay ever written on time management. It’s full of sobering and life-altering lessons. “Life isn’t short,” Seneca says, “it's that we waste a lot of it.” Or, in my favorite mind-blowing argument, he points out that death isn’t something that happens at the end of our life but throughout our lives. We are dying every minute, he says, and the time that passes belongs to death. [This book](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6vn2gzcvhqew58r0hoh45vgd/e0hph7h7ov0vp9a8/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MTk_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9ZTZlMThlMmU1Jl9zcz1y) helps you take back control of your time and your life, which is a skill that will set you apart from the majority.

A young man is born into royalty. His destiny is to inherit the throne. He doesn’t yet understand that he will have to forsake all this in order to achieve enlightenment. This book follows him as he wrestles with life’s biggest questions–why we suffer, what we’re here for, how to live an honorable life (not unlike the dilemmas Marcus Aurelius would face centuries later). It’s scholarly without being pedantic, inspiring without being mystical. Armstrong is actually a former Catholic nun (who teaches at a college of Judaism), so I loved the diverse and unique perspective. And Armstrong never misses the point of a good biography: to teach the reader how to live through the life of an interesting, complicated but important person.

I thought I’d end this email with a great passage from Emerson’s Self-Reliance, that I think encapsulates the career advice we want to give–or at least the kind of resilience young people will need to cultivate.
If our young men miscarry in their first enterprises, they lose all heart. If the young merchant fails, men say he is ruined. If the finest genius studies at one of our colleges, and is not installed in an office within one year afterwards in the cities or suburbs of Boston or New York, it seems to his friends and to himself that he is right in being disheartened, and in complaining the rest of his life. A sturdy lad from New Hampshire or Vermont, who in turn tries all the professions, who teams it, farms it, peddles, keeps a school, preaches, edits a newspaper, goes to Congress, buys a township, and so forth, in successive years, and always, like a cat, falls on his feet, is worth a hundred of these city dolls. He walks abreast with his days, and feels no shame in not `studying a profession,' for he does not postpone his life, but lives already. He has not one chance, but a hundred chances. Let a Stoic open the resources of man, and tell men they are not leaning willows, but can and must detach themselves; that with the exercise of self-trust, new powers shall appear; that a man is the word made flesh, born to shed healing to the nations, that he should be ashamed of our compassion, and that the moment he acts from himself, tossing the laws, the books, idolatries, and customs out of the window, we pity him no more, but thank and revere him, -- and that teacher shall restore the life of man to splendor, and make his name dear to all history. It is easy to see that a greater self-reliance must work a revolution in all the offices and relations of men; in their religion; in their education; in their pursuits; their modes of living; their association; in their property; in their speculative views.
