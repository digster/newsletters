​The Consolation of Philosophy (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/3ohphkh3mvqd78ar/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtY29uc29sYXRpb24tb2YtcGhpbG9zb3BoeS1wZW5ndWluLWNsYXNzaWNzP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWE3YTM2MDNhOCZfc3M9cg==
) by Boethius
​One of the frustrating parts about being mostly self-educated is
that you don’t know about so many classic texts. The amazing part
is that you are constantly discovering amazing, mind-blowing
books that you didn’t even know existed. I not only didn’t know
about Boethius’s The Consolation of Philosophy (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/3ohphkh3mvqd78ar/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtY29uc29sYXRpb24tb2YtcGhpbG9zb3BoeS1wZW5ndWluLWNsYXNzaWNzP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWE3YTM2MDNhOCZfc3M9cg==
)…I had to ask ChatGPT to tell me how to pronounce it because
I’ve never even heard the name before! By the way, it was a good
example of how use of AI requires a good bullshit detector (which
I wrote about here (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/n2hohvhvqeno3ds6/aHR0cHM6Ly9yeWFuaG9saWRheS5uZXQveW91cmUtZ29ubmEtbmVlZC10aGlzLW5vdy1tb3JlLXRoYW4tZXZlci8=
)) because it told me something that didn’t sound right, so I
found some videos of professors talking about Boethius and they
pronounced it totally differently. I showed this to ChatGPT which
immediately told me, ‘Oh yeah, you’re right, sorry.’ In any case,
in the Stoic tradition of the philosopher who is dealt a bad hand
by fate, Boethius–living in the last centuries of the Roman
empire–is brought up on false charges and sentenced to death.
Angry, scared, sad, confused, he’s not sure what to do…except
turn to philosophy. We are lucky he did…and lucky he wrote this
dialogue/poem/fable about his discourse with Lady Philosophy. I
found it very moving and a great example of what philosophy is
supposed to do–which is to help us in our darkest moments. A
related book recommendation that I sent to a friend who was in
the middle of a similarly dark experience is Oscar Wilde’s De
Profundis (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/wnh2hghq78roq5a7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kZS1wcm9mdW5kaXMtYW5kLW90aGVyLXByaXNvbi13cml0aW5ncz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD01Y2M0NjY3ZjgmX3NzPXI=
) (also written in prison). The other book I give out in such
circumstances (someone at my talk in Phoenix was in tears over
the loss of a loved one) is Seneca’s Hardships and Happiness (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/reh8hohmdnq5m3b2/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9oYXJkc2hpcC1hbmQtaGFwcGluZXNzLXRoZS1jb21wbGV0ZS13b3Jrcy1vZi1sdWNpdXMtYW5uYWV1cy1zZW5lY2E_X3Bvcz0yJl9zaWQ9ODA2NzdhYjI0Jl9zcz1y
).

​Inside the Box: How Constraints Make Us Better (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/8ghqhohodkg5opak/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pbnNpZGUtdGhlLWJveC1ob3ctY29uc3RyYWludHMtbWFrZS11cy1iZXR0ZXI_X3Bvcz03Jl9zaWQ9MTllMmEwMTc1Jl9zcz1y
) by David Epstein
​Ok, I read this book in December and have been tortured by not
being able to tell you about it. I love David Epstein’s writing.
I have raved for years about Range (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/vqh3hrho08nkodbg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDE3P19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWFkMjg3ZDc0OSZfc3M9cg==
) (which I call a parenting book in disguise) and have been
eagerly awaiting a sequel. In this one, he looks at the power of
constraints. We think we want total freedom. We think we want to
avoid obstacles and limitations. In fact, these are precisely the
things we need to be creative, successful, even happy. The book
is great, as expected, and reminds me of a famous line I’ve
quoted before (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/l2hehmhl2woklkf6/aHR0cHM6Ly9yeWFuaG9saWRheS5uZXQvZXZlcnl0aGluZy1pLWxlYXJuZWQtZnJvbS1pcm9uLW1haWRlbi1hYm91dC1saWZlLw==
) from Cleanthes, who said that the ‘fetters’ of poetry unlock
creativity (the rhythm and structure are both limiting and
generative). What you think is constraining you is actually
directing you, channeling you into being something great. I loved
this book (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/8ghqhohodkg5opak/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pbnNpZGUtdGhlLWJveC1ob3ctY29uc3RyYWludHMtbWFrZS11cy1iZXR0ZXI_X3Bvcz03Jl9zaWQ9MTllMmEwMTc1Jl9zcz1y
).

​The Committed (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/m2h7h5h35won3oam/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtY29tbWl0dGVkLXRoZS1zeW1wYXRoaXplci0yP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPThjNTIwM2MyNyZfc3M9cg==
) by Viet Thanh Nguyen
​I read The Sympathizer (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/dpheh0he6rq7e0um/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtc3ltcGF0aGl6ZXItYS1ub3ZlbC1wdWxpdHplci1wcml6ZS1mb3ItZmljdGlvbi10aGUtc3ltcGF0aGl6ZXItMT9fcG9zPTImX3NpZD0zYzQzZGRiN2QmX3NzPXI=
) earlier this year as part of my Vietnam research. I am writing
about Stockdale so I have read a lot about the American
perspective and what I loved about The Sympathizer (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/dpheh0he6rq7e0um/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtc3ltcGF0aGl6ZXItYS1ub3ZlbC1wdWxpdHplci1wcml6ZS1mb3ItZmljdGlvbi10aGUtc3ltcGF0aGl6ZXItMT9fcG9zPTImX3NpZD0zYzQzZGRiN2QmX3NzPXI=
) is that it captures the Vietnamese perspective–actually all the
perspectives because the narrator is actually a Communist double
agent serving in the South. And then he flees to America after
the war so you get the Vietnamese refugee experience, too. In The
Committed (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/m2h7h5h35won3oam/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtY29tbWl0dGVkLXRoZS1zeW1wYXRoaXplci0yP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPThjNTIwM2MyNyZfc3M9cg==
), well, it’s not totally clear what you’re getting at first
because it is a trippy, consciousness-bending book. But in time,
you find the narrator in France–giving you a very different
immigrant experience. I am interviewing Nguyen next week, and I
have so many questions. I will say, I love a good novel in the
second person and while this is not technically that, it bends
and breaks so many conventions that you really have to pay close
attention. It reminded me, in a way, of one of my favorite second
person novels, Lancelot (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/7qh7h8h9l3049wuz/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9sYW5jZWxvdD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD00OWI2MWVmNGUmX3NzPXI=
) by Walker Percy.

​I am still reeling from reading Darkness at Noon (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/p8heh9h468974rfq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kYXJrbmVzcy1hdC1ub29uLWEtbm92ZWw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MDc2YjllMzdiJl9zcz1y
) a few months ago and when I recommended it, someone wrote in to
tell me that Koestler had written a memoir I should read. In
fact, he wrote Darkness at Noon (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/p8heh9h468974rfq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kYXJrbmVzcy1hdC1ub29uLWEtbm92ZWw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MDc2YjllMzdiJl9zcz1y
) (a novel about being tortured and executed in a communist
prison) as he awaited being thrown into a French concentration
camp (where he faced execution) in the early days of WWII. And
that was after he was sentenced to death and spent three months
in prison in Málaga, Spain. The point is: Darkness at Noon (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/6qheh8hlr0p2lrfo/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kYXJrbmVzcy1hdC1ub29uLWEtbm92ZWw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9YTRhZDQ0OWJlJl9zcz1y
) was based on real darkness. This book hit me very hard given
what’s happening in the world right now–and the dangers we face
with the dogs of hate and war loose. I don’t know if Koestler
ever read War is a Racket (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/kkhmh6hnw489nvil/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXItaXMtYS1yYWNrZXQtd2FyYmxlci1jbGFzc2ljcy1hbm5vdGF0ZWQtZWRpdGlvbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1mMmUyNDI2NTcmX3NzPXI=
) (which I recommended last month), but I think he would have
liked it. He also would have liked another book I’ve raved about
and is painfully timely: Kathrine Kressmann Taylor’s Address
Unknown (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/58hvh7hgxl59g8c6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hZGRyZXNzLXVua25vd24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTI3MDNjZmEwJl9zcz1y
), which is a series of letters between two business partners
(one Jewish, one not) during the rise of Hitler in Germany. One
is slowly corrupted by the events happening around him, his heart
closing to the people and ideas he once believed in. I am reading
now about his tragic death, which echoes a third book/author I
have raved about: Stefan Zweig and his short book, Montaigne (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/25h2hoh39n7o3ph3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9iaW9ncmFwaHkxNz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0yMzdiNmIwYTYmX3NzPXI=
). In any case, if you haven’t read Darkness at Noon (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/p8heh9h468974rfq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kYXJrbmVzcy1hdC1ub29uLWEtbm92ZWw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MDc2YjllMzdiJl9zcz1y
), you must. If you want a great memoir that has tones of The
World of Yesterday (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/o8uwqdm03xhqh6904q7cvhqwnqxp2uoh45vgd/9qhzhnhdwng2dea9/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL0h0akNNcmk=
), read Scum of the Earth (
