​​Fahrenheit 451 ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/p8heh9h4pqed5euqh3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9maWN0aW9uMTA_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9ODQzZGE1Yzk1Jl9zcz1y ) by Ray Bradbury
​The only book more ironic to ban than Fahrenheit 451 ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/x0hph6he4qmzdgc5hl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9maWN0aW9uMTA_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MDQ0YTYzMDJjJl9zcz1y ) would be 1984 ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/6qheh8hl394wdvfohk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9maWN0aW9uMT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1lNWY2M2JlNmYmX3NzPXI= ) (which–surprise!–has also been banned in Florida and Colorado). But here we are. When I read Bradbury’s Fahrenheit 451 ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/p8heh9h4pqed5euqh3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9maWN0aW9uMTA_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9ODQzZGE1Yzk1Jl9zcz1y ) in high school, I took the book as a warning against totalitarian censorship by the government. It was only later, re-reading it as an adult, that I realized Bradbury—who had written the book on purchased time at a library typewriter—was depicting something much more insidious. This horrendous burning of books hadn’t been forced on them by a tyrant. Censorship was what the people wanted. They had chosen this. “Colored people don’t like Little Black Sambo,” he says, using terms that today would render the book politically incorrect, if not entirely canceled. “Burn it. White people don’t feel good about Uncle Tom’s Cabin. Burn it. Someone’s written a book on tobacco and cancer of the lungs? The cigarette people are weeping. Burn the book.” Bradbury’s message is a much more salient warning to modern Americans than many of us are ready to acknowledge upon first reading. America has always been in less danger of top-down Chinese or Soviet style suppression and much more vulnerable to short-sighted or even well-intentioned democratic censorship. I didn’t grasp this as a high schooler, but I can see it now.

​Anne Frank: The Diary of a Young Girl ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/kkhmh6hnoxpe3dblh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hbm5lLWZyYW5rLXRoZS1kaWFyeS1vZi1hLXlvdW5nLWdpcmw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NGY1NTg2ZWU2Jl9zcz1y ) by Anne Frank
​Anne Frank’s diary is one of the books on the Lieutenant Governor of Texas’s list. The whole reason Anne Frank’s father allowed her diary ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/kkhmh6hnoxpe3dblh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hbm5lLWZyYW5rLXRoZS1kaWFyeS1vZi1hLXlvdW5nLWdpcmw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NGY1NTg2ZWU2Jl9zcz1y ) to be published in the first place was so that something like the Holocaust would never happen again. If you want to keep your kids safe, you can’t hide history from them. If you want your kids to be good and decent and kind, show them the costs of a world that is not decent or kind or good. Don’t raise kids who are afraid of the world. Raise kids who know what the world is and choose to be good and decent and curious anyway.

I don’t want to spoil the plot of this book, but he needs a new butt because his has a crack in it. That’s funny, especially if you’re four or five or six or seven. My boys love the sequels too: I Broke My Butt! and My Butt Is So Noisy! Well, back in 2022, Toby Price, an assistant principal in Mississippi, read I Need a New Butt! ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/25h2hoh3qmxgegs3h4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pLW5lZWQtYS1uZXctYnV0dC1pLWJyb2tlLW15LWJ1dHQtbXktYnV0dC1pcy1zby1ub2lzeS0zLWhpbGFyaW91cy1zdG9yaWVzLWluLW9uZS1ub2lzeS1ib29rP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTMzMGEzYTlmYiZfc3M9cg== ) to a group of second graders. ​A Calendar of Wisdom: Daily Thoughts to Nourish the Soul, Written and Selected from the World's Sacred Texts ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/qvh8h7hdlk3v02ilhk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTVlNjNjYzc1Jl9zcz1y ) by Leo Tolstoy
​Sometimes seeing things happen in other countries helps you see events in your own country more clearly. Tolstoy’s page-a-day masterpiece was originally published in 1904, but was banned by the Soviet Union after the Russian Revolution because of the book’s “spiritual orientation and its number of religious quotes.” It was basically lost to history until it was published again in 1995 and sold over 300,000 copies. Tolstoy said that this book–not his novels–was his most essential work. I’ve had this book for years and I still reach for it. I’ve given it to countless Daily Stoic Podcast ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/g3hnh5hm0q2nkkfrh9/aHR0cHM6Ly9wb2RjYXN0cy5hcHBsZS5jb20vdXMvcG9kY2FzdC90aGUtZGFpbHktc3RvaWMvaWQxNDMwMzE1OTMx ) guests and to my friends when they come to town. Clearly I’m a fan of the daily-read format, seeing that I’ve written two of them (The Daily Dad ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/9qhzhnhde58xl2t9h3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtZGFpbHktZGFkLTM2Ni1tZWRpdGF0aW9ucy1vbi1wYXJlbnRpbmctbG92ZS1hbmQtcmFpc2luZy1ncmVhdC1raWRzLXByZS1vcmRlci1yZWxlYXNlLW1heS0ybmQ_X3Bvcz0yJl9zaWQ9Mzk1ZDM2MDdlJl9zcz1y ) and The Daily Stoic ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/3ohphkh3kp8rnqurhn/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtZGFpbHktc3RvaWMtMTB0aC1hbm5pdmVyc2FyeS1lZGl0aW9uLTM2Ni1tZWRpdGF0aW9ucy1vbi13aXNkb20tcGVyc2V2ZXJhbmNlLWFuZC10aGUtYXJ0LW9mLWxpdmluZz9fcG9zPTUmX3NpZD01NDNlYTU4MzgmX3NzPXI= ), which we’re celebrating the tenth anniversary of this month with this special edition ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/3ohphkh3kp8rnqurhn/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtZGFpbHktc3RvaWMtMTB0aC1hbm5pdmVyc2FyeS1lZGl0aW9uLTM2Ni1tZWRpdGF0aW9ucy1vbi13aXNkb20tcGVyc2V2ZXJhbmNlLWFuZC10aGUtYXJ0LW9mLWxpdmluZz9fcG9zPTUmX3NpZD01NDNlYTU4MzgmX3NzPXI= )!), so if you enjoy those books, you’ll love this one ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/qvh8h7hdlk3v02ilhk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTVlNjNjYzc1Jl9zcz1y ). ​The 48 Laws of Power ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/n2hohvhvl74gk3u6hg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy80OC1sYXdzLW9mLXBvd2VyLXNpZ25lZD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD02N2UyYjExNjMmX3NzPXI= ) by Robert Greene
​Not only is this one of the most shoplifted books, it’s also the most requested and most banned book in the federal prison system. A friend of mine who works on death penalty cases told me that he has to warn defendants against reading it, as prosecutors will use possession of it against them in sentencing! The irony of banning this book is that it made it that much more controversial, which he talks about in Law 6: “Court attention at all costs,” and Law 7: “Get others to do the work for you.” As I’ve said before, people mistake this book as a how-to book when they should look at it, at the very least, from a defensive standpoint. Robert is not just a kind and generous person but he is a person of integrity and decency. He didn’t write this to say ‘this is how the world should work.’ He wrote it to say ‘this is how the world works.’ There’s a darkness to this book, but there’s a darkness to this world. You have to understand it to be able to defend against it.

Scott Fitzgerald
​Perhaps you read The Great Gatsby ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/48hvhehm7x39nmaxh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtYW5ub3RhdGVkLWdyZWF0LWdhdHNieS0xMDB0aC1hbm5pdmVyc2FyeS1kZWx1eGUtZWRpdGlvbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD03YzExYTcwMGYmX3NzPXI= ) in high school, although maybe you didn’t if you grew up in South Carolina or Florida because they tried to ban it. Yes, Gatsby! One of the greatest novels ever written in English was challenged because they thought it was ‘vulgar’ and had ‘strong’ language in it. When I read about the attempts to ban Gatsby, I thought of that famous scene in Gatsby’s library, where Carraway is going through the books and notices that none of the pages had been cut. Gatsby only interacted with his idea of what books were, not what was actually in them. Which is fitting here, and unfortunately a bit ironic. Had these people ever actually opened a book? Did they not understand how powerful this book is, what it’s supposed to teach? When I was on Real Time with Bill Maher earlier this year, I told him ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/wnh2hghql0d43xc7hx/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuaW5zdGFncmFtLmNvbS9yZWVsL0RjWkc1ZEZCbF9sLw== ) Gatsby is the one book every American needs to read. If you haven’t read Gatsby in a while, I recommend picking it back up with this incredible 100th-anniversary annotated edition ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/48hvhehm7x39nmaxh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtYW5ub3RhdGVkLWdyZWF0LWdhdHNieS0xMDB0aC1hbm5pdmVyc2FyeS1kZWx1eGUtZWRpdGlvbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD03YzExYTcwMGYmX3NzPXI= ). ​Stefan Zweig is one of my favorite authors. In 1933, his work was banned in Germany and his books were publicly burned (let’s not wait for things to get that bad here). Most people know him for his book The World of Yesterday ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/08hwh9h2pl4xkqflh5/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL0VsQTBqOFc= ), which is about the Europe that was lost in the destruction of World War I, but his best work was written just before he committed suicide. Living in exile in Brazil, reeling from the censorship and closed-mindedness of his time, Zweig discovered a “dusty old edition” of the essays of Montaigne ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/8ghqhoho7r83xwukh9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zZWxmaGVscDE0P19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPThmZDNjMmVmNSZfc3M9cg== ), who himself had lived through the religious persecution and violence of his time. Montaigne writes about the certainty it takes to sentence someone to death or to suppress ideas, about the intellectual hubris of it. That’s an important thing to keep in mind when we talk about book banning. We think we know what’s appropriate. We think we know what’s right. And that tends not to age well. Zweig’s biography of Montaigne ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/reh8hohmo6p7exh2h6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tb250YWlnbmU_X3Bvcz00Jl9zaWQ9MTZkNjE5ZmQ4Jl9zcz1y ) itself wasn’t banned, but last year it almost went out of print. I bought up all 1,000 remaining copies, and thankfully the publisher agreed to another printing. We’re waiting for those copies to come in, but preorder it here ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/reh8hohmo6p7exh2h6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tb250YWlnbmU_X3Bvcz00Jl9zaWQ9MTZkNjE5ZmQ4Jl9zcz1y ) because you need to read it.

​Banning The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/vqh3hrhoqw64dlighl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtYWR2ZW50dXJlcy1vZi1odWNrbGViZXJyeS1maW5uP19wb3M9MiZfc2lkPTQ2ZDM2NGQ2MSZfc3M9cg== ) is a good example of political correctness as opposed to censorship. Huck Finn has been banned because of its harsh language and racial stereotypes–even though the book (originally published in 1885) is meant to push back against those stereotypes and against racism. Instead of banning these books, we need to contextualize them and refute them, which is exactly what Percival Everett did when he wrote James ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/l2hehmhldxp4z2h6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9qYW1lcy1hLW5vdmVsP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWEyMTc5YmU1MyZfc3M9cg== ). In James ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h982o40hlhvv5kmwdcnhnqgv8/l2hehmhldxp4z2h6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9qYW1lcy1hLW5vdmVsP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWEyMTc5YmU1MyZfc3M9cg== ), Everett retells the story of Huckleberry Finn from Jim’s perspective. His portrayal of Jim is incredible. He gives him a rich emotional and mental life that forces you to rethink the entire book.
