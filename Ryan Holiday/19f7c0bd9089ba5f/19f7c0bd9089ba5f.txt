It was a very busy June. We did a long road trip down the West Coast. I had talks in Portland and San Francisco (you can come see me in the Midwest, Australia and East Coast starting next month ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/reh8hohmg9n6m5u2h6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZGFpbHlzdG9pY2xpdmUuY29tLw== )) which we used as an excuse to fly into Seattle and meander our way down as far as Sacramento. We spent time in Bremerton, Cannon Beach, Klamath, Mendocino and San Francisco. We saw the redwoods. We saw the huge rocks off the beaches in Oregon. We saw Alcatraz. We saw Chonkers the Sea Lion. I took my kids to see the house I grew up in. We went to my grandfather’s grave.

And then after a couple weeks home, we flew to Italy where we did Rome, Pompeii, Sorrento, Capri, Ischia and Ostia. It was all lovely…it was also incredibly difficult, as traveling with kids can be. There were all sorts of airport delays. My wife was sick. My son got sick. I got food poisoning. It was literally as hot as it has ever been in Europe. Oh and in Ischia, a bunk bed collapsed on my youngest kid like that scene in Step Brothers.

Why travel? As I wrote recently ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/08hwh9h230ql2mulh5/aHR0cHM6Ly9yeWFuaG9saWRheS5uZXQvdGhpcy1pcy1hbHdheXMtd29ydGgtYWxsLXRoZS10cm91YmxlLw== ), we travel because even though it’s expensive and exhausting and sometimes even dangerous, it is one of the best ways to learn. “Travel is fatal to prejudice, bigotry, and narrow-mindedness,” Mark Twain famously wrote, “and many of our people need it sorely on these accounts.” And although my family travels in pretty privileged fashion these days, it doesn’t have to be expensive (as my friend’s book How To Travel the World on $75 a Day ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/8ghqhohomekrolbkh9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9ob3ctdG8tdHJhdmVsLXRoZS13b3JsZC1vbi03NS1hLWRheS10cmF2ZWwtY2hlYXBlci1sb25nZXItc21hcnRlcj9fcG9zPTExJl9zaWQ9YzBhZTZhNDg5Jl9zcz1y ) illustrates). So for this month’s list, I thought I’d recommend some of my favorite books about travel…one of which my wife and I discovered in a little bookstore in Rome and have not stopped raving about. Again, in the meantime, come see me ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/reh8hohmg9n6m5u2h6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZGFpbHlzdG9pY2xpdmUuY29tLw== ) on tour this summer and fall as I use my talks as an excuse to lug the family around to places they’ll probably only appreciate years from now.

​Under the Jaguar Sun ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/vqh3hrhoz28woohghl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy91bmRlci10aGUtamFndWFyLXN1bi0xP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTg5MTkyNDIxNCZfc3M9cg== ) by Italo Calvino
​This book was originally designed to be a series of essays/explorations of the five senses. The first one–on sight–however, is probably the greatest travel piece you will ever, ever read. The narrator and his travel companion, Olivia, are traveling through Oaxaca in Mexico. The story weaves in and out of their relationship and their travels, capturing the oddness of tour guides and exchanges with locals. The second essay, about the perspective of a king, is one of the most moving meditations of power and fame that I think has ever been produced. I could imagine Marcus Aurelius reading it and weeping. I had that exact thought as I walked down “the emperor’s shortcut,” a hidden, 300-yard passageway of zigzagging ramps that carried the emperor from the palace on the Palatine down to the Forum, safely separated from the streets of Rome. The last time we were in Rome–back in 2014–this wasn’t open to the public and now it is. But because of what I’d read–both from Calvino and from Meditations ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/l2hehmhl85wxl6a6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tZWRpdGF0aW9ucy1tYXJjdXMtYXVyZWxpdXMtZ3JlZ29yeS1oYXlzLXRyYW5zbGF0aW9uLXByZW1pdW0tbGVhdGhlci1lZGl0aW9uP19wb3M9NSZfc2lkPTg2YWJhNGVjZCZfc3M9cg== )–I was able to see this lonely corridor with eyes that I don’t think the rest of the tourists could. Just an incredible book–and of the only times I’ve read something and immediately needed to re-read it to make sure I got it all.

​Travels with Herodotus ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/m2h7h5h3dkw43eimhq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90cmF2ZWxzLXdpdGgtaGVyb2RvdHVzLXZpbnRhZ2UtaW50ZXJuYXRpb25hbD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD00N2ZhNTBiMDgmX3NzPXI= ) by Ryszard Kapuscinski
​Ok, so the first travel writer was not Marco Polo. In fact, he predates him by 18 centuries. The first and in some ways, the best travel writer was Herodotus, writing in 440 BC. He is known as ‘the father of history,’ but really he was a kind of tour guide of the ancient world–-traveling by boat and foot and wagon throughout the ancient Mediterranean, Middle East, and parts of Eastern Europe. It’s from Herodotus that we hear the story of the 300 Spartans ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/dpheh0helzrpertmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vd2F0Y2g_dj1JSU5LLXFvNDFqZw== ) and so many other great Greek legends and stories. I first read Herodotus in college and have come back to him many times, including two very full circle moments: the first was to tell the story of the 300 Spartans at Thermopylae, in Part III of Courage is Calling ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/e0hph7h7895r7ms8h2/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jb3VyYWdlLWlzLWNhbGxpbmctZm9ydHVuZS1mYXZvcnMtdGhlLWJyYXZl ); and the second to write an entire chapter (titled “Hit the Road”) inspired by Herodotus’ travels in Wisdom Takes Work ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/7qh7h8h9rv3n9qizh6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93aXNkb20tdGFrZXMtd29yay1sZWFybi1hcHBseS1yZXBlYXQtdGhlLXN0b2ljLXZpcnR1ZXMtc2VyaWVzP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWYwYjQ1NDM2MSZfc3M9ciZ2YXJpYW50PTQ2MjYwMTI2MDU2NzAx ). In any case, in the late 1950s, Ryszard Kapuscinski, a Polish journalist living behind the Iron Curtain, discovers Herodotus–one of the few books that hadn’t been censored or repressed in his home country. In Travels with Herodotus ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/m2h7h5h3dkw43eimhq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90cmF2ZWxzLXdpdGgtaGVyb2RvdHVzLXZpbnRhZ2UtaW50ZXJuYXRpb25hbD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD00N2ZhNTBiMDgmX3NzPXI= ), we follow Kapuscinski as he’s sent all around the world to cover foreign events–his observations and reports shaped by his study of the Greek historiographer. It’s a lovely meditation not just on the ecstasies of travel, but the perennial foibles of humanity–of which Herodotus was our greatest observer. (Here’s me with Herodotus in Rome.)

I’ll also include my favorite Greek travel book (albeit with a horrible title), Henry Miller’s The Colossus of Maroussi ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/owhkhqhw235qw9hvhr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtY29sb3NzdXMtb2YtbWFyb3Vzc2ktbmV3LWRpcmVjdGlvbnMtcGFwZXJib29rP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWJhMDc3ZWRlZiZfc3M9cg== ). It follows Miller–then an infamous but still struggling writer–on his 1939 trip to Greece to visit some friends. What ensues is a rapturous, incredible travel memoir that holds up remarkably well 86 years later. Definitely read it ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/owhkhqhw235qw9hvhr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtY29sb3NzdXMtb2YtbWFyb3Vzc2ktbmV3LWRpcmVjdGlvbnMtcGFwZXJib29rP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWJhMDc3ZWRlZiZfc3M9cg== ), whether you’re interested in going to Greece or not.

​Ghost Rider: Travels on the Healing Road ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/z2hghnheq90lexbph0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9naG9zdC1yaWRlci10cmF2ZWxzLW9uLXRoZS1oZWFsaW5nLXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9OGQxNzNkNzY4Jl9zcz1y ) by Neil Peart
​Speaking of road trips, this book is an all-timer. I first read Ghost Rider ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/z2hghnheq90lexbph0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9naG9zdC1yaWRlci10cmF2ZWxzLW9uLXRoZS1oZWFsaW5nLXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9OGQxNzNkNzY4Jl9zcz1y ) on a Kindle in May 2010, but it wasn’t until I read it again–nearly 15 years later–that I could fully appreciate its greatness. Neil Peart, probably the greatest drummer ever (of the band Rush), lost his 19-year-old daughter and then his wife 10 months apart. A car accident and cancer shattered his life… so he hit the road on a motorcycle, trying to preserve his “small baby soul.” His 55,000-mile journey by motorcycle took him across much of North America, down through Mexico to Belize, and back again. As David McCullough detailed in his book Mornings on Horseback ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/p8heh9h4nk8q40tqh3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tb3JuaW5ncy1vbi1ob3JzZWJhY2stdGhlLXN0b3J5LW9mLWFuLWV4dHJhb3JkaW5hcnktZmFtaWx5LWEtdmFuaXNoZWQtd2F5LW9mLWxpZmUtYW5kLXRoZS11bmlxdWUtY2hpbGQtd2hvLWJlY2FtZS10aGVvZG9yZS1yb29zZXZlbHQ= ), when Theodore Roosevelt lost his wife and mother in the same house on the same day, he did something very similar. “Black care,” Roosevelt said, “rarely sits behind a rider whose pace is fast enough.” Ghost Rider ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/z2hghnheq90lexbph0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9naG9zdC1yaWRlci10cmF2ZWxzLW9uLXRoZS1oZWFsaW5nLXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9OGQxNzNkNzY4Jl9zcz1y ) hit me very differently now that I have a family of my own, and because I now know that after Peart picked up the pieces of his life, he would be struck down by cancer in 2020. What a life and what a talent, though. I saw Rush on their 30th-anniversary tour in high school. I was very lucky to witness his brilliance in person.

​In A Sunburned Country ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/x0hph6he58xqevu5hl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pbi1hLXN1bmJ1cm5lZC1jb3VudHJ5P19wb3M9MyZfc2lkPTc4NDQzZjYxYSZfc3M9cg== ) by Bill Bryson
​You can’t write about travel books without mentioning Bill Bryson. Bill Bryson’s In A Sunburned Country ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/6qheh8hlmv09lmaohk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pbi1hLXN1bmJ1cm5lZC1jb3VudHJ5 ) is not only one of the greatest travel books ever written, but it’s also the funniest. And I just love Australia so much. We were there two summers ago for our kid’s first trip and spent a month. We’re going back in October for my talks (and New Zealand as well) ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/reh8hohmg9n6m5u2h6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZGFpbHlzdG9pY2xpdmUuY29tLw== ). Bryson captures the vastness of Australia, the absurdity of it, the beauty of it…and of course, what the distance of it–how far it is from the rest of the West–and what that perspective gives you. My favorite passage in this book (which was particularly beneficial being there in the runup to the 2024 election) is worth reading in its entirety. “It always amazes me how seldom visitors bother with local paper,” he writes, “I personally can think of nothing more exciting, certainly nothing you could do in a public place with a cup of coffee than to read newspapers from a part of the world you know almost nothing about. What a comfort it is to find a nation preoccupied by matters of no possible consequence to oneself. I love reading about scandals involving ministers of whom I have never heard, murder hunts in communities whose names sound dusty and remote, features on revered artists and thinkers whose achievements have never reached my ears, whose talents I must take on faith.” His other books are, of course, just as amazing. I recommend A Walk In the Woods ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/kkhmh6hnkg4xnxclh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hLXdhbGstaW4tdGhlLXdvb2RzLXJlZGlzY292ZXJpbmctYW1lcmljYS1vbi10aGUtYXBwYWxhY2hpYW4tdHJhaWwtb2ZmaWNpYWwtZ3VpZGVzLXRvLXRoZS1hcHBhbGFjaGlhbi10cmFpbA== ), which is about the Appalachian Trail and Notes from a Small Island ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/58hvh7hgrpl6g4a6h4/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL0NpekI= ), about the United Kingdom.

​South and West: From a Notebook ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/25h2hoh3krnm3xt3h4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zb3V0aC1hbmQtd2VzdC1mcm9tLWEtbm90ZWJvb2s_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MzUxYzgwMzRkJl9zcz1y ) by Joan Didion
​This is another great road trip book through the American South (Louisiana, Mississippi, and Alabama) that, despite being from an experience over 40 years ago, holds up extremely well. My favorite observation of hers is how in the South, the Civil War feels very recent…but 1960 is made to seem like it was an eternity ago. You can pair it with a few other classics from the region: A Journey Through Texas: Or a Saddle-Trip on the Southwestern Frontier ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/qvh8h7hd9gqkdxclhk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90ZXhhczg_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9ODE4MDIzYjMyJl9zcz1y ) by Frederick Law (1857) and Olmsted and Spying on the South: An Odyssey Across the American Divide ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/g3hnh5hm47xq67urh9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zcHlpbmctb24tdGhlLXNvdXRoLWFuLW9keXNzZXktYWNyb3NzLXRoZS1hbWVyaWNhbi1kaXZpZGU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NDY5MTJkYTM3Jl9zcz1y ) by Tony Horwitz (2019). In 1856, Olmsted (of Central Park fame) took a 2,000-mile roundtrip journey across the state of Texas on horseback and did some of the best travel and historical writing in American history (especially important since this was all on the eve of the Civil War.) There’s a great little passage where people are complaining that Austin is getting too expensive. And that was back in 1857! The journalist Tony Horwitz recreated Olmsted’s exact journey in 2016 and wrote an entire book about it. Not only that, but it was the last thing he worked on before suddenly dying of a heart attack at just 60-years-old. WHAT? Horwitz does a much wider swath of territory, too. He travels through Appalachia, down the Mississippi River, into bayou Louisiana, and across Texas to the contested Mexican borderland. Both are readable on their own, but sometimes it’s helpful to have a modern popularizer kick things off. Olmsted believed every American needed to understand the cultural and economic forces pulsating through the South in the 1850s, and Horwitz makes it clear how and why this is still true in our time.

​The Warmth of Other Suns: The Epic Story of America's Great Migration ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/9qhzhnhdokn5zqa9h3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtd2FybXRoLW9mLW90aGVyLXN1bnMtdGhlLWVwaWMtc3Rvcnktb2YtYW1lcmljYXMtZ3JlYXQtbWlncmF0aW9uP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWJiZmZhZGUxZiZfc3M9cg== ) by Isabel Wilkerson
​This isn’t a travel book in the traditional sense, but it’s worth mentioning here because it’s a beautiful, painful and eye-opening look at the greatest migration journey in American history. The Warmth of Other Suns ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/9qhzhnhdokn5zqa9h3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtd2FybXRoLW9mLW90aGVyLXN1bnMtdGhlLWVwaWMtc3Rvcnktb2YtYW1lcmljYXMtZ3JlYXQtbWlncmF0aW9uP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWJiZmZhZGUxZiZfc3M9cg== ) looks at the Great Migration through the biographical sketches of individuals who left the Jim Crow South for a chance at a better life in California, in Chicago, in New York City. This book is just an absolutely epic masterpiece that everyone needs to read. I have to also mention Wilkerson’s other book, Caste ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/3ohphkh3e6vp42brhn/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jYXN0ZS10aGUtb3JpZ2lucy1vZi1vdXItZGlzY29udGVudHM_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NGM0YWFjNGJlJl9zcz1y ), which–while not a travel book–is another must-read. Here’s a little video of my raving about it ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/n2hohvhvzwe76gh6hg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vd2F0Y2g_dj1qYzQ2SnlvS3hUUQ== ) on Morning Joe recently.

​Travels with Charley in Search of America ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/48hvhehm4zkx6qcxh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90cmF2ZWxzLXdpdGgtY2hhcmxleS1pbi1zZWFyY2gtb2YtYW1lcmljYT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0xN2U4ZTBjM2YmX3NzPXI= ) by John Steinbeck
​Steinbeck wrote this book after he had already become one of America’s most celebrated writers, and after winning the Nobel Prize. At age 58, Steinbeck realized he no longer knew the country he wrote about, so he bought a pickup truck, packed up his poodle Charley, and headed out to rediscover America. They didn’t set out for popular landmarks or bustling cities; the two visit small towns, diners, forests, and farms. This book ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/lmup45xwvobmhnx0vp0a6h8dr5n88cgh2opx4/48hvhehm4zkx6qcxh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90cmF2ZWxzLXdpdGgtY2hhcmxleS1pbi1zZWFyY2gtb2YtYW1lcmljYT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0xN2U4ZTBjM2YmX3NzPXI= ) isn’t just entertaining—it will change how you move through the world. Leave your phone at home, take the long way, strike up a conversation with a stranger, and get reacquainted with a place you thought you couldn’t learn more about.
