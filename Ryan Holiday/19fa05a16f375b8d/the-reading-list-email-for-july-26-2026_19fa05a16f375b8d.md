[Close to Shore: The Terrifying Shark Attacks of 1916](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/x0hph6he5l4g32c5hl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jbG9zZS10by1zaG9yZS10aGUtdGVycmlmeWluZy1zaGFyay1hdHRhY2tzLW9mLTE5MTY_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NjJjOGNhY2QzJl9zcz1y) by Michael Capuzzo
I think I finally did it–found a book that I can rave about like I have with The Tiger and Night of the Grizzlies! And this one could not have come from more absurd circumstances. My youngest son picked out a book about shark attacks at a bookstore (it was one of what we call the “big head” books in our family, titled [What Were the 1916 Shark Attacks?](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/6qheh8hlmd3qoeuohk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93aGF0LXdlcmUtdGhlLXNoYXJrLWF0dGFja3Mtb2YtMTkxNj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1lMzcyNWIxZTAmX3NzPXI=)). My wife read it to him and then–and I mean this in the most loving way possible–she just would not shut up about it. She was telling everyone she talked to about this series of shark attacks in 1916 as if she’d read a real book about it and not something meant for children! Thankfully somebody pointed us to Capuzzo’s book which is not just for adults but features some absolutely incredible writing (I do not make the comparisons to [The Tiger](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/kkhmh6hnk3o7mqtlh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9ub25maWN0aW9uMTc_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MzkwYjY5Zjc2Jl9zcz1y) or [Night of the Grizzlies](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/58hvh7hgrz4e7dh6h4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9uaWdodC1vZi10aGUtZ3JpenpsaWVzP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTY2OGM4NmI3MSZfc3M9cg==) lightly but it’s that good). The reason Samantha was hooked even from a kid’s book version is that these shark attacks were insane (a rogue Great White killed four people on the coast of the Jersey shore over a 12-day span, the first known attacks on swimmers in the U.S.)–and the characters are unreal (some millionaire friend of Theodore Roosevelt would jump off his yacht and swim with sharks, convinced they were harmless). The original subtitle of the book was “A True Story of Terror in an Age of Innocence” which, I think, better gets to the essence of what makes this book so great. At the turn of the century, people were just coming out of the Victorian era and rediscovering the beach and swimming. They simply did not believe that sharks killed people…just as they could not conceive of the horrors of the First World War which was just then beginning. This book gives you not just an incredible story but really puts you inside a world that no longer exists. [Under the Jaguar Sun](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/9qhzhnhdole798u9h3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy91bmRlci10aGUtamFndWFyLXN1bj9fcG9zPTImX3NpZD00NTFhZGViMzgmX3NzPXI=) by Italo Calvino
It’s unreal how good this little book is and that’s why I am recommending it again despite talking about it just last week. It was originally designed to be a series of essays/explorations of the five senses. The first one–on sight–is probably the greatest travel piece you will ever, ever read. The second essay, about the perspective of a king, is one of the most moving meditations of power and fame that I think has ever been produced. I could imagine Marcus Aurelius reading it and weeping. I had that exact thought as I walked down “the emperor’s shortcut,” a hidden, 300-yard passageway of zigzagging ramps that carried the emperor from the palace on the Palatine down to the Forum, safely separated from the streets of Rome. The last time we were in Rome–back in 2014–this wasn’t open to the public and now it is. But because of what I’d read–both from Calvino and from [Meditations](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/3ohphkh3enkwzwbrhn/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tZWRpdGF0aW9ucy1tYXJjdXMtYXVyZWxpdXMtZ3JlZ29yeS1oYXlzLXRyYW5zbGF0aW9uLXByZW1pdW0tbGVhdGhlci1lZGl0aW9uP19wb3M9NSZfc2lkPTg2YWJhNGVjZCZfc3M9cg==)–I was able to see this lonely corridor with eyes that I don’t think the rest of the tourists could. Just an incredible book–and one of the only times I’ve read something and immediately needed to reread it to make sure I got it all.
[The Time of Cherries](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/n2hohvhvzkldewu6hg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtdGltZS1vZi1jaGVycmllcy1hLW5vdmVsLW1vZGVybi1saWJyYXJ5LXRvcmNoYmVhcmVycz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0xNjEyYWE5ZTgmX3NzPXI=) by Montserrat Roig
In one of Admiral Stavridis’ books–I forget which one but I liked [Sailing True North](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/48hvhehm4n7ok5hxh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zYWlsaW5nLXRydWUtbm9ydGg_X3Bvcz0yJl9zaWQ9Y2RmNTZkMzQ3Jl9zcz1y) and [To Risk it All](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/wnh2hghqe3lz8ki7hx/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90by1yaXNrLWl0LWFsbC1uaW5lLWNvbmZsaWN0cy1hbmQtdGhlLWNydWNpYmxlLW9mLWRlY2lzaW9uP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWNkZjU2ZDM0NyZfc3M9cg==)–he talked about how as the Supreme Allied Commander of NATO, he tried to read one novel from each of the 28 countries in the alliance at the time. I’m not exactly trying to do the same thing but it did spur me to try to read more international fiction. I read [You Be Mother](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/reh8hohmgeo8n7i2h6/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL1dSdGlmc3c=) from Meg Mason in Australia a few months ago. I’ve been reading Claire Keegan (see below). I loved Italo Calvino’s book (see above). When I heard Anne Applebaum recommend [The Time of Cherries](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/08hwh9h23kp8qwulh5/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtdGltZS1vZi1jaGVycmllcy1hLW5vdmVsLW1vZGVybi1saWJyYXJ5LXRvcmNoYmVhcmVycz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD02YzJhMDM0YjQmX3NzPXI=) (set at the end of Franco’s reign), I thought it sounded like the right book for me because it checked not only my international fiction box but my “The best way to understand the present is to look at an analogous moment in the past.” How does a society accommodate itself to fascism? (rationalization, busyness) What does it do to your soul? (degrades) How does ordinary, daily life change (not as much as you think). The author, Montserrat Roig, was a Catalan writer, activist and journalist who grew up under Franco's dictatorship and spent her career exploring the moral compromises and injustices of the era.
[The Forester's Daughter](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/8ghqhohomx74kxbkh9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YWxrLXRoZS1ibHVlLWZpZWxkcy1zdG9yaWVzP19wb3M9MyZfc2lkPTFlNzJlZGVjNiZfc3M9cg==) by Claire Keegan

You’ll notice the link above does not go to The Forester’s Daughter, but to her collection [Walk the Blue Fields](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/8ghqhohomx74kxbkh9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YWxrLXRoZS1ibHVlLWZpZWxkcy1zdG9yaWVzP19wb3M9MyZfc2lkPTFlNzJlZGVjNiZfc3M9cg==). I also love [So Late in the Day](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/l2hehmhl8zdgwoa6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zby1sYXRlLWluLXRoZS1kYXktc3Rvcmllcy1vZi13b21lbi1hbmQtbWVuP19wb3M9MiZfc2lkPTQ2MGRjNGMxYSZfc3M9cg==) and [Foster](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/m2h7h5h3d7lrw8bmhq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9mb3N0ZXI_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9YjQ4ZGE4ZGJjJl9zcz1y). [My Reading Life](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/dpheh0helm23rehmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9teS1yZWFkaW5nLWxpZmU_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9OGMxYjdmZmI2Jl9zcz1y) by Pat Conroy
When I flew from Atlanta to San Francisco in March to do my chat with General Mattis on Firing Line ([you can listen here](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/e0hph7h782dx5qi8h2/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vd2F0Y2g_dj0zWXJzN2FNVWk1QQ==)), a lady was reading this a couple of seats over. I’m not saying that’s how I find out about a lot of the books I read but it’s been more than a few. I emailed the manager of The Painted Porch and it was waiting for me when I got home. The book reminded me of another very similar book, Louis L’Amour’s [Education of a Wandering Man](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/7qh7h8h9rmxg3zizh6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9tZW1vaXI4P19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTQ3MWY5MjYzMSZfc3M9cg==). (It also reminded me to reread [The Great Santini](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/0vu90rq6zpu9h9lmvonulhvw0pgn2unhnqgv8/owhkhqhw270z55avhr/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL2hVc2ZtNA==)) The stories are lovely and inspire you to read more. Pat Conroy’s reading life covered everything from Milton to Tolkien, Philip Roth to Thucydides. He read poetry, he read history, he read philosophy. It’s a perfect reminder that reading is more than just something to do for pleasure. Every time you pick up a book, you take something from it. A lesson on how to live, how to learn, how to understand the past and prepare for the future, how to be a better person.
