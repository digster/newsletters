​The Path Between the Seas: The Creation of the Panama Canal, 1870-1914 ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/vqh3hrho6pwkn3ughl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtcGF0aC1iZXR3ZWVuLXRoZS1zZWFzLXRoZS1jcmVhdGlvbi1vZi10aGUtcGFuYW1hLWNhbmFsLTE4NzAtMTkxND9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1jMjg2NzNlZmImX3NzPXI= ) by David McCullough
​Last time I flew to Australia, I packed one book–knowing it would last me through the flight and most of the trip: Doris Kearns Goodwin’s Team of Rivals ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/l2hehmhlp3xkozi6h0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90ZWFtLW9mLXJpdmFscy10aGUtcG9saXRpY2FsLWdlbml1cy1vZi1hYnJhaGFtLWxpbmNvbG4_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9YWRkZjIwNmU3Jl9zcz1y ). This time, I took another big, thick masterpiece with me: McCullough’s book on the building of the Panama Canal ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/vqh3hrho6pwkn3ughl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtcGF0aC1iZXR3ZWVuLXRoZS1zZWFzLXRoZS1jcmVhdGlvbi1vZi10aGUtcGFuYW1hLWNhbmFsLTE4NzAtMTkxND9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1jMjg2NzNlZmImX3NzPXI= ). I’ve always meant to read it and finally decided to do it. Honestly, the book itself is a similar engineering feat–how McCullough manages to tell the 44-year story of one of the most incredible works in the history of civilization, as well as the financial and political machinations behind it. It’s riveting. It’s amazing. I finished it faster than I thought, and never once resented having to carry it around. I emailed Derek Thompson after finishing it to tell him that it made me think of his book Abundance ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/m2h7h5h3x84nonfmhq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hYnVuZGFuY2U_X3Bvcz0yJl9zaWQ9Yzc1NjI4NjE3Jl9zcz1y ) (which everyone should read, also here’s my interview with Ezra Klein ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/dpheh0hex0p7qqfmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vd2F0Y2g_dj1PYW9kUGtyNDhxZw== ) about it). It made me think of it because on the one hand, the story of the Panama Canal is about how to do really hard things–how to conquer nature and technology problems to make the world better (one of the most fascinating parts of the book is the advances in medicine to combat yellow fever and malaria since that was impeding construction). On the other hand, it’s also a story of corruption and destruction and outright theft. Many of the laws and regulations we have today are there in response to the kind of brazen bulldozing–literally and figuratively–that is the dark legacy of the canal. But these regulations also create the kind of red tape and learned helplessness that make so many necessary building projects impossible. Anyway, great book and a book with serious impact. Jimmy Carter read it and that contributed to the Panama Canal Treaty which restored the canal back to the country of Panama.

​Stone Yard Devotional ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/e0hph7h7z0rnk0b8h2/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zdG9uZS15YXJkLWRldm90aW9uYWw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9YjVlMDRjYTRhJl9zcz1y ) by Charlotte Wood
​I started this book before I left for Australia and I was just completely blown away by it–I don’t think I’ve read a book this beautiful since Anne Morrow Lindbergh’s Gift from the Sea ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/7qh7h8h9eon409szh6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9naWZ0LWZyb20tdGhlLXNlYS03MHRoLWFubml2ZXJzYXJ5LWVkaXRpb24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NzYxNjQ5OTdjJl9zcz1y ). Honestly, I thought it was a memoir at first, but it’s actually a novel. It’s the story of a woman, burned out from her job and despairing at the future, who in the depths of the pandemic goes to live at a convent in Monaro, Australia. There she battles not just the pandemic we all lived through but a plague of mice, her own childhood guilt and grief, and the whirling distractions and turmoil that prevent all of us from experiencing even momentary bits of stillness. I ended up quoting this book onstage at nearly all of my stops in Australia–not just because it was written by a homegrown author–but because it’s one of the most incredible paragraphs I’ve ever read on what the Stoics would call ataraxia. “I lifted the sheet with two hands and it billowed slowly,” she writes of making her bed. "I picked up one of the pillows from the floor and placed it back on the bed, smoothed the sheet down to make a flat, empty expanse. I stood looking at the bed and breathing. It isn't something I ever told anyone—how could you say this?—but the lift and descent of that sheet, the air inside it, the peace when it settled, showed me what I wanted. I knew it in that moment, but it took years to find it." I want that, too. I’m guessing you do, too.

​The Emerald Mile: The Epic Story of the Fastest Ride in History Through the Heart of the Grand Canyon ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/owhkhqhwerq84gbvhr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtZW1lcmFsZC1taWxlLXRoZS1lcGljLXN0b3J5LW9mLXRoZS1mYXN0ZXN0LXJpZGUtaW4taGlzdG9yeS10aHJvdWdoLXRoZS1oZWFydC1vZi10aGUtZ3JhbmQtY2FueW9uP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTUwY2U2M2IyYiZfc3M9cg== ) by Kevin Fedarko
​Ok fine, I must credit Samantha with finding an utterly world-class, totally captivating, one-of-the-best-I’ve-ever-read books of narrative nonfiction. Again. She found The Art Thief ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/z2hghnhez3ldowaph0/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtYXJ0LXRoaWVmLWEtdHJ1ZS1zdG9yeS1vZi1sb3ZlLWNyaW1lLWFuZC1hLWRhbmdlcm91cy1vYnNlc3Npb24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTc0NGMxNDIwJl9zcz1y ). She found Tunnel 29 ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/p8heh9h4ezq798uqh3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90dW5uZWwtMjktdGhlLXRydWUtc3Rvcnktb2YtYW4tZXh0cmFvcmRpbmFyeS1lc2NhcGUtYmVuZWF0aC10aGUtYmVybGluLXdhbGw_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MmYwNjFiZTFlJl9zcz1y ). In a weird, indirect way, she found Close to Shore ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/6qheh8hl4792pziohk/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9jbG9zZS10by1zaG9yZS10aGUtdGVycmlmeWluZy1zaGFyay1hdHRhY2tzLW9mLTE5MTY_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9YmFiMWNmNTg1Jl9zcz1y ) about the New Jersey shark attacks. This, I don’t know how she found it, but oh man. On one level, it’s the riveting story of the fastest trip ever down the Grand Canyon and that alone is really good. But the circumstances as to how that trip became possible—277 miles in 37 hours–put this incredible ticking clock on the story and allows Fedarko to tell the story of the efforts to dam and control both the Colorado and all the other water across the Southwest. Like McCullough ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/kkhmh6hnplx98wulh7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtcGF0aC1iZXR3ZWVuLXRoZS1zZWFzLXRoZS1jcmVhdGlvbi1vZi10aGUtcGFuYW1hLWNhbmFsLTE4NzAtMTkxND9fcG9zPTcmX3NpZD0xN2Y4MzU2N2YmX3NzPXI= ), he makes the science and the engineering of all this readable and fascinating. He gets into the geology and the hydrology…as well as an absolutely crazy cast of characters. Seriously, this book is on par with anything Michael Lewis or Jon Krakauer. Must read.

​Diary of a Man in Despair ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/58hvh7hgwm6952f6h4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9kaWFyeS1vZi1hLW1hbi1pbi1kZXNwYWlyLW5ldy15b3JrLXJldmlldy1ib29rcy1jbGFzc2ljcz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1jZjc3ZDQ2NzQmX3NzPXI= ) by Friedrich Reck
​As I have said here–never enough times though–the best way to understand current events is by reading about the past. I wish I’d read this book earlier. It’s the hidden diary of a German writer who documented life in Nazi Germany from 1936 to 1944, detailing his disgust for Hitler and the Nazis. There are so many great lines in this book but the best ones have to do with him just shaking his head at the thuggery, the obvious incompetence, the shamelessness of the people who had taken over his country. A bit relevant today, no? I’ve raved about Address Unknown ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/25h2hoh3x2mo7zc3h4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hZGRyZXNzLXVua25vd24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9OTIwYTYzM2RiJl9zcz1y ) many times–this is like the real-life version of that. Reck knew how potent what he was writing was too, he kept it hidden in a tin box in the woods on his property in Bavaria and frequently changed its hiding spot so the Nazis wouldn’t find it. And in the end, at some level, the Nazis knew he had to be dealt with as well. In the waning days of the war, Reck was arrested and taken to Dachau concentration camp, where he died in 1945. It’s tragic and heartbreaking–a powerful cautionary tale for all societies, but especially ours. It made me think of a famous soldier who was put to death near the end of Nero’s reign. Standing over the grave he was about to be dispatched into, the man looked down, shook his head with disgust. “Ugh,” he said, “even this is not up to code.”

I thought about it a lot the last month ( https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/mvue04dxqzs5hqrrnw8fmhrrp80r7c3hervk9/g3hnh5hm23qze4arh9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuaW5zdGFncmFtLmNvbS9wL0Rlbm1PeFpEUV9sLz9obD1lbiZpbWdfaW5kZXg9MQ== )–swimming in Icebergs pool as the waves crashed over the side, riding down an underground river in a cave lit up by glowworms, seeing hundreds and hundreds of penguins waddle up from the ocean like clockwork as the sun set behind them, an early misty run through Waverley Cemetery as a full rainbow set overhead…this is what Robert calls the sublime. And he wrote a whole book about how to seek it out, cultivate it, and experience it on all these levels we take for granted. Most of Robert’s books will help you succeed…but this one will help you live a better life.
