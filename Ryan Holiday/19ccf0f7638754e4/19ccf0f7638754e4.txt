Ok, so I have a secret that I use when I am in a reading rut: I
read something really short. I love the feeling of sitting down
and finishing a book…in that sitting. I get the whole rush of
cracking a new book and getting all the way through it in
compressed form. On a plane, in bed, on a long car ride, an
afternoon at the beach–those short (but wonderful) books get me
excited about reading again. I know I’m not the only one with
this feeling because lately at The Painted Porch, I’ve been
highlighting some books that I raced through and the videos have
done millions of views (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/7qh7h8h9qn9ezeaz/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuaW5zdGFncmFtLmNvbS9wL0RWWks1cFlnZjlOLw==
). They’ve also flooded me with a bunch of recommendations for
books that I want to re-read, or that I hadn’t even heard of and
now need to check out.

So, with that in mind, here are some short books that you can
(more or less) finish in one sitting. Enjoy!

​On the Shortness of Life (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/owhkhqhwpqweozav/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MTk_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MjM0MzI4YmFiJl9zcz1y
) by Seneca (105 Pages)
​If you only read one thing from the Stoics, let it be Seneca’s
essay On the Shortness of Life (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/owhkhqhwpqweozav/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MTk_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MjM0MzI4YmFiJl9zcz1y
). In it, he makes one of the most important arguments of all
time: “It’s not that life is short, but that we waste a lot of
it.” Or, in my favorite mind-blowing argument, he points out that
death isn’t something that happens at the end of our life but
throughout our lives. We are dying every minute, he says, and the
time that passes belongs to death. Intellectually, we all
understand that we need to value our time and avoid wasting it at
all costs, but how many of us can actually say they truly live?
This book will help you take back control of your life and reach
your full potential. It’s a short little collection put together
by Penguin’s Great Ideas series and contains three of Seneca’s
best pieces, including: On the Shortness of Life, Consolation to
Helvia, and On Tranquility of Mind. If you haven’t picked this
one up, you absolutely must (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/owhkhqhwpqweozav/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MTk_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MjM0MzI4YmFiJl9zcz1y
). And if you’ve already read it…read it again!

​Small Things Like These (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/z2hghnhenlez09sp/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zbWFsbC10aGluZ3MtbGlrZS10aGVzZT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0wY2NiYmRjNjImX3NzPXI=
) by Claire Keegan (128 Pages)
​My wife Samantha told me I’d be shocked by this book and boy was
she right. Small Things Like These (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/p8heh9h4mq4e83tq/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zbWFsbC10aGluZ3MtbGlrZS10aGVzZT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1mMzZmYmYwNzQmX3NzPXI=
) is set in Ireland and it starts off slow and simple…and then
holy shit. I don’t want to spoil the ending, but in a less
extreme way, it reminded me of that scene in The Road (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/x0hph6heoqemxpb5/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yb2FkP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPWMxYWY3YTVmOCZfc3M9cg==
) (also an amazing novel) where they descend into that cellar and
find it’s a dungeon and they’re eating the prisoners! Anyway, to
me, the most haunting question of the book is when the husband is
talking to his wife about what he suspects and she tells him they
have their own children to worry about. “But what if it was one
of ours?” he asks. I think that’s a very powerful question that
we have stopped asking as a society. As I wrote in a Daily Dad
email (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/dpheh0hevpexrkhm/aHR0cHM6Ly9kYWlseWRhZC5jb20vd2hhdC1pZi1pdC13YXMtb25lLW9mLXlvdXJzLw==
) a while back (subscribe here for a parenting meditation each
morning (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/e0hph7h76r7z5wa8/aHR0cHM6Ly9kYWlseWRhZC5jb20v
)), we have to stand up and do what’s right for “our kids,” the
ones within our family, yes, but also our community, our country
and our world.

​War Is a Racket (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/7qh7h8h9qn9e3mcz/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXItaXMtYS1yYWNrZXQtd2FyYmxlci1jbGFzc2ljcy1hbm5vdGF0ZWQtZWRpdGlvbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD04ZDE1ODhmNjAmX3NzPXI=
) by General Smedley Butler (80 Pages)
​Considering what’s happening right now in the world, I think
everyone needs to read this book. Written in 1935, this classic
by one of the most decorated Marines in U.S. history is even more
relevant today than it was when it came out. I love his
definition of a racket, which he describes as “something that is
not what it seems to the majority of people. Only a small inside
group knows what it is about. It is conducted for the benefit of
the very few at the expense of the very many.” (I actually used
this definition in the preface of Trust Me I’m Lying (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/owhkhqhwpqwe58cv/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yeWFuNj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0wZDMxYzg4MGEmX3NzPXI=
)). This is a powerful book written by someone who saw 19th and
20th century imperialism up close, who was sent to fight and die
in wars that the vast majority of people would not have supported
if they had been given the choice (or the full story). History
repeats itself. Will you fall for it?

​Montaigne (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/z2hghnhenlez0otp/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9iaW9ncmFwaHkxNz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD03YWEzYWRjMzMmX3NzPXI=
) by Stefan Zweig (160) Pages
​Why do I keep raving about this book? Zweig was right when he
said that Montaigne was a man for our times. This is the book for
our times. It’s about a man (Montaigne) who turned inward amidst
the cruelty and close-mindedness of his time (religious
persecution, violence and disruption), written by a man (Zweig)
living in exile and fleeing the brutality and horrors of his own
time after the Nazis burned his books and banned his work. This
is one of the most beautiful and eye-opening perspectives you’ll
get on Montaigne. I loved it so much that I bought 1,000 copies
of it (read more about that here (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/p8heh9h4mq4e8ziq/aHR0cHM6Ly9yeWFuaG9saWRheS5uZXQvcmVhZC10aGlzLWJvb2std2hlbi10aGUtd29ybGQtZmVlbHMtbGlrZS1pdHMtZmFsbGluZy1hcGFydC8=
)). I think we’re one of the only bookstores in the country that
carries it, so be sure to grab yours at this link (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/z2hghnhenlez0otp/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9iaW9ncmFwaHkxNz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD03YWEzYWRjMzMmX3NzPXI=

​Gift from the Sea (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/x0hph6heoqemxes5/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9naWZ0LWZyb20tdGhlLXNlYS01MHRoLWFubml2ZXJzYXJ5LWVkaXRpb24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTFkMjFjNWY5Jl9zcz1y
) by Anne Morrow Lindbergh (160) Pages
​The original subtitle of this book? “An answer to the conflicts
in our lives.” Writing in 1955, Lindbergh was referring to the
challenging demands of what was then the newly modern world, the
changing roles of women in that world and workplace, the looming
Cold War and the timeless need for rest and relaxation and
perspective. It was the bestselling book in the country the year
it came out…and continues to sell for the same reasons–because
the world is just as noisy and complicated and overwhelming as it
was then. For each chapter, Lindbergh takes a shell from the
beach as the starting point for a meditation on topics like
solitude, love, happiness, contentment, and so on. For a
67-year-old book, it feels surprisingly modern–especially, I
would think, for women. I talked about this book at length in a
recent YouTube video, which you can check out here (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/6qheh8hlz9l40nuo/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vd2F0Y2g_dj1FZnV1bExLRDVmVQ==
). (It’s worth pointing out that both the Lindberghs were
seriously problematic people, but that doesn’t change that there
is serious wisdom in this book.)

​The War of Art: Break Through the Blocks and Win Your Inner
Creative Battles (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/kkhmh6hnrxnp4mfl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXItb2YtYXJ0LWJyZWFrLXRocm91Z2gtdGhlLWJsb2Nrcy1hbmQtd2luLXlvdXItaW5uZXItY3JlYXRpdmUtYmF0dGxlcz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1kNjdlNzdiNzYmX3NzPXI=
) by Steven Pressfield (190) Pages
​Before I start any book project, I take a few hours and re-read
The War of Art (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/58hvh7hgq6gwlls6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXItb2YtYXJ0LWJyZWFrLXRocm91Z2gtdGhlLWJsb2Nrcy1hbmQtd2luLXlvdXItaW5uZXItY3JlYXRpdmUtYmF0dGxlcz9fcG9zPTQmX3NpZD1jYzhjMDM3ODQmX3NzPXI=
) by Steven Pressfield, which is maybe the greatest book ever
written on the creative process–a series of meditations on the
battle that any artist (or person) fights as they strive to
battle their demons. In it, Pressfield breaks down what he calls
“The Resistance”, or the force within us that we allow to hold us
back from success. It’s the fundamental battle between the lower
and higher self that all creative people face. If you want to
succeed, you have to be heroic in that battle. This book helps
you do that. I also like Turning Pro (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/25h2hoh36m3xnlt3/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL3RTVHJv
), if you’ve already read The War of Art (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/qvh8h7hdzkd3qzil/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy93YXItb2YtYXJ0LWJyZWFrLXRocm91Z2gtdGhlLWJsb2Nrcy1hbmQtd2luLXlvdXItaW5uZXItY3JlYXRpdmUtYmF0dGxlcz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD02MjMwMGI0MWYmX3NzPXI=
).

​84, Charing Cross Road (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/g3hnh5hmrqm2x9hr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy84NC1jaGFyaW5nLWNyb3NzLXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NzE5MTgwOGY4Jl9zcz1y
) by Helene Hanff (112 Pages) and Address Unknown (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/9qhzhnhd05d8nrt9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hZGRyZXNzLXVua25vd24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTI1MmRlOTA3Jl9zcz1y
) by Kathrine Kressmann Taylor (96 Pages)
​You wouldn’t think that you could get a book out of a New York
TV writer and a British bookseller exchanging letters in the
aftermath of WWII, but you can. And it’s awesome. It’s a nice
celebration of the joys of discovery, of reading and of
friendship in unlikely places. It’s also a reminder to any of us
who have been planning to take a trip somewhere or have been
meaning to go see a friend who lives far away: Don’t wait, you
might miss your chance. This book reminded me of another
epistolary novella, Address Unknown (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/9qhzhnhd05d8nrt9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hZGRyZXNzLXVua25vd24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTI1MmRlOTA3Jl9zcz1y
). Unlike Charing Cross Road (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/g3hnh5hmrqm2x9hr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy84NC1jaGFyaW5nLWNyb3NzLXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NzE5MTgwOGY4Jl9zcz1y
), Address Unknown (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/9qhzhnhd05d8nrt9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9hZGRyZXNzLXVua25vd24_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9MTI1MmRlOTA3Jl9zcz1y
) is fictional, but the book similarly follows the exchanges
between two business partners (one Jewish, one not) during the
rise of Hitler in Germany. One is slowly corrupted by the events
happening around him, his heart closing to the people and ideas
he once believed in. It’s a heartbreaking but eye-opening look at
the banality of fascism. People don’t just suddenly become evil
or awful. It’s a process, a slide, even a response to incentives.
It can happen to anyone. We should all be careful.

​Zen in the Art of Archery (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/3ohphkh30p38vlbr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MjQ=
) by Eugen Herrigel (96 Pages) and Book of Five Rings: A Classic

) by Miyamoto Musashi (160 Pages)
​Zen in the Art of Archery (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/3ohphkh30p38vlbr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MjQ=
) is ultimately not a book about archery, but about Zen, and the
mastery of the soul. In it, Eugen Herrigel learns that a good
archer is one who shoots well, not necessarily the one always
hitting the target. Because what matters is the moment and
practice, not the outcome. I would recommend this book (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/48hvhehmvxm3k4tx/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9jb2xsZWN0aW9ucy92ZW5kb3JzP3E9RXVnZW4lMjBIZXJyaWdlbA==
) to anyone who is doing anything that requires focus and
concentration, or anyone whose ego and impatience are causing
them problems. And a related recommendation would be Musashi’s
Book of Five Rings (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/n2hohvhvm7v4e9t6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9ib29rLW9mLWZpdmUtcmluZ3MtYS1jbGFzc2ljLXRleHQtb24tdGhlLWphcGFuZXNlLXdheS1vZi10aGUtc3dvcmQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9NzdhNGY4MjUxJl9zcz1y
), which is also about the mindset, the discipline, and the
perception necessary to win in life-or-death situations. As a
swordsman, Musashi fought mostly by himself, for himself. His
wisdom, therefore, is mostly internal. He tells you how to
out-think and out-move your enemies. He tells you how to fend for
yourself and live by a code. And isn’t that precisely what so
many of us need help with every day?

​Ain’t I A Woman (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/wnh2hghqn0qd80c7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9haW50LWktYS13b21hbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD03NGQ2YThhOGEmX3NzPXI=
) by Sojourner Truth (80 Pages)
​​In her time, Sojourner Truth was triply discriminated against.
A woman. Black. A former slave. She would fight tirelessly for
the post-war amendment, even though it effectively only gave
Black men their rights. But once the ice was cracked, she said,
she kept stirring and stirring. Ain’t I A Woman (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/wnh2hghqn0qd80c7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9haW50LWktYS13b21hbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD03NGQ2YThhOGEmX3NzPXI=
) is a collection of Sojourner’s best speeches and stories. She
actually would never have said something like ‘Ain’t I A Woman’
because she was not from the South…she was a slave in New York
City and in fact, spoke Dutch! The slave dialect attributed to
her was a journalistic invention. But this is a powerful book
from a civil rights pioneer, whose courage and eloquence made us
all better off. Definitely pick this one up (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/38ud9v6kreskh2m977dtrh4pz9qogb7h4g0oz/wnh2hghqn0qd80c7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9haW50LWktYS13b21hbj9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD03NGQ2YThhOGEmX3NzPXI=
)–it’s part of the same collection as On the Shortness of Life (
