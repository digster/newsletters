was to do Oprah (which will come out soon). Why on earth did
Oprah want to talk to me? It was because of Maria Semple’s
absolutely lovely new book, Go Gentle (

https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkxlzndbphwl3m62vf6h384w7/z2hghnhexe25m2fp/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtYmVzdC1hbmQtdGhlLWJyaWdodGVzdD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD01ZDAwMDVhZDcmX3NzPXI=
) as the absolute masterpiece that it was. I just don’t know how
he does it–not just the reporting, but his ability to get to the
essence of the people he writes about. It’s not journalism,
honestly, it’s literature. Like if you told me The Best and the
Brightest (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkxlzndbphwl3m62vf6h384w7/z2hghnhexe25m2fp/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtYmVzdC1hbmQtdGhlLWJyaWdodGVzdD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD01ZDAwMDVhZDcmX3NzPXI=
) was a novel about a fictional war, I’d still be recommending
it! (Both because it’s literature and because it would teach you
about human folly). How did we blunder into this war? How did we
mismanage it once we did? How did the personality flaws–but also
the virtues of Kennedy, LBJ, Dean Rusk, McNamara, Maxwell Taylor,
George Ball, John Kenneth Galbraith–make that not only possible,
but in a way, make it a foregone conclusion? As always, the way
to understand the present is by studying the past. I chose to
read this book instead of watching the news and it’s made me a
more informed citizen. It also gives me a pit in my stomach about
our recent foreign interventionisms (General Mattis said the same
thing at Stanford).

​Speaking of Stoicism and Stanford…I also got to speak to Jim
Collins this month (
https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/zluo4w6lr9tnhkxlzndbphwl3m62vf6h384w7/9qhzhnhdrdv48xa9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cueW91dHViZS5jb20vd2F0Y2g_dj1veXV4QWFrZ01rYw==

Stockdale (and is based on Stoicism). His new book is really good

culture or business figures and how they dealt with the problems
and opportunities that life gave them. He studies the diverging
paths taken when faced with similar, life-altering “cliff
moments,” and uses those patterns to explore how we can most
meaningfully answer the question of what to make of our lives.
Jim sent me an early edition of the book back in January, in
which he included a note that read: “Ryan, this is the best work
of my life to date. It transformed me over 12 years. I’ll be
curious how it changes how you look at life. Enjoy, Jim.” And it
did. It changed how I look at life. It’s a lovely book (
