One of my favorite feelings is tearing through a book. Like when you get hooked and you feel like you can’t stop. Just a few more pages, you tell yourself. I’ll read to the next chapter. Closely related is the feeling–which is compounded when you are researching–when you find exactly the book you were looking for. How did this author read my mind? How did they know I needed this? It’s like that line from James Baldwin about how you think what you’re going through or thinking about is so unique and special and then you read and discover it’s actually an experience that connects you to countless other people. I had both those experiences this month with multiple books (I’ll tell you which ones below). I used to get that experience more when I was younger, but I cherish it more now that I am older. [Over the Beach: The Air War in Vietnam](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/g3hnh5hm5dw8xnfr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9vdmVyLXRoZS1iZWFjaC10aGUtYWlyLXdhci1pbi12aWV0bmFtP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTgyZThkZjFjZSZfc3M9cg==) by Zalin Grant
I’ve been raving about James Salter’s [The Hunters](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/9qhzhnhdmqrvn3t9/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy90aGUtaHVudGVycz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1kMzg4ZmI4MGMmX3NzPXI=) recently, which I re-read on a lark. It’s a Hemingway-esque novel about a fighter pilot trying to make Ace in Korea, written by a criminally underrated writer (and real-life fighter pilot). I read [Over the Beach](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/g3hnh5hm5dw8xnfr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9vdmVyLXRoZS1iZWFjaC10aGUtYWlyLXdhci1pbi12aWV0bmFtP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTgyZThkZjFjZSZfc3M9cg==) after, which is basically the nonfiction, Vietnam version of this same book. It was exactly what I was looking for in my research for the Stockdale project I am on and I was riveted by every page. I literally had to stop at times because I was taking so many notes. It follows Squadron 162, flying off the USS Oriskany at Yankee Station in the South China Sea. Zalin Grant interviewed the pilots during the war and then checked in with them some twenty years later when he decided to write the book–so there is the perfect amount of detail and reflection. Ignore the subtitle, it makes it sound like a history when really it feels more like narrative nonfiction. It’s an amazing book–very “New Journalism”–one that I hope to bring back to a wide audience as I’ve done for some other books that I stumbled upon (like [Night of the Grizzlies](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/3ohphkh3log5vncr/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9uaWdodC1vZi10aGUtZ3JpenpsaWVzP19wb3M9MSZfc2lkPTVmZjk3NzUyNCZfc3M9cg==)).

Ok, so I read [Small Things Like These](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/48hvhehme58dk0ax/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9zbWFsbC10aGluZ3MtbGlrZS10aGVzZT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD05ZmEyZWVmZTgmX3NzPXI=) last year and was blown away. I’ve raved about it many times since. Maybe it was so perfect that I was a little intimidated to pick up anything else by Claire Keegan, but I finally got over it and read this one on my wife’s recommendation. I started it at lunch and finished it over dinner. The first story, I immediately texted Ramit Sethi about and told him he needed to read. The second, I texted Steven Pressfield about and told him he needed to read. And the third? I called my wife and said, WHAT?!? Is there anyone who can, page for page, [match Keegan’s little books](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/wnh2hghqp5628wa7/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9zZWFyY2g_dHlwZT1wcm9kdWN0JnE9Y2xhaXJlJTIwa2VlZ2FuJTJB)? [In The Days of My Youth I Was Told What It Means To Be A Man: A Memoir](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/08hwh9h25or6qgtl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pbi10aGUtZGF5cy1vZi1teS15b3V0aC1pLXdhcy10b2xkLXdoYXQtaXQtbWVhbnMtdG8tYmUtYS1tYW4tYS1tZW1vaXItY29weT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0zYjAyMmY0NGEmX3NzPXI=) by Tom Junod
You might not know Tom Junod…but you do. You have almost certainly read an article of his over the years because he’s one of the great longform journalists of our time. [His famous piece on Mr. Rogers](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/8ghqhohov0n6k9ck/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZXNxdWlyZS5jb20vZW50ZXJ0YWlubWVudC90di9hMjcxMzQvY2FuLXlvdS1zYXktaGVyby1lc3ExMTk4Lw==)? That got turned into the Tom Hanks movie. His [piece on that famous, heartwrenching 9/11 photo](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/vqh3hrho7xr988hg/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZXNxdWlyZS5jb20vbmV3cy1wb2xpdGljcy9hNDgwMzEvdGhlLWZhbGxpbmctbWFuLXRvbS1qdW5vZC8=). Well, he spent the last two decades or so working on this book. It’s a memoir–an investigation really–into his father, a complicated, twisted, tragic figure. I don’t know how to describe this book really, except that it’s profoundly personal and moving…and also unfolds like a mystery within a mystery within a mystery. You get to read Tom Junod turn his considerable skills on himself. [You just gotta read it](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/08hwh9h25or6qgtl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pbi10aGUtZGF5cy1vZi1teS15b3V0aC1pLXdhcy10b2xkLXdoYXQtaXQtbWVhbnMtdG8tYmUtYS1tYW4tYS1tZW1vaXItY29weT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0zYjAyMmY0NGEmX3NzPXI=). Also, related recommendation, if you had issues with your father: Kafka’s [Letter to the Father](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/l2hehmhl907mwqt6/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9sZXR0ZXItdG8tdGhlLWZhdGhlci1icmllZi1hbi1kZW4tdmF0ZXItYmlsaW5ndWFsLWVkaXRpb24tdGhlLXNjaG9ja2VuLWthZmthLWxpYnJhcnk_X3Bvcz0yJl9zaWQ9NGJkZmM1MDAxJl9zcz1y) is a very underrated, short little book. I asked Tom if he read it and he co-signs on this recommendation. Tom came to the store and signed a bunch of copies when we filmed an episode of [The Daily Stoic Podcast](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/m2h7h5h3pgz6wvcm/aHR0cHM6Ly9wb2RjYXN0cy5hcHBsZS5jb20vdXMvcG9kY2FzdC90aGUtZGFpbHktc3RvaWMvaWQxNDMwMzE1OTMx) (coming soon), [you can grab those signed copies here](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/08hwh9h25or6qgtl/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9pbi10aGUtZGF5cy1vZi1teS15b3V0aC1pLXdhcy10b2xkLXdoYXQtaXQtbWVhbnMtdG8tYmUtYS1tYW4tYS1tZW1vaXItY29weT9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD0zYjAyMmY0NGEmX3NzPXI=).
[Light Years](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/dpheh0heg4n9r6sm/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9saWdodC15ZWFycz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1kOTAxY2NmZjkmX3NzPXI=) by James Salter
Another Salter book? Yup. This is Salter’s portrait of a marriage slowly coming apart. But before it does, you get some of the most beautiful passages you will ever read about family life (of course you will, Salter is writing them!). As it happens, there is a great passage in the book…about great passages! “The book was in her lap; she had read no further. The power to change one’s life comes from a paragraph, a lone remark. The lines that penetrate us are slender, like the flukes that live in river water and enter the bodies of swimmers. She was excited, filled with strength. The polished sentences had arrived, it seemed, like so many other things, at just the right time. How can we imagine what our lives should be without the illumination of the lives of others?” Reading [this book](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/dpheh0heg4n9r6sm/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9saWdodC15ZWFycz9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD1kOTAxY2NmZjkmX3NzPXI=), I kept thinking of Yates’ [Revolutionary Road](https://bc5923e4.click.convertkit-mail4.com/n4u3gkew8pbvhxp02mxb6h64l2wdltlhonl5q/e0hph7h7gpo35os8/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9yZXZvbHV0aW9uYXJ5LXJvYWQ_X3Bvcz0xJl9zaWQ9ODE2ZGFkODg2Jl9zcz1y), which my wife and I talked about recently. That idea of putting plans–dreams–off to the future, well, it can be a mortal blow to a marriage. Then again, so can naivete and selfishness. That’s sort of the theme of both these books. Great fiction hits you hard, doesn’t it?
