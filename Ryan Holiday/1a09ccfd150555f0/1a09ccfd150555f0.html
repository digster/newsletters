This Is The Secret To Being a Great Reader

I’ve been sending out this Reading List Email since May 2009.

Looking back, I’m struck by how much better I’ve gotten at reading over these seventeen-plus years. It’s sort of a strange thing to say because we tend to think of reading as something you either do or don’t do, not as a practice, as a skill, as something you can get better at. But of course it is. There is a huge difference between reading and reading well.

Reading is a kind of craft, something you have to work to improve at just as one pursues the mastery of any other skill. And also like every other skill, you don’t become a better reader by accident—or even simply doing it a lot. As Epictetus said, “I cannot call somebody ‘hard-working’ knowing only that they read.” He needed to know what they were reading. It’s always dangerous to try to improve on Epictetus but I think we need to add that it also matters how they read.

Sadly, too many people are not taught how to read well. I certainly wasn’t. Becoming a better reader has been a long, slow process of trial and error, taking habits and practices from great readers I’ve gotten to know, and studying how history’s greatest readers read—gradually piecing together a system, a process that actually makes for better, more productive reading. I’ve been lucky enough to have some great mentors in this process (Robert Greene teaching me his notecard system for example). A lot of it has come down to identifying as a professional reader, and by that I don’t just mean I read as part of my job as a writer or a bookstore owner, but that I see reading as my job.

It makes me a better person. It makes me a better parent. It makes me a better husband. It makes me a better boss. It makes me better at everything I do. So I take it seriously. I invest in learning how to do it well.

That’s the idea behind Read to Lead: A Daily Stoic Reading Challenge—which we are re-launching right now.

It was painstakingly designed to distill everything I’ve learned about being a great reader to help you learn in a few weeks what took me nearly two decades to figure out.

​Read to Lead takes place across 6 weeks at a pace of one challenge every three days (15 challenges and more than 30,000 words of exclusive content), with each challenge built around a specific practice for becoming a better reader. The challenges cover everything from…

How do you find books with the potential of changing your life?

How do you retain more of what you read?

When is it better to quit a book than push through it?

When is a book worth rereading?

How do you tackle a book that’s above your level?

How do you get out of a reading slump?

And most importantly, How do you take what you read and apply it in your actual life?

What I’m not interested in are hacks or tricks. I won’t tell you how to learn how to be a speed reader—that’s a scam. I’m not going to show you how to use AI to get out of having to pick up a book. I’m not going to tell you to play your audiobooks at 3X speed. I’m not going to show you how to shut off that little voice in your head so you can supposedly race through pages faster.

The whole point of reading is that it’s hard, but that the hard is worth it. There is a payoff and that payoff is priceless wisdom. Reading is not about impressing people or winning some game of mental gymnastics. It’s not about winning trophies for your shelf. It’s not about collecting random pieces of information. It’s about accumulating a mass of true wisdom—that you can turn to and apply in your actual day-to-day life.

In Read to Lead, you’ll learn how to do exactly that.
