Many different cultures observed that trepanning sometimes worked for hematomas. These cases were rare, but they were very dramatic: a comatose person on the edge of death suddenly wakes up and feels fine. The prehistoric people who observed this didn’t know what a hematoma was, so they came up with some other explanation (classically “it lets out the evil spirits”). Then they tried the same procedure for other ailments, (5-10% of people do get migraines; if you already know that drilling a hole in the skull to let the evil spirits out works sometimes, it probably feels pretty tempting several hours into a migraine attack) Next question: how did these people survive, when modern brain surgery requires careful sterilization? The most technically correct answer is that it didn’t; occasional examples survive into historical times, and surgeons use a functionally-similar procedure to relieve hematoma today. But it stopped happening to 5-10% of people, probably because the rate of injuries declined with the advent of civilization. As people got fewer head traumas, there were fewer cases of miraculous recovery, people stopped being so impressed with “letting the evil spirits out” as a cure for everything, and they came up with better placebos like prayer or amulets or resveratrol.
