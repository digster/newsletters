AI companies like Anthropic and OpenAI have long mentioned the possibility that their products could be existentially dangerous, but this has recently crescendoed into even more dire warnings, including suggestions that we have to In response, cynical commentators have suggested this is a conspiracy to boost the companies’ stocks. The exact motives imputed vary from person to person. Some say the companies are hyping the product by talking about how dangerous it is; if AI is smart enough to kill all humans, it must be smart enough for your business workflow. Others say they’re trying to distract from near-term harms; every inch of newspaper space devoted to human extinction is one less inch that can be spent on plagiarism and water use. But the most popular story involves regulatory capture: if the product is unsafe, maybe it can only be trusted to a few big politically-connected companies, and everyone else should be banned. Many debates about AI are complicated and hard to resolve, but this one isn’t: all of these stories are clearly false. Some evidence: The AI experts who aren’t affiliated with big companies are just as concerned as the ones who are. The AI company leaders giving these warnings were giving the exact same warnings before they had AI companies - and in many cases, before they worked in the industry, or before the industry existed. If these corporate plots were real, they would be incredibly dumb.

III. If This Is A Plot, It’s A Really Dumb OneHaving shown that these men would have to be the smartest people in the world to pull this off, it’s worth noting that the plot they apparently dedicated their lives to is really dumb. For example, if the Pacing The Frontier consortium attempt was a plot to pump stock prices, you might expect that, upon it being announced, stock prices would go up. Anthropic and OpenAI aren’t publicly traded companies, so they don’t have an easily-tracked stock price. But there are complicated cryptocurrency bets called “perpetual futures” that traders use to approximate the stock prices of private companies (usually these are some kind of prediction-market like instrument on eventual IPO price). When the slowdown call happened, Anthropic’s value on the biggest cryptocurrency futures platform dropped 4.57%, and OpenAI’s stock dropped 13.42%. (two other AI companies that endorsed the slowdown proposal, Google DeepMind and X.AI, are subsidiaries of larger publicly traded companies; Google and SpaceX respectively. Both companies went down modestly the day after the announcement, although they have so many other things going on that I take this less seriously than the AI company futures.) Why wouldn’t the stock market (or at least this particular cryptocurrency simulation of the stock market) approve of the slowdown as a potential regulatory moat? It seems like traders thought the first-level effects (slowing down) were more important than any secondary regulatory effects that might or might not materialize.
