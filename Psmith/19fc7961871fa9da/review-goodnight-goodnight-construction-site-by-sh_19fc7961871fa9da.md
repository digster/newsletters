I have read aloud a great many books about construction vehicles. So obviously, I can recite this entire book from memory. It’s a nice book. The pictures are pretty. The words actually scan, which should be a given for children’s books and unfortunately is not. The plot, such as it is, consists of a whole slew of smiling trucks (Crane Truck, Cement Mixer, Dump Truck, and so forth) finishing their “rough-and-tough construction play” and going peacefully to bed beneath the moon. (The quiet hope is of course that the small child on your lap will emulate the trucks instead of getting up in five minutes to see if the big people are doing something interesting like eating ice cream or having an uninterrupted conversation.) The sunset colors of the illustrations are eye-catching without being lurid, the truck characters manage to strike the right balance between realism and friendly anthropomorphism, and my toddler loves to point out Crane Truck’s teddy bear. So I really do hate to be a pedant here when I point out that trucks do not, in fact, go to sleep. The people who operate trucks go to sleep. Trucks are like guns in this way. Today all these things look the same. Whether Mom is putting on music, sending a photo, reading a book, or ordering pizza: there she is, wherever she happens to be, head bent over a shiny black rectangle, poking it with her fingertip.
