Before Joon has an advantage in rehearsing these encounters, and thousands of others. He is the Co-founder and CEO of Simile, an applied AI company building a foundation model of human behavior. That involves creating digital twins of real people – like a writer or podcaster – and simulating how they might react to certain circumstances. As you might imagine, that’s an attractive proposition for many companies: CVS Health, for instance, can test a new pricing policy against thousands of simulated patients. A bank can model how customers will react before announcing a fee change. Joon’s ambitions stretch far beyond these cases, though, and Simile has raised more than $300 million, most recently at a $2 billion valuation, to pursue them. My notebookThe portrait artist always paints themselves. I loved this line from Joon, who happens to be a gifted painter in his own right. When apprehending a piece of art – whatever the medium – I can sometimes forget to consider what it tells us about the author rather than the subject. “Emotional rigor.” Another phrase of Joon’s that I’ve been sitting with. This was how he described the intensity one needs to build to paint a beautiful painting of someone. You need to build this deep, rigorous connection. This is a universal lesson, I think, and especially true in writing. Contradictory superpowers. Another really sharp heuristic (can you tell I enjoyed this conversation quite a lot?). When it comes to hiring, this is what Joon looks for: someone with two strengths that rarely go together. For example, a colleague with deep scientific seriousness and a very playful side. Working backwards. Joon’s ultimate goal is not to give Simile a question and have it make a prediction. It is to give a “wicked” problem like climate change and have it simulate a superabundance of possible paths to help us find the best one. Who wins when everything is predictable? If simulation becomes as ubiquitous as Simile suggests it might, how do companies compete with one another? Is “strategy,” as a broad discipline, solved? Joon pointed to a few thoughts on where edge might still live – namely in point of view, proprietary data, and compute. Now, this is just a thought experiment, of course. But two of those three presumably redound to the world’s best-resourced companies, which is far from ideal.
