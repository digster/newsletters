For the rest of today's newsletter, I wanted to re-share a popular reflection I wrote on agency.

As I've been creating the curriculum for More To Build, one thing I've realized is just how important it is to be curious and to learn things yourself. It's probably one of the greatest skill sets to cultivate as the landscape continues to change.

But that desire to learn must come from within; otherwise, it will always be fleeting.

This section of the reflection sums it up well:

So how do you cultivate the desire to learn? Well, the first thing is to decouple an interest from its practical value. Instead of embarking on something with an end goal in mind, you do it for its own sake. You don’t learn because of the career path it’ll open up, but because you often wonder about the topic at hand.

Paradoxically, this sense of wonderment is what will lead to career opportunities as well. Because in the long-run, wonderment is what leads to persistence, which is what the world rewards.

A Book I've Been Enjoying

​Co-Existence by Ethan Mollick — I started this book last week, and it's been a wonderful read so far. Ethan has a way of explaining AI in a way that's approachable and fun, which is a rare combination in this field.

There was a statistic in here that surprised me, which I'll share below (emphasis mine):

In September 2025, OpenAI released the first large-scale analysis of actual ChatGPT usage. The findings offer a clearer picture of what a billion people are actually doing with AI. Companionship and emotional support? Less than 3 percent of all messages. Computer programming, despite dominating the discourse? Just over 4 percent. Nearly 80 percent of all conversations fall into just three categories: practical guidance (tutoring, how-to advice, personalized recommendations), seeking information (the AI version of Google), and writing (drafting, editing, translating).

I'm guessing that Codex took up most of the unreported coding tasks (and this study was done before 2026), but still... Only 4% of usage was spent on coding, which feels so low!

It's a reminder that you're not too late when it comes to AI, and that if you actually build things with it, you represent a tiny fraction of people that use AI each day.
