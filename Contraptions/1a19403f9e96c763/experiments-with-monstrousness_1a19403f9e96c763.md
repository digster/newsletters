This post is a rare handwritten one because the topic is personal enough it’s easier to just write it than to convey enough about myself to an AI to generate it. But by default, I always use AI for writing these days, and am enjoying my centaur-writing more with every post. With every post, I learn new tricks and techniques and improve steadily. Having to drink blood, avoid garlic, and not being able to go out in the sun is a small price to pay for this growing sense of nameless power. And I am getting good enough at it (logging a genuine AI-assisted viral hit is one sign) that even many of those who declare that they hate generated writing find themselves enjoying the writing, and then discovering after reading, much to their chagrin, that it was written with AI. I don’t even try to disguise the AI voice or characteristic LLM tics. Quite the opposite. I leave the natural terroir of the models I use almost untouched. Full collaborators deserve to speak in their own voice. I edit AI text the way I edit other human writers — in a maximally laissez-faire way, minimizing how much I reshape their native voice. The technology really is getting good enough that you can work this way. It feels increasingly silly to fight quixotic battles to go against the grain of the medium for no real benefit beyond pandering to the tastes of human supremacists. I saw a comment go by that I must have fine-tuned an AI model on my extensive (~2.5m words) corpus of handwritten archives to write with AI the way I do. Nope. It is vanilla ChatGPT (I save my Claude tokens for coding). I can probably teach people how to write like this, but 90% of it is just the courage to be slightly monstrous. Only 10% is teachable tips and tricks with prompts and harnesses. If you think you have an aptitude for writing, all you really need to do to transform that into an aptitude for writing with AI is to risk the scorn, contempt, and opprobrium of your tastefully aghast writerly friends. And once you get experimenting, the learning and dopamine follow, you make new friends, and life goes on. It’s really not that hard. As Douglas Adams observed, “if life is going to exist in a Universe of this size, then the one thing it cannot afford to have is a sense of proportion.” So you have to periodically choose how exactly you’re going to distort your sense of proportions. A dash of psychosis seems to work very well for 2026, the way a dash of neurosis was helpful in getting through 2007-14. The optimal amount of derangement these days is not zero. Jokes aside, as you can probably tell, this is not really a bit. Be Slightly Monstrous is a serious philosophy for me. One that has taken shape over a decade, and that I’ve bet money and reputation on. What I do not mean by my motto is monstrousness in the sense of fantasy. What I do mean is monstrousness in a particular science-fictional sense (a Labatutian-Lovecraftian-Ballardian sense) — a slightly monstrous human is one who has been transformed through co-evolution with emerging technology in a Gramsci Gap, and who has come to viscerally embody the strange new rules of that technology. This notion emerged out of my other big obsession over the last 5 years, protocols. This has been the other half of my work in recent years, and it has shaped my philosophy and writing a lot (not least because it involved a lot of writing and editing of other people). We’ve dubbed this new genre protocol fiction. One of my own contributions to this emerging genre is this story, This leads to an interesting thought. What does it mean to not embrace co-evolution with technology; to not accept such transformation in what it means to be human? It means being attached to a particular, historically contingent frozen snapshot idea of what it means to be human, based on the people alive and grappling with the question of AI circa 2024. A chosen people, presumably representing the pure acme of human development, who are choosing to head down a cul-de-sac of being and becoming. Who increasingly inhabit an enervating fantasy of humanism; “acting dead” to borrow a term from Bruce Sterling. There’s a deep irony here that I’m still thinking through: That attempting to preserve your humanity as you navigate the Gramsci Gap may be the most deadening thing you can do, while embracing monstrousness may allow you to tap into the deepest kinds of liveness you can access today. Again, this is not a bit. And it’s not a drill either. It’s a real unfolding praxis for me. If using AI is going to do irreversible horrifying things to my brain, I’m already partway there.
