Thoughts On...📦 [Amazon's FedEx and UPS Killer](https://spyglass.org/r/7071c647?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) – This was [always](https://spyglass.org/r/dca8526c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) [inevitable](https://spyglass.org/r/90ec52cd?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). And [obvious](https://spyglass.org/r/a3c3881a?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). 'Amazon Supply Chain Services' may be an awful, generic name, but it will soon just be called 'ASCS', just like 'Amazon Web Services' is 'AWS'. Speaking of, that was clearly the model here. Build up the capabilities to do it for yourself, then open it up to the world. One big difference is the direct competition Amazon faces here in the aforementioned FedEx and UPS – both of which, are, of course, also partners. Or were. We'll see how both react now with [their stocks dropping around 10% on the news](https://spyglass.org/r/ce568628?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). And, of course, there's the US Postal Service, which Amazon just [struck a new deal with](https://spyglass.org/r/cb419749?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) after nearly ending their 250+ year run simply by suggesting they may no longer deal with them. While Amazon may not have a smartphone ([yet?](https://spyglass.org/r/64108844?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c)), they do have the physical logistical mechanism by which their competitors increasingly must play ball with them... [[Reuters](https://spyglass.org/r/242f91a5?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c)] 📲 [Can Intel Land the iPhone?](https://spyglass.org/r/bd03ab43?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) – Are we about to see Apple with Intel Inside yet again? Not so fast. While it is potentially big news that Apple would work with Intel to make chips for their "main devices", it sounds like such discussions are super early. And concurrent to ones with Samsung, who would seem far better suited to handle such a task in the short term given their current position as a foundry (though obviously Intel's American roots [help narratively here](https://spyglass.org/r/06425fc1?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c), if nothing else). This is all an offshoot of [earlier reports](https://spyglass.org/r/3e2e78b6?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) that Apple was talking to Intel about working with them to fabricate chips for some lower-volume devices (read: not iPhones). But now that NVIDIA has started to dominate the TSMC relationship, to the point where even almighty Apple is being pushed back in the line, well, Apple needs to have other options. That said, it still feels like we're at least a few years from Intel reliably making chips on the type of cutting-edge nodes that TSMC can do – [if Intel can get there at all](https://spyglass.org/r/a0d1055b?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). Then again, TSMC is also [apparently](https://spyglass.org/r/c1c9e68a?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) cutting back on some costs for [cutting edge](https://spyglass.org/r/afa25c55?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) chips, so maybe there's a window... [It would be fun](https://spyglass.org/r/efdd2297?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) to have Intel back with Apple, even just in a way. [[Bloomberg](https://spyglass.org/r/eb446a4d?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) 🔒] 🥽 [Is Apple Killing the Vision Pro?](https://spyglass.org/r/031a090d?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) – No. But because of [an earlier report](https://spyglass.org/r/8751fc60?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) saying as much, John Gruber had to lay out the common sense argument as to why a funeral is not happening – at least not any time soon. Which is not to say the device is a success. Obviously, [it's not](https://spyglass.org/r/4314140c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). And while I think Apple [made a mistake](https://spyglass.org/r/79e202b3?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) is launching it before it was fully baked (including with developer support), it's slowly but surely getting more baked as an ecosystem [and product](https://spyglass.org/r/0509bda0?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) over time. A bunch of the [recent](https://spyglass.org/r/dba935e7?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) content [releases](https://spyglass.org/r/8c32d323?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) have been [good](https://spyglass.org/r/6dbbefbf?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c)! The problem is that the device itself remains far too cumbersome and [laborious to use](https://spyglass.org/r/660a0763?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). And, of course, is too expensive, perhaps by an order of magnitude. But if they can bring new elements of "Spatial Computing" up from the ground with a [new](https://spyglass.org/r/03e6fadc?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) Smartglasses product, while bringing others from the top down from Vision Pro, perhaps there's a sweet spot [in the middle](https://spyglass.org/r/f4216e4c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). The question is [timing](https://spyglass.org/r/126f699a?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) on that – is that a few years away, or a decade. It will be one of the key first questions [for John Ternus](https://spyglass.org/r/54dd4869?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c). But unless something goes wrong and Apple needs to start cutting costs, it's more like a product on ice for the moment. Entertaining relatively few of us! [[Daring Fireball](https://spyglass.org/r/78f3b099?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c)] 🛎️ [The End of Ask.com, RIP Jeeves](https://spyglass.org/r/300e0c1c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) – I was never much of an Ask Jeeves user – I went from Lycos and Yahoo to Metacrawler before switching, like everyone else, to Google – but I definitely have a fondness for both the idea of an "answer engine" and the [P.G. Wodehouse](https://spyglass.org/r/28d85e69?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c)-inspired [Jeeves](https://spyglass.org/r/443d0dde?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) character. Both seem more top of mind than ever in a way in our current Age of AI. LLMs now power "answer engines" in a way that web search [never fully could](https://spyglass.org/r/f42c26e4?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c), of course. And there's an open question as to whether or not to anthropomorphize the chatbots. Certainly, Siri and Alexa kicked this off in one direction, Microsoft has since tried to give newer AI [a face](https://spyglass.org/r/b872471e?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c), and voice [may be](https://spyglass.org/r/c087dfec?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) the thing that pushes it all over the edge. Regardless, how long until an AI service buys the ask.com domain from IAC (now, incidentally [called](https://spyglass.org/r/a7517ccb?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) 'People' after their publication) for a lot of money? Though probably less than the $1B+ IAC paid for the entire company back in 2005.
