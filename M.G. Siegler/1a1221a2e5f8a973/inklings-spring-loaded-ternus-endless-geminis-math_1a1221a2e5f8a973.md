[[Scientific American](https://spyglass.org/r/3a95d64d?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c)] 💸 [OpenAI's "Missing" $20B in Revenue](https://spyglass.org/r/5d967559?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) – Immediately, another movie scene pops into my mind, the viral clip of Robert Pattinson as Chris Hansen, ["You see how this looks, right?"](https://spyglass.org/r/a88ba11c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) And clearly, Wall Street agreed with that (damning) initial assessment. But actually, if you read beyond the headline, it's clear what happened here, and it's mostly optics (and a lack of visibility into private company numbers). As many are well aware by now, Anthropic and OpenAI's reported (as in via leaked reports and presentations, not by the company's themselves to the public) ARR numbers are not apples-to-apples. Because OpenAI largely strips out the cut of revenue that goes to their cloud partners while Anthropic does not (there are accounting reasons for doing this too, so it's not just about number inflation). So OpenAI ([or their investors](https://spyglass.org/r/536d9f00?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c)) tried to showcase what their ARR number would look like if they used Anthropic's method and, well, it's a lot higher than the number they usually report. Duh. This is also why ARR is [not a GAAP number](https://spyglass.org/r/0f0efb02?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) (though [those have](https://spyglass.org/r/112b99ad?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) issues [at times](https://spyglass.org/r/90252617?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c) too!) – because there are about two dozen ways to twist it for your own means. Better to just state revenue but that's obviously going to look less impressive (and, if we want to be fair here at all, doesn't showcase huge growth as clearly). Anyway, don't trust everything you read!
