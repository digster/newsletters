I Note...

💫 Google Announces Their 16th Agent and 12th Gemini – On one hand, I get it: Google is combating Microsoft, which just unified Copilot after a few years of confusing and conflicting sprawl. On the other, they're seemingly running the old Copilot playbook, by launching Gemini anywhere and everywhere in such a way that no one knows what to use for what. This new 'Gemini Agent' is not 'Gemini Spark' nor 'CC' nor any of the other agentic projects they have at the moment. Nor is it Gemini (chatbot) itself. Even better: this new Gemini (agent) can be powered by Claude and not Gemini (model). Too many cooks. Too many kitchens. Patrons confused. Going elsewhere. [VentureBeat]

🧮 The Mathpocalypse – First, they came for coding, and we said nothing. Then, they came for math, and... honestly, people are saying a lot about this. And it's probably both overblown and yet under-appreciated how much AI is impacting the field. The technology is seemingly solving things in ways we don't understand, which feels a lot like that Good Will Hunting scene, but with a robot. But here, it's yet another example of not just smarts, but scale. AI can run nearly infinite scenarios to brute force its way to solutions in ways humans cannot. And while everyone is just starting to gear up for the future where quantum computers can leverage similar dynamics to crack our encryption protocols, what if AI beats those machines to it? That feels like the real thing we should be focused on here and not academics complaining. [Scientific American]

💸 OpenAI's "Missing" $20B in Revenue – Immediately, another movie scene pops into my mind, the viral clip of Robert Pattinson as Chris Hansen, "You see how this looks, right?" And clearly, Wall Street agreed with that (damning) initial assessment. But actually, if you read beyond the headline, it's clear what happened here, and it's mostly optics (and a lack of visibility into private company numbers). As many are well aware by now, Anthropic and OpenAI's reported (as in via leaked reports and presentations, not by the company's themselves to the public) ARR numbers are not apples-to-apples. Because OpenAI largely strips out the cut of revenue that goes to their cloud partners while Anthropic does not (there are accounting reasons for doing this too, so it's not just about number inflation). So OpenAI (or their investors) tried to showcase what their ARR number would look like if they used Anthropic's method and, well, it's a lot higher than the number they usually report. Duh. This is also why ARR is not a GAAP number (though those have issues at times too!) – because there are about two dozen ways to twist it for your own means. Better to just state revenue but that's obviously going to look less impressive (and, if we want to be fair here at all, doesn't showcase huge growth as clearly). Anyway, don't trust everything you read! [FT 🔒]

🥧 First Googlebooks Taste Underbaked – The Verge seems a bit kind in their initial assessment here. Everything I'm reading points to a product not just underbaked, but half-baked at the moment. The move from Chromebooks to these should-be-called-Androidbooks seems filled with creaky or janky bits and the app ecosystem, while robust, sounds like a mess to actually use quite often (multiple versions of apps to choose from, issues with x86 compatibility, etc). Also, there's no Samsung phone support yet (they've said it's coming – but it's also interesting that Samsung is not actually making any Googlebooks, at least not yet), which seems like a massive issue given the scale of those devices. At least the "Glowbar" sounds better than the Pixel 'HiLight'... [Verge]
