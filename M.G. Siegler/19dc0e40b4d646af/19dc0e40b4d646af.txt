Thoughts On...

🍁 Cohere + Aleph Alpha [https://spyglass.org/r/7b507556?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – Begun, the great AI consolidation has. The newly combined $20B AI startup seemingly has a better shot at government and business contracts across the EU now, given Aleph's German roots and Cohere's bigger scale and more well-known pedigree. Still, beyond OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and everyone else [https://spyglass.org/r/8e2ac4d5?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] (all the "open" Chinese models and players?), they'll presumably be running head into Europe's bigger AI champion: Mistral. That is, unless Elon Musk can successfully buy them [https://spyglass.org/r/35e916ab?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] as well, which, while perhaps good for Mistral's tech and fundraising capabilities would immediately shove them into the US 'Big AI' bucket. And that plus the Musk factor would likely push more business Cohere's way. We'll see. Regardless, this is clearly the start of more consolidation in the space, following the news that SpaceX has a call option [https://spyglass.org/r/6f2a4fa7?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] to buy Cursor later this year. Cohere – by my count [https://spyglass.org/r/f39a1c33?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], backed by AMD, Cisco, NVIDIA, Oracle, and Salesforce clearly needed [https://spyglass.org/r/75611c90?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] an answer beyond being Canada's AI champion [https://spyglass.org/r/38085c5d?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], and they found one, but is even that enough? [FT [https://spyglass.org/r/3d6ae07a?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] 🔒]

🥔 GPT 5.5 [https://spyglass.org/r/e9acdb34?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – "Spud" seems nice and fast thus far. Greg Brockman, when not constantly saying "I see things a little differently..." to every single (good) question put forth by Alex Kantrowitz [https://spyglass.org/r/df5ea7d3?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] still gave a pretty good overview of the state of the model – generally how it can do more with less guidance. And it puts OpenAI back at the top [https://spyglass.org/r/8f95bea6?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] of most (but not all) of the key AI benchmarks, beating out last week's Anthropic Opus 4.7 and even the mythical Mythos [https://spyglass.org/r/11a31e9f?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] here and there (but Mythos still seems like it will be ahead if and when it's released broadly). Of course, that performance comes at a cost, quite literally, a higher cost on the API (though OpenAI is saying it's far more "token efficient" which should negate that). Mainly, I want to see how/if this alters the perception of OpenAI vs. Anthropic. Does this (plus the impressive new image model [https://spyglass.org/r/32dc33c3?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]) help them regain momentum? 5.5 will apparently [https://spyglass.org/r/cbfb8d3e?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] power the forthcoming [https://spyglass.org/r/0a62516c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] "Super App" [https://spyglass.org/r/e6686869?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]. More importantly for them [https://spyglass.org/r/8feb2c60?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], does it help power Codex versus Claude Code? It also sure feels like there's an effort to change the tone and tenor when talking about such releases by OpenAI's outward facing folks from Sam Altman on down? Though that could be just as much about the fact that Altman (and Brockman) are about to be thrown head-first into a very public trial against Elon Musk [https://spyglass.org/r/b8081aa4?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] next week [https://spyglass.org/r/ddcc4ff9?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]... [Axios [https://spyglass.org/r/bbc94584?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]]

🐳 DeepSeek V4 [https://spyglass.org/r/7f2c8d14?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – Do you hear that? No you don't hear anything? That is the sound of everybody talking about the latest version of DeepSeek. "No one cares." [https://spyglass.org/r/bdc5077c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] That's not fair, of course – it may end up another step for open source models with regard to coding and agentic usage – but relative to the "DeepSeek Moment" [https://spyglass.org/r/8e13d056?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] last year, it's sort of wild how few shits anyone gives now. The stock market is not only not crashing, it's up at the moment. All of this is undoubtedly [https://spyglass.org/r/f0b5683e?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] because of what ended up being the actual fallout [https://spyglass.org/r/dfe2f66e?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] from the famous "Sputnik Moment" – which was... not a whole lot [https://spyglass.org/r/abb6be61?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]. Some cheaper models, sure. But it's not like the top model makers were displaced, let alone world economies. If anything, it just opened up Big Tech's eyes to the potential problems [https://spyglass.org/r/ebdcdeba?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] of distillation and the US government to looking into the situation, including, potentially, with unauthorized usage of NVIDIA chips. Anyway, a couple new DeepSeek models are here now in preview. One seems to be the largest [https://spyglass.org/r/039083cb?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] open model ever and the other is perhaps the cheapest [https://spyglass.org/r/669eaca2?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]. But they're also not on par with the top frontier models – which unlike DeepSeek, are getting updated left and right at the moment (see: above). So... the question remains if open models [https://spyglass.org/r/bb8d9bae?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] truly reach "good enough" status for their cost advantages to eat into frontier's take. Meanwhile, some key models in China are following Meta [https://spyglass.org/r/ff1df3ff?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] and going the other way, locking down [https://spyglass.org/r/644fde19?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] the cutting edge... [Bloomberg [https://spyglass.org/r/9ca97f59?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] 🔒]

📈 Intel's Bounce Back [https://spyglass.org/r/cafaa0b2?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – With the stock up – checks ticker – 25% today, Intel share price is now past its Dot Com era peak. This is wild any way you slice it – that was 26 years ago – but especially given how [https://spyglass.org/r/7e9e657b?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] downtrodden [https://spyglass.org/r/e25b4414?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] Intel has been over the past many years after famously failing to capitalize on the shift to mobile and then, at first, to AI. But the broader chip boom is raising all boats. Intel may not make GPUs, but their CPUs are still vital for data centers (and yes, some PCs) and now it seems like Pat Gelsinger's big foundry bet [https://spyglass.org/r/f8837e85?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] may be starting to pay off, with Tesla signed on [https://spyglass.org/r/22ccff83?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] to use their advanced '14A' node (eventually). Sadly, Gelsinger didn't survive the Board [https://spyglass.org/r/786287d0?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] and Lip-Bu Tan was smart [https://spyglass.org/r/49135970?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] in playing the game on the field, as it were – which meant giving the government a taste [https://spyglass.org/r/7feb4d2e?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] of the upside here. That $8.9B for a 10% stake is now worth over $40B. Might Apple [https://spyglass.org/r/4bec4d16?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] get back on board with using Intel for at least some of their chip needs – maybe modems [https://spyglass.org/r/869c156a?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], the business they bought from Intel? Even just packaging? Seems like Amazon [https://spyglass.org/r/e88c735d?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] and Microsoft [https://spyglass.org/r/645732c0?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] are hanging around the rim. Intel isn't quite back at their peak market cap, which was just over $500B in August 2000, but they're close. The only problem? [https://spyglass.org/r/f6e54ca0?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] NVIDIA's market cap just shot past $5T again today by taking advantage of an opportunity Intel missed at first. [NYT [https://spyglass.org/r/4a9918af?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]]
