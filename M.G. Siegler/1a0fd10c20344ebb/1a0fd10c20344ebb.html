I Note...

🍎 A More Nimble Apple – While Apple has had some layoffs here and there over the past few years, the reality is that unlike their Big Tech peers, they haven't done the big shakeup, both post-COVID and now to reorient for the Age of AI. As unfortunate as it is for those losing jobs, this is the most obvious and natural time for Apple to do this with John Ternus taking charge. That said, Apple has never operated like the other Big Tech companies, so the most interesting element will be if they can actually operate at a more nimble cadence, moving away from once or twice-a-year big launches and more towards constant iteration. The body may well reject such organs! But yeah, you sort of have to try if you truly believe AI is going to fundamentally alter the way business is done. [Bloomberg 🔒]

🛰️ AT&T CEO Scoffs at Notion of SpaceX Phone Service – Is it viable for Starlink to build out a network and service to fully compete with the big players in telecom? I don't know, Elon Musk says a lot of things, and his timetables are always off. But I do know that if I were John Stankey, I probably wouldn't be giving such juicy quotes on the record that can easily come back to bite him in the ass. This space already contains two of the most well-known scoffs in business history – Palm CEO Ed Colligan dismissing the notion of the "PC guys" figuring out the smartphone space ahead of the iPhone and Steve Ballmer laughing off the prospect of the iPhone competing with his Windows Mobile strategy – "Nothing beats it" (with regard to the fiber network AT&T is laying) seems like one to keep track of, but there are a few in here. Then again, Stankey is not new to the old foot-in-mouth game – this is the guy who nearly ran HBO into the ground before he was eventually elevated to CEO of all of AT&T and spun out/merged the media properties (smart move) with David Zaslav's Discovery. Which, of course, paved the way for today's MountDanceWarBroDisco mega merger. [Axios]

🏔️ MountDanceWarBroDisco's Musical Merger Chairs – Speaking of, Matt Belloni has the latest on who is staying and going, when the deal actually and finally closes next week. (One prediction I will have gotten decidedly wrong for 2026.) I cannot wait to hear/see what name and logo they actually choose; so many options! (I would still advise to keep it simple and have Skydance the overall parent with each studio retaining their names, that's it. Especially if Skydance keeps buying up some other brands down the road...) So many butts, so few seats. Casey Bloys gets all of streaming (Paramount+ Plus HBO Max – can't wait for that eventual new name/brand too!) as Cindy Holland seemingly gives her two minutes notice when not picked. Mike De Luca and Pam Abdy are out after following up their insanely great 2025 with an insanely awful 2026. They'll be replaced by another duo, Josh Greenstein and Dana Goldberg to expand from running Paramount to Warner Bros as well with the comically bad mandate to hit quantity targets for film output (what could possibly go wrong?). Mark Thompson staying to run CNN with some sort of assurances that Bari Weiss won't go near it. Etc. [Puck 🔒]

🐉 Gemini 4 Argon's Conquest? – That sound you hear around the Gemini 4 'Argon' release is... near silence. Which is weird since it's the first flagship from Google in nearly a year – after failing to launch 3.5 Pro. Then again, it's only in select security preview with a few customers. But when Anthropic and OpenAI use that strategy – I mean, Mythos is still in such testing – there are seemingly endless headlines and talk still about the models. The major headline around Google's new model is that some people internally think it still might not be good enough. Oof. All Wall Street wants to know is: where is their Muse answer? One million output tokens seems wild though! [Bloomberg 🔒]

👊 David Fincher Backs Away from Netflix – First and foremost I'm sad that seemingly no headline went with 'Flight Club' here. Second, this does seems like yet more bad optics for Netflix. Not only does it follow The Duffer Brothers, Shawn Levy, and Noah Baumbach taking their talents elsewhere, Fincher was the OG in working with Netflix, starting with House of Cards and continuing on through many projects – including my beloved and missed Mindhunter. The upcoming Cliff Booth movie may not be his absolute last with Netflix, but it will be his last that's exclusive to them. Interestingly, that movie is getting a big theatrical release (thanks in part to Narnia shifting to next Spring), so presumably that's not the reason for ending the marriage, but it clearly was with both the Duffers and, to some extent, Levy, as Ted Sarandos directly confirmed in an interview this week (though he also downplayed the idea of it being a trend – yeah, I mean what else would he say?). So yeah, it sure feels like Netflix may be on the verge of doing a bigger theatrical push, with a literal vengeance. [Bloomberg 🔒]
