I Note...

💰 OpenAI's Path to $100B in Ad Revenue [https://spyglass.org/r/ec2759b7?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – Look, extrapolating future growth for startups is hard, if not impossible. Especially for high-growth startups and especially when launching new business lines. But suggesting that OpenAI's ads business will surpass $100B in revenue in 2030 – which is just four years from now – seems reckless? Even for internal projections. That would be 36% of the business that year as modeled. What if they don't hit it? What if they don't even come close? It's just a wild number. I mean, maybe they hit it, but there's basically no way they can no that now as again, they just started rolling out ads in tests [https://spyglass.org/r/b4dc14ad?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] on ChatGPT. So either this is an extremely loose model or they're planning to absolutely cram ads into the service over the next few years, perhaps regardless of the ROI. I will say, given Anthropic's very public – as in Super Bowl ad public – stance on ads, it's one area OpenAI can absolutely own versus their rival. Then again, there's Google sitting right there. Oh yes, and Meta. [Information [https://spyglass.org/r/722b3deb?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] 🔒]

💫 The Government Tries to Tackle Sports Streaming Rights [https://spyglass.org/r/a186c740?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – Well this will be a fun one for legal scholars. The DoJ is looking into if the NFL is being anticompetitive by spreading their games across so many different streaming options. Yes, in a sense, there is too much competition for their rights, which is spreading them wide [https://spyglass.org/r/7e72b27d?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], thus making it harder for consumers to watch. The old school networks, consumers, and the government will be on the same side of this [https://spyglass.org/r/64236494?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], while the tech companies and the NFL will be on the other side. This was all inevitable given the splintered mess [https://spyglass.org/r/767b5b35?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] that everyone now sees because there is no true unifying layer [https://spyglass.org/r/069da729?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] to make this consumer-friendly – like, you know, cable. And because of the myriad streaming options, we're likely now paying more [https://spyglass.org/r/25c65b1c?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], certainly to far more players, than we ever did to... cable. But how might the government tell the NFL that they can't maximize their profits? By no longer allowing the teams to band together for such rights packages? Regardless, this all just delays the inevitable: Big Tech will fully control sports rights [https://spyglass.org/r/01fad3b4?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] in a decade or two. [WSJ [https://spyglass.org/r/29aadf89?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] 🔒]

💻 MacBook Neo as the Best Thing to Happen to Windows [https://spyglass.org/r/6a53baa2?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – Tom Warren makes the case that Apple's new low-priced computer [https://spyglass.org/r/2ef5121f?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] will force Redmond to wake up to the reality that the PC market is sort of a mess and Windows in particular, sucks [https://spyglass.org/r/9f3a7cc4?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]. As he notes, Microsoft and the broader PC industry has historically performed better when a true challenger enters the picture – such as was the case with the shift to Apple Silicon. I mean, maybe. But it's also possible that Apple just eats the PC market's lunch here after years and years of refusing to compete at these price points. The MacBook Neo is that good [https://spyglass.org/r/c9983fe5?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c], and the current state of Windows is that bad [https://spyglass.org/r/66d33eac?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] – in particular as Microsoft keeps trying to shove Copilot [https://spyglass.org/r/0577d8e1?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] on to all surfaces – literally on the Surfaces. They're now pulling back [https://spyglass.org/r/330b393b?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c] after (predictable [https://spyglass.org/r/13368964?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]) backlash. But what other answer do they have here? [Verge [https://spyglass.org/r/1e1f22c0?m=1ed23d55-8119-4b10-b903-392c96d1198c]]
